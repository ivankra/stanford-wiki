# Materials and `past` redesign

How course pages rate what a non-enrolled reader can open, record their past offerings, and move to the new schema. The rules here go into AGENTS.md and the skills ([§11](#11-skills), [§12](#12-agentsmd-edits)). The prompt for migration agents is in [§14](#14-migration-prompt).

## 1. Goals

- **A finer overall access scale**, anchored on one principle ([§2](#2-principles)).
- **A separate dimension for self-study support**, so extra help for outside learners doesn't masquerade as an access level.
- **Current and past offerings kept apart.** A term rollover stops discarding history, historical rows in the term tables stop naming the current instructor, and every older offering's syllabus is captured while it can still be found, so the Evolution work gets its data.
- **A flat, simpler frontmatter.**
- **Migration of all 378 course pages**, course by course, with the judgement left to the migrating agent.

## 2. Principles

1. **Parity.** `access` measures the gap between what an enrolled student can open outside the classroom and what an outside reader can open. The comparison is with a student who doesn't attend in person. Material the course gives nobody (never recorded, never set) is not a gap. Parity is a strong signal toward `open`; coverage and relevance still decide.
2. **Judgement, not formula.** These are guidelines. Agents have the freedom and the responsibility to pick the best level within their spirit, not their letter. Consistency comes from calibration pages ([§4.8](#48-calibration-pages)) and from the decisive reason stated in the Materials section's opening sentence, never from counting types.
3. **`unknown` beats a guess.** `closed` needs positive evidence of a gate. `none` needs the course's own statement or its documented format; a failed search is never `none`.
4. **`past` holds observations, not inferences.** A key that is present was verified for that offering; a missing key means not recorded, never "same as now".

## 3. Course frontmatter

### 3.1 Key order

```text
Identity:       type, code, title, description, cross_listed, formerly, level
Offering:       term, terms_offered, instructors, schedule, units, grading, prerequisites
Content:        homepage, access,
                syllabus, slides, notes, videos, assignments, solutions, exams, projects, repo,
                sites, self_study, textbook, topics, past
Classification: tags
Provenance:     checked, status, generated, exceptions, sources
```

The `materials` map is gone. Keys that don't change are defined as they are today. Build sorts every key into this order, including the keys inside each `past` entry ([§9.3](#93-build)).

**Required and optional.** The Identity and Offering keys keep today's rules. In Content, `access` and `topics` are required, and `checked` is required once anything is rated. Everything else in Content is optional, as is `exceptions`. A URL list, `sites` and `self_study` are never empty: omit the key instead.

### 3.2 Material types

These nine keys are the material types. Each is rated; an omitted type is `unknown`.

| Key | Covers |
| --- | --- |
| `syllabus` | syllabus, schedule, grading policy, reading list |
| `slides` | lecture slides |
| `notes` | lecture notes, section handouts, a course reader or a staff-written book |
| `videos` | lecture and section recordings |
| `assignments` | problem sets, project specs, lab handouts |
| `solutions` | assignment and exam solutions |
| `exams` | past or practice exams |
| `projects` | student project reports, posters, showcases |
| `repo` | public code: repositories (usually GitHub), starter code, autograders |

`repo` replaces `materials.code`, since top-level `code` is the course code.

### 3.3 Value shapes

Each material type takes one of these forms:

| Form | Example | Meaning |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Bare rating | `videos: closed` | A rating with no link. |
| Bare URL | `slides: https://…` | Shorthand for `{ access: open, url }`. The author asserts that the collection is open; the migration never infers this from a URL. |
| List of URLs | `repo: [https://…, https://…]` | An open collection spread across several places. |
| Object | `{ access, url?, note?, checked? }` | `url` is one URL or a list, and `checked` dates a recheck of this entry alone ([§3.8](#38-checked)). `access` rates the whole collection, so several working links don't prove it's complete. |

- **A bare value starting with `http://` or `https://` is a URL; anything else is a rating.** That lets lint tell a mistyped rating from a malformed URL.
- **Entries carry no `term`.** The offering is either the page's `term` (top level) or the enclosing `past` term.

### 3.4 Per-type ratings

| Rating | Meaning |
| --- | --- |
| `open` | The offering's collection opens with no account, or with a free account anyone can create (a Google account, an email link), on reasonable inspection and representative checks. An exhaustive audit isn't required. When a free sign-in is needed, the note says so. |
| `partial` | A substantive part is public, with a meaningful gap. The note says where the boundary is. |
| `closed` | The material exists, and there is positive evidence of a gate: Stanford SSO, Canvas, Ed, Gradescope, the `code.stanford.edu` GitLab sign-in, a Google sign-in limited to Stanford accounts (`hd=stanford.edu` in the redirect), a login limited to enrolled students, or a 403. |
| `none` | The course produces it for nobody, per the course's own statement or its documented format. A lecture course that says it doesn't record gets `videos: none`. |
| `unknown` | Not checked, not found, or not enough evidence to say. Silence about recordings is `unknown`. |

Per-type ratings record access. Coverage (whether slides and notes together cover the lectures) is judged at the course level ([§4](#4-overall-access)), with any gap described in `note`.

### 3.5 `textbook`

- **Shape**: a free-text string, or a list of strings, naming the course's main textbooks. It has no rating and needs no URL; a link inside the text is allowed.
- **When**: only if the course has a textbook. Otherwise the key is omitted.
- **Scope**: it has no rating of its own. A textbook that is public, such as a free staff-written book, also counts as instruction under `notes`, and supports `access` there. `textbook` is allowed in `past`, and it appears as a "Textbook" bullet in the Materials section.

### 3.6 `sites`

A list of `{ url, note? }`: other sites a reader would use for the current offering, without repeating `homepage`. An older offering's sites go in its `past` entry. A relevant resource that no offering produced, or whose offering can't be established, goes here too, with the uncertainty in its note ([§5.3](#53-invariants)). List only sites a rating relies on, or that hold material nothing else does.

### 3.7 `self_study`

- **Shape**: a curated list of `{ url, note }` entries, for concrete support beyond the core materials that helps an outside learner complete or check the work. Examples:
  - a leaderboard or evaluator that people outside Stanford can use, such as CS 336's [assignment leaderboard](https://github.com/stanford-cs336/assignment1-basics-leaderboard). This is an important kind of evidence, because it gives an outside learner feedback on their own work;
  - guidance addressed to readers "following along at home", such as CS 336's cloud-GPU pricing, or instructions that make the course's infrastructure usable without it, such as CS 312's guide to running on your own CUDA GPU or Slurm cluster instead of the course's Modal setup;
  - a community that is open to outside learners.
- **What doesn't qualify**: incidental links. Being in the list is an assertion that the resource helps an outside reader today. An expired leaderboard whose landing page still loads doesn't qualify.
- **Older offerings**: a resource from an older offering goes in that offering's `past` record. It still counts as self-study while it's useful.
- **Effect**: it never raises `access` and never compensates for gated core material. It only changes the icon ([§7](#7-tables-and-rendering)).
- **Lint**: every entry needs a `url` and a `note`; the note is the evidence.

### 3.8 `checked`

- **`checked`**: the date (`YYYY-MM-DD`) of the last completed access check covering every rating on the page, including `past` and `self_study` entries.
  - A narrower check leaves the date unchanged. It is recorded on the entries it covered instead, as their own `checked` in the object form, so a recheck of one type is visible without claiming the rest.
  - An entry's `checked` must be later than the page's; once a full check catches up, it's dropped (lint warns).
  - Moving keys or importing records never advances it.
- **A `sources` entry's `checked`** is a different date: when someone last confirmed that source still backs the page (AGENTS.md → Frontmatter). It says nothing about material access, and the page's `checked` doesn't cover it.

### 3.9 `exceptions`

- **Shape**: a map from fixed names to a non-empty reason. A name silences exactly one lint warning, and lint warns about an exception that no longer silences anything.
- **Names**: only `pre_cutoff` so far ([§5.6](#56-pre-cutoff-material)).
- **Title overrides need none**, because lint doesn't compare titles ([§5.4](#54-titles-instructors-topics)).

### 3.10 Example

An excerpt, showing the Content keys only, with values from today's [CS 229](../courses/CS%20229.md) page. The ratings are examples; the migrating agent decides the real ones.

```yaml
term: Summer 2026
homepage: https://cs229.stanford.edu/
access: mostly-open
syllabus:
  access: open
  url: "https://docs.google.com/spreadsheets/d/1X8OpCbt6SyJ3_qlj5LxvZFoOtbjBadBVRzdmY4s6vb4/edit?gid=0#gid=0"
  note: the course's own sheet, plus a logistics and FAQ document
slides:
  access: partial
  url: https://cs229.stanford.edu/summer2026/CS229_LR_LLMs.pdf
  note: only the RL and LLMs deck is posted
notes:
  access: open
  url: https://cs229.stanford.edu/main_notes.pdf
  note: "278-page course notes, plus linear algebra, probability and decision-tree handouts"
videos:
  access: closed
  note: recordings go to Canvas
assignments:
  access: closed
  note: "Ed only, \"to prevent public exposure of solutions\""
projects: none
past:
  Spring 2022:
    slides:
      access: open
      url: https://docs.google.com/spreadsheets/d/1OEsqqhihH-n2OPHsT8jSA8BkLdqUMWY-GiWHgkBs3Z8/edit
      note: about 14 decks, most of the lectures
  Spring 2026:
    videos: https://www.youtube.com/playlist?list=PLaqpC4kq8Gpw
```

The level is `mostly-open`, not `open`: assignments are gated, and videos exist only in a relevant older offering, which counts. The level is not `partial` because the notes, the older decks and the full Spring 2026 recordings cover the instruction ([§4.4](#44-assignments)).

## 4. Overall access

### 4.1 Levels

| `access` | Icon | Guideline |
| --- | --- | --- |
| `unknown` | *(blank)* | Core material not yet checked, or none found. Search results that turn up nothing are not a gate. |
| `closed` | ⛔ | The expected core is gated, with positive evidence, and no useful public core was found. |
| `mostly-closed` | 🟥 | A syllabus or schedule shows what the course covers, but little of its teaching or practice material is public. |
| `partial` | 🟨 | Useful parts are public, but substantial parts of the expected material are missing or gated. |
| `mostly-open` | 🟩 | Most of what a reader needs is public, with one important limitation. |
| `open` | ✅ | The expected core is public, at parity with an enrolled student. |

These are guidelines, not thresholds. Exceptional support for outside learners is a separate key, `self_study` ([§3.7](#37-self_study)), not a level.

### 4.2 Expected core, by format

- **Lecture course**: a syllabus; instruction (slides and notes, which together cover the lectures); assignments; and recordings wherever the course makes them.
- **Seminar**: the schedule and reading list, which can be its whole core. Talk recordings count when the course makes them for its students. A seminar that also sets coursework owes that too: [CS 348I](../courses/CS%20348I.md)'s reading list is public, but its homework, quiz, project and decks are gated, so it is `partial`.
- **Studio or project course**: the schedule and the project briefs.
- **Lab**: the lab handouts and the material they require.

Slides and notes can complement each other, and a complete slide collection can be the instruction on its own: extensive notes like CS 229's aren't required. An optional handout doesn't substitute for a missing sequence of lectures.

### 4.3 Videos

- **Recordings the course is known to make gate `open`.** Relevant recordings from the current offering or an older one since `cutoff_term` satisfy this, and a pre-cutoff set can by documented exception ([§5.6](#56-pre-cutoff-material)). The classic case is [CS 224N](../courses/CS%20224N.md): current recordings are Canvas-only, but a relevant recent playlist is public, so it stays `open`. Don't demand that an older set cover every newer lecture.
- **A stated no-recording policy is `videos: none`, not a gap.** Together with complete other material, it can justify `open`; [CS 355](../courses/CS%20355.md) is the example.
- **Recorded but gated is `closed`, and it is a gap.** This includes recordings posted only to Canvas.
- **Silence about recordings is `unknown`, and no gap once someone has looked.** Look beyond the course site first, since sites often don't link their public recordings (CS 229's Spring 2026 playlist is an example). If nothing turns up and no source says the lectures are recorded, the course is rated on the rest of its material: a recording nobody can show exists can't be shown to reach enrolled students either. The type stays `unknown`, never `closed` or `none`. Only recordings shown to exist and be gated hold a lecture course below `open`. (Changed 2026-10-01: silence used to hold a lecture course at `mostly-open`, which made the level report how talkative a syllabus was rather than what a reader can open.)
- **A standing archive that each offering adds to belongs to the current offering**, such as a talk series' playlist ([CS 547](../courses/CS%20547.md)). A documented short delay before public posting ("within a couple of weeks") doesn't lower the level.
- **Short clips are not lecture recordings, unless they are the course's video content.** On their own, five-minute explainers don't settle whether the lectures are recorded. But when the course sends students who miss a lecture to those clips, parity applies, because enrolled students get nothing more. [CS 145](../courses/CS%20145.md)'s FAQ does exactly that: "Open the Schedule and find the pages listed for that day. Each page includes a short video." So its unrecorded lectures are no gap.

### 4.4 Assignments

- **How much gated assignments matter depends on the course.** Where students learn mostly by doing, gated assignments are a strong downgrade signal and hold the course at `mostly-open` at best, even with everything else open. `mscs-si` (significant implementation, generated from the program pages) is an advisory hint that assignments are central, never a rule.
- **Older relevant assignments count**, just as older recordings do. A public copy of a recent offering's assignments closes the practice gap even when the current offering's copies sit behind a login.
- **Starter code the assignments need is part of the assignments.** If it goes only to enrolled students, the practice material is only partly available, even when every brief is public.
- **A free sign-in anyone can create is not a gate**, whether it saves progress or unlocks the material. Parity holds: an outside reader gets what an enrolled student gets.
- **Supplementary activities behind a login**, such as in-class exercises, don't lower the level when public assignments supply the practice. The Materials section mentions them.

Examples:
- [CS 229](../courses/CS%20229.md) is `mostly-open`: its problem sets are gated, but extensive notes, full relevant recordings and a stated reason keep it above `partial`.
- [CS 106L](../courses/CS%20106L.md) is `open`: its [assignments repository](https://github.com/cs106l/cs106l-assignments) is public, while its in-class activities stay behind 106l.com.
- [CS 107E](../courses/CS%20107E.md)'s assignment files count as public: they remain in its site repository's history, from before an "unrelease" commit removed them.
- On [CS 145](../courses/CS%20145.md), any Google account opens the videos, and the site says its sign-in takes "Google or an email link".

### 4.5 Older material

`access` weighs the current offering together with relevant older offerings in `past`, for every type: older recordings (CS 224N), older assignments (CS 106L) and older decks (CS 229) all count, as long as they still represent the course. It is never an automatic maximum over the history. A public archive of a substantially different curriculum (CS 349F's financial-systems offering) doesn't raise the level just because it shares the course number. The Materials section explains which older collections the level relies on.

### 4.6 Offerings in progress

- **Rate what has been released**, and say that the collection is still growing.
- **Unreleased future work is not gated.** Early releases don't imply a complete archive.
- **Promised recordings get a few weeks' slack.** When a current offering says a public set is coming ("YouTube playlist TBA"), don't hold its absence against the course early in the term; [CS 312](../courses/CS%20312.md) gets that slack. An older offering gets no such credit: if its promised recordings never appeared, they don't count.
- **Recheck after the term ends**; lint prompts this ([§9.2](#92-lint-checks)).

### 4.7 Borderline calls

- **What the reader can open decides.** In borderline cases, context such as an instructor's stated reason may inform the course's level.
- **Context never changes a type's own rating.** Gated stays `closed`, and a missing recording that was expected doesn't become `none`.
- **State the decisive reason** in the Materials section's opening sentence.

## 5. Past offerings

### 5.1 Shape and semantics

`past` maps written-out term names to sparse records:
- **A missing key means not recorded**, never the current value or the value from a newer term.
- **Repeated values are kept** when they were verified independently.
- **A list is the complete recorded value**, not an addition to the current list.
- **Never derive `terms_offered` from `past`.**

### 5.2 Allowed keys

```text
title, instructors, homepage,
syllabus, slides, notes, videos, assignments, solutions, exams, projects, repo,
sites, self_study, textbook, topics
```

- **Sources**: no fact from memory, here as everywhere else. Every value in a `past` record comes from a source in the page's top-level `sources`; there are no per-record citations.
- **Not allowed** in `past`: `access`, `checked`, `exceptions`, program tags, lifecycle metadata, or a nested `past`. Units, grading and prerequisites are left out until something needs them.

### 5.3 Invariants

- Every term is earlier than `term` and appears in `terms_offered`. The one exception is pre-cutoff material ([§5.6](#56-pre-cutoff-material)).
- An empty record is an error.
- **A collection spanning several offerings** (a playlist mixing years) is filed under the offering whose part it rates, with the wider scope stated in its note. It is never filed wholesale under one term. The exception is a standing archive that each offering adds to, which belongs to the current offering ([§4.3](#43-videos)).
- **A resource no offering produced**, such as Boneh's online course that CS 255 points to, goes in the top-level `sites` (or `self_study`). So does a relevant resource whose offering can't be established, such as a public assignments repository that doesn't say which term it holds; its note states the uncertainty, and it can still support the level. Neither gets an invented term to fit `past`.
- Build sorts terms oldest first, and each record's keys in top-level order.
- Moving a page to a newer term keeps every source entry that backs a `past` value.

### 5.4 Titles, instructors, topics

- **Every older offering has a record with `instructors`.** Each term in `terms_offered` from `cutoff_term` on, other than `term`, gets `past.<term>.instructors`, so its term-table row names that offering's professor ([§7](#7-tables-and-rendering)). The value is the catalog's principal instructors (role PI) for that term, in catalog order, from its lecture or seminar sections, or from its discussion or lab sections when it has nothing else. When the catalog names nobody, it is `[]`, which still counts as recorded. The helper backfills these ([§8](#8-historical-rows-and-catalog-sources)), and lint warns about a term without one ([§9.2](#92-lint-checks)).
- **`title`**: the catalog's title for that term, verbatim, without the code prefix the top-level key carries and without the cross-listed codes the catalog appends in parentheses. It is recorded whenever it differs from the page's current title, as for [CS 349F](../courses/CS%20349F.md)'s "Technology for Financial Systems" offerings, and omitted when it's the same, since the row falls back to the current title. The course's own site may name an offering differently; then its name may be used, with a line in Source notes. No `exceptions` entry is needed, because lint doesn't compare titles.
- **`instructors`**: catalog spelling and order, with the lead first when a source says who leads.
- **Every older offering's syllabus is sought, and captured when public.** A syllabus (the schedule, syllabus or reading list) is the evidence of how a course changed, and it is the first thing to disappear. So for each older offering since `cutoff_term`, `past.<term>.syllabus` records what exists: `open` with its URL when a copy is public, with the document ingested into `references/`; `closed` when the syllabus repository shows a Stanford-only or enrolled-only upload and no public copy turned up; omitted when nothing was found. An offering with its own site also gets `homepage`, and an ingested syllabus gives that offering's `topics`. **The class archive is the exception to ingesting**: `web.stanford.edu/class/archive/…` keeps whole offering sites durably, so for a course it covers, one index reference (`<code>-offering-archive-<years>.md`: each offering's path, its term read off the page, and what it holds) stands in for per-offering copies, and those offerings' `topics` wait for the Evolution pass, which ingests what it compares. The first batch showed why: CS 103, 106A, 106B, 107 and 109 have 25 to 27 archived offerings each. Hosts that disappear (GitHub Pages, Google Docs, personal and lab sites) still get a copy per offering. The helper records what the syllabus repository shows, and the migrating agent looks for public copies ([§8](#8-historical-rows-and-catalog-sources)).
- **`topics`**: from that offering's syllabus or schedule, or its catalog description when nothing else exists, with that source in `sources`. Evolution then limits its claims to what the source supports: "the catalog description changed" is not the same as "the course started teaching X". Two representative topic lists that differ don't prove the teaching changed.

### 5.5 Ratings under `past`

A rating under an older term is today's observation, made on `checked`, of material belonging to that offering. It does not claim the material was public at the time. Historical titles and instructors, by contrast, are claims about that offering, and they need dated evidence.

### 5.6 Pre-cutoff material

- **What's allowed**: a `past` record for a term before `cutoff_term`, holding only material types, `sites` and `self_study`. No title, instructors or topics, so it never triggers catalog research. It doesn't extend `terms_offered` or create a term page.
- **Lint**: it warns unless `exceptions.pre_cutoff` gives the reason. The exception silences only that warning; bad URLs, missing sources and every other error still fire.
- **Legacy data already has some**: 19 pages attribute material or sites to terms before Winter 2020, mostly Autumn 2018. The helper moves them into pre-cutoff records and flags them ([§10.1](#101-the-helper-script-temporary)).
- **Default**: such material is usually too old to count. Relying on it for `access` needs the reason in the Materials section. It can always be mentioned in prose without a record.

### 5.7 Moving to a newer offering

In this order. It is a numbered step in `wiki-course` and `wiki-term`, because a skipped or misordered snapshot can't be recovered.

1. **Migrate first.** A legacy page is migrated under its original `term` ([§10](#10-migration)), so entries that already describe older offerings land under their own terms, not under the outgoing one.
2. **Move the outgoing offering into `past.<old term>`**: its verified `title`, `instructors`, `homepage`, material entries, `sites`, `self_study`, `textbook` and `topics`. If a record for that term already exists, merge without overwriting and report any conflict. The sources stay in `sources`.
3. **Clear the top level**: the material types, `sites`, `self_study`, `textbook` and `homepage`, so no observation of the old offering is left there. Then fill them for the new offering. A type not yet checked stays `unknown`.
4. **Reassess `access`** with the older evidence now in `past`, reconsidering whether it's still relevant.

## 6. The Materials section

- **Open with a verdict** that gives the decisive reason for the level: what's public, what's gated, and which older offering the level relies on.
- **One bullet per material type**, all nine in type order, including `closed`, `none` and types nothing was found for, linking straight to the material. Types with the same one-line verdict may share a bullet (`**Solutions**, **Exams**: none; the course sets neither`). Each bullet describes the current offering and the best relevant older material, naming its offering.
- **Then a "Textbook" bullet**, if there is a textbook, a "Self-study" bullet for the `self_study` entries, and any extras. Every `sites` entry is linked somewhere in the section, with what it holds.
- **For an offering in progress**, say that its material is still being released.
- **Pre-cutoff material** can be mentioned and linked here, whether or not it has a `past` record.

The frontmatter and this section must agree: the section never contradicts it. It is still the readable version of nested values, but it names the collections the level relies on and summarizes a long run of similar `past` records rather than repeating each; CS 103's 27 archived offerings can't be mirrored in 350 words.

## 7. Tables and rendering

| Page state | Icon |
| --- | --- |
| `open` / `mostly-open` | ✅ / 🟩 |
| `partial` / `mostly-closed` / `closed` | 🟨 / 🟥 / ⛔ |
| `unknown` | blank |
| **Legacy page (transition only)** | old `open` shows 🟩?, `partial` 🟨?, `closed` ⛔?, `unknown` blank |

- **The legacy mapping deliberately understates.** The `?` means "rated under the old rules", the legend says so, and the count of `?` icons doubles as the migration progress bar.
- **The Public column shows the page's current `access` on every row**, historical rows included. Past levels are never rendered.
- **The icon carries the level; markers after it flag things such as open assignments and `self_study`.** `publicIcon` in `scripts/wiki.ts` defines them. (Changed 2026-10-01: the levels were colored circles, and self-study turned two of them into hearts.)
- **Historical rows in term tables**:
  - **Professor** comes from `past.<term>.instructors`, or from the page's `instructors` when the row's term is the page's `term`. It is blank when that list is empty, or when the record is missing (which lint warns about).
  - **Title** comes from `past.<term>.title`, a bare title shown as it is, or else the page's current title without its code prefix.

## 8. Historical rows and catalog sources

There is no per-term catalog map. Historical rows take their title and professor from `past` ([§7](#7-tables-and-rendering)).

- **The helper backfills every older offering** as it migrates a page ([§10.1](#101-the-helper-script-temporary)): `instructors` for each term in `terms_offered` from `cutoff_term` up to, but not including, `term`, and `title` where it differs ([§5.4](#54-titles-instructors-topics)). It reads the full-year catalog files (`references/explorecourses-cs-YYYY-YYYY.md`, one per academic year from 2019-20 on), falling back to the current year's per-term files (`explorecourses-cs-autumn-2026.md`), and cites each file it used. This is mechanical, so it isn't left to the migrating agent: the first migration run, which left it out, produced pages whose older rows had no professor.
- **What it can't settle, it flags.** A dry run over the 378 legacy pages found a catalog section for every older offering but 4, and 49 offerings whose sections name no instructor, 19 of them in Spring 2020. Most of those are ordinary TBA listings, but a TBA-only listing can also be an offering that never ran: CS 377G's Winter 2022 listing has two TBA sections and no instructor, and the course's own blog says that quarter was canceled and ran as a study group.
- **Rows fill in as terms roll over**, through the snapshot in [§5.7](#57-moving-to-a-newer-offering).
- **After the migration**, whoever adds an older term to a page records its catalog instructors, and its title where it differs, like any other verified fact ([§11](#11-skills)).

**Past syllabi** ([§5.4](#54-titles-instructors-topics)) come in two layers, the mechanical one first:

- **The syllabus repository, through the helper.** Stanford's syllabus repository lists every section of a term with whether a syllabus was uploaded, who can read it, and its Canvas course (`syllabus.stanford.edu/syllabus/searchCourses/<TermCode>/CS/`; term codes in `wiki-materials` → Find the site). One ingest before the batches saves it as one reference per academic year, `references/syllabus-repository-cs-YYYY-YYYY.md` (the current year per term, like the catalog), with a `## <Term>` block per term and one line per CS section: `- CS 229 (01): INSTITUTION https://canvas.stanford.edu/courses/…`, `COURSE` (enrolled students only) or `PUBLIC` (readable) in place of `INSTITUTION`, or `- CS 229 (01): none` when nothing was uploaded. The helper reads these as it reads the catalog files. For an older offering with a Stanford-only upload, it writes `past.<term>.syllabus: { access: closed, note: "uploaded to Canvas, Stanford-only (syllabus repository)" }` ("enrolled students only" for `COURSE`), with the listing's fetch date as the entry's `checked` when that is later than the page's, and cites the file. A `PUBLIC` upload is flagged, not rated, since the agent opens it first. It never overwrites a `syllabus` entry the legacy map already had, and flags one that disagrees.
- **Public copies, by the migrating agent.** For every older offering since `cutoff_term` without an `open` syllabus, the agent looks in these places, once each: the page's own sources and Source notes; the current site's links to earlier offerings and its per-offering paths (`/spring2022/`, a GitHub Pages subpath, a term subdirectory); a `PUBLIC` Canvas syllabus the repository lists; the class archive, `web.stanford.edu/class/archive/cs/<slug>/<slug>.<termId>/` with computed term ids; and the Internet Archive's CDX listing for the course's URLs, where one query per URL prefix covers every term at once (`wiki-materials` → Find the site). It reads the term off the document, never from a path or id. A copy it finds is ingested as `<code>-syllabus-<term>.md` (or, for the class archive, listed in the course's archive index, [§5.4](#54-titles-instructors-topics)) and rated `open` with its URL, replacing a repository `closed` (the note keeps that finding), and gives the offering its `homepage`, and its `topics` when ingested. Where nothing turns up, the rating stays as the repository left it, and the report says which offerings have no syllabus.

**A separate sourcing fix**, small and worth doing regardless: 19 term pages (Winter 2020 through Summer 2024) cite only the graduate extract (`explorecourses-cs-grad-*`), although their tables list undergraduate courses. They should cite the full-year files (`explorecourses-cs-YYYY-YYYY.md`), which exist for every academic year from 2019-20 on.

## 9. Lint and build

### 9.1 Two states

- **Detection**: a top-level `access` key means the page is migrated, whatever its value (`access: unknown` counts). Without it, the page is legacy. Registrations are skipped.
- **Legacy pages** get today's checks, plus one summary line: "N of 378 course pages not yet migrated".
- **Migrated pages** get the new checks.
- **Half-converted pages fail, as errors.** Today lint's unknown-key check only *warns*, which would let a mixed page through. So each validator rejects the other schema's keys as errors:
  - on a migrated page, a leftover `materials`;
  - on a legacy page, a top-level `checked`, any material type, `sites`, `self_study`, `textbook`, `past` or `exceptions`.

  Tests cover both directions. There's still no third state.

### 9.2 Lint checks

**Errors on migrated pages:**
- key order, and allowed values for `access` and the per-type ratings;
- the value shapes in [§3.3](#33-value-shapes), with http(s) URLs only and separate messages for a bad URL and a bad rating;
- `checked` required once anything is rated; an entry's own `checked` must be `YYYY-MM-DD`;
- the `past` invariants in [§5](#5-past-offerings). A record holding only `instructors: []` is not empty;
- a rating icon or table marker (`RATING_ICONS` in `scripts/wiki.ts`, the earlier set included) anywhere in the body: the rating system stays out of the prose (AGENTS.md → Body). Legacy pages are exempt, since four of them (CS 220, CS 272, CS 273C, CS 273D) carry icons that their migration removes;
- every `self_study` entry has a `url` and a `note`;
- `exceptions` names come from the fixed list, and each has a non-empty reason;
- registrations reject every Content key, `checked` and `exceptions`.

**Warnings:**
- `checked` more than 120 days old (as today);
- `access: unknown` with no `checked`: never checked, the backlog warning today's `materials: { access: unknown }` gets;
- the post-term check: the page's `term` has ended and `checked` predates that term's `end_date` (term pages already carry it); this can't fire early in a term;
- a pre-cutoff `past` record without `exceptions.pre_cutoff`, or an exception that silences nothing;
- an entry's own `checked` no later than the page's `checked`, which already covers it;
- a term in `terms_offered` from `cutoff_term` on, earlier than `term`, with no `past.<term>.instructors` ([§5.4](#54-titles-instructors-topics));
- a body over the length ceilings (AGENTS.md → Body): 700 words above `## Source notes` in all, and per section 120 for the summary, 350 for Materials, 300 for Syllabus, 100 for Prerequisites and 150 for Related. Only prose words count, not headings or link URLs. Measured that way, the first run's Materials sections ran to 276 words at most and its pages to 593; across all 378 pages today, only one Syllabus section (320) would exceed its ceiling.

Lint never compares a level with the per-type ratings. Levels are judgement.

### 9.3 Build

- **Key order**: build sorts migrated pages into the [§3.1](#31-key-order) order, and recursively inside each `past` record.
- **Legacy pages** keep today's `materials` sort (the `materials` branch in `yamlLines`) until the old code comes out.
- **One normalizer** reads both shapes and returns the level, a legacy flag and the markers, for the Public column (`icon` in `render`). Build never migrates a page or rates it as a side effect.
- **The historical-row rules** in [§7](#7-tables-and-rendering) replace today's current title and first instructor on every row (`title` and `instructor` in `render`). A `past` title is bare and shown as it is.
- **Code that changes together**: `ICON`, `MAT_TYPES` and `MAT_KEYS` in `scripts/wiki.ts`; `KEY_ORDER`, `OPTIONAL`, `COURSE_ONLY` and the `materials` checks in `scripts/lint.ts`; and the fixtures in `scripts/*.test.ts`.

## 10. Migration

### 10.1 The helper script (temporary)

`node .agents/2026-09-30.materials.ts [--dry-run] "courses/CS 229.md" [...]`, kept next to this doc, gives every page the same mechanical start. `--dry-run` prints the report without writing.

- **Input check**: it runs only on a complete legacy page: a `materials` map, no top-level `access`, and no `past` or flattened keys. On anything else it exits without writing. This makes a second run harmless even before the agent has set `access`.
- **Resolving the offering**:
  - A material type belongs to its own `term`, else `materials.term`, else the page's `term`.
  - A site belongs to its own `term`, else the page's `term`. A site never inherits `materials.term`. For example, [CS 341](../courses/CS%20341.md) has `materials.term: Spring 2019` and an undated textbook site (mmds.org), and that site belongs to the current offering.
- **Moving**: current-offering entries go to the top level, and everything else goes to `past.<term>`. Per-entry `term` fields are dropped, `code` becomes `repo`, and every URL, note and rating is kept.
- **Collisions**: if two entries would land in the same slot, it writes nothing and reports them.
- **Catalog backfill**: `past.<term>.instructors` for every older offering, and `title` where it differs, from the catalog files, citing each file it used under a new `catalog-YYYY-YY` source unless the page already cites it ([§8](#8-historical-rows-and-catalog-sources)).
- **Syllabus repository**: `past.<term>.syllabus` as `closed` for every older offering with a Stanford-only upload, from the repository files, citing each file it used ([§8](#8-historical-rows-and-catalog-sources)).
- **Dates**: `materials.checked` moves to the top-level `checked` unchanged. The helper never advances it, and never writes `access` or `generated`.
- **Prose**: never touched.
- **Report**: everything it moved, plus what needs judgement:
  - pre-cutoff records (19 pages today), for which the agent either adds `exceptions.pre_cutoff` with a reason or moves the mention into prose and deletes the record;
  - notes filed under an older offering that describe a later one, such as a note under Spring 2022 saying what Summer 2026 posts;
  - terms missing from `terms_offered` (incomplete offering history), or later than the page's `term` (a page that probably needs re-anchoring);
  - `sites` entries that repeat `homepage` or a material entry's URL;
  - types the current offering has no rating for;
  - an older offering with no catalog section under the page's codes, and one whose catalog sections name no instructor ([§8](#8-historical-rows-and-catalog-sources));
  - an older offering with a `PUBLIC` repository syllabus to open, and a legacy `syllabus` entry the repository disagrees with.
- **Self-contained**: it spells out both schemas' type lists itself, and imports only the YAML reader and writer and a few small helpers that `scripts/wiki.ts` already exported before the redesign, so it runs whatever state the scripts are in.

The page the helper leaves has flattened keys and no `access`. Once the new lint from step 1 of [§10.3](#103-plan) is in place, it treats such a page as legacy and rejects the flattened keys as errors, so it can't be committed until the agent sets `access`. Until then, today's lint only warns, so don't run the helper for real before step 1 lands.

### 10.6 Acceptance tests

These protect the design's central promises, without adding permanent metadata.

- **Attribution**: every legacy entry lands at the offering its old rules gave it (types: own term, `materials.term`, page term; sites: own term, page term). Nothing is dropped, URLs, notes and ratings are unchanged, and `checked` carries over.
- **Reruns**: the helper refuses non-legacy input and leaves it byte-identical, and a second run changes nothing.
- **Mixed schemas**: rejected as errors in both directions.
- **Rollover**: after [§5.7](#57-moving-to-a-newer-offering), the top level holds nothing observed for the old offering, and `past` holds all of it.
- **Pre-cutoff**: a pre-cutoff record warns without `exceptions.pre_cutoff`; the exception silences only that warning; an unused exception warns.
- **Backfill**: every older offering since `cutoff_term` gets its catalog instructors, in catalog order, and a title only where it differs, without cross-listed codes; an offering naming nobody gets `[]` and a flag; one missing from the catalog gets a flag and no record; each catalog file is cited once.
- **Syllabus repository**: an older offering with a Stanford-only upload gets a `closed` syllabus with the repository note, and a `checked` only when the listing is newer than the page's; a `PUBLIC` upload is flagged and not rated; a legacy `syllabus` entry is never overwritten, and flagged when it disagrees; each repository file is cited once.
- **Missing instructors**: lint warns about an older term without `past.<term>.instructors`, and accepts `[]`.
- **Icons**: an icon in a migrated page's body is an error; a legacy page's isn't.
- **Length**: a migrated page over the total or a section ceiling warns, naming the section; a legacy page doesn't; link URLs don't count.
- **Entry `checked`**: accepted in the object form, at the top level and in `past`; a malformed date is an error, and one no later than the page's `checked` warns.
- **Post-term recheck**: the warning fires once the page's `term` has ended and `checked` predates its `end_date`, and not before.
- **Rendering**: legacy pages show `?`; a historical row takes its professor and title from `past`, and its professor is blank when `instructors` is `[]`.

## 11. Skills

### `wiki-course`

1. Confirm the course's identity and the offering.
2. On a legacy page, run the helper (`.agents/2026-09-30.materials.ts`) first, under the page's original `term`, and migrate the whole page in the same edit. Agents only ever write the new shape.
3. **When advancing to a newer offering, follow [§5.7](#57-moving-to-a-newer-offering) in order**: move the outgoing offering into `past`, clear the top level, then fill in the new offering. **When adding an older offering**, record its catalog instructors, and its title where it differs, in `past`, and look for its syllabus ([§5.4](#54-titles-instructors-topics)).
4. Rate, set `access`, and rewrite the Materials section.
5. Update `checked` only for checks actually completed.
6. Build, lint, and confirm that no URL, note, rating or source link was lost.

### `wiki-materials`

1. Work out the course's format and its expected core ([§4.2](#42-expected-core-by-format)).
2. Search the current offering and relevant older ones, checking that each is the same course and still relevant.
3. Look beyond the course site: recordings under cross-listed codes and on Stanford Online, and assignments and starter code in the course's GitHub organization. The playlist inventory is a lead, not proof of absence.
4. Rate each collection. Tell gated (`closed`), never produced (`none`) and not found (`unknown`) apart.
5. Put older material in `past`, record `self_study` and `textbook`, and assign `access` against the calibration pages, with the reason in the Materials section's opening sentence.
6. Record the actual scope of the check, ingest the evidence, build and lint.

### `wiki-term`

1. Snapshot outgoing offerings into `past` before re-anchoring pages to the new term. A term added to a page as an older offering gets its catalog instructors, and a title that differs, in `past`, and its syllabus is looked for.
2. Cite the catalog file that actually covers the term's rows. Never delete offering history because a partial extract omits it.
3. A sweep is not a materials recheck. Current-term pages get their post-term recheck through lint.

### `wiki-ingest`

- Keep each source's scope, the offering it describes, and its fetch date; an old offering can be fetched today.
- A catalog extract states what it kept and what it dropped.
- A search summary, a landing page that loads, or a playlist title never substitutes for inspecting the material itself.
