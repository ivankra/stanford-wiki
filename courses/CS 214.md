# CS 214: Selected Reading of Computer Science Research

The graduate number of [CS 114](CS%20114.md): eight papers from across computer science, each over two sessions — a context lecture, then the author's own talk. Spring 2022 ran from the optimality of the Johnson–Lindenstrauss lemma through stolen Facebook accounts and the Plasticine accelerator to Mesos, then robotics, HCI, devices and imaging. There are no exams, only a two-page report per paper and a final related-work report, and graduate reports tie each paper to the student's own area.

## Materials

One page holds the whole of Spring 2022 and withholds nothing: every paper, every supporting work, a deck for both halves of each pairing, and the coursework written out. The only open question is whether the guest lectures were recorded, which no source answers.

- **Syllabus**: the [schedule](https://web.stanford.edu/class/cs114/) names all 8 papers, both sessions on each, the guest and their biography, and the reading around it, and says which students should take this number rather than [CS 114](CS%20114.md).
- **Slides**: [15 decks](https://web.stanford.edu/class/cs114/CS114_Moses_Charikar.pdf), the instructor's or an expert's context lecture and the visiting author's own talk for most papers. All 15 download.
- **Notes**: 55 reading PDFs on the site — the 8 papers, 37 supporting works listed 4 to 8 per paper, and 3 pieces by Keshav, Levis and Mitzenmacher on how to read a research paper.
- **Videos**: no recording is mentioned, though "the guest lectures are open to the entire Stanford community" in person. Nothing turned up under either number or the instructor's name.
- **Assignments**: the brief is the coursework, stated on the page — "a 2 page report on each paper as well as final report in which you write a more detailed discussion of the related work and surrounding scholarship on one of the papers", with CS 214 reports focused on "the ideas in the paper and their potential implications to the student's area of specialization" where the undergraduate version's stay conceptual.
- **Solutions**, **Exams**, **Projects**, **Repo**: none. The course is reading and writing about it.

## Syllabus

The [Spring 2022 schedule](https://web.stanford.edu/class/cs114/) opens with "Course Goals and How to Read a Research Paper", then gives each paper two sessions — a context lecture, then its author:

1. **Theory**: the optimality of the Johnson–Lindenstrauss lemma, introduced by Moses Charikar, with Jelani Nelson of UC Berkeley on the paper.
2. **Security**: "SocialHEISTing: Understanding Stolen Facebook Accounts", introduced by Zakir Durumeric, with Jeremiah Onaolapo of Vermont.
3. **Architecture**: "Plasticine: A Reconfigurable Architecture For Parallel Patterns", introduced by Levis against Moore, Dennard and the TPU papers, with Kunle Olukotun.
4. **Systems**: "Mesos: A Platform for Fine-Grained Resource Sharing in the Data Center", read against MapReduce, Dryad and Spark, with Anthony Joseph of UC Berkeley.
5. **Robotics, HCI, devices and imaging**: further papers with Jeanette Bohg, Monroe Kennedy, James Mickens, Roger Howe, Kris Dorsey, Ehsan Adeli and Audrey Bowden.

The site recommends against taking it without the CS core: "Due to the depth of the technical material, we do not recommend the course to students who have not completed the CS core ([CS 111](CS%20111.md) and [CS 161](CS%20161.md))."
