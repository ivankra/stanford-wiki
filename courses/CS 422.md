# CS 422: Interactive and Embodied Learning

Nick Haber's seminar starts from a contrast the catalog draws sharply: today's successful systems learn from carefully curated human labels or from vast experience aimed at a well-defined goal in a fixed environment, while people learn by acting on their surroundings and building a model of them that transfers. The course reads current work on closing that gap — intrinsic motivation, exploration, world models, embodied agents — with students presenting in turn. The deliverable is not an implementation but "a proposal of a feasible next research direction". It is cross-listed into the Graduate School of Education and meets once a week for three hours.

## Syllabus

No schedule or reading list is published. The catalog states the framing:

> Most successful machine learning algorithms of today use either carefully curated, human-labeled datasets, or large amounts of experience aimed at achieving well-defined goals within specific environments. In contrast, people learn through their agency: they interact with their environments, exploring and building complex mental models of their world so as to be able to flexibly adapt to a wide variety of tasks. One crucial next direction in artificial intelligence is to create artificial agents that learn in this flexible and robust way. Students will read and take turns presenting current works, and they will produce a proposal of a feasible next research direction.

## Prerequisites

- Machine learning at the level of [CS 229](CS%20229.md), [CS 231N](CS%20231N.md) or [CS 234](CS%20234.md), or equivalent. Reinforcement learning is the most directly relevant of the three, since the seminar's subject is what an agent does when the reward is not given.
