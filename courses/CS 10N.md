# CS 10N: Computer Play: An Unconventional Introduction to CS and EE

A first-year seminar the instructor describes as a pilot for a future CS/EE-listed course: students write programs that respond in real time to the sound of actual instruments, and reach assembly language early so the foundations are felt rather than described. The IntroSem listing warns that the ideal student will "be a bit courageous", since it is a trial run.

## Syllabus

No published syllabus, so the catalog is the source: "Experimental introduction to computer science and electrical engineering. Students will learn to create interactive programs that understand sounds made by real-world musical instruments and the big ideas behind some of the core discoveries, surprises, and impossibilities at the core of CS and EE."

The IntroSem listing adds two specifics: students learn assembly language "to understand the foundations of computer programs in a visceral way", and the course takes up how computers handle continuous sound waves — signals with infinitely many points — as one of its core surprises. It positions completers for [CS 106B](CS%20106B.md), ENGR 76M or EE 102A without substituting for their prerequisites.
