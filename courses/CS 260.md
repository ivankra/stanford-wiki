# CS 260: Geometry of Polynomials in Algorithm Design

One tool, followed through a decade of results: encode a combinatorial object as a polynomial, then read the algorithm off the polynomial's roots. Anari taught this once, the year after the technique produced breakthroughs in approximate counting and matroid sampling, and the course runs from the fundamental properties to applications in sparsification, high-dimensional expanders and random walks.

## Syllabus

The [archived schedule](https://web.archive.org/web/20200920144010/https://nimaanari.com/cs260-winter2020/) runs twenty sessions:

1. **Weeks 1–3, the polynomials**: real-rootedness, interlacing and the matching polynomial; real stability, the multivariate matching polynomial and closure properties; determinantal distributions and correlation bounds; capacity and approximating the permanent; hyperbolic polynomials, log-concavity and convex programming.
2. **Weeks 4–6, counting and walks**: determinant maximization and entropy approximation; approximate counting and sampling; high-dimensional random walks and mixing-time analysis; local-to-global analysis of f-divergence; Poincaré and modified log-Sobolev inequalities.
3. **Weeks 7–9, matroids and beyond**: certifying log-concavity, matroids and exchange properties with a tight random-walk analysis; discrete convexity, Plücker relations and gross substitutes; fractional log-concavity; Barvinok's method and the permanent again; bounding roots and interlacing families.
4. **Week 10**: two Zoom Q&A sessions — the quarter ended in March 2020.

Grading offers three routes: two problem sets, a short presentation or write-up on one or two papers, or a course project in pairs. "For letter grades, please do either 1+2, or 3."
