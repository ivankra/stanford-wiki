# CS 260: Geometry of Polynomials in Algorithm Design

One tool, followed through a decade of results: encode a combinatorial object as a polynomial, then read the algorithm off the polynomial's roots. Anari taught this once, the year after the technique produced breakthroughs in approximate counting and matroid sampling, and the course runs from the fundamental properties to applications in sparsification, high-dimensional expanders and random walks.

## Syllabus

From the catalog description; no public syllabus.

1. The setting: algorithms built by bridging the discrete and continuous worlds, as linear programming relaxations and spectral graph theory do.
2. Fundamental properties of polynomials, their roots and their analytic properties — the "geometry of polynomials" — that are useful in designing algorithms.
3. Applications in combinatorial optimization.
4. Graph sparsification.
5. High-dimensional expanders.
6. Analysis of random walks on combinatorial objects.
7. Counting algorithms.
