# CS 177: Introduction to Product Management with AI

Product management framed as relationships rather than roadmaps: discovery interviews, competitive analysis and business models on one side; negotiation, creative conflict and behavioral feedback on the other. Teams carry one product from problem to pitch, presented to working PMs. The Autumn 2026 title adds AI, which the catalog describes as an everyday collaborator through the whole process, and a rapid-prototyping hackathon.

## Materials

This offering's syllabus is public in full, on the class blog rather than the course site, and it is candid about where everything else goes: Canvas. The course's own site at `cs177.stanford.edu` has not been rolled over and still serves Autumn 2025, whose decks remain public.

- **Syllabus**: the [Autumn 2026 syllabus](https://highercommonsense.com/cs-177-human-centered-product-management/cs177-human-centered-product-management-syllabus/) — learning goals, the weekly shape of a team project assignment, a Product Sense Pushup and an ethics response, the grading split (attendance 20%, individual work 20%, quarter project 50%, team reviews 10%), the studio format and the absence policy.
- **Slides**: "All readings, slides, and assignments will be posted on Canvas." [Autumn 2025's site](https://cs177.stanford.edu/) still links a public Google Slides deck for each of its 17 sessions, plus guest decks, but that is the course before it was rebuilt around AI.
- **Notes**: readings are on Canvas. The syllabus recommends 15 product-management books instead — Cagan's *Inspired*, Fitzpatrick's *The Mom Test*, Patton's *User Story Mapping* and the rest — none required.
- **Videos**: none. The sessions "are highly interactive, a mix of lecture and team exercises, so they are not recorded"; a missed class is made up by reviewing the slides and writing 500 words.
- **Assignments**: briefs and rubrics are on Canvas, and the quarter-project page the syllabus points at is not on the public blog.
- **Solutions**, **Exams**: neither. The quarter ends in a one-pager, a working prototype shown at a science fair, and a final pitch.
- **Projects**: some assignments are posted to the [class blog](https://highercommonsense.com/), which carries this quarter's student writing — "Who Gets to Say No?", "Three Ways to Organize a Company" — beside earlier offerings' back to 2022.
- **Repo**: none; teams prototype with AI tools of their own choosing.

## Syllabus

The published Autumn 2025 schedule runs the team project in two phases with two reading strands beside it:

1. **Weeks 1–2**: what product management is; team norms, exploratory research, brainstorming, and market sizing (TAM/SAM/SOM).
2. **Weeks 3–5, discovery**: customer interviews and competitive analysis, a first business-model canvas, synthesis of the competitive landscape, a pitch presentation, and journey mapping.
3. **Weeks 6 onward, delivery**: story mapping and user stories, assumption testing, and a team roadmap.

Threaded through: ETHICS readings (ethical jobs, fair use versus piracy, "Internet's Original Sin") answered on the class blog, and BUSINESS exercises on revenue models and adoption.

The catalog adds, for Autumn 2026, generative AI used throughout — research, synthesis, ideation, prototyping, analysis and communication — with the accompanying skill of evaluating AI output, "recogniz[ing] hallucinations and weak assumptions, and determin[ing] when human research and judgment are essential", plus a required weekly studio.
