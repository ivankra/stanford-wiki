# CS 348N: Neural Models for 3D Geometry

Guibas's argument is that 3D design is where language and images were a few years earlier — that generative models could take modeling out of the hands of game and VFX specialists, but that 3D data is scarce enough to have held it back. The course surveys what has been tried: point-cloud networks, deepSDF and other coordinate-based implicits, GANs, flows, diffusion, NeRFs, and generation conditioned on an image or a sentence. Three coding assignments, a presentation and a small project.

## Syllabus

[The schedule](https://graphics.stanford.edu/courses/cs348n-23-spring/schedule_v2.html) runs from representations to generation.

1. Representations (Apr 3-10): classical geometry — voxels, point clouds, meshes, parametric and implicit surfaces — then volumetric neural representations and point-cloud architectures from PointNet to KPConv.
2. Generative models (Apr 12-19): autoencoders and VAEs, neural implicits and DeepSDF, public 3D datasets from ShapeNet to Objaverse, then flows and autoregressive models like PolyGen.
3. Later weeks: 3D GANs, denoising diffusion and score-based models, hierarchical structure generation, deep mesh architectures, conditional generation from images and language, NeRFs and neural rendering, shape edits and deformations, scene generation.
