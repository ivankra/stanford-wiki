# CS 324: Advances in Foundation Models

Taught by Ré, Liang and Hashimoto in the year foundation models became the field's organizing idea. Four weeks of instructor lectures cover the fundamentals — data, architectures, emergence, adaptation, infrastructure — then six weeks hand the room to guest speakers building these systems in industry: FlashAttention, PaLM scaling, mechanistic interpretability, dataset poisoning, compression as a route to AGI. Three quarters of the grade is a project that either builds something with an FM or investigates one.

## Syllabus

[Two halves](https://stanford-cs324.github.io/winter2023/).

1. Weeks 1-4, instructor lectures: what foundation models are, how data shapes them, architectures and training objectives, emergent behaviors, adaptation to new tasks, training infrastructure; then a survey of text, image-text and multimodal models; then security and privacy, environmental impact and legal considerations.
2. Weeks 5-10, guest talks: Tri Dao on FlashAttention, Aakanksha Chowdhery on PaLM and scaling, Stella Biderman on mechanistic interpretability, Colin Raffel on building models like open-source software, Raphael Townshend on RNA, Rob Reich on ethics, Nicholas Carlini on poisoning web-scale datasets, Jack Rae on compression for AGI.
