# CS 24: Minds and Machines

The gateway to Symbolic Systems, and the one course a student must take before declaring that major: a flipped, five-code introduction to cognitive science that asks what a mind is, what computation is, and whether either explains the other — taught from philosophy, mathematics, empirical psychology and computational modeling at once.

Two public documents, five years apart; everything they point at — videos, readings, problem sets, projects — is on Canvas by design.

At five years old it is a guide to the shape of the course, not to this year's version.

## Syllabus

No dated schedule is public. The syllabus gives the shape: a flipped course where students watch videos and read each week, then attend both a small TA-led section and an all-class lecture. Its questions are the course's outline — what minds are, what computation is, what rationality and intelligence mean, whether human behavior can be predicted, whether computers can be truly intelligent, and how people and technology interact — approached through the methods of philosophy, mathematics, empirical research and computational modeling.

Assessment is continuous rather than examined: weekly responses 10%, problem sets 30%, participation 25%, module projects 35%, with the lowest grade in each category dropped. The honor-code policy is unusually blunt for a course listed under CS: "You may not use LLMs ('AI') at all."

## Prerequisites

- None. The material is pitched at an introductory level and the course is meant to be taken early.
