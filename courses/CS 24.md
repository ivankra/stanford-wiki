# CS 24: Minds and Machines

The gateway to Symbolic Systems, and the one course a student must take before declaring that major: a flipped, five-code introduction to cognitive science that asks what a mind is, what computation is, and whether either explains the other — taught from philosophy, mathematics, empirical psychology and computational modeling at once.

## Materials

What the course covers is public — a dated schedule and a complete reading list survive for 2 offerings — but everything a student does with it is on Canvas: the lecture videos, the readings themselves, the weekly responses, the problem sets and the module projects.

- **Syllabus**: this offering's is a Canvas upload readable only inside Stanford, and there is no public site. Two older schedules stand in. [Winter 2026's](https://web.stanford.edu/~bkrejci/SYMSYS1-Winter26.pdf), on the coordinator's own page, gives 10 dated weeks in 4 modules — what a mind is, synthetic minds, organic minds, interacting minds — with each week's videos, readings, lecture, guest speaker and due date; [Autumn 2021's](https://docs.google.com/document/d/1EmkvxxdqH7BAtwWr_SmfyVrrYYa3LjPC3-VkGbmRTJU/edit), a Google Doc, does the same over 3 modules and is still what `web.stanford.edu/class/symsys1/` redirects to. The [Summer Session syllabus](https://summer.stanford.edu/files/summer/media/file/symsys_1_syllabus_summer_2026.pdf) adds what neither has: grade weights, the late policy and the honor code.
- **Slides**: none found for any offering; in a flipped course the teaching is in the weekly videos.
- **Notes**: no textbook — "Please access course material via Canvas". Winter 2026's reading list is half-public: the Open Encyclopedia of Cognitive Science articles, Shiffman's *Nature of Code*, the HAI and TED pieces open, while the 4 module folders are Canvas links; Autumn 2021's roughly 40 Drive readings return 401.
- **Videos**: closed everywhere. Both schedules link their lecture videos — 21 in Winter 2026, about 25 in Autumn 2021 — as Panopto viewer URLs that redirect to a Stanford login.
- **Assignments**, **Projects**: a weekly response, a problem set most weeks and a module project per module, 75% of the grade between them, all set and handed in on Canvas. The schedules give their due dates and nothing else.

- **Exams**: none; the course sets no midterm or final.

## Syllabus

This offering publishes no schedule. [Winter 2026's](https://web.stanford.edu/~bkrejci/SYMSYS1-Winter26.pdf) is the closest, 10 weeks in 4 modules, each week a set of videos and readings done before a lecture, a guest and a discussion:

1. **What is a mind?** — minds and bodies (Flanagan); embodied to abstract machines (Hillis); automata and computation; minds as automatic formal systems; levels of analysis (Marr, and whether neuroscience could understand Donkey Kong); grammar as a machine (Pinker); Searle's Chinese room and imitation games.
2. **Synthetic minds** — a short history of AI; large language models; machine learning and the Nature of Code's neural networks; TensorFlow Playground hands-on; catastrophic AI risk, fairness and algorithmic bias.
3. **Organic minds** — Bayesian models of perception and action; optimality versus heuristics in judgment and decision-making; how babies think, and cognitive development as learning.
4. **Interacting minds** — social learning and the naive utility calculus; Grice's implicature; game theory (Binmore); cultural evolution and cultural attractors. The last week has students pick 3 articles from the Open Encyclopedia of Cognitive Science and relate them.

[Autumn 2021](https://docs.google.com/document/d/1EmkvxxdqH7BAtwWr_SmfyVrrYYa3LjPC3-VkGbmRTJU/edit) ran the same material in 3 modules, with a week on "current controversies" — GPT-3 and machine understanding, Bender and Koller — that later offerings fold into the AI module.

Assessment is continuous rather than examined: weekly responses 10%, problem sets 30%, participation 25%, module projects 35%, with the lowest grade in each category dropped. The honor-code policy is unusually blunt for a course listed under CS: "You may not use LLMs ('AI') at all."
