# CS 271: Artificial Intelligence in Healthcare

Organized by data type rather than method: 2 weeks on medical images, 2 on electronic health records and clinical text, then multimodal models and genomics — each time asking what the modality does to the modeling. The last third turns to what deployment in medicine demands: interpretability, fairness, robustness, privacy. 3 assignments tied to the data types, a midterm and a poster-session project.

## Syllabus

[The schedule](https://web.stanford.edu/class/biods220/syllabus.html) moves through the data types.

1. Medical images (Sep 26-Oct 5): classification, then detection and segmentation, then 3D and video.
2. Electronic health records (Oct 10-17): introduction, advanced topics, text data and representations.
3. Multimodal and genomics (late October): multimodal models, weakly and self-supervised learning, transformers, genomics, then the midterm.
4. Deployment concerns (November): unsupervised learning and RL, interpretability, fairness and ethics, distributed learning, security and privacy — interleaved with guest speakers, ending on a legal perspective from Zach Harned.

The quarter closes with a poster session.

## Prerequisites

The catalog lists none. The syllabus schedules a deep-learning fundamentals review in week 1 and a Numpy/TensorFlow session in week 2, so the assumed background is programming plus some machine learning.
