# CS 184: Bridging Policy and Tech Through Design

A project course whose point is the mix: it "aims to bring together students from computer science and the social sciences to work with external partner organizations at the nexus of digital technology and public policy".

## Materials

This offering published nothing. What is public is the whole of **Spring 2021**, one year earlier, which Jeffrey Ullman still keeps on his own Stanford page: the syllabus, every deliverable's specification, the information-session deck and its recording, a dozen project briefs and the dataset list the teams work from. The course code's own path redirects there.

- **Syllabus**: nothing for Spring 2022. The [Spring 2021 syllabus PDF](http://i.stanford.edu/~ullman/cs184/Syllabus2021.pdf) gives the two kinds of weekly meeting, every deliverable with its due date, the attendance policy and the honor code.
- **Slides**: no teaching decks from any offering — the weekly sessions are working sessions, guest lectures and workshops. Spring 2021's [information-session deck](http://i.stanford.edu/~ullman/cs184/Infosession2021.pdf) is the one that is published, and it carries that year's project list.
- **Notes**: Spring 2021's [class page](http://i.stanford.edu/~ullman/cs184.html) carries an SQL primer with tutorial links and about a dozen public datasets the projects use — Facebook's Hateful Memes set, the Political Ad Archive, California's legislative downloads, the CIA World Factbook, World Bank data.
- **Videos**: closed. Class content goes to Canvas, and only the first half of each session is recorded, "not the second half, which is discussion-based". Spring 2021's information-session recording is the one public video.
- **Assignments**: nothing for this offering. Spring 2021's syllabus specifies each — a weekly progress-journal entry, a two-page proposal in week 2, a technical prototype with a midterm presentation in week 5, a final pitch, and a six-to-eight-page written summary — and its [project briefs](http://i.stanford.edu/~ullman/cs184/Projects2021.zip) ship as a 3 MB zip of a dozen one-page documents, one per partner.
- **Solutions**, **Exams**: none. The course is assessed on project deliverables.
- **Projects**: nothing students produced is published from any offering.
- **Repo**: not found.

## Syllabus

The [Spring 2021 syllabus](http://i.stanford.edu/~ullman/cs184/Syllabus2021.pdf) has no topic sequence — the project is the content. Teams of three or four, with "at least one student with a social science background and one with a computing background", meet once a week as a class for a working session, guest lecture or workshop, and once a week with their mentor and partner organization. The deliverables pace the quarter: proposal in week 2, prototype and midterm presentation in week 5, final pitch and written summary in week 10.

That year's partners and questions, from the project briefs: classifying trends in human-rights articles for the Business and Human Rights Resource Centre; a user-oriented open-data handbook for California's Government Operations Agency; cataloguing the most harmful dark patterns in user interfaces; "what hate speech policies are both equitable and implementable by computer?" with a Facebook AI researcher, on the Hateful Memes dataset; accessible navigation of immigration forms; a workable system for online community governance with MetaGov; equitable elementary-school assignment with San Francisco Unified; and sentiment analysis of student-loan tweets.

## Prerequisites

None fixed. "Appropriate preparation depends on the nature of the project proposed, and will be verified by the teaching staff based on your application."
