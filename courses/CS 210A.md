# CS 210A: Industry Innovation Lab

The opening quarter of Jay Borenstein's two-quarter lab. Corporate partners pay in and seed a loosely defined challenge from their R&D labs; student teams are run as startups, with a budget and a technical advisory board drawn from the staff and the partner's liaisons, and some travel to the partner's headquarters, occasionally internationally. Winter is where teams form, scope the challenge and start building; the shipping and the Software Fair belong to [CS 210B](CS%20210B.md). It meets twice a week in what the catalog calls an "open loft classroom format such as found in Silicon Valley software companies".

## Materials

A reader can see what the teams finished and what tools the staff point them at, and nothing of the course itself: no schedule, no grading scheme, no project brief. The syllabus goes to Canvas, Stanford-only, and the brief belongs to the corporate partner.

- **Syllabus**: not public. The [CS 210 site](https://web.stanford.edu/class/cs210/) is 3 marketing paragraphs, an [About page](https://web.stanford.edu/class/cs210/about.html) naming the teaching team, and a Software Fair page; the syllabus itself is a Stanford-only Canvas upload.
- **Slides**: none found. Teaching is studio sessions and guest lectures from "industry leaders and visionaries", and no deck of either is published.
- **Notes**: the [CS 210 Build Guide](https://cs210.github.io/build-guide/), shared with [CS 210B](CS%20210B.md) — 11 pages of quick-start links for web, databases, analytics, ML and LLMs, VR and mobile, plus a class-resources page on deployment credits and team tooling. It is a reading list for building, not a course reader, and 3 of its GitHub links now 404.
- **Videos**: no source says the sessions are recorded, and no public set turned up.
- **Assignments**: only [Assign0](https://github.com/cs210/Assign0) is public, a 7-step exercise in creating a GitHub account, branching and opening a pull request. The real work is a brief a corporate partner sets, and none of it is published.
- **Solutions**, **Exams**: none; the course sets no problem sets and no exam, and is graded on a two-quarter team software product.
- **Projects**: the [2026 Software Fair program guide](https://cs210.github.io/SoftwareGuide-2026/) describes what each of 40+ teams from CS 210, [CS 194](CS%20194.md) and [CS 191](CS%20191.md) built, with an interactive booth map. It covers the finished work, which ships in the Spring quarter. The [2025](https://cs210.github.io/SoftwareGuide/) and [2023](https://cs210.github.io/fair-guide/) guides are still served too.
- **Repo**: the [cs210 organization](https://github.com/cs210) has 11 public repositories, of which 3 are teams' own code — [AAM-1](https://github.com/cs210/AAM-1) from this sequence, Pivot from Winter 2025's and kenja from Winter 2024's. The other 40-odd teams a year work in private repositories.

## Syllabus

No schedule is published. The catalog gives the shape and the intended exposure:

> Two-quarter project course. Focus is on real-world software development. Corporate partners seed projects with loosely defined challenges from their R&D labs; students innovate to build their own compelling software solutions. Student teams are treated as start-up companies with a budget and a technical advisory board comprised of instructional staff and corporate liaisons.

Exposure, as the catalog lists it: current practices in software engineering; techniques for stimulating innovation; significant development experience with creative freedoms; working in groups; real-world software engineering challenges; public presentation of technical work; creating written descriptions of technical work.

## Prerequisites

None stated. The Build Guide assumes a student can stand up a full-stack application, and teams are expected to pick their own stack, so practical programming experience at the level of a CS 100-series project course is the working bar.

## Related

- [CS 224G](CS%20224G.md): the other project studio, shipping LLM applications to a Demo Day over a single quarter rather than to a corporate partner over two.
