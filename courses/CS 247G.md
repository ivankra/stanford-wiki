# CS 247G: Design for Play

Christina Wodtke's game-design studio, taught as a public blog: students post their critical plays, sketchnotes and project write-ups to The Mechanics of Magic, which the course shares with [CS 377G](CS%20377G.md). The quarter is 2 projects, a social game and then an environmental game of puzzles and levels (in Spring 2022, optionally a physical escape room). Games can be paper or digital, and Unity is optional.

## Materials

Wodtke's decks, written lecture and video series, the syllabus and every student's work are public, but the decks and syllabus are Spring 2022's: this offering's syllabus and dated schedule sit on Canvas, and no brief for a single deliverable is public.

- **Syllabus**: Summer 2026's is on Canvas. The site's [247G Syllabus](https://mechanicsofmagic.com/sample-page/) is Spring 2022's, served unchanged since: attendance and studio sections, 2 projects over 9 weeks, grading (projects 50%, critical play 15%, team reviews 15%, participation 10%, sketchnotes 10%), a late policy and a prototyping-kit list. It sends students to Canvas for "a more precise schedule".
- **Slides**: 13 published Google Slides decks, one per lecture page from [Week One Lecture A](https://mechanicsofmagic.com/sample-page/design-for-play-week-one-lecture-a/) to Week 8 Lecture B, all from Spring 2022.
- **Notes**: Wodtke's written lecture [The Formal Elements of Game Design](https://mechanicsofmagic.com/sample-page/the-formal-elements-of-game-design-2/), under a share-and-attribute license, and the [reading library](https://mechanicsofmagic.com/the-library/): *Theory of Fun*, *Game Design Workshop*, *Game Feel*, *Rules of Play* and others.
- **Videos**: the written lecture embeds her own 6-part YouTube series on players, objectives, outcomes, procedures and rules, and boundaries. No class session is public; the Spring 2022 syllabus warns that "watching recordings will not replicate the learning environment".
- **Assignments**: the syllabus outlines both projects, a social game (Space Team, Among Us) and an environmental game of puzzles and levels (Firewatch, Portal), plus weekly critical plays and sketchnotes.
- **Solutions**, **Exams**: none; the grade is projects, critiques, team reviews, participation and sketchnotes.
- **Projects**: the [CS247G category](https://mechanicsofmagic.com/category/cs247g/) is the students' public work: 602 posts from Summer 2026 alone, from critical plays of escape-room games to "Games In Space" project write-ups and final reflections.
- **Repo**: none found.
- **Extras**: [community resources](https://mechanicsofmagic.com/cs247g-community-game-design-resources/), campus makerspaces and GameCrafter for self-publishing.

## Syllabus

No Summer 2026 syllabus is public. The [Spring 2022 syllabus](https://mechanicsofmagic.com/sample-page/) lays out 9 weeks:

1. **Week 1**: core concepts of game design and critical play.
2. **Weeks 2–4, Project 1, Social Mediation Game**, "a game for friends (or friends to be)": target players, game architecture, aesthetics and poetics, graphic and information design.
3. **Weeks 5–9, Project 2, Environmental Storytelling**: narrative in space, puzzle design, level design, playtesting "with friends, family, and friendly strangers", game balance.

Studio sections, run by the course assistants, teach tools including Unity. Summer 2026's student posts follow the same second project, "Games In Space".

## Prerequisites

- Design research, prototyping and interface design: [CS 147](CS%20147.md) or equivalent background. The catalog waives [CS 147](CS%20147.md) for non-CS majors and welcomes "music, art and writing students"; the syllabus states no technical requirements.
