# CS 181W: Computers, Ethics, and Public Policy (WIM)

[CS 181](CS%20181.md) with the writing requirement attached, and the larger of the two: the cap is 75 here against 50 for [CS 181](CS%20181.md). The extra work is one assignment revised and resubmitted after a session with a Technical Communication Program instructor.

## Materials

Everything is the shared [CS 181](CS%20181.md) site, and the only difference is the WIM revision, which the site describes but does not publish. The readings are open, the lectures are never recorded, and the dated calendar is still TBD.

- **Syllabus**: the [shared site](https://stanfordcs181.github.io/) spells out the WIM revision and links the sign-up, alongside the learning goals and the grading split. No dated calendar.
- **Slides**: the one shared deck, ["The Greatest Good"](https://stanfordcs181.github.io/slides--the-greatest-good.pdf).
- **Notes**: the shared reading set, about 2 dozen papers, essays and articles; 5 of its links are on a host that has since gone.
- **Videos**: none; the class is not recorded.
- **Assignments**: the shared [project brief](https://docs.google.com/document/d/1gbRriOP1aMHVVJgiGdFplU_A6pAG6hu3ysrBPdLxnEE/edit?usp=sharing) exports without an account. The short writing assignments are not posted, and neither is the WIM revision, which is described only in the site's own sentence.
- **Solutions**, **Exams**: none; there is no final.
- **Projects**: the shared project ends in a poster session, and nothing from this offering is published.
- **Repo**: none.
- **Extra**: the same 11 years of student project sites on [Eric Roberts's page](https://cs.stanford.edu/people/eroberts/courses/cs181/) that [CS 181](CS%20181.md) records — 1995-96 to 2010-11, last touched 2013, far too old to rate.

## Syllabus

Identical to [CS 181](CS%20181.md): recognizing ethical implications, reasoning from disciplinary norms, and arguing persuasively, assessed through short memos, in-class exercises and a quarter-long project outside the campus. Grading is writing 40%, project 30%, section 20%, lecture 10%, with no final exam.

The addition, in the site's words: "181W students will meet with an instructor from the Technical Communication Program to revise and resubmit one of their assignments."
