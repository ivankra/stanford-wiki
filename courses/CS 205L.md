# CS 205L: Continuous Mathematical Methods with an Emphasis on Machine Learning

Ron Fedkiw's replacement for CS 205A: the continuous mathematics underneath machine learning, taught as numerical analysis rather than as ML. It ends where most ML courses begin — the last two units derive momentum and ADAM as ODE integrators applied to gradient flow. Grading is 50% problem sets and 50% weekly **oral** quizzes, one-on-one with a TA over Zoom; there are no exams, and the course states it can be completed fully remotely.

## Materials

The whole lecture set is public; the problem sets and recordings are not.

- **Syllabus**: the [lectures page](https://web.stanford.edu/class/cs205l/lectures.html) carries the ten-week schedule and a per-unit topic list; the [home page](https://web.stanford.edu/class/cs205l/) gives the grading split and prerequisites, and the [assignments page](https://web.stanford.edu/class/cs205l/assignments.html) the problem-set and quiz rules.

- **Assignments**: released on Ed, submitted on Gradescope. The rules are public — five questions a week rated 1–3 stars, pick any combination summing to six — the sets themselves are not.
- **Exams**: none. The quizzes replace them.
- **Projects**: none.

## Syllabus

Twenty-one units over ten weeks, per the [lectures page](https://web.stanford.edu/class/cs205l/lectures.html).

1. **Weeks 1–3, linear algebra**: interpolation, overfitting and error sources; Gaussian elimination, LU, pivoting and sparsity; eigenvalues, SVD, condition number and norms; diagonally dominant and SPD matrices, Cholesky; conjugate gradient.
2. **Week 4, approximation**: Taylor series, sampling, splines; Newton-Cotes and Gaussian quadrature, the curse of dimensionality, Monte Carlo.
3. **Weeks 5–6, least squares**: weighted minimization, critical points and quadratic forms, normal equations, Gram-Schmidt, QR and Householder; underdetermined systems, the pseudo-inverse, PCA, QR iteration and the power method; Tikhonov regularization.
4. **Week 7, nonlinear problems**: Jacobians and linearization, fixed-point iteration, Newton, secant and bisection; golden section search.
5. **Week 8, derivatives**: activation functions and soft-margin classification; symbolic differentiation, finite differences, automatic differentiation, dropout; Broyden, DFP, BFGS, L-BFGS, Gauss-Newton and Levenberg-Marquardt.
6. **Weeks 9–10, descent and momentum**: steepest descent; gradient flow as an ODE, forward Euler and Runge-Kutta, stability analysis, adaptive time stepping as adaptive learning rates, implicit SGD, Nesterov momentum, ADAM and Newmark methods.

## Prerequisites

- Linear algebra and multivariable calculus: MATH 51.
- Real analysis or applied matrix theory: MATH 104 or MATH 113 — or, the site says, "comfort with the associated material".

No machine-learning background is assumed; the ML content is motivation, not prerequisite.
