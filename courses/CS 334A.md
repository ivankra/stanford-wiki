# CS 334A: Convex Optimization I

The course whose textbook everyone owns. Boyd and Vandenberghe's aim is narrow and stated plainly: learn to recognize when a problem is convex, because after that it is solved. Stanford listed it under a CS number only through 2019-20; it has run ever since as EE 364A, and the materials have been free the whole time.

## Materials

The textbook, a deck per lecture, the exercise set, the solver and a full lecture recording are all free, and have been for two decades. What a reader doesn't get is the week-by-week schedule, which lives on Ed, and the homework solutions, which need a SUNet login.

- **Syllabus**: the [site](https://web.stanford.edu/class/ee364a/) carries the description, lecture times, staff and announcements, but says "up to date and detailed information is on Ed", and the `homework.html`, `midterm.html` and `final.html` pages earlier offerings kept are gone. A reader gets the shape of the course, not its dated form.
- **Slides**: an open listing of [24 files](https://web.stanford.edu/class/ee364a/lectures/), one deck per lecture from [convex sets](https://web.stanford.edu/class/ee364a/lectures/sets.pdf) through duality, statistical estimation and [interior-point methods](https://web.stanford.edu/class/ee364a/lectures/barrier.pdf), plus the whole set as one PDF, 4 extra-topic decks and the CVX and Convex.jl tutorials. Boyd's own page keeps the [combined deck](https://web.stanford.edu/~boyd/cvxbook/bv_cvxslides.pdf) and the [pre-2023 version](https://web.stanford.edu/~boyd/cvxbook/bv_cvxslides_original.pdf).
- **Notes**: [*Convex Optimization*](https://web.stanford.edu/~boyd/cvxbook/), the Cambridge textbook, free in full with its errata list.
- **Videos**: the course's own recordings are CGOE's and go to enrolled students only, but Stanford Online has [18 lectures under Boyd](https://www.youtube.com/playlist?list=PLoROMvodv4rMJqxxviPa4AmDClvcbHi6h), the whole course as taught in 2023 and the only offering anyone has published. Every video's description points back at the EE 364A site, so they track these same slides.
- **Assignments**: the homework is textbook exercises by number plus the [additional exercises](https://github.com/cvxgrp/cvxbook_additional_exercises), a public repository whose own description reads "Additional exercises and data for EE364a. No solutions; for public consumption", with data files in Python, Julia and MATLAB. Which ones a given week asks for goes out on Ed.
- **Solutions**: "Access to solutions requires SUNetID", and every solution PDF on the class host redirects to Stanford's SSO.
- **Exams**: 2 open listings nothing links any more, [midterm/](https://web.stanford.edu/class/ee364a/midterm/) with 4 past papers and [finals/](https://web.stanford.edu/class/ee364a/finals/) with 8, among them this offering's midterm of July 16 2020 under A. Fu and Winter 2020's midterm and take-home final under Duchi.
- **Projects**: none; the work is homework, a midterm and a final.
- **Repo**: [CVXPY](https://www.cvxpy.org/), with CVX, Convex.jl and CVXR as the other bindings, plus the [DCP analyzer](https://dcp.stanford.edu).
- **Textbook**: the same *Convex Optimization*, which is also the lecture notes.

## Syllabus

From the catalog description, which the site reproduces.

1. **Convex analysis**: convex sets, convex functions, convex optimization problems, and the basics of convex analysis.
2. **Problem classes**: least squares, linear and quadratic programs, semidefinite programming, geometric programming, minimax and extremal volume problems.
3. **Theory**: optimality conditions, duality theory, theorems of alternative, and their applications.
4. **Algorithms**: numerical methods for smooth and equality-constrained problems; interior-point methods for inequality-constrained problems.
5. **Applications**: signal processing, communications, control, analog and digital circuit design, computational geometry, statistics, machine learning and mechanical engineering.

## Prerequisites

- Linear algebra at the level of EE 263.
- Basic probability.

The site's own statement of audience is broad: anyone who will use scientific computing or optimization in engineering or related work.
