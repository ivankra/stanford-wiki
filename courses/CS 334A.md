# CS 334A: Convex Optimization I

The course whose textbook everyone owns. Boyd and Vandenberghe's aim is narrow and stated plainly: learn to recognise when a problem is convex, because after that it is solved. Stanford listed it under a CS number only through 2019-20; it has run ever since as EE 364A, and the materials have been free the whole time.

## Materials

Everything except the homework and the recordings is public, and has been for two decades.

- **Syllabus**: the [site](https://web.stanford.edu/class/ee364a/) carries the description, objectives, intended audience and requirements. The dated schedule now lives on Ed, which is gated — so a reader gets the shape of the course but not its week-by-week form.
- **Slides**: the [complete deck](https://www.stanford.edu/~boyd/cvxbook/bv_cvxslides.pdf) as one PDF, with the [original slides](https://www.stanford.edu/~boyd/cvxbook/bv_cvxslides_original.pdf) used until Summer 2023 kept beside it, plus separate PDFs on [examples](https://web.stanford.edu/class/ee364a/lectures/examples.pdf), [stochastic programming](https://web.stanford.edu/class/ee364a/lectures/stoch_prog.pdf), [chance-constrained optimization](https://web.stanford.edu/class/ee364a/lectures/chance_constr.pdf) and filter design.
- **Notes**: [*Convex Optimization*](https://www.stanford.edu/~boyd/cvxbook/), the Cambridge textbook, free online in full.
- **Videos**: the course is on CGOE, so lecture recordings go to enrolled students only.
- **Assignments**: homework goes out on Ed and comes back through Gradescope; neither is public.
- **Code**: [CVXPY](https://www.cvxpy.org/), with CVX (MATLAB), Convex.jl and CVXR as the other bindings, and the [DCP analyzer](https://dcp.stanford.edu).

## Syllabus

From the catalog description, which the site reproduces.

1. **Convex analysis**: convex sets, convex functions, convex optimization problems, and the basics of convex analysis.
2. **Problem classes**: least squares, linear and quadratic programs, semidefinite programming, geometric programming, minimax and extremal volume problems.
3. **Theory**: optimality conditions, duality theory, theorems of alternative, and their applications.
4. **Algorithms**: numerical methods for smooth and equality-constrained problems; interior-point methods for inequality-constrained problems.
5. **Applications**: signal processing, communications, control, analog and digital circuit design, computational geometry, statistics, machine learning and mechanical engineering.

## Prerequisites

- Linear algebra at the level of EE 263.
- Basic probability.

The site's own statement of audience is broad: anyone who will use scientific computing or optimization in engineering or related work.
