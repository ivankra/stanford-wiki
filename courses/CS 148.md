# CS 148: Introduction to Computer Graphics and Imaging

Graphics taught towards images you can show: eight homeworks that start in Blender and end in a ray tracer, no exams, and a final project judged on what it renders. The site keeps a per-quarter showcase of the best student reports going back to 2020.

## Materials

All eight homeworks, their starter files and ten quarters of student project reports are public; the decks go up lecture by lecture and three of sixteen are out so far, though [Summer 2026](https://web.stanford.edu/class/cs148/summer26/materials/) left thirteen of its own behind. Recordings are Canvas-only.

- **Syllabus**: the [lecture schedule](https://web.stanford.edu/class/cs148/lectures.html), each class with its supplemental reading, and grading and prerequisites on the [home page](https://cs148.stanford.edu/).
- **Slides**: [Introduction](https://web.stanford.edu/class/cs148/materials/class_01_intro.pdf), Working with Light and Virtual World are on disk; the schedule's other thirteen links 404 until posted, which the page says outright. Summer 2026's [materials directory](https://web.stanford.edu/class/cs148/summer26/materials/) has thirteen decks, geometry through simulation and advanced topics.
- **Notes**: none of its own; the schedule sets chapters of Shirley and Marschner instead.
- **Videos**: live stream and recordings through Canvas Panopto.
- **Assignments**: [HW1](https://web.stanford.edu/class/cs148/hws/cs148-hw1.pdf) — Blender setup — through HW8 (texturing), all as PDFs with release and due dates, plus the final-project handout. The home page's `materials/FinalProject.pdf` link 404s.
- **Solutions**: none published; the homeworks are rendered images, graded on Gradescope.
- **Exams**: none — "50% Homework and 50% Final Project. No exams!"

- **Textbook**: Shirley and Marschner, *Fundamentals of Computer Graphics*, cited chapter by chapter as supplemental reading.

## Syllabus

From the [schedule](https://web.stanford.edu/class/cs148/lectures.html), one homework a week trailing each pair of lectures:

1. **Weeks 1–2, building scenes**: introduction, working with light, virtual worlds, triangles — homework in Blender, then triangles and transformations.
2. **Week 3, ray tracing**: ray tracing and recursive ray tracing.
3. **Week 4, appearance**: optics and shaders — lighting and shading.
4. **Week 5, transport**: global illumination and photon mapping.
5. **Week 6 onward**: sampling and antialiasing, advanced rendering, then geometric modeling and texturing.

The last month is the final project: a ray tracer extended into an image of the student's choosing, graded on the demo.

## Prerequisites

- Systems and programming: [CS 107](CS%20107.md), plus fluency in Python.
- Mathematics: MATH 51 — vectors and vector spaces, matrices, and solving linear systems.

## Related

- [CS 44N](CS%2044N.md): a first-year introductory seminar on graphics ideas, without the rendering pipeline.
- [CS 248B](CS%20248B.md): the next course in the sequence, on animation and simulation.
- [CS 348B](CS%20348B.md): physically based rendering in depth on pbrt: radiometry, Monte Carlo integration, path tracing and volumes.
