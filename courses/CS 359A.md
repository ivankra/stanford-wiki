# CS 359A: Research Seminar in Complexity Theory

Concrete complexity rather than the P-versus-NP kind: what can be proved about decision trees, DNF formulas, halfspaces and juntas, using the analysis-of-Boolean-functions toolkit. Tan opens with an overview, hands the bulk of the quarter to student-led deep dives, and spends the last few meetings with the class working on open problems together. No coursework beyond leading a discussion or two and writing meeting summaries.

## Syllabus

4 topic blocks ([site](https://cs359a.stanford.edu/)), covered in the opening overview and then in student-led depth.

1. **Decision trees**: query complexity and the relationships among complexity measures; every decision tree has an influential variable; the Aaronson-Ambainis conjecture and the power of quantum computation; P = NP ∩ coNP for decision trees; the sensitivity theorem.
2. **DNF formulas**: random restrictions and switching lemmas; Linial-Mansour-Nisan on the Fourier spectrum of DNFs; Mansour's conjecture; Razborov-Smolensky polynomial approximators; Bazzi's theorem that bounded independence fools DNFs; Fredman-Khachiyan and monotone dualization.
3. **Halfspaces**: noise sensitivity; halfspaces and the Central Limit Theorem; the Mossel-O'Donnell-Oleszkiewicz invariance principle; the Gotsman-Linial conjecture; bounded independence fools halfspaces.
4. **Juntas**: learning in the presence of irrelevant information; Mossel-O'Donnell-Servedio; Valiant's light bulb problem.

Applications run to circuit lower bounds, derandomization, quantum complexity, learning theory and applied probability.
