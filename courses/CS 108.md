# CS 108: Object-Oriented Systems Design

The course after [CS 106B](CS%20106B.md) for people who want to build big things in Java: several large projects, taught around design patterns and the discipline of working in a team on a codebase too large to hold in your head.

## Materials

The public page is a stub that names Canvas as the class website and says it "will not be updated". The one thing it links is a real course-information handout for this offering: objectives, grading and policies, but no schedule and no specification of any of the five assignments. The course's own teaching material has not been public since 2015.

- **Syllabus**: the [course-information handout](https://web.stanford.edu/class/cs108/01CourseInformation.pdf) gives the 5 class objectives, the 66.6/33.3 split between individual homework and the team project, three late days with none allowed on the last assignment, and an unusually exacting rule for asking for debugging help — any request must include three conjectures about the cause and the steps taken to test them.
- **Slides**, **Assignments**, **Projects**: closed, on Canvas. 5 individual assignments run through week 7, then a group project; none of them is published, and no student work is.
- **Notes**, **Videos**, **Solutions**, **Repo**: not found.
- **Exams**: none. "There is no midterm and no final for this class."
- **Older offerings**: the Internet Archive holds five complete ones, Spring 2013 to Spring 2015 — about 37 numbered handouts a quarter (Intermediate Java, unit testing, generics, OOP design, inner classes, Swing, threads, networking), the lecture directories and the homework specs, including [HW2 Tetris](https://web.archive.org/web/20160122100707/http://web.stanford.edu/class/cs108/handouts152/15HW2Tetris.pdf). Eleven years old and from before the wiki's window, so nothing rests on them, but nothing since has been public.

## Syllabus

The catalog gives the content: "Software design and construction in the context of large OOP libraries. Taught in Java. Topics: OOP design, design patterns, testing, graphical user interface (GUI) OOP libraries, software" engineering practice.

The site states five objectives, which say more about the course's intent than the catalog does:

- "To substantially strengthen students' programming ability by requiring them to program a number of large, interesting projects."
- "To teach students to find information on their own and solve problems on their own using available documentation; to give them the confidence in their own abilities they will need when programming in industry or as grad students."
- "To solidify students understanding of object-oriented principles."
- "To provide team programming experience and to show students how use of software engineering principles can greatly improve team programming."
- "To provide exposure to a broad range of programming areas including multi-threaded programs, communication between processes, and interacting with databases."

It adds that in this offering "the GUI portion of the assignments will be targetting the Android platform."
