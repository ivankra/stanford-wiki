# CS 323: The AI Awakening: Implications for the Economy and Society

Erik Brynjolfsson's Digital Economy Lab seminar on what AI is doing to the economy. Ten weekly guest speakers — economists, researchers and founders — plus a team project. Spring 2026 emphasised venture creation: small teams prototype an AI-enabled product or startup and present it at the end of the quarter. Admission is by application.

## Syllabus

From the [Spring 2025 syllabus](https://digitaleconomy.stanford.edu/app/uploads/2026/02/Econ295_CS323-Spring-2025-Syllabus-05-13.pdf); Spring 2026 kept the shape and shifted the emphasis to venture creation.

Tuesdays are an 80-minute guest lecture with assigned readings: the AI awakening and the bitter lesson, robotics and embodied AI, scientific progress in AI, national security and superintelligence strategy, frontier-lab research, AI and labour, entrepreneurship, policy.

Thursdays are workshops around the team project: forming groups, two rounds of peer feedback, careers in AI research, a VC pitch workshop, and a session on macroeconomic growth models under partial or full automation. The quarter ends with team presentations of a venture, policy or research proposal.

Grading is 20% each: weekly assignments, progress updates, final presentation, final document, and class discussion.

## Prerequisites

None stated. Admission is by application, closing mid-March for the Spring quarter, and the course is aimed at graduate students in economics, business and computer science.
