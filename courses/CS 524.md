# CS 524: AI for Science

Organized around recurring method themes rather than scientific fields: multi-scale modeling, multi-modal architectures, differentiable simulation, search-augmented reasoning and data valuation, each week pairing a milestone with a current result. 20 student pairs give graded lightning talks through the quarter, and 10 guests from DeepMind, Arc Institute, Isomorphic Labs, NVIDIA and Osmo take a session each.

## Materials

The site publishes the shape of the course and nothing of its content: a 10-week schedule, the speaker list and a project spec detailed down to a reproducibility checklist, but not one citation, deck or recording. A seminar built on paper pairs that never names a paper leaves a reader with the plan and none of the reading.

- **Syllabus**: 5 modules over 10 weeks with topics, guest slots and milestone dates, then the grading split — project 60%, presentations and reading responses 20%, participation 20% ([course site](https://web.stanford.edu/class/cs524/)).
- **Slides**: none posted. Student pairs upload their own lightning-talk slides to Canvas by 8:30 a.m. on their slot day, and none of those is public either.
- **Notes**: no reading list. Each session gets a sentence naming systems — NeuralGCM, AlphaFold, Gato, Evo, MuZero, AlphaProof, TransE, AlphaChip — and the page's data file holds no external link at all.
- **Videos**: the modality is in-person and no recording is mentioned.
- **Assignments**: the [project spec](https://web.stanford.edu/class/cs524/#assignments) is complete — pitch (10%), mid-term review with a 3 to 4-page memo (20%), lightning talk (10%) with its rubric, final report with repo (25%) — alongside the reading-response requirement, a 72-hour slip-day bank and a reproducibility checklist covering seeds, runtimes, ablations and negative results.
- **Solutions**, **Exams**: none.
- **Projects**, **Repo**: the final report must ship with a repository; none is published, and the course publishes no starter code of its own.

## Syllabus

5 modules ([schedule](https://web.stanford.edu/class/cs524/#schedule)). **A. Foundations** (weeks 1-3): the AI-for-science landscape, reasoning and inference-time scaling; multi-scale modeling and weather foundation models (NeuralGCM); graph-structured learning, message passing, AlphaFold and world modeling. **B. Modalities and simulation** (4–5): cross-domain models (Gato, Evo, IgLM), differentiable simulation, Earth-2/CorrDiff. **C. Reasoning and data** (6–7): the AlphaGo→AlphaZero→MuZero arc, AlphaTensor→AlphaEvolve, AlphaProof and AlphaGeometry; curriculum learning, Data Shapley, human-in-the-loop optimization and AI for fusion. **D. Knowledge and systems** (8–9): knowledge graphs, TransE, neural theorem proving, RTX-KG2, AlphaChip, virtual labs and virtual cell. **E. Symposium** (10): project lightning talks.

## Prerequisites

- Machine learning: [CS 229](CS%20229.md) or [CS 230](CS%20230.md).
