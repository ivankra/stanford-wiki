# CS 326: Topics in Advanced Robotic Manipulation

A reading course built to teach research practice as much as manipulation. Every session pairs a foundational paper — Ferrari and Canny's 1992 grasp metric, Santello's 1998 hand synergies — with recent tutorial papers, and every student reviews each one, presents one tutorial, argues Pro in one debate and Con in another, then writes and peer-reviews a research proposal. The 4-unit option adds 30 hours implementing that proposal and drafting it as a conference submission.

## Materials

The whole course is public except the student work: the session table, all 82 paper links, the grading split and a brief for every kind of coursework. The one thing an outside reader can't reach is the tutorial recordings, which are the course's only lecture content.

- **Syllabus**: the [session table](https://web.stanford.edu/class/cs326/syllabus.html) dates all 14 topics and cites every foundational and tutorial paper with a link; the [grading page](https://web.stanford.edu/class/cs326/grading.html) gives the split, reviews 25%, debating 25%, tutorials 20%, proposal 30%. [Autumn 2021's](https://web.archive.org/web/20221226011913/http://web.stanford.edu/class/cs326/syllabus.html) survives in the Internet Archive; Autumn 2020's and Autumn 2023's are Stanford-only.
- **Slides**, **Notes**: none. The lectures are student tutorials and the reading is the papers, so neither exists for anyone.
- **Videos**: each student records their tutorial and the rest of the class watches it and submits a question, for 1% of the grade. Those recordings go only to the class.
- **Assignments**: a page each for the [paper review](https://web.stanford.edu/class/cs326/review.html), the [tutorial presentation](https://web.stanford.edu/class/cs326/presentation.html), the [debate](https://web.stanford.edu/class/cs326/debate.html) and the [research proposal](https://web.stanford.edu/class/cs326/research.html), with what each must contain and how it is marked.
- **Solutions**, **Exams**: none. Reviews, debates and proposals have no answer key, and there is no exam.
- **Projects**: proposals are written, presented and peer-reviewed in class; none is published.
- **Repo**: none. The 4-unit option adds about 30 hours implementing the proposal, but no code is published.
- The readings themselves are mostly on publisher sites, and the syllabus warns: "To access readings while off campus, connect to the Stanford network via VPN."

## Syllabus

[14 topics](https://web.stanford.edu/class/cs326/syllabus.html) over the quarter, classical first.

1. Grasping (topics 1-4): contact-based grasp quality metrics, grasp synergies and hand taxonomies, exploiting contact constraints, learning to grasp.
2. Motion and policy (5-8): trajectory optimization for motion planning, reinforcement learning for manipulation, imitation learning, sensorimotor adaptation.
3. Perception (9-12): high-level reasoning and planning, visual tracking and 6D pose estimation, interactive and active perception, tactile sensing.
4. Scaling up (13-14): representation learning, robotic system building.

A guest lecture on LLMs for peer review sits in week 2, matching the course's focus on reviewing.

## Prerequisites

Nothing is required. The catalog and site recommend at least one of robotics, computer vision, machine learning, control, filtering and recursive estimation, or optimization — named as [CS 131](CS%20131.md), [CS 223A](CS%20223A.md), [CS 229](CS%20229.md) or equivalents.
