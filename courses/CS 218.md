# CS 218: Information Integrity

A CS course that spends most of its time before the Internet. Its argument is that every information technology from writing onward was exploited by "creators and destroyers of reliable information" in the same pattern, and that a reader who knows how societies adapted each time is better placed to manage today's systems. Lectures and assignments work through the harms of each technology, "real and imagined".

## Syllabus

No public syllabus exists; the catalog entry is the only source for content. It frames three questions — how we decide what information to trust, how AI and online media support or degrade those decisions, and what society needs beyond technology — then surveys, in order, the invention of writing, the book, the printing press, economical newsprint, the telegraph, broadcast media and the Digital Age, with assignments on each era's harms and adaptations.
