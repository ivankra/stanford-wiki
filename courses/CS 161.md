# CS 161: Design and Analysis of Algorithms

Taught in two versions. Autumn through Spring share one lineage of decks, notes and Python notebooks, from Karatsuba to max-flow. Summer is Matthew Sotoudeh's own eight-week course: handouts instead of slides, heavier on sorting and search trees than on graphs, with Tarjan's SCC in place of max-flow.

## Materials

Every core type is public for Summer 2026 and on the Autumn 2025 and Winter 2026 sites; only recordings are gated, and the canonical URL links none of it.

- **Syllabus**: the [Summer 2026 course pack](https://masot.net/content/classes/161_summer_26.pdf) opens with the syllabus, grading (two midterms 20% each, final 30%, weekly quizzes and one-on-one homework interviews 15% each) and a dated calendar with readings.
- **Slides**: [Winter 2026](https://stanford-cs161.github.io/winter2026/lectures/) posts eighteen lectures as PDF and `.pptx`; [Autumn 2025](https://cs161-stanford.github.io/lectures/) seventeen.
- **Notes**: the Summer pack's twenty lecture handouts and eleven write-ups (quicksort recurrence, AVL balance…); Winter 2026 writes up every lecture.
- **Videos**: Canvas only; older sites' Panopto links hit Stanford login, and no public upload exists.
- **Assignments**: each Summer handout ends in numbered practice problems. Autumn 2025 posts [HW1–HW7](https://cs161-stanford.github.io/homework/) and Winter 2026 [eight](https://stanford-cs161.github.io/winter2026/homework/).
- **Solutions**: Autumn 2025 publishes solutions for all [section worksheets](https://cs161-stanford.github.io/sections/) and practice exams; its homework-solution links are dead.

- **Code**: Sotoudeh's [C implementations](https://lair.masot.net/git/161-code.git/tree/) of each lectured algorithm; the other quarters' Python notebooks are in [winter2025-extra](https://github.com/stanford-cs161/winter2025-extra).
- **Textbooks**: Summer 2026's main book is Sedgewick and Wayne's *Algorithms*, which the calendar cites lecture by lecture alongside CLRS, Knuth and Erickson. Autumn 2025's primary text is Roughgarden's *Algorithms Illuminated*, Volumes I–III, with CLRS as the supplement.
- **Extras**: Autumn 2025's eighteen [concept checks](https://cs161-stanford.github.io/resources/).

## Syllabus

The [Summer 2026 calendar](https://masot.net/content/classes/161_summer_26.pdf) runs in four modules:

- **Sorting**: selection and insertion sort, Big-O and average case, merge sort and recurrences, quicksort and quickselect, heapsort, the comparison lower bound; Midterm 1.
- **Searching**: BSTs and rotations, AVL trees, 2–3/red–black/B-trees, amortization and the potential method, splay trees, hash tables; Midterm 2.
- **Algorithm design**: DFS and topological sort, dynamic programming, greedy algorithms, shortest paths (Bellman-Ford before Dijkstra, then Floyd-Warshall), MSTs with union-find, Tarjan's SCC.
- **Last week**: loop invariants and correctness proofs, open puzzles.

[Autumn 2025](https://cs161-stanford.github.io/schedule/) adds Karatsuba, linear-time selection, bucket sort, LCS and knapsack, and Ford-Fulkerson, and skips AVL, B- and splay trees.

## Prerequisites

- Probability: [CS 109](CS%20109.md) or STATS 116, to the point of "manipulating probabilities and expected values".
