# CS 161: Design and Analysis of Algorithms

Taught in 2 versions. Autumn through Spring share one lineage of decks, notes and Python notebooks, from Karatsuba to max-flow. Summer is Matthew Sotoudeh's own 8-week course: handouts instead of slides, heavier on sorting and search trees than on graphs, with Tarjan's SCC in place of max-flow.

## Materials

Summer 2026's course pack and the older offerings' sites put the syllabus, notes, decks and problem sets in public; every offering checked keeps its recordings on Canvas or Panopto, and most keep homework solutions there too.

- **Syllabus**: the [Summer 2026 course pack](https://masot.net/content/classes/161_summer_26.pdf) opens with the syllabus, grading (2 midterms at 20% each, a 30% final, weekly quizzes and one-on-one homework interviews at 15% each) and a dated calendar with readings. Schedules survive for every offering since Winter 2020 except Rubinstein's Autumn quarters and Spring 2026, and Summer 2023.
- **Slides**: Summer 2026 lectures at the whiteboard from handouts. [Winter 2026](https://stanford-cs161.github.io/winter2026/lectures/) posts 18 decks as PDF and `.pptx`, [Autumn 2025](https://cs161-stanford.github.io/lectures/) 17 as PDF, and Winter 2021–2024 a `.pptx` per lecture; Summer 2020 and 2021 shared [Google Slides](https://web.stanford.edu/class/archive/cs/cs161/cs161.1218/lectures.html).
- **Notes**: the Summer pack's 20 lecture handouts and 11 write-ups (quicksort recurrence, AVL balance…); Winter 2021–2026 post a notes PDF for every lecture, and the [Summer 2024 pack](https://masot.net/content/classes/161_summer_24.pdf) is the previous format, full notes.
- **Videos**: Canvas only in Summer 2026, per the pack's recording notice; the Winter 2021–2026 sites' Panopto links end at a sign-in. No public upload turned up.
- **Assignments**: each Summer handout ends in numbered practice problems. Autumn 2025 posts [HW1–HW7](https://cs161-stanford.github.io/homework/) and Winter 2026 [8 problem sets](https://stanford-cs161.github.io/winter2026/homework/), both with code zips.
- **Solutions**: Autumn 2025 publishes solutions for all 13 [section worksheets](https://cs161-stanford.github.io/sections/) and its practice exams, Winter 2026 for 9 sections; homework solutions go to Canvas or Ed, and Autumn 2025's 7 links are dead.
- **Exams**: Autumn 2025's [exams page](https://cs161-stanford.github.io/exams/) holds 2 practice midterms, the "Official" practice final and 3 sample finals from other quarters, all with solutions. Summer 2026's pack has a final-exam study guide.
- **Projects**: none. Sotoudeh says the class has "no actual programming component".
- **Repo**: Sotoudeh's [C implementations](https://lair.masot.net/git/161-code.git/tree/) of each lectured algorithm; the Autumn–Winter Python notebooks are in [winter2025-extra](https://github.com/stanford-cs161/winter2025-extra).
- **Textbooks**: Summer 2026's main book is Sedgewick and Wayne's *Algorithms*, cited lecture by lecture alongside CLRS, Knuth and Erickson. Autumn 2025's is Roughgarden's *Algorithms Illuminated*, Volumes I–III, with CLRS as the supplement.
- **Extras**: Autumn 2025's 18 [concept checks](https://cs161-stanford.github.io/resources/).

## Syllabus

The [Summer 2026 calendar](https://masot.net/content/classes/161_summer_26.pdf) runs in 4 modules:

- **Sorting**: selection and insertion sort, Big-O and average case, merge sort and recurrences, quicksort and quickselect, heapsort, the comparison lower bound; Midterm 1.
- **Searching**: BSTs and rotations, AVL trees, 2–3/red–black/B-trees, amortization and the potential method, splay trees, hash tables; Midterm 2.
- **Algorithm design**: DFS and topological sort, dynamic programming, greedy algorithms, shortest paths (Bellman-Ford before Dijkstra, then Floyd-Warshall), MSTs with union-find, Tarjan's SCC.
- **Last week**: loop invariants and correctness proofs, open puzzles.

[Autumn 2025](https://cs161-stanford.github.io/schedule/) adds Karatsuba, linear-time selection, bucket sort, LCS and knapsack, and Ford-Fulkerson, and skips AVL, B- and splay trees. [Winter 2021–2024](https://stanford-cs161.github.io/winter2024/schedule/) run the same 18 lectures with stable matching added; [Summer 2022](https://stanford-cs161.github.io/summer2022/schedule/) was a 14-lecture version with Bloom filters and bipartite matching.

## Prerequisites

- Programming and data structures: [CS 106B](CS%20106B.md) or CS 106X.
- Proofwriting and discrete mathematics: [CS 103](CS%20103.md) or CS 103B — "be able to write proofs about numbers that follow the 'proofwriting checklist'".
- Probability: [CS 109](CS%20109.md) or STATS 116, to the point of "manipulating probabilities and expected values".
