# CS 193C: Client-Side Internet Technologies

A summer-only course, and the oldest of Stanford's web-programming numbers still running: the browser stack on its own, without a server half. The catalog's reference points are Google Maps and Gmail — single-page applications built out of the DOM and Ajax.

## Materials

The site is one screen that says where the course really lives: "We will be using Stanford's Canvas Learning Management System for our website. The web.stanford.edu website you are currently viewing will not be updated regularly." The one thing it links is a course-information handout, and that handout is Summer 2020's.

- **Syllabus**: closed. Canvas is the course site, and the syllabus repository lists this term's upload as Stanford-only. The [course-information handout](https://web.stanford.edu/class/cs193c/h00CourseInfo.pdf) the page still offers as "the syllabus" heads itself Summer 2020 and carries the scope, the prerequisite, the 30/10/60 grading split across midterm, final quiz and 5 assignments, the late policy and the exam format — but no schedule for any term.
- **Slides**, **Assignments**, **Exams**: closed, on Canvas. The handout describes 5 assignments, a 24-hour midterm and a final quiz; none of them is published.
- **Notes**, **Videos**, **Solutions**, **Repo**: not found. The handout mentions that SCPD posted two videos a week in Summer 2020, but to enrolled students.
- **Projects**: none; the course sets assignments and exams only.
- **Older offerings**: the Internet Archive holds the **Summer 2015** offering whole — 4 assignments as PDFs with starter zips, 8 numbered handouts (CSS layout, events, dynamic content, Ajax, AngularJS, submission, honor code), a midterm with a prologue and a starter zip, and the lectures and resources pages. Eleven years old and from before the wiki's window, so nothing rests on it; nothing between it and the Summer 2020 handout is public.

## Syllabus

No published syllabus. The catalog lists the content: "Client-side technologies used to create web sites such as Google maps or Gmail. Includes HTML5, CSS, JavaScript, the Document Object Model (DOM), and Ajax."

The site records the format: two two-hour lectures a week, Tuesday and Thursday, in Gates B1.

## Prerequisites

- Programming at the level of [CS 106A](CS%20106A.md), and nothing further.
