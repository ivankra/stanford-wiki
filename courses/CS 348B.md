# CS 348B: Computer Graphics: Image Synthesis Techniques

The rendering course, taught by Fatahalian and James with Matt Pharr — co-author of *Physically Based Rendering*, whose pbrt system the assignments build on. It works from the physics up: radiometry and the light field, Monte Carlo integration, then the rendering equation and path tracing, bidirectional transport, participating media, and finally what real-time ray tracing and denoising change about all of it. Three implementation assignments and a final project.

## Syllabus

[Twenty lectures](https://gfxcourses.stanford.edu/cs348b/spring22) over the quarter.

1. Foundations (Mar 29-Apr 14): the goals of rendering, ray tracing and acceleration structures, radiometry and the light field, Monte Carlo integration, cameras and lenses.
2. Surfaces (Apr 19-28): signal processing and sampling, BRDFs and the reflection equation, then advanced surface models.
3. Transport (May 5-12): global illumination and path tracing — the rendering equation, Russian roulette, path guiding — then bidirectional path tracing and photon mapping, then participating media.
4. Frontier (May 19-31): anisotropic surfaces and subsurface scattering, real-time ray tracing and denoising.

## Prerequisites

Not formally required, but the site is explicit: working knowledge of graphics at the level of [CS 248](CS%20248.md) — "the basic 3D display pipeline, viewing and modeling transformations, simple geometric modeling using polygons and quadrics, and hidden surface algorithms like the z-buffer algorithm" — plus integral calculus.
