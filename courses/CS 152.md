# CS 152: Trust and Safety

Taught jointly by a practitioner and a communication professor, the course pairs each category of online harm with the operational and legal machinery platforms use against it — and asks teams to build a moderation classifier of their own. It fulfils the Technology in Society requirement.

## Materials

Nothing is held back by policy except the quizzes: the schedule, the reading syllabus, the project brief, all 3 milestone specifications and 10 of the 19 lecture decks are on the course's own site, and the lectures are not recorded for anyone, the room having no equipment for it. Five earlier offerings also left their Canvas syllabus readable.

- **Syllabus**: the [dated schedule](https://cs152.stanford.edu/schedule/) of 19 lectures, each row anchored to its readings, and the [policies page](https://cs152.stanford.edu/policies/) with grading (attendance 10%, pre-read quizzes 20%, the 3 milestones 70%), the 2-absence allowance and the recording and AI policy. The Canvas syllabi for [Winter 2021](https://canvas.stanford.edu/courses/130238/assignments/syllabus) through [Spring 2025](https://canvas.stanford.edu/courses/205814/assignments/syllabus) are public too, each with its own dated schedule; only Winter 2020's is Stanford-only.
- **Slides**: [10 decks](https://cs152.stanford.edu/slides/L01_introduction.pdf) for the 19 lectures, the gaps falling on guest and project sessions, plus the hate-speech precision/recall exercise handout.
- **Notes**: the [reading syllabus](https://cs152.stanford.edu/syllabus/), required and optional citations lecture by lecture, linked at their publishers, from the *Journal of Online Trust and Safety* to the Trust & Safety Professionals Association's abuse types.
- **Videos**: none, by policy. "This classroom does not have video recording equipment", and "full audio/video recording is prohibited", with AI notetakers allowed in their place. Spring 2023 and Spring 2024 did record, for Canvas only; Spring 2025 stopped.
- **Assignments**: the [project brief](https://cs152.stanford.edu/project/) and all 3 milestones, [1](https://cs152.stanford.edu/milestone1/) an abuse study and content policy, [2](https://cs152.stanford.edu/milestone2/) a moderation bot, [3](https://cs152.stanford.edu/milestone3/) the final system, now with a CI/CD pipeline, a PRD in GitHub and code review. The pre-read and attendance quizzes are on Gradescope and Canvas.
- **Solutions**: none; the reading quizzes are open-notes with questions "drawn directly from the materials", and no key is published.
- **Exams**: none; the quarter ends in a poster session, not a paper.
- **Projects**: one previous year's [final poster](https://cs152.stanford.edu/assets/example-poster.pdf) is linked as the example, and the brief lists past topics (cryptocurrency scams, AI nudifiers, sextortion, terrorist recruitment). ## Syllabus

From the [schedule](https://cs152.stanford.edu/schedule/), lectures alternate between harm categories and the institutions that respond to them:

1. **Foundations**: introduction to trust and safety; how tech companies work — designing for trust, safety and privacy; AI safety.
2. **Speech harms**: hate speech, incitement and harassment, with an in-class precision/recall exercise on classifier tradeoffs.
3. **Law and governance**: the US legal system, privacy, surveillance and law enforcement (Riana Pfefferkorn); free speech on the internet (Evelyn Douek).
4. **Severe harms**: online child sexual exploitation; suicide and self-harm; terrorism and violent extremism.
5. **Economic harms**: fraud, scams and pig-butchering, with a guest lecture from Meta's head of cybersecurity policy.
6. **Project**: group setup in week 4, milestone 1 due 17 April, ending in the poster session and an alumni panel.

The course's stated goal includes being able to "design and implement a content moderation system powered by a machine learning classifier".

## Prerequisites

- For CS credit, programming at the level of [CS 106B](CS%20106B.md) or equivalent. Students taking it as COMM 122 or INTLPOL 267 have no programming prerequisite.

The catalog carries a content note: "This class will cover real-world harmful behavior and expose students to potentially upsetting material."
