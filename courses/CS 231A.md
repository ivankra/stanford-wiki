# CS 231A: Computer Vision: From 3D Perception to 3D Reconstruction and Beyond

The geometric counterpart to [CS 231N](CS%20231N.md): projection, calibration and multi-view reconstruction first, learned representations second, ending on radiance fields and splatting. Five problem sets, a midterm worth 20%, and a team project taken to a poster session.

## Materials

An unusually complete public set — decks, notes, problem sets with code — behind no login.

- **Syllabus**: the [schedule](https://web.stanford.edu/class/cs231a/syllabus.html) gives each lecture with readings into Forsyth & Ponce and Hartley & Zisserman, plus every deadline; the [grading page](https://web.stanford.edu/class/cs231a/grading.html) has the breakdown and collaboration policy.
- **Slides**: lecture decks (e.g. [camera models](https://web.stanford.edu/class/cs231a/lectures_2025/lecture2_camera_models.pdf), [Gaussian splatting](https://web.stanford.edu/class/cs231a/lectures_2025/Lecture17_GaussianSplatting.pdf)) and section decks.
- **Notes**: ten [course notes](https://web.stanford.edu/class/cs231a/course_notes.html) written by the staff, from camera models to optimal estimation — the geometry half in textbook form.
- **Assignments**: problem sets 0–4 as [handout](https://web.stanford.edu/class/cs231a/hw_2025_spring/ps1.pdf), [starter code](https://web.stanford.edu/class/cs231a/hw_2025_spring/ps1_code.zip) and LaTeX template; the [project page](https://web.stanford.edu/class/cs231a/project.html) covers proposal, milestone, poster and report.
- **Videos**: only the 7 April lecture was recorded, to Canvas.

## Syllabus

Eighteen lectures ([schedule](https://web.stanford.edu/class/cs231a/syllabus.html)): introduction; camera models; camera calibration; single view metrology; epipolar geometry and the fundamental matrix; stereo systems; active and volumetric stereo; fitting and matching; low-level representations and representation learning; neural network background; optical and scene flow; optimal estimation over two lectures; neural radiance fields; Gaussian splatting; and a guest lecture. Problem sets track the geometry sequence, with the project running proposal (week 4) → milestone (week 7) → poster and report in finals week.

## Prerequisites

- Linear algebra and calculus (MATH 51 or equivalent), basic probability ([CS 109](CS%20109.md) or similar).
- Prior vision or ML exposure: [CS 131](CS%20131.md), [CS 221](CS%20221.md) or [CS 229](CS%20229.md).
- Python with numpy; some project libraries are C++.

## Related

- [CS 231N](CS%20231N.md): deep learning for vision; this course keeps the 3D geometry that 231N skips, and the sites invite a combined project.
- [CS 227A](CS%20227A.md): robot perception, applying much of this geometry on hardware.
- [CS 348K](CS%20348K.md): the systems side of visual computing.
