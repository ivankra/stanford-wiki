# CS 231A: Computer Vision: From 3D Perception to 3D Reconstruction and Beyond

The geometric counterpart to [CS 231N](CS%20231N.md): projection, calibration and multi-view reconstruction first, learned representations second, ending on radiance fields and splatting. 5 problem sets, a midterm worth 20%, and a team project taken to a poster session.

## Materials

An unusually complete public set: decks, staff-written course notes, all 5 problem sets with starter code, and a past midterm with its solution, none of it behind a login. The course does not record its lectures, bar 2 sessions that replaced class and went to Canvas.

- **Syllabus**: the [schedule](https://web.stanford.edu/class/cs231a/syllabus.html) gives each lecture its readings into Forsyth and Ponce and Hartley and Zisserman, plus every deadline; the [grading page](https://web.stanford.edu/class/cs231a/grading.html) has the 11-line breakdown (problem sets 29%, midterm 20%, project 46%, participation 5%) and the collaboration policy. Every offering back to [Winter 2021](https://web.archive.org/web/20210415033358/http://web.stanford.edu/class/cs231a/syllabus.html) has its own.
- **Slides**: 17 lecture decks, from [camera models](https://web.stanford.edu/class/cs231a/lectures_2025/lecture2_camera_models.pdf) to [Gaussian splatting](https://web.stanford.edu/class/cs231a/lectures_2025/Lecture17_GaussianSplatting.pdf), plus the section decks. The archived offerings keep their own sets, 22 for [Winter 2022](https://web.stanford.edu/class/archive/cs/cs231a/cs231a.1214/syllabus.html) and 19 plus 9 section decks for [Spring 2024](https://web.stanford.edu/class/archive/cs/cs231a/cs231a.1246/syllabus.html).
- **Notes**: 10 [course notes](https://web.stanford.edu/class/cs231a/course_notes.html) written by the staff, camera models through optimal estimation: the geometry half in textbook form.
- **Videos**: only 2 sessions were recorded, both marked "Recorded on Canvas, no in-person class", and both are Canvas-only. Nothing else is filmed, this offering or any earlier one.
- **Assignments**: problem sets 0 to 4, each a [handout](https://web.stanford.edu/class/cs231a/hw_2025_spring/ps1.pdf) with [starter code](https://web.stanford.edu/class/cs231a/hw_2025_spring/ps1_code.zip) and a LaTeX template, all 10 files downloading. The [project page](https://web.stanford.edu/class/cs231a/project.html) covers proposal, milestone, poster and report. Each archived offering keeps its own 5.
- **Solutions**: only for the exam. No problem-set solution is published.
- **Exams**: the syllabus links [Winter 2023's midterm](https://web.stanford.edu/class/cs231a/231aMidterm2023.zip) as practice, questions and solution together; [Winter 2023's own syllabus](https://web.stanford.edu/class/archive/cs/cs231a/cs231a.1234/231aMidterm2022.zip) did the same with Winter 2022's, in 3 parts and without a solution. A [2016 midterm](https://web.stanford.edu/class/cs231a/exams_old2/midterm-2016-wsol.pdf) with worked solutions is still served too, far older than anything else here.
- **Projects**: the project is 46% of the grade and ends in a poster session, but no student report or poster is published.
- **Repo**: the starter-code zips.
- **Textbook**: Forsyth and Ponce's *Computer Vision: A Modern Approach* and Hartley and Zisserman's *Multiple View Geometry*, cited chapter by chapter on the schedule.

## Syllabus

18 lectures ([schedule](https://web.stanford.edu/class/cs231a/syllabus.html)): introduction; camera models; camera calibration; single view metrology; epipolar geometry and the fundamental matrix; stereo systems; active and volumetric stereo; fitting and matching; low-level representations and representation learning; neural network background; optical and scene flow; optimal estimation over 2 lectures; neural radiance fields; Gaussian splatting; and a guest lecture. Problem sets track the geometry sequence, with the project running proposal (week 4) → milestone (week 7) → poster and report in finals week.

## Prerequisites

- Linear algebra and calculus (MATH 51 or equivalent), basic probability ([CS 109](CS%20109.md) or similar).
- Prior vision or ML exposure: [CS 131](CS%20131.md), [CS 221](CS%20221.md) or [CS 229](CS%20229.md).
- Python with numpy; some project libraries are C++.

## Related

- [CS 131](CS%20131.md): the undergraduate vision course, which borrows this course's notes for its geometry unit.
- [CS 227A](CS%20227A.md): robot perception, applying much of this geometry on hardware.
- [CS 231N](CS%20231N.md): deep learning for vision; this course keeps the 3D geometry that 231N skips, and the sites invite a combined project.
- [CS 348K](CS%20348K.md): the systems side of visual computing.
- [CS 468](CS%20468.md): a rotating geometry seminar; one edition asked how much 3D understanding foundation models have.
