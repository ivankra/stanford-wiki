# CS 225A: Experimental Robotics

Programming articulated robots, hands on. The first half of the quarter reviews manipulator control theory over four homeworks; then teams of three or four build and demonstrate a motor task on one of the lab's arms. Projects usually mix control, computer vision and some mechanical work; the choice of task is the team's.

## Materials

The schedule and grading are public and the starter code is a public repo, but lectures publish no handouts and the homework handouts are on Canvas.

- **Syllabus**: the [course site](https://cs.stanford.edu/group/manips/teaching/cs225a/index.html#schedule) carries the dated Spring 2026 timeline and the grading split (homework 40%, project progression and demo 50%, project report 10%).
- **Slides**: the schedule's Handouts column is empty on every row; no deck found.
- **Videos**: none. Lectures are in person and not recorded.
- **Assignments**: the [homework folder](https://github.com/manips-sai-org/cs225a/tree/main/homework) holds the C++ skeleton, simulation program and world model for hw0–hw3; the written handouts go to Canvas and submissions to Gradescope.
- **Exams**: none; grading is homework plus the project.
- **Projects**: the [previous projects](https://cs225a.stanford.edu/previous-projects) page links final reports and slides for the Spring 2021 and Autumn 2020 teams (Roborigger, Volleybot, Tennis Tweeners and others), one with a demo video.
- **Code**: [manips-sai-org/cs225a](https://github.com/manips-sai-org/cs225a) also holds project templates for the fixed and mobile Panda, an OptiTrack client and URDF models for the Panda and Flexiv arms. It builds on [OpenSai](https://github.com/manips-sai-org), the lab's simulation and control stack, public in the same organization.

## Syllabus

Per the [timeline](https://cs.stanford.edu/group/manips/teaching/cs225a/index.html#schedule), content lectures run through April and the rest of the quarter is project work.

1. Introduction and course review; simulation and graphics; a software setup section (hw0).
2. Operational space dynamics, then operational space control (hw1).
3. Compliant motion control; task redundancy control (hw2). Teams and project ideas due.
4. A Stanford Robotics Center demo; project proposal presentations (hw3).
5. An OpenSai control tutorial, then project discussion.
6. Outline presentations, progress updates and support meetings, ending in final demos at the Stanford Robotics Center with reports and videos.

## Prerequisites

- Robot kinematics, dynamics and control, or equivalent background: [CS 223A](CS%20223A.md), Introduction to Robotics.
- Programming: the homework and projects are C++ against the OpenSai libraries, with Redis for inter-process state.
- Teams are expected to bring some mechanical expertise alongside programming.
