# CS 377E: Designing Solutions to Global Grand Challenges

A studio built around one theme a year. Spring 2023's was foundation models for educational equity, and teams ran the full design-thinking arc — preliminary research, needfinding, journey maps, lo-fi prototypes, rapid experimentation — to a public project expo in June with awards for best design and best use of foundation models. No exams; 60% of the grade is the group project.

## Materials

The calendar, every deck and all 11 teams' weekly deliverables are public, in both offerings. The gap is the briefs: of the 13 the Spring 2023 calendar links, 8 are Google files that answer 401, and in Autumn 2020 all 11 do.

- **Syllabus**: the [calendar](https://hci.stanford.edu/courses/cs377e/2023/sp/calendar.html) gives each studio its material and the assignment set and due that day, and [logistics](https://hci.stanford.edu/courses/cs377e/2023/sp/logistics.html) gives the grading split (group project 60%, individual work 30%, participation 10%) and the class values statement. [Autumn 2020's](https://hci.stanford.edu/courses/cs377e/2020/au/calendar.html) site is still served under its own term path.
- **Slides**: 7 decks in this offering's [lectures directory](https://hci.stanford.edu/courses/cs377e/2023/sp/lectures/02-needfinding.pdf), needfinding and journey maps through both Equitable AI parts, lo-fi prototyping, interaction design and the project fair. Autumn 2020 posted 8, adding define-and-ideate, med-fi prototyping and heuristic evaluation.
- **Notes**: 2 resources documents the calendar links, on the design-thinking method and the studio's tools, both open.
- **Videos**: the calendar links a [17-minute journey-map lecture](https://hci.stanford.edu/courses/cs377e/2017/sp/lectures/03-journey-map-lecture-video.mp4) for that session, a file kept under the 2017 path and reused in both offerings, and this offering also posts a [recording of the awards ceremony](https://hci.stanford.edu/courses/cs377e/2023/sp/lectures/awards-20230607.mp4). The studios themselves are not recorded.
- **Assignments**: 5 of the 13 briefs open (preliminary research, background research and needfinding, the poster, the final prototype and presentation, the reflection); the other 8 answer 401, among them needfinding and empathy work, the lo-fi prototype, all 3 medium-fi prototypes and both heuristic evaluations.
- **Solutions**, **Repo**: none. The work is open-ended design, prototyped in Figma rather than code.
- **Exams**: none, explicitly: "No quizzes or exams".
- **Projects**: [class/cs377e/projects/](https://web.stanford.edu/class/cs377e/projects/) is an open listing of 11 team sites, 7 from this offering (BuddyBuilder, ChatLibs, MelodyMeadow, PupUp, TechWise, TuitionTally, nurturEd) and 4 from Autumn 2020 (ask.in, wello, Lecturely, eduCulture). Each carries the team's weekly deliverables as PDFs, from the literature review and journey map to the final presentation, so the student side of every assignment is readable even where the brief is not.

## Syllabus

[Studios run twice weekly](https://hci.stanford.edu/courses/cs377e/2023/sp/calendar.html), A and B.

1. Week 1: course introduction and team formation, then opportunity-area pitches; preliminary research assigned.
2. Week 2: equitable AI, background-research share-outs, needfinding and empathy work, with two AIGA readings on giving and receiving design critique.
3. Middle weeks: journey maps, lo-fi prototyping, rapid experimentation, interaction design, and an individual heuristic evaluation.
4. Week 10: the project expo, June 7, with awards.
