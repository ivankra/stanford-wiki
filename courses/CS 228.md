# CS 228: Probabilistic Graphical Models: Principles and Techniques

The course behind the Koller and Friedman book, taught in its own order: represent a domain as a Bayesian or Markov network, then answer queries about it exactly and approximately, then learn the parameters and finally the graph itself. 5 homeworks pairing proofs with code carry 70% of the grade and a final exam the rest, with no project. Its best-known artefact is the open course-notes site, which students are invited to improve by pull request for extra credit.

## Materials

The schedule and a complete written treatment of the subject are public, and the coursework is not: the 5 homeworks go out on Ed, the final paper is never posted, and the lecture videos sit in Canvas.

- **Syllabus**: the [course site](https://ermongroup.github.io/cs228/) gives 10 weeks of topics with PGM chapter numbers, each homework's open and due dates, the grading split (70% homework, 30% final, up to 3% extra credit), a detailed late-day policy, and the topics of the 5 optional Friday TA sessions: d-separation, variable elimination, junction tree, Gibbs sampling, EM. The Internet Archive keeps the same page for [Winter 2021](https://web.archive.org/web/20211009072154/https://ermongroup.github.io/cs228/), [Winter 2022](https://web.archive.org/web/20220418033800/https://ermongroup.github.io/cs228/) and [Winter 2023](https://web.archive.org/web/20230130155934/https://ermongroup.github.io/cs228/); Winter 2020 has no capture.
- **Slides**: none posted, by any offering. The site thanks 4 colleagues "for sharing material used in slides and homeworks", so decks exist, but the schedule links none.
- **Notes**: [cs228-notes](https://ermongroup.github.io/cs228-notes/) is the instruction, and a full written treatment in its own right: preliminaries, representation, inference, learning and an extra chapter on variational autoencoders, with [source on GitHub](https://github.com/ermongroup/cs228-notes). It is maintained across offerings rather than tied to one, and students earn extra credit for improving it by pull request.
- **Videos**: the site's "Lecture Videos" link is a Canvas external tool.
- **Assignments**: the 5 homeworks, written and programming, go out on Ed and come back on Gradescope. Only the [LaTeX template](https://cs.stanford.edu/~ermon/cs228/hwtemplate.tex) is public.
- **Solutions**: never published. The collaboration policy goes further than most: referring to a previous year's set, "either official or written up by another student", is stated to be an honor code violation.
- **Exams**: one final, 21 March, 15:30 to 18:30. No paper from any offering is posted.
- **Projects**: none; the grade is homework and the final.
- **Repo**: the programming parts ship with the homeworks on Ed.
- **Textbook**: Koller and Friedman's *Probabilistic Graphical Models*, which the syllabus tracks chapter by chapter. The further reading is mostly free online: Wainwright and Jordan on variational inference, MacKay, Barber, Bishop.

## Syllabus

[10 weeks](https://ermongroup.github.io/cs228/), tracking the textbook.

1. Representation (weeks 1-2): probability theory, Bayesian networks, undirected models — PGM ch. 1-4.
2. Learning structure first (week 3): learning Bayes nets, ch. 16-17.
3. Inference (weeks 4-6): exact inference and message passing (ch. 9-10), sampling (ch. 12), MAP inference and structured prediction (ch. 13).
4. Learning (weeks 7-8): parameter learning (ch. 19-20), Bayesian and structure learning (ch. 17-18).
5. Theory (weeks 9-10): exponential families and variational inference (ch. 8, 11 and Wainwright-Jordan), then advanced topics.

## Prerequisites

- Probability and statistics, programming, algorithm design and analysis. The site offers a practical test: "If you are able to comfortably complete homework 1 then you likely have all the relevant background knowledge."
