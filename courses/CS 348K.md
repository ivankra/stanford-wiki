# CS 348K: Visual Computing Systems

Efficiency for visual and spatial workloads, argued end to end: the algorithm, the programming framework that expresses it, and the hardware underneath, on the premise that a systems architect should be innovating in all three at once. Almost every session reads a paper and argues about it; the other half of the grade is a term project.

## Materials

Everything the course asks a student to do is public: the schedule, the decks for every lecture the staff give, the reading list with the prompts each paper is discussed against, and the project guidelines. Only Ed and the course's own student login are gated, and every offering back to Spring 2020 keeps its whole site at its own term path.

- **Syllabus**: the [schedule](https://cs348k.stanford.edu/spring26) summarizes each of the 19 sessions in a few lines with its reading, and the [course-info page](https://gfxcourses.stanford.edu/cs348k/spring26/courseinfo) carries the grading split and the instructor's advice on arriving strong in either parallel systems or visual algorithms. [Spring 2020's](https://gfxcourses.stanford.edu/cs348k/spring20/) to [Spring 2025's](https://gfxcourses.stanford.edu/cs348k/spring25) are all still served; Canvas holds a Stanford-only copy for Spring 2021 and Spring 2022.
- **Slides**: a page of slide thumbnails and a [PDF download](https://gfxcourses.stanford.edu/cs348k/spring26content/media/intro/01_intro.pdf) for each non-guest lecture; the 5 guest lectures, from NVIDIA, World Labs and Roblox, post nothing.
- **Notes**: the [readings repository](https://github.com/stanford-cs348k/readings) lists the required papers lecture by lecture, each with the instructor's own discussion prompts. It tracks the current offering rather than keeping a copy per year.
- **Videos**: nothing, and none is promised. The sessions are paper discussions that "all students are required to come to class" for, so a recording is not what this course hands out.
- **Assignments**: the [term project guidelines](https://github.com/stanford-cs348k/project), the only assignment besides the readings; its deadlines go out on Ed.
- **Solutions**, **Exams**: none; the grade is 50% paper reading and in-class discussion, 50% the project.
- **Projects**: presented in class on the last day and not published, in this offering or any earlier one.
- **Repo**: none; students pick their own system to build and the course ships no starter code.

## Syllabus

Nineteen sessions, per the [schedule](https://cs348k.stanford.edu/spring26), moving from image processing to generative 3D.

1. **Framing** (Mar 31): why goals and constraints drive system design.
2. **Camera pipelines** (Apr 2-9): multi-shot alignment and merging, Gaussian and Laplacian pyramids, HDR and local tone mapping, portrait mode and autofocus; the Frankencamera and modern camera APIs; Halide and its scheduling algebra.
3. **Kernels and languages** (Apr 14-21): LLM-written ML kernels and tensor-core DSLs (CuTile, ThunderKittens, Triton); automatic differentiation primitives in Slang; differentiable rendering and procedural modeling.
4. **Controllable generation** (Apr 23-May 5): inserting predictable control into image synthesis, neurosymbolic representations and code as a content representation; neural postprocessing in the DLSS mould; generating 3D objects and scenes.
5. **World models and agents** (May 7-28): action-conditioned video models and latent world representations; agents that give design feedback; building blocks for 3D world generation; interactive worlds at Roblox; batch simulators for training agents; distillation and caching for real-time video models.

Guest lecturers came from NVIDIA, Stanford, World Labs and Roblox.

## Prerequisites

The catalog requires [CS 107](CS%20107.md) or equivalent. The site's own advice is that students arrive strong in either parallel systems or visual algorithms, rarely both, and that either is enough:

- Parallel systems: [CS 149](CS%20149.md) (parallel computing) or a computer architecture course such as [CS 282](CS%20282.md)/EE 282.
- Graphics, vision or image processing: [CS 248A](CS%20248A.md) or [CS 248B](CS%20248B.md) for 3D graphics, [CS 231A](CS%20231A.md) or [CS 231N](CS%20231N.md) for vision, [CS 232](CS%20232.md)/EE 368 for image processing.

Systems and architecture students with no graphics background are explicitly the target audience, provided they pick up the workloads during the term.
