# CS 234: Reinforcement Learning

Emma Brunskill's survey of reinforcement learning, from tabular MDPs to RLHF. What distinguishes it from a [deep RL](CS%20224R.md) course is where the weight falls: 3–4 of the 14 lectures are on exploration and strategic data gathering, and it keeps returning to regret and sample complexity, not just returns.

## Syllabus

14 lectures over 10 weeks, per the [schedule](https://web.stanford.edu/class/cs234/) and the decks on [Lecture Materials](https://web.stanford.edu/class/cs234/modules.html).

1. **Weeks 1–2, planning and tabular RL**: Markov reward processes and MDPs; policy and value iteration, with the contraction proof; Monte Carlo and TD policy evaluation; SARSA and Q-learning; function approximation and DQN. Readings from Sutton and Barto chapters 1, 3–6.
2. **Weeks 3–4, policy search**: REINFORCE, baselines and actor-critic; performance bounds, PPO and generalized advantage estimation; then imitation learning with behavior cloning, DAgger and max-entropy inverse RL.
3. **Week 5, learning from preferences**: Bradley-Terry reward models, RLHF and DPO.
4. **Weeks 6–8, data-efficient RL**: bandit regret, UCB, Thompson sampling and Gittins indices; PAC RL in MDPs with MBIE-EB and posterior sampling, contextual bandits, and meta-learned exploration with the Decision-Pretrained Transformer; then Monte Carlo tree search, AlphaGo and AlphaZero. Lecture 10 also opens value alignment, with sycophancy and agentic AI as cases.
5. **Weeks 9–10**: Shane Gu's guest lecture on world models; value alignment again, from top-down to participatory approaches; the poster session.

## History

Emma Brunskill created the course in Spring 2017 and still teaches it.

- **Spring 2017**: heavy on guest lectures, among them policy gradients, safe RL and cooperative inverse RL, with sample-efficient RL already given 2 lectures. Its decks, 3 assignments with code and 2 solutions are public; its recordings, posted on the course site, are Stanford-only.
- **Winter 2018–2023**: moved to Winter and settled into the arc it keeps: tabular planning, model-free evaluation and control, function approximation and deep Q-learning, imitation learning, policy search, exploration, batch RL and Monte Carlo tree search, with an in-class midterm and quiz. Winter 2019 added a guest lecture on meta-learning; its [15 lectures](https://www.youtube.com/playlist?list=PLoROMvodv4rOSOPzutgyCTapiGlY2Nd8u) are on YouTube, and its site keeps every assignment solution and the 2017 and 2018 midterms. Winter 2022 ran 5 assignments and closed on POMDPs; Winter 2023 added problem sessions with solutions and a lecture on value alignment.

## Prerequisites

- Python: every assignment is in it.
- Multivariable calculus and linear algebra: MATH 51 or CME 100.
- Probability and statistics: [CS 109](CS%20109.md) or equivalent.
- Machine learning foundations: [CS 221](CS%20221.md) or [CS 229](CS%20229.md), since the course writes cost functions and optimizes them by gradient descent; convex optimization helps but isn't required.

## Related

- [CS 224R](CS%20224R.md): Chelsea Finn's applied deep RL for robots and language models, with a week on RLHF and reasoning but no exploration lectures; this course is the one built on regret and PAC bounds, and neither is a prerequisite for the other.
- [CS 238](CS%20238.md): decision making under uncertainty, covering MDPs and POMDPs mostly from the planning side, with RL as one unit.
