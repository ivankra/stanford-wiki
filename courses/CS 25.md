# CS 25: Transformers United V6

A one-unit seminar that is mostly consumed from outside Stanford: the talks are livestreamed on Zoom, posted to a public YouTube playlist, and discussed in a 5,000-member Discord. "Anybody is free to audit in-person or join our Zoom livestreams - you don't have to sign-up or be affiliated with Stanford."

## Materials

The seminar is built to be watched from outside: "Anybody is free to audit in-person or join our Zoom livestreams - you don't have to sign-up or be affiliated with Stanford", the talks go to a public playlist within a week or two, and the schedule, abstracts and most decks are on the site. Attendance is the only coursework, so there is nothing else to withhold.

- **Syllabus**: the [dated talk schedule](https://web.stanford.edu/class/cs25/), 9 Thursday sessions each with an abstract and the speaker's biography, on the same page as the attendance rule and the audit invitation. All 5 earlier offerings keep their own site in the class archive, [Autumn 2021's](https://web.stanford.edu/class/archive/cs/cs25/cs25.1222/) to [Spring 2025's](https://web.stanford.edu/class/archive/cs/cs25/cs25.1256/); Canvas also holds a Stanford-only copy for 3 of them.
- **Slides**: a Google Drive deck for 6 of the 9 talks, the instructors' overview lecture among them; every one downloads without an account.
- **Notes**: the opening lecture lists the 6 arXiv papers it works through, on small-scale and multilingual pretraining, RAG-considerate pretraining, curriculum-guided layer scaling, cross-network attention for fMRI, and a unified definition of hallucination.
- **Videos**: the [public playlist](https://www.youtube.com/playlist?list=PLoROMvodv4rNiJRchCzutFw5ItR_Z27CM) every offering adds to, 50 talks uploaded between 2022 and 2026 across all 6 versions, 9 of them this offering's, arriving "within one-to-two weeks of each lecture".
- **Assignments**, **Solutions**, **Exams**, **Projects**, **Repo**: none. "The only homework for students is weekly attendance to the talks/lectures."
- **Self-study**: the course's [Discord](https://discord.gg/eKu6RNr3aG), over 5,000 members, which the site opens to anyone: "feel free to join and chat with hundreds of others about Transformers".

## Syllabus

Nine Thursday sessions in [Spring 2026](https://web.stanford.edu/class/cs25/):

1. **2 April** — Overview of Transformers, by the instructors: the history of ML and NLP, how Transformers work, recent trends and current challenges.
2. **9 April** — Hazel Nam and Lucas Maes on JEPA-based world models: latent prediction over reconstruction, object-level relational bias, end-to-end training from pixels.
3. **16 April** — Albert Gu (CMU, Cartesia) on the tradeoffs between state space models and Transformers, and tokenizer-free H-Nets.
4. **23 April** — Nouamane Tazi (Hugging Face) on ultra-scale training: 5D parallelism, Mixture-of-Experts stability, communication patterns.
5. **30 April** — Shrimai Prabhumoye (Mistral) on two-phase pretraining, reasoning-rich data and Reinforcement Learning during Pretraining.
6. **7 May** — Andrew Lampinen (Anthropic) on how generalization differs between information in context and in parameters.
7. **14 May** — Vivek Natarajan (DeepMind) on the AI co-scientist and the AMIE co-physician.
8. **21 May** — Victoria Lin (Thinking Machines) on native multimodal intelligence.
9. **28 May** — Charles Frye (Modal) on serving Transformer inference in production.
