# CS 25: Transformers United V6

A one-unit seminar that is mostly consumed from outside Stanford: the talks are livestreamed on Zoom, posted to a public YouTube playlist, and discussed in a 5,000-member Discord. "Anybody is free to audit in-person or join our Zoom livestreams - you don't have to sign-up or be affiliated with Stanford."

## Materials

Everything is public by design — schedule, abstracts, most decks, and the recordings.

- **Syllabus**: the [dated talk schedule](https://web.stanford.edu/class/cs25/), each entry with an abstract and the speaker's biography; a separate logistics page covers the rest.
- **Slides**: a Google Drive deck for most talks, linked from the schedule.
- **Notes**: the opening lecture lists the six arXiv papers it works through, on small-scale and multilingual pretraining, RAG-considerate pretraining, curriculum-guided layer scaling, cross-network attention for fMRI, and a unified definition of hallucination.
- **Videos**: the [public YouTube playlist](https://www.youtube.com/playlist?list=PLoROMvodv4rNiJRchCzutFw5ItR_Z27CM), spanning all six iterations; new talks arrive "within one-to-two weeks of each lecture".
- **Assignments**, **Exams**, **Projects**: none. Attendance is the whole requirement.

## Syllabus

Nine Thursday sessions in [Spring 2026](https://web.stanford.edu/class/cs25/):

1. **2 April** — Overview of Transformers, by the instructors: the history of ML and NLP, how Transformers work, recent trends and current challenges.
2. **9 April** — Hazel Nam and Lucas Maes on JEPA-based world models: latent prediction over reconstruction, object-level relational bias, end-to-end training from pixels.
3. **16 April** — Albert Gu (CMU, Cartesia) on the tradeoffs between state space models and Transformers, and tokenizer-free H-Nets.
4. **23 April** — Nouamane Tazi (Hugging Face) on ultra-scale training: 5D parallelism, Mixture-of-Experts stability, communication patterns.
5. **30 April** — Shrimai Prabhumoye (Mistral) on two-phase pretraining, reasoning-rich data and Reinforcement Learning during Pretraining.
6. **7 May** — Andrew Lampinen (Anthropic) on how generalization differs between information in context and in parameters.
7. **14 May** — Vivek Natarajan (DeepMind) on the AI co-scientist and the AMIE co-physician.
8. **21 May** — Victoria Lin (Thinking Machines) on native multimodal intelligence.
9. **28 May** — Charles Frye (Modal) on serving Transformer inference in production.
