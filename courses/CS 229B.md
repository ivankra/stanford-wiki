# CS 229B: Machine Learning for Sequence Modeling

The course argues that sequence data "are not just 1D images", and proves it by teaching both traditions in one quarter: 4 weeks of classical time series — stationarity, ARMA, ARIMA, Kalman filtering — before RNNs and transformers arrive, so the deep models are met as answers to problems the classical ones already framed. It ends in continuous time, with Hawkes processes, neural temporal point processes and neural ODEs. 4 homeworks, 5 concept quizzes and a project.

## Syllabus

[Four modules](https://stanford-cs229b.github.io/) over the quarter.

1. Traditional time series methods (weeks 1-4): stationarity and autocorrelation, AR and MA processes, ARMA and forecasting, estimation, ARIMA/SARIMA, multivariate processes, then state space models with Kalman filtering and smoothing and HMMs.
2. Deep sequence models: RNNs and gated RNNs, backpropagation through time, CNNs for sequences, transformers.
3. Advanced topics: representation learning for time series, state-of-the-art sequence models.
4. Continuous-time modeling: Poisson and Hawkes processes, neural temporal point processes, neural ODEs and hybrid models.
