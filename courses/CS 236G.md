# CS 236G: Generative Adversarial Networks

A whole course on one model family, taught in the window when GANs were still the state of the art in image generation. It runs the failure modes as carefully as the successes — mode collapse, why binary cross-entropy loss breaks down, what the Wasserstein distance fixes and what enforcing 1-Lipschitz continuity costs — then moves to steering the output: conditional generation, latent vector algebra, disentanglement, and the evaluation metrics that tell you whether any of it worked.

## Syllabus

[Week by week](https://cs236g.stanford.edu/syllabus), each with its own sub-outline.

1. Building your first GAN: generative versus discriminative models, discriminator and generator roles, BCE loss, DCGANs with a PyTorch and convolution review.
2. Where BCE fails: mode collapse, Earth Mover's distance, Wasserstein loss, conditioning the critic, 1-Lipschitz enforcement.
3. Steering output: conditional generation, controllable generation and how the two differ, vector algebra in latent space, classifier gradients, supervised disentanglement.
4. Evaluation: Inception Score, Fréchet Inception Distance.
