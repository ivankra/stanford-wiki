# CS 384: Seminar on Ethical and Social Issues in Natural Language Processing

The structure is the argument: weeks alternate between "Red" — do no harm, covering where training data comes from, who labelled it and for how much, bias, influence, intellectual property, privacy — and "Blue", where NLP is turned on social problems instead. A three-hour Thursday seminar in breakout groups, with two paragraphs due on every reading the night before and everyone leading discussion two or three times. Prerequisites are unusually strict: both [CS 224N](CS%20224N.md) and [CS 224U](CS%20224U.md).

## Materials

## Syllabus

[Six session pairs](https://web.stanford.edu/class/cs384/), each split at 4:10.

1. April 6: introductions, then where the data comes from — the Belmont Report, and either "Anatomy of an AI System" or Time's report on Kenyan workers labelling for OpenAI.
2. The local community, participatory research and decolonization; then a survey of the harms of language models.
3. Bias and stereotype; then influence.
4. Intellectual property; then privacy and libel.
5. Regulation of LLMs and AI generally; then the role of the technical community — "Should we build it? And other actions we can take."
6. AI for the people; dreaming up AI.
