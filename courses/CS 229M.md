# CS 229M: Machine Learning Theory

The theory course that sits behind [CS 229](CS%20229.md): not how to fit a model but how much data it takes to learn one, and what breaks when the model is larger than the dataset. 4 units of classical theory (generalization, optimization, unsupervised learning), then a deep-learning coda on benign overfitting, the neural tangent kernel and diffusion. Proof-based throughout, graded on 4 homeworks and a paper-review project rather than exams.

## Syllabus

[The schedule](https://docs.google.com/spreadsheets/d/1hPm8GVaJzYcJ75zq0jFnCt1VHKSzdDhZH8wswRMbIiE/pubhtml) runs 4 units and a special topic.

1. Intro to supervised learning (9/23-9/25): empirical risk minimization, generalization, asymptotic convergence via the CLT.
2. Generalization theory (9/30-10/9): uniform convergence over finite and infinite hypothesis classes, Rademacher complexity and symmetrization, margin theory, VC dimension.
3. Optimization (10/14-10/30): gradient descent rates for smooth and strongly convex functions, SGD, online convex optimization and follow-the-regularized-leader, Lasso regularization, non-convex landscapes.
4. Unsupervised learning (11/4-11/13): k-means, spectral clustering and Cheeger's inequality, the method of moments for Gaussian mixtures, kernels and kernel PCA.
5. Deep learning (11/18-12/4): implicit regularization and overparameterization, benign overfitting and double descent, the neural tangent kernel, Langevin dynamics and score matching for diffusion.

## Prerequisites

- Calculus: MATH 19, 20 or 21.
- Linear algebra: MATH 51 or [CS 205L](CS%20205L.md)'s subject.
- Probability: STATS 116, [CS 109](CS%20109.md) or MATH 151.
- A first machine learning course: [CS 229](CS%20229.md) or STATS 315A.
