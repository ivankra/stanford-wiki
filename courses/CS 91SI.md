# CS 91SI: Digital Canvas: An Introduction to UI/UX Design

A student-initiated course aimed at people who have never designed anything, and explicit about the atmosphere: students "learn digital design in a low-stress environment". Two units, capped, and the deliverable is a realistic artefact rather than an exercise — "handoff-ready interactive high-fidelity mockups for a realistic product feature".

## Materials

Nothing is public. There is no site under the course code, the class archive has no copy, and every one of the five records the syllabus repository holds for this course is marked readable inside Stanford only.

- **Syllabus**: closed. Spring 2022 and Spring 2023 each uploaded one to Canvas, both Stanford-only; Winter 2025's Canvas course is published with the same restriction and no file.
- **Slides**, **Notes**, **Videos**, **Assignments**, **Solutions**, **Projects**, **Repo**: not found. A course whose deliverable is "handoff-ready interactive high-fidelity mockups" would leave Figma files behind, but none is published, and nothing says whether the sessions are recorded.
- **Exams**: none. The course is project-based and graded satisfactory/no credit.

## Syllabus

No published syllabus. The catalog gives the content: "This course covers what makes a good or bad interface, effective design techniques from the ground up, and how to execute on design principles using the tool Figma", taught "in a project-based format". An older catalog entry adds the artefacts students produce along the way — wireframes, high-fidelity mockups and clickable prototypes — and names Marvel alongside Figma.

## Prerequisites

None required. Enrollment is limited and decided by a short application. The catalog recommends "some prior experience in product design, human-computer interaction, or front-end engineering", and an older entry says the course also suits people with web development or HCI backgrounds who want to sharpen visual design skills.
