# CS 286: Advanced Topics in Computer Vision and Biomedicine

An advanced, discussion-centric course on vision foundation models: each model family gets a general lecture, then one on its use in biomedicine. Coursework is paper-analysis reports, a presentation of recent work, and a project with large vision or vision-language models.

## Materials

The [course site](https://web.stanford.edu/class/bmds276/) is public and carries the [schedule](https://web.stanford.edu/class/bmds276/syllabus.html) and a two-page [syllabus PDF](https://web.stanford.edu/class/bmds276/resources/syllabus.pdf). Autumn 2026 slides are posted per lecture (1–2 so far); assignments are "posted soon", and submissions go through Gradescope.

The [Autumn 2024 site](https://web.stanford.edu/class/bmds276/2024/index.html) has the same lecture sequence with all 13 slide decks on its [schedule](https://web.stanford.edu/class/bmds276/2024/syllabus.html), both [assignments](https://web.stanford.edu/class/bmds276/2024/assignments.html), the discussion-presentation instructions and the [final project guidelines](https://web.stanford.edu/class/bmds276/2024/resources/final_project.pdf).

## Syllabus

Twenty sessions, marked tentative:

1. Introduction; deep learning for image data, from CNNs to transformers
2. Vision representation learners, then in biomedicine
3. Vision-language representation learners, then in biomedicine
4. Vision diffusion and generative models, then in biomedicine
5. Vision-language generative models, then in biomedicine
6. GPUs and compute
7. Student-led discussions of recent advances, one per family above plus computing efficiency
8. Three guest lectures (TBD) and a conclusion

## Prerequisites

Deep learning and computer vision at the level of [CS 231N](CS%20231N.md) or [CS 271](CS%20271.md) (BIODS 220). NLP ([CS 224N](CS%20224N.md)) and biomedical AI (BIODS 260) help but aren't required.
