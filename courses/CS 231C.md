# CS 231C: Computer Vision and Image Analysis of Art

David Stork's course on the part of computer vision that photographs do not prepare you for. The images are paintings, drawings and murals — non-realistic, stylized, sometimes abstract — and the questions are art-historical: whose hand made this mark, what order were the layers applied in, which painter influenced which. Classic problems such as object recognition come back in a setting where the usual physical constraints on an image do not hold.

## Syllabus

From the catalog description; no public syllabus.

1. Rigorous image processing, computer vision, machine learning, computer graphics and AI applied to fine art: paintings, drawings, murals and other two-dimensional works, abstract art included.
2. What makes art images unlike the physics-constrained images of photography, video and medical imaging.
3. Analysis of brushstrokes and marks, and of medium.
4. Inferring artists' working methods and compositional principles.
5. Stylometry — the quantification of style — and the tracing of artistic influence.
6. Art attribution and authentication.
7. Classic vision problems such as image-based object recognition, revisited in highly non-realistic, stylized artworks.

## Prerequisites

- Programming proficiency in at least one of C, C++, Python, Matlab or Mathematica.
- A vision toolkit: OpenCV or Matlab's Image Processing toolbox.
- Recommended: [CS 131](CS%20131.md) or EE 168 (introductory vision or image processing) and ARTHIST 1B.
