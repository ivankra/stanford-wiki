# CS 144: Introduction to Computer Networking

Networking taught through construction: eight lab checkpoints in which students build a working TCP implementation in C++ that talks to the real Internet, with lectures published as written handouts rather than slide decks.

## Materials

Unusually complete for a course in progress: the logistics handout, all 8 lab checkpoints, their starter repository, the lecture handouts and 4 past exams with full answer keys are public. Only the recordings, if any are made, are missing.

- **Syllabus**: the [logistics handout](https://cs144.github.io/logistics.pdf), with grade weights and exam dates on the [landing page](https://cs144.github.io/). The schedule table itself renders client-side and is empty to a plain fetch.
- **Slides**: none; the lectures are handouts and outline documents instead.
- **Notes**: 9 weekly handout PDFs — [reliability](https://cs144.github.io/handouts/week-1-reliability.pdf), [TCP](https://cs144.github.io/handouts/week-2-tcp.pdf), packet switching, 3 on congestion control, [routing](https://cs144.github.io/handouts/routing-intro.pdf) and Winstein's "Bad Attitude Guide" — plus 8 [per-lecture outline documents](https://docs.google.com/document/d/1s6_yZ6zCZqw18q4mZbQXgXmTkufTDDgNYUhIgY5Kwu8/view) on Google Docs, covering home networking, best-effort delivery, congestion control and security; every one of them exports without an account.
- **Videos**: the site and the logistics handout never mention recordings. The public [CS 144 MOOC playlist](https://www.youtube.com/playlist?list=PLEAYkSg4uSQ2dr0XO_Nwa5OcdEcaaELSG) is Levis and McKeown's from 2012, a different course from this one.
- **Assignments**: [checkpoint 0](https://cs144.github.io/assignments/check0.pdf) through checkpoint 7, plus a [lab FAQ](https://cs144.github.io/lab_faq.html).
- **Solutions**: answer keys for all 4 past papers; lab solutions are deliberately unpublished, and the starter repository asks readers not to post any.
- **Exams**: [Autumn 2021](https://cs144.github.io/exams/21fa-midterm.pdf) and [Spring 2023](https://cs144.github.io/exams/sp23_midterm.pdf) midterms and finals.
- **Projects**: none; the 8 checkpoints are the coursework.
- **Repo**: [CS144/imp](https://github.com/CS144/imp), the lab starter, public and updated this quarter though the site links it nowhere. It builds with CMake and has per-checkpoint test targets, but "most tests are only available after pushing to the Stanford server".
- **Older offerings**: the course rebuilds one site in place every year, so [its repository's history](https://github.com/cs144/cs144.github.io/blob/d65bd15/index.html) and Internet Archive snapshots are where Autumn 2020 through Autumn 2025 survive; the handouts and exams those pages link are still served live.

## Syllabus

The catalog and the published handouts give the arc: packet switching, layering and routing; datagrams, encapsulation and multiplexing; reliable delivery over an unreliable network; TCP, flow control and congestion control — three handouts ask why, what and how — then names, addresses and Ethernet switching.

The lab is the spine: eight checkpoints building portions of the Internet TCP/IP stack in C++, graded 20% on correctness and 18% subjectively, best six of eight with the last checkpoint compulsory. Weekly quizzes carry 16%, tutorials 3%, and the two exams 40% together.

## Prerequisites

- Systems programming at the level of [CS 110](CS%20110.md) or [CS 111](CS%20111.md), which the catalog names: processes, files and the C/C++ that the lab is written in.

## Source notes

- **The honor-code policy is documented by the CTL, not the course site.** Its [AIMES entry](https://ctl.stanford.edu/aimes/aimes-library/introduction-computer-networking-discussion) records that unpermitted collaboration explicitly includes "using output from a code-synthesis tool (e.g., an LLM) trained on past students' solutions", and that Winstein opens the quarter by inviting students to "stump the teaching team" with an honor-code question, then argues the cases out in class (Winter 2025, checked 2026-09-29).

- The site is a GitHub Pages build at `cs144.github.io`, not a `stanford.edu` host, and it is rebuilt in place each year rather than kept per offering. Autumn 2020, Autumn 2021 and Spring 2023 survive in [the site repository](https://github.com/cs144/cs144.github.io) under its `end-of-2023` tag, whose history runs back to Autumn 2019; `master` was squashed to a single "Initial commit (2026)" and keeps nothing older. Winter 2024, Winter 2025 and Autumn 2025 survive only as Internet Archive snapshots. Each term was read off the page's own heading (checked 2026-09-30).
- **The lab repository is not linked from anywhere on the site.** [CS144/imp](https://github.com/CS144/imp) was found through the [`cs144` GitHub organization](https://github.com/cs144), which holds only it and the site.
- The only public CS 144 lecture videos are the [2012 MOOC](https://www.youtube.com/playlist?list=PLEAYkSg4uSQ2dr0XO_Nwa5OcdEcaaELSG) by Philip Levis and Nick McKeown and a [2016 re-recording](https://www.youtube.com/playlist?list=PLK5LBGAqhW7_FNgTSY4UIB58ZEVao13IY); both predate `cutoff_term` and the current handout-based course, so neither is rated.
- The site says "Fall 2026"; the catalog and this wiki say Autumn.
- One of the optional texts, Peterson & Davie, is free online at `book.systemsapproach.org`, and the Kaashoek & Saltzer chapters are a free MIT OCW PDF.
