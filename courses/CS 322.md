# CS 322: Triangulating Intelligence: Melding Neuroscience, Psychology, and AI

Three instructors from three fields — Gweon in psychology, Yamins in neuroscience, Wu in AI — reading their literatures against each other on shared questions. The background reading sets the frame: Lake et al. on building machines that learn like people, Gopnik's "The Scientist as Child", Carey and Spelke on core knowledge. Then each theme pairs a developmental finding, a neural one and a model.

## Syllabus

[Themed sessions](https://cs322.stanford.edu/), each triangulating one question.

1. Background: what it would mean to build machines that learn and think like people; the child as scientist; core knowledge.
2. Introduction, read against ImageNet classification as the AI reference point.
3. Origins of knowledge: facial identity coding in the primate brain, and representational comparisons between brains and deep networks.
4. Physics and causality: simulation as an engine of physical scene understanding, the neuroanatomy of intuitive physical inference, and neural representations for physics prediction.
