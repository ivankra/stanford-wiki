# CS 250: Algebraic Error Correcting Codes

Coding theory taught algebraically and applied widely: the same Reed-Solomon machinery reappears as group testing, compressed sensing and distributed-storage repair. Run flipped — lectures are pre-recorded on YouTube, class time is group problem-solving — and graded entirely on three homeworks and a project, with no exam.

## Materials

Unusually complete for a theory course: the lectures are pre-recorded on a public YouTube channel, and the notes, the in-class problems and their solutions are all on the site. Only the homework solutions are held back.

## Syllabus

[Eighteen classes](https://web.stanford.edu/class/cs250/), the last two given to project presentations.

1. Foundations (classes 1-4): definitions and the Hamming bound, finite fields and linear codes, McEliece, asymptotics, GV, Singleton and Plotkin bounds, Reed-Solomon codes.
2. Constructions and decoding (5-9): Welch-Berlekamp, BCH and Reed-Muller codes, concatenation and the Zyablov bound, compressed sensing and group testing, achieving capacity on the binary symmetric channel.
3. List decoding (10-13): capacity and the Johnson bound, Guruswami-Sudan, list-recovery, folded RS codes.
4. Locality (14-16): Reed-Muller and locality, local list-decoding via Goldreich-Levin and Kushilevitz-Mansour, RS codes as regenerating codes in distributed storage.

## Prerequisites

- Linear algebra.
- Basic probability: [CS 109](CS%20109.md), CME 106 or EE 178.
- "Mathematical maturity" — the homework is proofs. Finite fields help but aren't assumed; class 2 builds them up.
