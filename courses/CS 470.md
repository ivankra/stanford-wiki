# CS 470: Music and AI

Ge Wang's course at CCRMA, and the only one in the Winter catalog whose stated first learning goal is to be able to frame a critical question rather than to build something. The making is real — four projects in ChucK, ChAI, Wekinator and PyTorch, from a word-embedding etude called "Poets of Sound and Time" to a final piece titled "A Story of You" — but each is paired with a written reading response and an in-class reading where the work is discussed rather than demoed. Enrollment is by application: prospective students email the instructor with something they are curious about and something they are genuinely worried about in AI and music. No prior AI or music experience is assumed.

## Materials

Everything the course hands students is open, and so is almost everything students hand back: the syllabus, all 4 assignment briefs, the ChucK starter code, the gallery, and the in-class feedback documents in which each piece is linked and critiqued by name.

- **Syllabus**: the [syllabus PDF](https://ccrma.stanford.edu/courses/356/music-and-ai-syllabus.pdf) gives the learning goals, the tool list and the workload expectation, and the [home page](https://ccrma.stanford.edu/courses/356/) carries the dated assignment calendar, the application instructions and the teaching philosophy. There is no dated lecture schedule.
- **Slides**: none; sessions are making, in-class readings and discussion.
- **Notes**: the readings are a curated list of outside articles the site does not publish, with students bringing their own.
- **Videos**: no source mentions recording.
- **Assignments**: 4 briefs on the CCRMA wiki — ["Poets of Sound and Time"](https://ccrma.stanford.edu/wiki/356-winter-2026/etude1), ["Featured Artist"](https://ccrma.stanford.edu/wiki/356-winter-2026/hw2), ["Wekinate Your World"](https://ccrma.stanford.edu/wiki/356-winter-2026/hw3) and ["A Story of You"](https://ccrma.stanford.edu/wiki/356-winter-2026/final) — each with its tools, prompts and deadlines.
- **Solutions**, **Exams**: none; the work is pieces and written reflections, and there is no exam.
- **Projects**: the [gallery](https://ccrma.stanford.edu/courses/356/gallery/) collects this offering's design projects and reading reflections, alongside the 2 reading prompts, "What do you (really) want from AI music generation?" and "Power to the People / Humans in the Loop". The in-class feedback documents the calendar links go further: each lists every student's piece by URL with their classmates' critiques beneath it, and all of them open anonymously.
- **Repo**: a [code directory](https://ccrma.stanford.edu/courses/356/code/) with a folder per assignment holding the ChucK starter files and sample data; the tools themselves — ChucK, ChAI and Wekinator — are public projects.
- **Textbook**: supplemental only, the instructor's own *Artful Design: Technology in Search of the Sublime*.

## Syllabus

No lecture schedule is published. The course is structured by its four assignments, per the [home page](https://ccrma.stanford.edu/courses/356/):

1. **Weeks 1–2, "Poets of Sound and Time"**: a programming etude built on a word embedding model, due 18 January, followed by an in-class reading of the work.
2. **Weeks 3–5, "Featured Artist"**: a programming project with a milestone, final deliverables and in-class presentations.
3. **Weeks 6–9, "Wekinate Your World"**: an etude in interactive machine learning with Wekinator — training a mapping from gesture to sound by example rather than by code.
4. **Weeks 9–11, "A Story of You"**: the final project, closing with a reading on 19 March.

Readings are a curated list of articles on the technical, philosophical, social and cultural dimensions of AI and music, with students bringing their own.

## Prerequisites

- One to two years of programming. No prior AI or music experience is needed.
- Recommended but not required: MUSIC 256A / [CS 476A](CS%20476A.md), or MUSIC 220B — both CCRMA courses in computer music.
- Instructor consent, by application, is required to enroll.
