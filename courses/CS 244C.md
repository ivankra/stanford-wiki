# CS 244C: Advanced Networking and Distributed Systems

Mazières and Winstein alternate lectures across 20 papers, and the grade is almost all project: there are no problem sets, and the only other components are attendance, participation and in-class reading quizzes. The paper list pairs canon with current work — Saltzer, Reed and Clark's end-to-end arguments next to Wallbleed, Raft next to Simplex, Kung's-era congestion-control math next to starvation results and Remy. Teams of 2-4 build or replicate a system and present it as the final exam. It satisfies the [CS 244](CS%20244.md) or CS 244b program-sheet slot.

## Materials

Everything is open, including the papers themselves, the instructors' notes under an explicit redistribution grant, and the students' finished projects with their code.

- **Syllabus**: the [schedule](https://www.scs.stanford.edu/26wi-cs244c/sched/) lists every session with its paper and serves all 20 readings as PDFs from `sched/readings/`. Grading and learning goals are on the [home page](https://www.scs.stanford.edu/26wi-cs244c/).
- **Slides**, **Notes**: [16 sets](https://www.scs.stanford.edu/26wi-cs244c/notes/), mostly plain text and 2 as Google Slides. The page grants "permission hereby granted for anyone to copy, modify, and redistribute any lecture note material from this class".
- **Videos**: the home page links a lecture Zoom for attending, with its password on the syllabus page, but nothing says the sessions are recorded.
- **Assignments**: the [project guidelines](https://www.scs.stanford.edu/26wi-cs244c/proj/) are the only coursework — scope, group size, and the January-to-March deadline chain.
- **Solutions**, **Exams**: none; there are no problem sets, and the presentation is the final.
- **Projects**, **Repo**: the [presentation program](https://www.scs.stanford.edu/26wi-cs244c/proj/program.html) lists each team with a link to its paper and its code — DCTCP beyond the datacenter, striped tunnels, residential proxy detection, learned congestion control compared across decision trees, deep RL and LLM-guided evolution, and several replication studies.

## Syllabus

Five blocks over ten weeks, per the [schedule](https://www.scs.stanford.edu/26wi-cs244c/sched/).

1. **Internet fundamentals** (weeks 1–2): the end-to-end argument; Tussle in Cyberspace, with network neutrality optional; Wallbleed and how the Great Firewall discovers hidden circumvention servers.
2. **Distributed systems fundamentals** (weeks 2–4): two-phase commit; ZooKeeper; Raft; Simplex.
3. **Congestion control** (weeks 5–6): the underlying math; starvation in end-to-end congestion control; Bolt; Remy, i.e. computer-generated congestion control.
4. **Hyperscale and datacenter networking** (weeks 7–8): VL2; FaRM; eRPC.
5. **Wide-area and consumer systems** (weeks 9–10): Spanner; Puffer; Coral; CausalSim.
