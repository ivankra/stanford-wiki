# CS 275B: Computational Music Analysis

The second half of the CCARH pair: where [CS 275A](CS%20275A.md) is about how a score becomes data, this is about what you do with it once it is. The lab runs on the open-source Humdrum Toolkit against three synchronized datasets — the same music in notation and analysis encodings — and the course's recurring concern is data quality, since an analysis is only as good as the encoding under it. Graded primarily on projects.

## Syllabus

From this offering's own syllabus. The first four weeks teach analysis and retrieval and the Humdrum Toolkit; the remaining six are individual project work with weekly reports.

1. **Computational musicology and melodic search** (week 1), with Verovio, harmony and keyscapes.
2. **Musical similarity** (week 2): approaches to melodic and other similarity assessment, regular expressions as the search tool, Themefinder.
3. **Style synthesis** (week 3): David Cope's work, "From Emmy to Emily", and performance similarity across Chopin mazurkas.
4. **Practical realities of research, and audio similarity** (week 4).
5. **Music and AI after Cope** (week 5).
6. **Schemata and programming** (week 6): compositional schemata, programming against Humdrum files, the Tasso in Music Project, artificial counterpoint, and sonority distributions by bass-line scale degree in Corelli.
7. **Project development** (weeks 7–10), ending in presentations and a written report.

The catalog's version describes "three synchronized sets of symbolic data resources for notation and analysis", the Humdrum Toolkit, and "issues of data content and quality as well as methods of information retrieval, visualization, and summarization", graded "based primarily on student projects".
