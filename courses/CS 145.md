# CS 145: Intro to Modern Data Systems

A database course organized as twenty questions with names attached — how Spotify searches 100 million tracks, how one Stripe charge becomes four writes that all commit or none, how an agent remembers anything between sessions — and written as a public website that invites readers who aren't enrolled.

## Materials

Nearly everything is open, and deliberately so: the site offers non-students reading paths aimed at an exam, a SQL screen or a systems interview. The sign-in that the videos and PSETs ask for takes any Google account or an email link, so it isn't restricted to Stanford; for the PSETs it only saves progress.

- **Syllabus**: the [Autumn 2026 schedule](https://cs145.web.app/schedule-fa26.html), with every PSET, project and exam date.
- **Notes**: the course reader is the site — [module pages](https://cs145.web.app/Module1-SQL/joins.html) and the twenty case studies.
- **Slides**: the [lecture PDF archive](https://cs145.web.app/pdfs/lectures/) keeps a fixed copy of each deck.
- **Videos**: a five-minute video mode on each notes page, which needs a sign-in with any Google account, plus short CA section walkthroughs. These are the course's video content: the FAQ sends students who miss a lecture to these pages and their videos, not to recordings.
- **Assignments**: the [PSET scheme](https://cs145.web.app/psets/psets.html) and [project pages](https://cs145.web.app/projects/projects.html) are public; answering a set asks for the same sign-in (any Google account or an email link), so that scores persist.
- **Exams**: an in-person final on December 7; no past papers.
- **Projects**: two, with no published student work.
- **Code**: section Colabs run query plans, WAL traces and distributed jobs.

## Syllabus

Six modules, worked through the case studies listed on the [front page](https://cs145.stanford.edu/):

1. **SQL and intermediate SQL**: joins and query semantics, then the correctness problem of LLM-generated SQL — ambiguity, unit tests, eval sets, grounding.
2. **Systems**: Bloom filters, vector indexes and HNSW search.
3. **Storage and indexing**: B+Trees and inverted indexes, index selection and IO cost models, columnar storage, LSM trees.
4. **Transactions**: ACID, atomicity, two-phase locking.
5. **Distributed data**: replication, sharding, two-phase commit, Kafka's append-only log, GFS-style storage on unreliable drives.
6. **Data systems in practice**: data quality and observability, privacy and re-identification, SQL injection, KV-cache reuse for agents.

Five PSETs with unlimited attempts before the deadline, two projects, a final exam.
