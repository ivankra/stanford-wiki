# CS 360: Simplicity and Complexity in Economic Theory

Technology has made economic systems more complex than anyone can reason about, and some of the resulting problems are beyond both supercomputers and human comprehension. The course takes that tension as its subject: designs must be computationally tractable *and* simple enough for people to use. A topics course working through recent theoretical advances, meeting once a week for three hours.

## Syllabus

Only the catalog describes the content. The named key topics, "but not limited to", are resource allocation in complex environments; communication complexity and information aggregation in markets; robust mechanisms; dynamic matching theory; influence maximization in networks; and the design of simple, user-friendly mechanisms. The applications it names are paired kidney exchange, auctions for electricity and for radio spectrum, ride-sharing platforms, and the diffusion of information. The course "explores simplicity and complexity in economics, primarily via theoretical models" and focuses on recent advances.
