# CS 229: Machine Learning

The department's broad introduction to machine learning and statistical pattern recognition, from supervised learning through unsupervised learning to reinforcement learning and control. It runs most terms; the summer offering is compressed into eight weeks.

## Materials

The notes, syllabus and older slides are public; assignments (Ed) and recordings (Canvas) are not.

- **Syllabus**: the [syllabus sheet](https://docs.google.com/spreadsheets/d/1X8OpCbt6SyJ3_qlj5LxvZFoOtbjBadBVRzdmY4s6vb4/edit?gid=0#gid=0) and the [logistics document](https://docs.google.com/document/d/1PbQxBQTpp4K5hTzOJB9r8etKgsAuNPWs/edit#heading=h.xwyvj9qi2dka) (grading, policies, exam rules) open without an account, despite the homepage's login notice.

- **Notes**: the [course notes](https://cs229.stanford.edu/main_notes.pdf), 278 pages covering the whole syllabus, plus [linear algebra](https://cs229.stanford.edu/lectures-spring2022/cs229-linear_algebra_review.pdf), [probability](https://cs229.stanford.edu/lectures-spring2022/cs229-probability_review.pdf) and [decision tree](https://cs229.stanford.edu/cs229-notes-decision_trees.pdf) handouts.

## Syllabus

Sixteen lectures over eight weeks, per the [syllabus sheet](https://docs.google.com/spreadsheets/d/1X8OpCbt6SyJ3_qlj5LxvZFoOtbjBadBVRzdmY4s6vb4/edit?gid=0#gid=0), each mapped to a section of the notes, plus seven CA lectures.

1. **Weeks 1–2**: linear regression and least squares; gradient descent and the bias-variance tradeoff; regularization, ridge and LASSO; validation; logistic regression.
2. **Week 3**: generalized linear models and SGD; decision trees, bagging and boosting.
3. **Week 4**: neural networks, in two lectures.
4. **Week 5**: k-means, GMM and EM.
5. **Weeks 6–7**: PCA and ICA; reinforcement learning; policy gradient.
6. **Week 8**: RL and LLMs, then exam review.

CA lectures cover linear algebra, probability, evaluation metrics, sequence models and transformers.

## Prerequisites

- Programming: enough Python/NumPy to write a reasonably non-trivial program (CS 106A, CS 106B or CS 106X).
- Probability: CS 109, MATH 151 or STATS 116.
- Multivariable calculus and linear algebra: MATH 51 or CS 205; the logistics document also names MATH 104, MATH 113 and CME 100 as relevant background.
