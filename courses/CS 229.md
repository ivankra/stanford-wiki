# CS 229: Machine Learning

Stanford's broad machine learning introduction, taught every term by different instructors: Autumn and Spring keep the long-running sequence of GLMs, GDA and EM, Winter leans on neural networks, and Summer, described here, is an 8-week version with 3 problem sets, a final and no project.

## Materials

The whole course can be followed from outside: course notes are public, Spring 2026's lectures are on YouTube. Latest problemsets stay closed on Ed, but you can easily find older ones floating around.

- **Syllabus**: the [schedule sheet](https://docs.google.com/spreadsheets/d/1X8OpCbt6SyJ3_qlj5LxvZFoOtbjBadBVRzdmY4s6vb4/edit?gid=0#gid=0) and the [logistics document](https://docs.google.com/document/d/1PbQxBQTpp4K5hTzOJB9r8etKgsAuNPWs/edit#heading=h.xwyvj9qi2dka) are public: 16 lectures, 7 CA lectures, deadlines, grading and exam rules.
- **Slides**: this offering's decks are on Canvas. [Autumn 2022](https://cs229.stanford.edu/notes2022fall/) posted decks for 10 of its 20 lectures, [Autumn 2021](https://cs229.stanford.edu/notes2021fall/) decks or live notes for 12, and [Spring 2022](https://docs.google.com/spreadsheets/d/1OEsqqhihH-n2OPHsT8jSA8BkLdqUMWY-GiWHgkBs3Z8/edit) decks for 9.
- **Notes**: extensive [course notes](https://cs229.stanford.edu/main_notes.pdf) (278 pages) plus [linear algebra](https://cs229.stanford.edu/lectures-spring2022/cs229-linear_algebra_review.pdf), [probability](https://cs229.stanford.edu/lectures-spring2022/cs229-probability_review.pdf) and [decision trees](https://cs229.stanford.edu/cs229-notes-decision_trees.pdf) handouts. Summer 2026 term has additional notes on [RL and LLMs](https://cs229.stanford.edu/summer2026/CS229_LR_LLMs.pdf).
- **Videos**: this offering's videos are gated, but [Spring 2026](https://www.youtube.com/playlist?list=PLaqpC4kq8Gpw) and [Spring 2022](https://www.youtube.com/playlist?list=PLoROMvodv4rNyWOpJg_Yh4NSqI4Z4vOYy) are public on YouTube.
- **Assignments**: 3 problem sets of written and Python questions, on Ed "to prevent public exposure of solutions". [Summer 2020 problemsets](https://cs229.stanford.edu/summer2020/) are public with starter code but no solutions. GitHub has unofficial copies: [yuanboP](https://github.com/yuanboP/CS229) and [flawnn](https://github.com/flawnn/CS229) for Summer 2025, [suriya030](https://github.com/suriya030/cs229-MachineLearning-assignments) for Winter 2025. [Stanford Engineering Everywhere](https://see.stanford.edu/Course/CS229) has 4 old problemsets with solutions from 2008.
- **Exams**: a 3-hour printed, open-notes final; no practice paper is mentioned.
- **Projects**: none; "there is no project due to the shortened duration of the summer quarter". Reports are public for [Spring 2020](https://cs229.stanford.edu/proj2020spr/) (22), [Spring 2021](https://cs229.stanford.edu/proj2021spr/) (117) and [Spring 2022](https://docs.google.com/spreadsheets/d/1GIsGZKozmTxqzmXLt7FBmVRCHVJvgd_1DD-f9f5PwkU/edit) (94); Autumn 2022's and Autumn 2023's need a Google sign-in.
- **Repo**: the starter code comes with the assignments on Ed.

## Syllabus

16 lectures over 8 weeks, per the [Summer 2026 syllabus](https://docs.google.com/spreadsheets/d/1X8OpCbt6SyJ3_qlj5LxvZFoOtbjBadBVRzdmY4s6vb4/edit?gid=0#gid=0), plus 7 CA lectures:

1. **Weeks 1–2**: linear regression and least squares; gradient descent and the bias-variance tradeoff; regularization, ridge and LASSO; validation; logistic regression.
2. **Week 3**: generalized linear models and SGD; decision trees, bagging and kNN.
3. **Week 4**: neural networks, in 2 lectures.
4. **Week 5**: k-means, GMM and EM.
5. **Weeks 6–7**: PCA and ICA; reinforcement learning; policy gradient.
6. **Week 8**: RL and LLMs, then exam review.
7. **CA lectures**: linear algebra and probability reviews, boosting and evaluation metrics, sequence models, transformers, 2 exam reviews.

Other terms differ: [Autumn](https://cs229.stanford.edu/index.html-fall25) (Charikar, Guestrin and Ng) runs 20 lectures in the long-running order, from LMS and Newton's method through GDA, kernels and EM to value iteration, closing on LLMs and 2 lectures on fairness and privacy. [Spring](https://www.youtube.com/playlist?list=PLaqpC4kq8Gpw) (Ré and Ma) keeps GLMs, GDA, neural networks and EM, then turns to diffusion models, representation learning, LLMs and policy gradients. [Winter](https://cs229.stanford.edu/w24-index.html) (Koyejo) gives neural networks 5 lectures, transformers and CNNs among them, and ends on fairness and ethics.

## Prerequisites

- Programming: enough Python/NumPy to write a reasonably non-trivial program ([CS 106A](CS%20106A.md) or [CS 106B](CS%20106B.md)).
- Probability: [CS 109](CS%20109.md), MATH 151 or STATS 116.
- Multivariable calculus and linear algebra: MATH 51 or [CS 205L](CS%20205L.md), the course's own mathematical methods for machine learning; the logistics document also names MATH 104, MATH 113 and CME 100 as relevant background.

## Related

- [CS 129](CS%20129.md): the applied undergraduate version, formerly CS 229A: algorithms learned from Coursera videos, with the one weekly session spent deriving what they asserted.
- [CS 221](CS%20221.md): the other broad AI course, on search, logic and decision making rather than statistical learning.
- [CS 224N](CS%20224N.md): deep learning for language alone, at a faster pace.
- [CS 229M](CS%20229M.md): the theory behind these algorithms: uniform convergence, Rademacher complexity and optimization rates.
- [CS 230](CS%20230.md): deep learning in depth, where CS 229 gives it 2 lectures. Heavy project-based course.
- [CS 231N](CS%20231N.md): deep learning for vision alone, at a faster pace.
