# CS 294S: Research Project in Software Systems and Security

Not a lecture course: a remote research placement inside one project. Monica Lam's students build Almond, the Sloan-funded open-source virtual assistant whose skills live in Thingpedia rather than in a vendor's catalogue, and the syllabus is a list of research openings — better entity disambiguation, meta-learning new domains, dialogue design beyond simple transactions. The final project is 70% of the grade.

## Syllabus

Autumn 2020 ran on the premise that neural dialogue agents can now be generated from a knowledge base without hand-written dialogue trees, and organised its openings into four areas ([site](https://web.stanford.edu/class/cs294s/)):

1. **End-to-end projects**: the ten commonest assistant skills — music, radio, news, sports, podcasts, question answering, weather, timers, IoT control, third-party skills — aiming to match commercial assistants by June 2021.
2. **NLP**: entity disambiguation, naturalness of agent responses, meta-learning new domains, dialogues that recover from mistakes, localization by machine translation.
3. **HCI**: crowdsourcing skills, ontologies and multilingual data from a user community; multimodal interfaces that let end users program long-tail skills.
4. **Systems**: representing well-known data types such as time and space in ThingTalk; extending dialogue models from transactions to FAQs and on-topic discussion.

Lectures cover the group's own papers; the rest of the quarter is pitches, checkpoints and project work.

## Prerequisites

- Discrete mathematics and proofs: CS 103.
- Systems programming: CS 107.

The site states only that students have taken at least two CS courses, and the course is open to undergraduates, masters and PhD students alike.
