# CS 106S: Coding for Social Good

A student-led Thursday seminar that takes [CS 106B](CS%20106B.md)'s tools out to social-good problems — disease classification, hate-speech detection, mental-health chatbots, civic web apps — and teaches enough JavaScript and web development along the way to build them.

## Materials

Everything is public, and the pattern is consistent week by week: a deck, a handout PDF, a starter-code repo, and an `answers` branch with the worked solutions — which few Stanford courses publish at all. Autumn 2026 has only week 1 so far, the quarter having begun 24 September, and [Spring 2025](https://web.stanford.edu/class/cs106s/spr2025) is complete and still up.

- **Syllabus**: the [course information PDF](https://web.stanford.edu/class/cs106s/res/cs106s-course-information.pdf) and a separate [calendar PDF](https://web.stanford.edu/class/cs106s/res/cs106s-course-calendar.pdf). The site's schedule names all nine weeks up front.
- **Slides**: week 1's [Google Slides deck](https://tinyurl.com/cs106s-aut26-w1-slides), posted the day of class. The [nine Spring 2025 decks](https://web.stanford.edu/class/cs106s/slides/cs106s_spr25_week1.pdf) are all public, 9-13 MB each.
- **Notes**: a [JavaScript guide](https://web.stanford.edu/class/cs106s/handouts/js-tutorial.html) written for the course, plus a [handout PDF](https://web.stanford.edu/class/cs106s/res/cs106s-week01-handout.pdf) per week.
- **Videos**: nothing found, and no source says whether the seminar is recorded.
- **Assignments**: each week's starter code is its own repo under [github.com/yanbenjamin](https://github.com/yanbenjamin) — `cs106s-w1` for the cipher exercise, `cs106s-sentiment`, `CS106S-climatechange`, `cs106s-knn`.
- **Solutions**: an [`answers` branch](https://github.com/yanbenjamin/cs106s-w1/blob/answers/assignment.js) on each repo, covering both the main exercise and its [extensions](https://github.com/yanbenjamin/cs106s-w1/blob/answers/assignment-extension.js).
- **Exams**, **Projects**: none. Grading is attendance only, "and no work is required outside of class :)". The course "ends, by tradition, with a boba party on the last day".
- **Repo**: the four week repos above and [cs106s-website](https://github.com/yanbenjamin/cs106s-website), the site's own source.

## Syllabus

The Autumn 2026 schedule, one topic a week on Thursdays:

1. Intro to CS for social good, JavaScript, and cryptography (24 Sep)
2. Sentiment analysis and refugee tweets (1 Oct)
3. CS for climate change (8 Oct)
4. Disease detection: k-nearest neighbors and k-means clustering (15 Oct)
5. Cybersecurity and ethical web hacking (22 Oct)
6. Rethinking the Electoral College; alternate voting methods (29 Oct)
7. Internet trust and safety (5 Nov)
8. Mental health and psychotherapy (12 Nov)

No class in finals week (3 Dec). Spring 2025 ran nearly the same sequence, substituting satellite imagery via Google Earth Engine for the climate week and open-source web software for the Electoral College week. The instructor notes topics "freely rotate from quarter to quarter, and I'm enthusiastic for anything you'd like to suggest".

## Source notes

- **The site needs a browser.** A plain fetch returns 7.9 KB of section headings with nothing under them, and the schedule looks empty; an earlier check here read that as content hidden in HTML comments and rated the course `unknown`. It isn't hidden — the page builds its schedule and About text client-side, and a headless Chromium renders the whole thing (re-extracted 2026-09-27). Rechecking this course means rendering it, not fetching it.
- **The Spring 2025 slide paths are not where they look.** The archive page is at `/class/cs106s/spr2025` with **no trailing slash**, so its relative `slides/…` links resolve against the parent: the decks live at `web.stanford.edu/class/cs106s/slides/cs106s_spr25_weekN.pdf`, and `/spr2025/slides/…` 404s. `/spr2025/` with a slash 404s too.
- **The week repos are reused, not rebuilt.** `cs106s-w1` was last pushed in January 2025 and the other three in April 2025, so the repositories Autumn 2026's handouts point at are the ones Spring 2025 used; their `answers` branches are the solutions for both (checked 2026-09-30).
- Materials are spread across three hosts — PDFs on `web.stanford.edu`, decks on Google Slides behind `tinyurl.com` short links, code on GitHub — so no single URL indexes them.
- The course ran every quarter from Winter 2020 to Spring 2025 but is **absent from the 2025-2026 catalog year entirely**, returning in Autumn 2026. That gap in `terms_offered` is real, not a sourcing hole.
- The catalog's description changed with the return: 2024-25 called it a "Survey course" whose weeks are "designed and delivered by student instructors"; Autumn 2026 calls it a "Seminar course" and drops the student-instructor wording, though the site still credits a single student instructor with Prof. Cain as faculty sponsor.
