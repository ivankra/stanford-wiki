# CS 111: Operating Systems Principles

The undergraduate operating-systems course, built bottom-up from the Unix V6 filesystem through processes, threads and virtual memory, with four assignments in C against real system calls. The catalog states it substitutes for [CS 110](CS%20110.md) wherever that is required.

## Syllabus

The [calendar](https://web.stanford.edu/class/archive/cs/cs111/cs111.1272/calendar) runs three questions across ten weeks, with optional reading from Anderson and Dahlin:

1. **Filesystems (weeks 1–3)**: what a filesystem owes its users, the Unix V6 design in detail — inodes, large files, directories, lookup — then crash recovery and journaling, and the system-call interface of file descriptors.
2. **Processes (weeks 3–4)**: what a process is, `fork`, `waitpid` and `execvp`, then pipes and `dup2` for interprocess communication.
3. **Threads and the kernel (weeks 5–10)**: threads and race conditions, locks, condition variables and the monitor pattern; dispatching, scheduling and preemption, and how locks are implemented; then virtual memory, dynamic address translation, demand paging and the clock algorithm, finishing on modern OSes and trust.

Four assignments, weekly sections, a midterm (Oct 28) and a final (Dec 7).

## Prerequisites

- Systems programming in C at [CS 107](CS%20107.md)'s level: pointers, memory, the heap, and comfort at a Unix command line. `assign0` exists to re-establish the C and tooling the course relies on.
