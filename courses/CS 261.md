# CS 261: Combinatorial Optimization

Aaron Sidford's algorithms course, and one of the few where the whole content is public while nothing else about the offering is. It opens with Karger's minimum cut, works through graph exploration, matroids and the maximum-flow family, then re-derives the same problems through linear programming — geometry, duality, ellipsoid and interior point methods — before finishing with spectral graph theory, random walks, and the NP-hardness that motivates approximation. It carries the CME 310 and MS&E 315 codes as well, and was called "Optimization and Algorithmic Paradigms" until recently.

## Syllabus

No schedule is published. The [notes](https://drive.google.com/file/d/1B4iH9X3qWVnH4shhUUmXJgdBdPBvsAtH/view?usp=sharing), which the instructor says accompany the course, run in five arcs across 26 chapters.

1. **Graphs and cuts** (1–5): global minimum cut and Karger's algorithm; graph theory foundations — paths, reachability, arborescences; BFS, DFS and running times; spanning trees, matroids and linear matroid optimization; spanners and low-stretch trees.
2. **Flow** (6–12): maximum flow and minimum s-t cut; augmenting paths; blocking flows; flow applications and what lies beyond maximum flow; matching via algebraic methods; matrix multiplication.
3. **Linear programming** (13–17): the formulation, its geometry, duality; cutting plane and ellipsoid methods; interior point methods.
4. **Spectral methods** (18–21): spectral graph theory, Laplacian systems and electric flows, Cheeger's inequality, random walks.
5. **Hardness and approximation** (22–26): P versus NP, NP-completeness and reductions, then greedy, LP-based and TSP approximation algorithms.

## Prerequisites

The catalog states none. The material assumes an algorithms course at the level of [CS 161](CS%20161.md) — asymptotic analysis, graph algorithms, and comfort with writing proofs — and the linear programming half assumes linear algebra.
