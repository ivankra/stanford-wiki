# CS 265: Randomized Algorithms and Probabilistic Analysis

Probabilistic analysis and randomized algorithms, taught as a flipped class: students watch short mini-lectures or read notes beforehand, and class time is group problem-solving. Homework is ungraded; the grade comes from two exams.

## Materials

Because the class is flipped, its instruction is public by design: the 39 mini-lectures students watch before each session are on the instructor's YouTube channel, and the [site](https://web.stanford.edu/class/cs265/) links notes and an in-class agenda for all 18 classes. Only the homework solutions and the recordings of the in-class work are on Canvas.

- **Syllabus**: the [site](https://web.stanford.edu/class/cs265/) is the whole thing — the flipped structure, the grading split (participation 10%, midterm 30%, final 60%), the rule that homework is not graded, and the class-by-class resource list.
- **Slides**: the [in-class presentation](https://web.stanford.edu/class/cs265/Lectures/Lecture5/InClass5.pdf) for each class, in its lecture directory.
- **Notes**: [lecture notes](https://web.stanford.edu/class/cs265/Lectures/Lecture1/l1.pdf) for every class, each beside its [agenda of exercises](https://web.stanford.edu/class/cs265/Lectures/Lecture5/class5.pdf).
- **Videos**: ["Randomized Algorithms"](https://www.youtube.com/playlist?list=PLkvhuSoxwjI_JL7GYcJHK7-EK55t0KYGO), 39 mini-lectures on the instructor's own channel, the ones the schedule assigns before class. The site notes she doesn't monetize the channel and that a few errata are recorded in the video descriptions. Recordings of the in-class sessions, which "include a lot of dead time while we work on problems", are on Canvas.
- **Assignments**: [HW1](https://web.stanford.edu/class/cs265/Homework/HW1/HW1.pdf) is linked and is still the Winter 2026 file; that offering's [HW1 to HW8](https://web.stanford.edu/class/cs265/Homework/) all remain in the open listing, as PDF and LaTeX source.
- **Solutions**: [worked answers](https://web.stanford.edu/class/cs265/Lectures/Lecture5/class5-sols.pdf) to the in-class exercises are posted per class, and both practice papers have solutions. The homework solutions go to Canvas — though the homework is ungraded.
- **Exams**: a [practice midterm](https://web.stanford.edu/class/cs265/Exams/practice_midterm.pdf) and a [practice final](https://web.stanford.edu/class/cs265/Exams/practice_final.pdf), each with a solution set.
- **Projects**, **Repo**: neither; a theory course whose grade is 2 exams and participation.

## Syllabus

Two themes: analyzing random structures (random walks, random graphs and networks) and designing randomized algorithms. The catalog lists tail bounds, the probabilistic method, Markov chains and martingales, applied to random graphs, metric embeddings, random walks and randomized algorithms, with applications in machine learning, data analysis, networking and systems.

The class-by-class list runs to 18 sessions. It opens with the model of randomized computation and polynomial identity testing (Schwartz-Zippel), then linearity of expectation with Karger's and Karger-Stein min-cut and randomized quicksort, and works through concentration, Markov chains, martingales and their applications; each class assigns its mini-lectures, a Gradescope quiz on them, and an in-class agenda of exercises.

## Prerequisites

[CS 161](CS%20161.md) plus probability; the catalog says STAT 116 and the site says STAT 117/118, "or equivalents and instructor consent."

## Related

- [CS 166](CS%20166.md): data structures that put randomization to work, such as sketches and cuckoo hashing.
- [CS 254B](CS%20254B.md): asks whether the randomness in algorithms like these can be removed.
- [CS 263](CS%20263.md): counting and sampling, one corner of randomized algorithms taken in depth.
- [CS 368](CS%20368.md): sketching and streaming algorithms, built on the concentration bounds taught here.
