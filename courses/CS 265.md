# CS 265: Randomized Algorithms and Probabilistic Analysis

Probabilistic analysis and randomized algorithms, taught as a flipped class: students watch short mini-lectures or read notes beforehand, and class time is group problem-solving. Homework is ungraded; the grade comes from two exams.

## Syllabus

Two themes: analyzing random structures (random walks, random graphs and networks) and designing randomized algorithms. The catalog lists tail bounds, the probabilistic method, Markov chains and martingales, applied to random graphs, metric embeddings, random walks and randomized algorithms, with applications in machine learning, data analysis, networking and systems.

The site shows only the first two of 17 classes so far:

1. Introduction: model of randomized computation, polynomial identity testing (Schwartz-Zippel)
2. Linearity of expectation; Karger's and Karger-Stein min-cut algorithms; quicksort with random pivot

## Prerequisites

[CS 161](CS%20161.md) plus probability; the catalog says STAT 116 and the site says STAT 117/118, "or equivalents and instructor consent."
