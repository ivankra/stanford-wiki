# CS 170: Stanford Laptop Orchestra: Composition, Coding, and Performance

An ensemble of more than twenty laptops, controllers and hemispherical speaker arrays, each giving its instrument a sonic identity in the room. The quarter ends in two concerts, at the CCRMA Stage and Bing Concert Hall. It may be repeated four times for credit.

## Materials

The course page is an application notice, but the project briefs are on a public CCRMA wiki that the page links, and a syllabus PDF survives on an older offering's page. There is no dated schedule anywhere.

- **Syllabus**: the [page](https://ccrma.stanford.edu/courses/128/) has meeting time, location, application form and a summary — no schedule. The [Spring 2018 syllabus PDF](https://ccrma.stanford.edu/courses/128-spring-2023/slork-2018-syllabus.pdf), still served from the Spring 2023 page, has the grading scheme the current page omits.
- **Assignments**: the briefs are public wiki pages, one per project per year, current offering included — [Project #1](https://ccrma.stanford.edu/wiki/128-spring-2026/hw1) asks you to build a laptop-orchestra instrument in ChucK for a single hemispherical speaker array, "consider issues of playability, expressiveness, the sound through the hemispherical speaker arrays, and also use in an ensemble setting", then write and perform a one-to-two-minute statement for it. Project #2 scales the same idea to the full ensemble.
- **Notes**: each brief carries its own short reading list — Ge Wang's "The Birth and Rise of the Laptop Orchestra" from *Artful Design*, Dan Trueman's "Why A Laptop Orchestra?".
- **Videos**: the [SLOrk site](http://slork.stanford.edu/) holds concert media, as performance documentation rather than course material.
- **Slides**: not found. **Exams**: none. **Projects**: the pieces are performed publicly but not published as coursework.
- **Code**: the site links a `ck/` directory of ChucK examples, which 403s. The tools themselves — ChucK and the S.M.E.L.T. mapping toolkit — are public.
- **Extra**: the [2016 class wiki](https://chuck.stanford.edu/wiki/SLOrk/2016) and its 2017 successor carry the whole grading policy and a reading list of nine papers on laptop orchestras, gesture in performance and intimate musical control. Ten years old, so no rating.

## Syllabus

No dated schedule is published. The shape comes from the assignment briefs and the old wiki: two musical works, "approximately one by the end of the 4th week, and the second by the end of the 8th week — including instrument design, sound design, programming, and crafting musical presentation". The first is chamber music for two to four players, the second for the full ensemble; pieces run four to seven minutes and group work is encouraged. Each is first performed at an in-class dress rehearsal, then publicly — the chamber concert at the CCRMA Stage, the ensemble concert at Bing.

Rehearsal happens partly outside class in the "slorkshop", the CCRMA Listening Room — per the brief, "the only place in the world where you can work directly with an actual SLOrkstation for this assignment".
