# CS 170: Stanford Laptop Orchestra: Composition, Coding, and Performance

An ensemble of more than twenty laptops, controllers and hemispherical speaker arrays, each giving its instrument a sonic identity in the room. The quarter ends in two concerts, at the CCRMA Stage and Bing Concert Hall. It may be repeated four times for credit.

## Materials

The course page is an application notice with no schedule and no grading scheme, but the 2 project briefs are public wiki pages, for this offering and every year back to 2014, and the concerts are public: the June 2026 performance has a livestream recording and program notes on the orchestra's own site. Missing is the middle of the course, the rehearsals and workshops, which leave no public trace.

- **Videos**: no class session is recorded or posted.
- **Assignments**: [Project #1](https://ccrma.stanford.edu/wiki/128-spring-2026/hw1) asks you to build a laptop-orchestra instrument in ChucK for a single hemispherical speaker array, "consider issues of playability, expressiveness, the sound through the hemispherical speaker arrays, and also use in an ensemble setting", then write and perform a 1-to-2-minute statement for it; Project #2 scales the same idea to the full ensemble. Both are on the wiki, as are every year's since 2014.
- **Solutions**, **Exams**: none; the deliverables are 2 composed works and the concerts.

- **Extras**: the [2016 class wiki](https://chuck.stanford.edu/wiki/SLOrk/2016) and its 2017 successor carry the whole grading policy and the full reading list on laptop orchestras and gesture in performance. 10 years old, so no rating.

## Syllabus

No dated schedule is published. The shape comes from the assignment briefs and the old wiki: two musical works, "approximately one by the end of the 4th week, and the second by the end of the 8th week — including instrument design, sound design, programming, and crafting musical presentation". The first is chamber music for two to four players, the second for the full ensemble; pieces run four to seven minutes and group work is encouraged. Each is first performed at an in-class dress rehearsal, then publicly — the chamber concert at the CCRMA Stage, the ensemble concert at Bing.

Rehearsal happens partly outside class in the "slorkshop", the CCRMA Listening Room — per the brief, "the only place in the world where you can work directly with an actual SLOrkstation for this assignment".
