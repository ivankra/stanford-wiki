# CS 120: Introduction to AI Safety

Safety treated as a measurement and institutional problem rather than a training one: what claim is being made about a system, what would count as evidence for it, and which audits or institutions could check it under optimization pressure. Each meeting pairs a technical lecture with a seminar discussion, and the quarter ends in an assurance case built as a project.

## Syllabus

Five modules, from the [schedule](https://stanford-cs120.github.io/fall2026/):

1. **Framing safety and assurance**: hazards, harms, risks, reliability, alignment and governance; what the object of assurance is — model, pipeline, deployed system or institution; what counts as an assurance claim.
2. **Reactive model behavior**: how data and objectives produce behavior; Goodhart effects and shortcut learning; distribution shift and adversarial inputs; data curation and documentation as specification.
3. **Sequential decision-making and agency**: policies, planning and reinforcement learning; reward hacking, side effects, unsafe exploration; the orthogonality thesis and instrumental convergence.
4. **Adaptive interaction**: human feedback and reward models; preference aggregation under disagreement; sycophancy and manipulation; labor and organizational incentives in deployment.
5. **Evaluation and governance**: construct validity; benchmarks under contamination and gaming; red teaming and interpretability as evidence; audits, access and certification — ending in a workshop on building an auditable assurance case.
