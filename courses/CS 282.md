# CS 282: Computer Systems Architecture

Modern machines from the cache up: coherence and consistency, out-of-order cores, vectors and GPUs, then the hardware datacenters actually run on. The assignments move through three tools rather than one — an architecture simulator for design-space exploration, SystemVerilog for implementing processor optimizations, and a commercial model checker for formally verifying part of an open-source core.

## Materials

A reader gets the schedule and the grading scheme and nothing more. The course publishes everything else to Canvas, and its syllabus says so plainly: "All course materials linked from the website will redirect you to Canvas." The decks, the notes, the recordings, the assignments and their solutions are all behind that.

- **Syllabus**: the [course-info PDF](https://web.stanford.edu/class/ee282/sp26.ee282.syllabus.pdf) with the prerequisites, the outcomes and the grading, and the [schedule](https://web.stanford.edu/class/ee282/) with each lecture's readings and problem due dates. Spring 2025's own copy is on Canvas, Stanford-only.
- **Slides**, **Notes**: linked per lecture from the schedule, and every link goes to Canvas.
- **Videos**: every lecture and review session is recorded to Panopto and reached through Canvas; this is a CGOE course, so the recordings exist for paying remote students and for nobody else.
- **Assignments**: a 15-problem problem set and 3 programming assignments, on Canvas and Gradescope.
- **Solutions**: posted about a day after each deadline, on Canvas.
- **Exams**: an asynchronous online midterm on 5 May and an in-person final on 6 June; neither is published, and no practice paper is.
- **Projects**: none; the 3 programming assignments take their place.
- **Repo**: the assignments' simulator, SystemVerilog and formal-verification scaffolding goes through Canvas too.
- **Textbook**: Hennessy and Patterson's *Computer Architecture: A Quantitative Approach*, 7th edition, plus Morgan Claypool synthesis lectures through the library. Both required, neither free.

## Syllabus

Nineteen lectures, per the [schedule](https://web.stanford.edu/class/ee282/), with a Friday review session most weeks.

1. **Memory system** (1-7): multi-level caches and optimizations; prefetching; the coherence problem and coherence protocols; synchronization; memory consistency; main memory.
2. **Cores** (8-11): dynamic scheduling and branch prediction; speculation and superscalar issue; multithreading; vectors and SIMD.
3. **Specialized hardware** (12-14): GPUs; virtualization; AI processors.
4. **Systems at scale** (15-19): advanced I/O and networking; advanced memory management; non-volatile memory; datacenter hardware and management; microarchitectural attacks and defenses.

Grading is 60% final, 15% midterm, 15% programming assignments, 5% problem set, 5% attendance.

## Prerequisites

- Computer organization: [CS 180](CS%20180.md) (EE 180) or equivalent — pipelining, basic caching, assembly programming and virtual memory. The syllabus is blunt that arriving without it goes badly.
- Programming: C, C++ or similar.
- Operating systems ([CS 110](CS%20110.md) or [CS 240](CS%20240.md)-level) is useful but not required.
