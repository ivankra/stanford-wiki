# CS 498C: Introduction to CSCL: Computer-Supported Collaborative Learning

Roy Pea's seminar on a field he helped found. The definition the catalog gives is the course's organizing structure: collaboration mediated by a computational artefact, a triad of participant-artifact-participant, which is what separates CSCL from educational technology on one side and from group work on the other. The scope runs from two people at a task for ten minutes to a whole course cohort interacting digitally. The audience is primarily doctoral students in Learning Sciences and Technology Design and masters students in Learning Design and Technology — the CS code is the minority one here.

## Syllabus

No schedule or reading list is published. The catalog states the scope and audience:

> This seminar introduces students to foundational concepts and research on computer-supported collaborative learning (CSCL). It is designed for LSTD doctoral students, LDT masters' students, other GSE graduate students and advanced undergraduates inquiring about theory, research and design of CSCL. CSCL is defined as a triadic structure of collaboration mediated by a computational artefact (participant-artifact-participant). CSCL encompasses two individuals performing a task together in a short time, small or class-sized groups, and students following the same course, digitally interacting.

It meets once a week for three hours.
