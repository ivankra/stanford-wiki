# CS 103: Mathematical Foundations of Computing

The proofwriting course and the theory course in one: half a quarter of logic, sets, functions and graphs, then automata, Turing machines and complexity, with ten weekly problem sets carrying most of the grade.

## Materials

Every offering since Winter 2020 keeps its own archived site, so a reader wanting a finished quarter rather than this one's four lectures so far can take [Spring 2026](https://web.stanford.edu/class/archive/cs/cs103/cs103.1266/) whole: 28 decks, a summary per lecture, two dozen proofwriting guides, ten problem sets with starter projects. Only the recordings are gated, Panopto through Canvas with no public copy anywhere; solutions and practice exams sit under a `restricted/` path that redirects to Stanford login.

- **Syllabus**: [policies, grading and units](https://cs103.stanford.edu/syllabus); the dated schedule is a public [Google Sheet](https://docs.google.com/spreadsheets/d/1TmqX8wAUToDCSm3Szw0IZjemeiEzSRDn/edit). [Winter](https://web.stanford.edu/class/archive/cs/cs103/cs103.1214/), [Spring](https://web.stanford.edu/class/archive/cs/cs103/cs103.1216/) and [Summer 2021](https://web.stanford.edu/class/archive/cs/cs103/cs103.1218/) published one line apiece: "not accepting auditors this quarter due to everything being housed on Canvas".

- **Notes**: a written summary with every lecture and two dozen standing guides, from [Guide to Proofs](https://web.stanford.edu/class/archive/cs/cs103/cs103.1272/guide_to_proofs) and the [Proofwriting Checklist](https://web.stanford.edu/class/archive/cs/cs103/cs103.1272/proofwriting_checklist) to [Myhill-Nerode](https://web.stanford.edu/class/archive/cs/cs103/cs103.1272/guide_to_myhill_nerode). ## Syllabus

The [schedule](https://docs.google.com/spreadsheets/d/1TmqX8wAUToDCSm3Szw0IZjemeiEzSRDn/edit) runs ten weeks, one problem set a week:

1. **Weeks 1–4, proof and structure**: set theory, mathematical and indirect proofs, propositional logic, first-order logic, functions, set theory revisited, graphs.
2. **Week 5**: midterm, then mathematical induction.
3. **Weeks 6–7, languages**: finite automata, regular expressions, nonregular languages, context-free languages.
4. **Weeks 8–9, computability**: Turing machines, unsolvable problems.
5. **Week 10**: complexity theory and P versus NP.

Proofs must be typed, and are graded against the course's own proofwriting checklist as well as for correctness.

## Prerequisites

- Programming: [CS 106B](CS%20106B.md) or CS 106X, which may be taken **concurrently**. The C++ problems assume recursion at [CS 106B](CS%20106B.md)'s level, and are timed to follow it.
- Mathematics: high-school algebra only. Everything else is built up in the course.
