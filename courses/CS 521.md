# CS 521: Seminar on AI Safety

A one-unit Thursday talk series built around practitioners rather than papers: how OpenAI, Anthropic, Toyota Research, Torc Robotics and AWS actually handle alignment, verification and monitoring. Credit needs attendance at 7 of 9 talks and nothing else — no assignments, no project, no exam.

## Materials

A talk series that sets no coursework, so the schedule is the whole of its material — and it is public in full, with abstracts, speaker bios and the readings each speaker recommends. Nothing is recorded, for anyone: the course says the room cannot do it.

- **Syllabus**: the [2025 speaker schedule](https://cs521.stanford.edu/speaker-schedule-2025/) gives each date, speaker, affiliation, talk abstract, speaker bio and recommended readings — for the first talk, OpenAI's deliberative-alignment post and three hallucination surveys. The [home page](https://cs521.stanford.edu/) has the logistics and the attendance rule: 7 of 9 talks.
- **Slides**: speakers' decks are not posted, for any offering.
- **Notes**: none; nothing is written up beyond the abstracts.
- **Videos**: none. "No recording will be available since the course room is not outfitted to record." The one offering that was recorded is **Spring 2022**, whose [8 talks](https://www.youtube.com/playlist?list=PLoROMvodv4rNtnS3JSRRZzLWQo2dd6XNs) are on Stanford Online's channel.
- **Assignments**, **Solutions**, **Exams**, **Projects**, **Repo**: none. Credit is attendance and nothing else.
- **Older offerings**: the [2023](https://cs521.stanford.edu/speaker-schedule-2023/) and [2022](https://cs521.stanford.edu/speaker-schedule-2022/) schedules stay online beside the current one, each as complete as this year's.

## Syllabus

Nine talks, 3 April to 29 May ([schedule](https://cs521.stanford.edu/speaker-schedule-2025/)): alignment through reasoning in language models (Eddie Zhang, OpenAI); AI safety for Level 4 autonomous vehicles (Jerry Lopez, Torc Robotics); Eugenia Kim (Microsoft Research); Masha Itkina (Toyota Research Institute); Shannon Novak (Safe Space Alliance); Somil Bansal (Stanford); Dimitra Giannakopoulou (AWS); Mantas Mazeika (Center for AI Safety); and Nicholas Carlini (Anthropic). Across them the catalog's themes recur: verification and validation, reward misalignment and hacking, attack resilience, diagnosis and repair, and policy.
