# CS 521: Seminar on AI Safety

A one-unit Thursday talk series built around practitioners rather than papers: how OpenAI, Anthropic, Toyota Research, Torc Robotics and AWS actually handle alignment, verification and monitoring. Credit needs attendance at 7 of 9 talks and nothing else — no assignments, no project, no exam.

## Materials

The schedule with abstracts and readings is public; there is nothing else to publish.

- **Syllabus**: the [2025 speaker schedule](https://cs521.stanford.edu/speaker-schedule-2025/) gives each date, speaker, affiliation, talk abstract, speaker bio and recommended readings — for the first talk, OpenAI's deliberative-alignment post and three hallucination surveys. The [home page](https://cs521.stanford.edu/) has logistics and the attendance rule.
- **Videos**: [eight talks](https://www.youtube.com/playlist?list=PLoROMvodv4rNtnS3JSRRZzLWQo2dd6XNs) on Stanford Online's channel, uploaded April 2022, so the Spring 2022 offering. The Spring 2025 site's statement that the room is not equipped to record applies to that offering and is consistent with this — the recordings stopped after 2022.
- **Assignments, exams, projects**: none; the course is attendance-only.
- Earlier schedules stay online: [2023](https://cs521.stanford.edu/speaker-schedule-2023/) and [2022](https://cs521.stanford.edu/speaker-schedule-2022/).

## Syllabus

Nine talks, 3 April to 29 May ([schedule](https://cs521.stanford.edu/speaker-schedule-2025/)): alignment through reasoning in language models (Eddie Zhang, OpenAI); AI safety for Level 4 autonomous vehicles (Jerry Lopez, Torc Robotics); Eugenia Kim (Microsoft Research); Masha Itkina (Toyota Research Institute); Shannon Novak (Safe Space Alliance); Somil Bansal (Stanford); Dimitra Giannakopoulou (AWS); Mantas Mazeika (Center for AI Safety); and Nicholas Carlini (Anthropic). Across them the catalog's themes recur: verification and validation, reward misalignment and hacking, attack resilience, diagnosis and repair, and policy.
