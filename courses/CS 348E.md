# CS 348E: Character Animation: Modeling, Simulation, and Control of Human Motion

Moving a character believably, drawn from four fields at once — graphics for the rig and the keyframes, multi-body dynamics for the physics, biomechanics for muscles and human kinematics, and machine learning for the control policies. The catalog promises students will leave able to modify a physics simulator for animation or robotics, train locomotion and manipulation policies for virtual agents, and use motion capture to synthesize realistic humans. Three assignments and an open-ended research project.

## Syllabus

The [archived calendar](https://web.archive.org/web/20230202165444/https://tml.stanford.edu/cs348e-character-animation-modeling-simulation-and-control-human-motion) pairs each week's topic with the project it feeds. The five projects are the course:

1. **The Twister Game** (20%): "implement an inverse kinematics solver for a human figure by formulating and solving a multi-objective optimization problem."
2. **Rigid Body Sento** (16%): augment a rigid-body physics engine with a simple fluid model, to simulate articulated bodies in a swimming pool.
3. **Diving into the Deep End** (16%): keyframe a platform dive, then make a physically simulated character track it under PD feedback control.
4. **Rise Up!** (24%): a trajectory-optimization problem maximizing the height of a vertical jump off the diving board.
5. **Sink or Swim** (24%): deep reinforcement learning, training the character to swim back to the surface after the dive.

The catalog's topic list runs alongside: "numerical integration, 3D character modeling, keyframe animation, skinning/rigging, multi-body dynamics, human kinematics, muscle dynamics, trajectory optimization, learning policies for motor skills, and motion capture."
