# CS 245: Principles of Data-Intensive Systems

Zaharia's course on how a data system is actually built — the layers under a query, from disk layout and indexes through the optimizer to transactions and recovery. Its distinguishing feature as a public artefact is the exam archive: three past finals with full worked solutions, which almost no other course in this wiki publishes.

## Materials

The site is a frozen Winter 2021 offering and publishes essentially the whole course: the schedule, every deck, every assigned paper, the assignments, and — unusually — past exams with worked solutions.

- **Syllabus**: the [schedule](https://web.stanford.edu/class/cs245/#schedule), twenty dated sessions. Each reading row carries reading instructions and the question to answer — "Why did the storage system change from 'inversions' to B-trees during the project?", "How might the conclusions of this paper change if it ran on an NVMe SSD instead?"
- **Slides**: [eighteen numbered decks](https://web.stanford.edu/class/cs245/slides/), from the introduction through storage formats, query optimization, recovery, concurrency, distributed and cloud systems, streaming, and security and privacy, plus three guest talks — the lakehouse architecture, Delta Lake, and TensorFlow graph optimizations.
- **Notes**: the papers themselves, fourteen of them, served from the course host — System R, C-Store, Spark SQL, granularity of locks, Lamport's "Time, Clocks", Aurora, Dynamo, Delta Lake, the dataflow model, PINQ, the Netflix deanonymization paper — and Keshav's "How to Read a Paper" as optional week-one reading.
- **Assignments**: all three, as [PDFs](https://web.stanford.edu/class/cs245/assignments/assignment1.pdf).
- **Exams**: past finals and midterms from [Winter 2020](https://web.stanford.edu/class/cs245/exams/final_win20.pdf), Spring 2019 and Winter 2017.
- **Solutions**: every archived exam comes with its worked solutions — unusual, and the reason a self-studier would come here.
- **Videos**: not linked.

## Syllabus

No dated schedule is published. The catalog describes the scope: the principles behind systems that store and process data at scale, covering storage and indexing, query processing and optimization, transactions and recovery.
