# CS 281: Ethics of Artificial Intelligence

Ethics done with the tools of machine learning: define a fairness criterion, measure it, train a model that satisfies it, explain what the model did, and derive the limits of each technique. Case studies and two in-class socio-technical analyses sit alongside the maths, and a quarter-long project ends in a poster session.

## Syllabus

Ten weeks, per the [schedule](https://cs281.stanford.edu/syllabus.html), in three arcs with guest lectures between them.

1. **Fairness** (weeks 1-4): operationalized fairness criteria; calibration and fairness; learning group-fair models; fair models via constrained optimization; individual fairness; bias in NLP; fairness and causality.
2. **Explainability** (weeks 5-7): LIME; Shapley values and SHAP; saliency maps; exemplar-based, concept-based and counterfactual explanations.
3. **Privacy** (weeks 7-8): differential privacy, learning under it, and federated learning.
4. **Closing** (weeks 9-10): attacks on LLMs; citations for trust and verifiability; large language models and the ethics of AI.

Two sessions are socio-technical analyses rather than lectures, one on fairness and one on explainability, the latter run with Stanford's Ethics in Society toolkit. Guest lecturers were Bo Li, David Engstrom, Manuela Travaglianti and Chris Potts.
