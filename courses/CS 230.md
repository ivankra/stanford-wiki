# CS 230: Deep Learning

Stanford's wrapper around Andrew Ng's [Deep Learning Specialization](https://www.coursera.org/specializations/deep-learning) courses. The fundamentals, from logistic regression to CNNs, sequence models and transformers, come from 2 Coursera modules a week with autograded quizzes and notebooks. Stanford adds weekly lectures on extra topics, such as adversarial robustness, deep RL and LLM agents; a pen-and-paper midterm; and a TA-mentored team project worth nearly half the grade.

## Materials

Nearly all of Stanford's side of the course is public: lecture decks, TA section notes, project brief and starter code, past midterms with solutions, and every Autumn 2025 and Autumn 2018 lecture on YouTube. The Coursera half with quizzes and assignments is gated, though most of its videos are free on YouTube and unofficial copies circulate.

- **Syllabus**: the [home page](https://cs230.stanford.edu/) gives format, grading, late days and AI policy; this offering's dated schedule is a private Google Sheet. The [schedule page](https://cs230.stanford.edu/syllabus/) still shows Autumn 2025's; older ones are in the Internet Archive, e.g. [Autumn 2024](https://web.archive.org/web/20241113044952/https://cs230.stanford.edu/syllabus/).
- **Slides**: Autumn 2025's schedule links decks for 8 of its 9 meetings, and earlier folders such as [`fall_2024/`](https://cs230.stanford.edu/syllabus/fall_2024/) and [`winter2020/`](https://cs230.stanford.edu/winter2020/) hold up to 11 each. The 17 Coursera module decks are in [`files/`](https://cs230.stanford.edu/files/).
- **Notes**: 9 [TA section pages](https://cs230.stanford.edu/section/), from scoping a project and backpropagation to evaluation metrics and report writing, 4 with decks. 7 of their 11 Colab notebooks open without a sign-in.
- **Videos**: this offering's lectures go to Canvas. [Autumn 2025](https://www.youtube.com/playlist?list=PLoROMvodv4rNRRGdS0rBbXOUGA0wjdh1X) and [Autumn 2018](https://www.youtube.com/playlist?list=PLoROMvodv4rOABXSygHTsbvUz4G_YQhOb) were posted on YouTube. DeepLearning.AI's channel carries the videos for Courses [1](https://www.youtube.com/playlist?list=PLkDaE6sCZn6Ec-XTbcX1uRg2_u4xOEky0), [2](https://www.youtube.com/playlist?list=PLkDaE6sCZn6Hn0vK8co82zjQtt3T2Nkqc), [3](https://www.youtube.com/playlist?list=PLkDaE6sCZn6E7jZ9sN_xHwSHOdjUxUW_b) and [4](https://www.youtube.com/playlist?list=PLkDaE6sCZn6Gl29AoE31iwdVwSG-KnDzF), but only has 6 lectures of [Course 5](https://www.youtube.com/playlist?list=PLkDaE6sCZn6F6wUI9tvS_Gw1vaFAx6rd6).
- **Assignments**: the [project brief](https://cs230.stanford.edu/project/) is public: proposal, milestone, 5-page report and poster, with rubrics. Graded notebooks require [DeepLearning.AI](https://learn.deeplearning.ai/specializations/deep-learning/) membership ($25-30/mo), though many unofficial copies circulate on [GitHub](https://github.com/amanchadha/coursera-deep-learning-specialization).
- **Solutions**, **Exams**: the Winter 2020 and Autumn 2019 midterms are [posted](https://cs230.stanford.edu/section/6/) with solutions as practice, and the site's folders hold papers and solutions from Autumn 2018 to Autumn 2022. No official assignment solutions.
- **Projects**: the site calls the project the main focus of the class. The [past projects](https://cs230.stanford.edu/past-projects/) archive covers Winter 2018 to Autumn 2021.
- **Repo**: [project starter code](https://github.com/cs230-stanford/cs230-code-examples) for vision and NLP in PyTorch and TensorFlow, last updated 2023.
- **Extras**: [illustrated cheatsheets](https://stanford.edu/~shervine/teaching/cs-230/) on CNNs, RNNs and training tips by the Amidi twins, one a Winter 2019 TA; unofficial.

## Syllabus

From the [Autumn 2025 schedule](https://web.archive.org/web/20251208110119/https://cs230.stanford.edu/syllabus/), the latest public one, and its decks, grouped by the 5 Coursera courses:

1. **Neural networks and deep learning**: logistic regression, shallow and deep networks. Lectures: deep learning projects; case studies (trigger words, face verification with triplet loss, CLIP-style embeddings); the full cycle of a DL project.
2. **Improving deep neural networks**: initialization, regularization, gradient checking, optimization algorithms, hyperparameter tuning, batch normalization. Lecture: adversarial attacks and defenses, backdoors, data poisoning and prompt injection, then GANs and latent diffusion.
3. **Structuring ML projects**: ML strategy, with bird-recognition and autonomous-driving case studies. Lecture: deep RL, from DQN on Atari to the SFT and reward models of RLHF.
4. **Convolutional networks**: ResNets, transfer learning with MobileNet, YOLO detection, neural style transfer, face recognition, U-Net segmentation. Lecture: career advice, reading papers, AI and healthcare.
5. **Sequence models**: RNNs and LSTMs, word embeddings, sequence-to-sequence with attention, transformers. Lectures: building on LLMs with prompting and chain of thought, fine-tuning's pitfalls, RAG, agents with MCP, and evals; interpretability through saliency, occlusion, class activation maps and deconvolution, then how labs evaluate large models.

The Coursera notebooks use TensorFlow 2 from the quarter's fifth week on, as the specialization's April 2021 update left them; Stanford's section 5 compares it with PyTorch, and the project starter code comes in both.

## History

First offered in Autumn 2017, the course has kept one format throughout: the Coursera specialization for the fundamentals, a weekly in-class lecture from Andrew Ng and Kian Katanforoosh, and a team project.

- **Autumn 2017**: 10 advanced lectures that went deeper into the Coursera material, on the mathematics of backpropagation, regularization and initialization, object detection, deep RL, RNNs and attention, and beam search and CTC speech recognition, with assignments in TensorFlow. Its [site](https://cs230.stanford.edu/fall2017/) is still up, handouts included.
- **Winter 2018–Autumn 2019**: through 2018 the mathematics lectures gave way to practice: deep learning intuition and framing a problem, the full cycle of a project, adversarial examples and GANs, strategy case studies, interpretability, career advice, and an AI-in-healthcare talk by Pranav Rajpurkar that ran every term. A midterm came in with Winter 2018, and the [project archive](https://cs230.stanford.edu/past-projects/) starts there. [Autumn 2018's 10 lectures](https://www.youtube.com/playlist?list=PLoROMvodv4rOABXSygHTsbvUz4G_YQhOb) are public, and its midterm and those of Winter and Autumn 2019 sit with solutions in the site's [files](https://cs230.stanford.edu/files/).
- **Winter 2020–Spring 2023**: 2 or 3 offerings a year, with the same lecture set and new decks only now and then; the archive of project reports stops after Autumn 2021.
- **Autumn 2024–2026**: one offering a year, in Autumn. Autumn 2024 added stable diffusion, RAG and AI agents to the lectures. Autumn 2025's added backdoors, data poisoning and prompt injection, RLHF, and how labs evaluate large models, none of which Autumn 2024's decks cover, and were [posted on YouTube](https://www.youtube.com/playlist?list=PLoROMvodv4rNRRGdS0rBbXOUGA0wjdh1X).

## Prerequisites

- Programming in Python, enough to write a non-trivial program.
- Linear algebra, matrix and vector multiplication: MATH 51.
- Probability: [CS 109](CS%20109.md) or STATS 116, plus basic statistics; the course site asks for these, the catalog doesn't.
- Machine learning isn't required first: [CS 229](CS%20229.md) may be taken concurrently.

## Related

- [CS 129](CS%20129.md): machine learning beyond deep learning, in the same flipped format.
- [CS 221](CS%20221.md): AI beyond deep learning, from search and MDPs to games and Bayesian networks.
- [CS 229](CS%20229.md): the mathematical foundations of machine learning, from linear models through EM to reinforcement learning.
- [CS 231N](CS%20231N.md), [CS 224N](CS%20224N.md): one domain, vision or language, at a faster pace, with more focus on assignments than a project.
