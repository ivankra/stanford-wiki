# CS 168: The Modern Algorithmic Toolbox

Nineteen techniques in ten weeks, each landing in a mini-project on real data — a genomic dataset, a word co-occurrence matrix, the laurel/yanny clip, stock prices. Written notes rather than slides, and the whole set is public.

## Materials

Everything written is public — all 19 lectures' notes, all 9 mini-projects with their data and starter scripts, a practice final. The one thing withheld is the lectures themselves: "Lecture videos will be available via Canvas."

- **Syllabus**: the [site](https://web.stanford.edu/class/cs168/) lists all 19 lectures with dates and topics, every project's release and deadline, and the grading and collaboration policies.
- **Slides**: none — the course writes notes instead.
- **Notes**: an open [listing](https://web.stanford.edu/class/cs168/l/) of 25 PDFs covering all 19 lectures, from [consistent hashing](https://web.stanford.edu/class/cs168/l/l1.pdf) to differential privacy, with separate [regularization](https://web.stanford.edu/class/cs168/l/l6_reg.pdf), gradient-descent and stochastic-descent notes. The first 6 are co-written with Tim Roughgarden.
- **Videos**: Canvas only, and no public set turned up.
- **Assignments**: all [9 mini-projects](https://web.stanford.edu/class/cs168/p1.pdf) with their data files — a genomic dataset, a word co-occurrence matrix, the laurel/yanny clip, a list of national parks, stock prices — plus a LaTeX solution template.
- **Solutions**: none found, for the projects or the practice paper.
- **Exams**: a [practice final](https://web.stanford.edu/class/cs168/practice_final.pdf), unanswered; no real paper.
- **Projects**: none; the mini-projects are the coursework.
- **Repo**: per-project starter scripts beside the data — [`histogram.py`](https://web.stanford.edu/class/cs168/histogram.py), `plot_route.py`, and QWOP in both MATLAB and Python.

## Syllabus

From the [lecture list](https://web.stanford.edu/class/cs168/), in five arcs:

1. **Hashing and compression** (1–4): consistent hashing, property-preserving lossy compression, similarity search, the curse of dimensionality and the kissing number.
2. **Learning theory** (5–6): generalization — "how much data is enough?" — and regularization.
3. **Linear algebra** (7–12): understanding PCA, how PCA works, low-rank matrix approximations, tensor methods, graphs as matrices and the Laplacian, spectral techniques.
4. **Randomness** (13–14): reservoir sampling from a datastream, Markov chains and stationary distributions.
5. **Frontier** (15–19): Fourier methods over two lectures, online learning and multiplicative weights, compressive sensing and linear programming, and differential privacy.

## Prerequisites

- Discrete mathematics and probability: [CS 103](CS%20103.md) and [CS 109](CS%20109.md) or equivalents.
