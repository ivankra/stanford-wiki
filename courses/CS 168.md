# CS 168: The Modern Algorithmic Toolbox

Nineteen techniques in ten weeks, each landing in a mini-project on real data — a genomic dataset, a word co-occurrence matrix, the laurel/yanny clip, stock prices. Written notes rather than slides, and the whole set is public.

## Materials

One of the most completely published courses in the wiki: every lecture note, every mini-project with its data, and a practice final.

- **Syllabus**: the [site](https://web.stanford.edu/class/cs168/) lists all nineteen lectures with dates and topics, and every project's release and deadline.
- **Slides**: none — the course writes notes instead.
- **Notes**: [nineteen PDFs](https://web.stanford.edu/class/cs168/l/l1.pdf) under `/l/`.
- **Assignments**: [nine mini-projects](https://web.stanford.edu/class/cs168/p1.pdf) with their data files, plus a LaTeX solution template.
- **Exams**: a [practice final](https://web.stanford.edu/class/cs168/practice_final.pdf); no solutions, and no real paper.
- **Code**: starter scripts — `histogram.py`, `plot_route.py`, QWOP in MATLAB and Python.
- **Videos**, **Solutions**: not found. **Projects**: none beyond the mini-projects.

## Syllabus

From the [lecture list](https://web.stanford.edu/class/cs168/), in five arcs:

1. **Hashing and compression** (1–4): consistent hashing, property-preserving lossy compression, similarity search, the curse of dimensionality and the kissing number.
2. **Learning theory** (5–6): generalization — "how much data is enough?" — and regularization.
3. **Linear algebra** (7–12): understanding PCA, how PCA works, low-rank matrix approximations, tensor methods, graphs as matrices and the Laplacian, spectral techniques.
4. **Randomness** (13–14): reservoir sampling from a datastream, Markov chains and stationary distributions.
5. **Frontier** (15–19): Fourier methods over two lectures, online learning and multiplicative weights, compressive sensing and linear programming, and differential privacy.
