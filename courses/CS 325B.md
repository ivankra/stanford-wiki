# CS 325B: Data for Sustainable Development

Not a lecture course: one week of background, then 7 weeks where teams of 3 present their own project every Tuesday, then 2 weeks of writing. The problems are measurement problems, estimating building heights from Sentinel imagery to infer population and energy demand, scoring road quality, where the official statistics are missing and the satellite record isn't. Grading is explicit that accuracy isn't the target: "most projects will be risky and not guaranteed to work", so the marks go to method and communication. Admission is by application, capped at 24.

## Materials

A studio owes its schedule and its project briefs, and both are public, along with the format rules, the grading breakdown, both marking rubrics, 9 staff tutorials on the cloud toolchain and the sample code that goes with them. The student work is what isn't public.

- **Syllabus**: the [course site](https://ermongroup.github.io/cs325b/) carries learning goals, the 10-week schedule, the presentation rules (12 minutes, no open laptops, slides due 1pm) and the grading breakdown, with Google Doc rubrics for the [peer review](https://docs.google.com/a/stanford.edu/document/d/1JseJ5ta-31L-aJo2xOYwZESSObzNvJFbvAq5O8kiqMQ/edit?usp=sharing) and the [final paper](https://docs.google.com/document/d/1fJNiZQ2KCwRv1nzJkoAI-bXUvhlBVQqPXswgEb_xHj0/edit?usp=sharing) that both open to anyone.
- **Slides**: student decks go into one shared Google Slides deck whose link the TAs circulate each week. The only class decks on the site are the week-1 background lectures from Autumn 2017 and Winter 2018, too old to stand in for this offering.
- **Notes**: the [resources page](https://ermongroup.github.io/cs325b/resources.html) holds 9 staff-written tutorials, on setting up Compute Engine VMs, GDAL, Cloud Storage buckets, Git, computer clusters, TensorFlow, Tensorboard, a glossary of deep learning terms and an FAQ.
- **Videos**: no source mentions recording, and nothing turned up; the sessions are live presentations with laptops closed.
- **Assignments**: the [projects page](https://ermongroup.github.io/cs325b/projects.html) lists the quarter's 7 topics with their goal, data sources and outside collaborator, which is what a team picks from, and the schedule gives each weekly presentation its own brief.
- **Solutions**: none; the projects are open research and the rubrics mark method and writing.
- **Exams**: none.
- **Projects**: no final paper is published, though the site says accepted ones go to conferences with paid travel.
- **Repo**: 5 sample-code files on the class host, TensorFlow layers (a dilated layer for imagery whose bands differ in resolution among them), a VGG-F implementation from the Jean et al. poverty-mapping paper, a classification loss, a training loop and GDAL raster writing, plus a 2.4 MB TFRecords tutorial archive.

## Syllabus

[Three phases](https://ermongroup.github.io/cs325b/) over ten weeks.

1. Week 1: the SDGs, and an overview of the common geospatial datasets and tools.
2. Weeks 2-8: weekly group presentations, each with its own brief — a literature review and benchmark survey in week 2, data visualization in week 3, then simple models in week 4, including a human baseline where group members attempt the task by hand.
3. Weeks 9-10: a peer feedback session on a draft, then final presentations and the paper.
