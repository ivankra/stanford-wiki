# CS 64: Computation for Puzzles and Games

One unit, run once, and unusually candid about what it is for: "It is intended to be laid-back and fun, and I hope to get you (even more) excited about various aspects of CS theory and AI." Wednesdays are lectures; Fridays are "purely for fun -- I will bring various problems / puzzles to solve."

## Syllabus

From the [tentative schedule](https://web.stanford.edu/class/cs64/), each session pairing a deck with a reading list. Two examples:

- **28 September**: "How to win (and lose) at Scrabble", plus "Ghost" and tries. Links: the Quackle Scrabble AI, an IJCAI paper on inferring an opponent's tiles, an article arguing Scrabble AIs "still have a long way to go", and two cases of teams incentivised to lose — Olympic badminton disqualifications and Barbados 4–2 Grenada.
- **5 October**: "The obligatory chess day! Cheating at chess, since it's on everyone's mind right now. The Stockfish and Leela AIs."

Grading is by attendance at six of nine lectures, or "a small investigation / project in which you explore a topic of interest to you that pertains to puzzles/games… The main goal is to encourage you to explore something that you're passionate about."
