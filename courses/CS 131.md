# CS 131: Computer Vision: Foundations and Applications

The undergraduate entry to vision: two thirds of the quarter is classical — filters, features, epipolar geometry — before deep recognition arrives in week 6, and the whole grade rests on projects rather than exams.

## Syllabus

Four units on the [Spring 2026 syllabus](https://stanford-cs131.github.io/spring2026/syllabus.html):

1. **Low-level vision** (lectures 2–4): images and filters, edges and lines, local features and fitting. Recitations cover Python/NumPy and linear algebra.
2. **Geometry** (lectures 5–9): camera models and geometric primitives, calibration, single-view metrology, multi-view geometry in two parts.
3. **Visual pattern recognition** (lectures 11–15): machine learning for computer vision across four sessions, with a PyTorch recitation and an in-class project-idea clinic.
4. **Advanced topics** (lectures 16–17): motion, optical flow and video understanding, then a guest lecture by Koven Yu on physics-grounded world models.

Deliverables interleave: homework 0 in week 2, homework 1 and mini-project 1 in weeks 3–4, homework 2 and mini-project 2 through week 6, then proposal, milestone and Demo Day.

## Prerequisites

- Python with NumPy — every assignment is a notebook.
- Linear algebra: MATH 51. "matrix transpose, inverse, rotation, translation and other algebraic operations".
- Calculus: MATH 19 or 41, enough to differentiate and maximize.
- Probability: [CS 109](CS%20109.md) — Gaussians, mean, standard deviation.

Szeliski's *Computer Vision: Algorithms and Applications* is recommended, not required; the author's PDF is free.
