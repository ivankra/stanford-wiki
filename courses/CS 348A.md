# CS 348A: Computer Graphics: Geometric Modeling & Processing

The shape half of graphics, taught as mathematics: how curves and surfaces are represented, why polar forms make continuity constraints tractable, and how a scanned point cloud becomes a mesh. Guibas splits it between designed shapes — classical CAGD, Bézier, B-splines, NURBS — and acquired ones. The Winter 2021 description adds a section the older one lacks: VAEs and GANs for generating 3D geometry.

## Syllabus

The Winter 2017 [schedule](http://graphics.stanford.edu/courses/cs348a-17-winter/schedule.html) is the only session-level source; the catalog description for Winter 2021 adds the generative-models section at the end.

1. **Projective geometry** (Jan 9-18): homogeneous coordinates and the projective plane; oriented projective geometry; Euclidean, affine and projective transformations as matrices; rotations and quaternions.
2. **Differential geometry and geometry processing** (Jan 23-25): curvature and torsion; an introduction to geometry processing.
3. **Curves** (Jan 30-Feb 8): parametric and implicit shape models and the algebraic classification of parametric cubics; polar forms; derivatives and continuity constraints.
4. **Surfaces and meshes** (rest of the quarter): B-splines and the de Boor algorithm, rational curves, tensor-product and triangular patches, NURBS; subdivision surfaces and multi-resolution representations; triangle meshes and the quad-edge structure; volumetric representations; surface reconstruction from scattered points; mesh simplification and parametrization; scan alignment and shape matching.
5. **Generative models** (2020-21 only, per the catalog): parametric and implicit deep neural generative models for 3D geometry, VAEs and GANs.

Coursework is paper-and-pencil homework, a midterm and a programming project.
