# CS 368: Algorithmic Techniques for Big Data

What you can compute about a dataset you cannot store: how many distinct items a stream contained, which items were frequent, how far apart two points are after projecting into far fewer dimensions, whether a graph you only ever saw edge-by-edge is connected. The reading list runs from Morris's 1978 counting registers to HyperLogLog and modern graph sketching. Three homeworks and a project.

## Syllabus

[Three blocks](https://web.stanford.edu/class/cs368/lectures.html).

1. Sketching and streaming for basic statistics: distinct elements (Flajolet-Martin, HyperLogLog), heavy hitters, frequency moments, p-stable sketches, and the lower bounds that bound them.
2. Dimension reduction: the Johnson-Lindenstrauss lemma, plus lower bounds and impossibility results.
3. Graph stream algorithms: connectivity, cut and spectral sparsifiers, spanners, matching, graph sketching.
