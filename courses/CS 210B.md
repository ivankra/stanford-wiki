# CS 210B: Industry Innovation Lab

The spring half of CS 210. Teams of three to five students run as start-ups with a budget and an advisory board of staff and corporate liaisons, turning a challenge from a paying partner's R&D lab into a working product and demoing it at the June Software Fair. Teams typically travel to the partner's headquarters, sometimes abroad. Taught in an open loft classroom.

## Materials

The course publishes what teams build with and what they built, and nothing about how it is run: there is no syllabus or schedule in any year, and the grading rubric, the reading list and the deliverable templates sit in a private repository and behind a Google sign-in. What opens is the Build Guide, the onboarding assignment, 3 years of Software Fair guides and 3 teams' repositories.

- **Syllabus**: none, in any offering. The Build Guide's [class resources page](https://cs210.github.io/build-guide/docs/class-resources/) links the grading rubric, the reading list and the budget guidelines into the private `cs210/Home` repository, which 404s, and the deliverables contract, PRD template and ethics checklist as Google Docs that answer 401.
- **Slides**, **Videos**: nothing. The course runs as studio sessions and liaison meetings, and no deck or recording turned up for any offering.
- **Notes**: the [CS 210 Build Guide](https://cs210.github.io/build-guide/), the staff's class resources plus technical guides for web, ML, AR/VR, mobile and user research; its repository's last commit is February 2025.
- **Assignments**: [Assign0](https://github.com/cs210/Assign0), the GitHub onboarding assignment that "must be completed to be considered for acceptance into the class", in full. The partner's challenge brief is not published, and the templates each deliverable uses need a sign-in.
- **Solutions**, **Exams**: none; the deliverables are a product, a project wiki and a fair demo, and there is no exam.
- **Projects**: the [2026 Software Fair guide](https://cs210.github.io/SoftwareGuide-2026/) lists all 40-plus teams with genres, members, table numbers and descriptions, and a floor plan. [2025's](https://cs210.github.io/SoftwareGuide/) and [2023's](https://cs210.github.io/fair-guide/) are still served at their own paths; 2024's was overwritten.
- **Repo**: the [cs210 organization](https://github.com/cs210) has 11 public repositories. Team repositories are private by default, but 1 team per year got out: [Museum&](https://github.com/cs210/AAM-1) from this offering with its code and project wiki, [Pivot](https://github.com/cs210/Pivot) from Spring 2025 and [Kenja](https://github.com/cs210/kenja) from Spring 2024.

## Syllabus

No syllabus or schedule is public; this is the [catalog](../references/explorecourses/CS/2025-2026.xml) description and the [course site](https://web.stanford.edu/class/cs210/)'s account of the year.

1. Winter ([CS 210A](CS%20210A.md)): teams form, onboard onto GitHub, and meet their corporate liaison in late January or early February; discovery and concepting.
2. Late March: the team visits the partner's headquarters to present and take feedback.
3. Spring (CS 210B): an aggressive functional-prototype release cycle, with a project wiki documented alongside the product as a graded deliverable — "the intelligence of the design, not just the outcome".
4. June: a trade-show booth at the Software Fair, open to Computer Forum members.

The catalog names the exposure: software engineering practice, stimulating innovation, development with creative freedom, group work, and presenting and writing up technical work.

## Prerequisites

- [CS 210A](CS%20210A.md): this course is its continuation and the second half of one project. A two-quarter commitment, budgeted at 35% effort.
- Admission is by application, not coursework. The site carries a 2026 Student Application form, and Assign0 "must be completed to be considered for acceptance into the class".
