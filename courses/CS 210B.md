# CS 210B: Industry Innovation Lab

The spring half of CS 210. Teams of three to five students run as start-ups with a budget and an advisory board of staff and corporate liaisons, turning a challenge from a paying partner's R&D lab into a working product and demoing it at the June Software Fair. Teams typically travel to the partner's headquarters, sometimes abroad. Taught in an open loft classroom.

## Materials

The course publishes its build resources and its showcase, but nothing about how it is run or graded.

## Syllabus

No syllabus or schedule is public; this is the [catalog](../references/explorecourses-cs-grad-2025-2026.md) description and the [course site](https://web.stanford.edu/class/cs210/)'s account of the year.

1. Winter ([CS 210A](CS%20210A.md)): teams form, onboard onto GitHub, and meet their corporate liaison in late January or early February; discovery and concepting.
2. Late March: the team visits the partner's headquarters to present and take feedback.
3. Spring (CS 210B): an aggressive functional-prototype release cycle, with a project wiki documented alongside the product as a graded deliverable — "the intelligence of the design, not just the outcome".
4. June: a trade-show booth at the Software Fair, open to Computer Forum members.

The catalog names the exposure: software engineering practice, stimulating innovation, development with creative freedom, group work, and presenting and writing up technical work.

## Prerequisites

- [CS 210A](CS%20210A.md): this course is its continuation and the second half of one project. A two-quarter commitment, budgeted at 35% effort.
- Admission is by application, not coursework. The site carries a 2026 Student Application form, and Assign0 "must be completed to be considered for acceptance into the class".
