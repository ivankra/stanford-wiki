# CS 349G: Selected Reading of Ph.D. Dissertations

Five dissertations, two weeks each, and the person who wrote each one turns up to lecture about it. Winter 2021 read computer architecture from 1980 to 1992 through the theses of Mark Dean, Marc Hannah, Valerie Taylor, Kunle Olukotun and Timothy Pinkston, pairing every author's visit with a Stanford colleague — Horowitz on clocking, Hanrahan on graphics hardware, Kozyrakis on why multicore won — explaining what the field did with the idea afterwards. The whole grade is 4 two-page reports plus a fifth on a dissertation of the student's choosing.

## Syllabus

Ten weeks, per the [schedule](https://web.stanford.edu/class/cs349g/).

1. **Foundations** (Jan 11-13): logic, delay and timing; instructions, pipelining and caches — for students without an architecture course.
2. **Mark Dean** (Jan 20-Feb 8): how to read a dissertation, then Dean's work, then Dean himself on it. Report due Jan 22.
3. **Marc Hannah** (Jan 25-Feb 22): the Geometry Engine and graphics hardware, with Hanrahan on where graphics hardware went. Report due Feb 5.
4. **Valerie Taylor** (Feb 1-Mar 1): parallel performance analysis, with Horowitz on clocking today. Report due Feb 19.
5. **Kunle Olukotun** (Feb 3-Mar 8): speculation and chip multiprocessors, with Kjolstad on sparse systems and Kozyrakis on the billion-transistor era. Report due Mar 5.
6. **Timothy Pinkston** (Feb 24-Mar 18): interconnection networks, with Dally on interconnects today. Report due Mar 18.

## Prerequisites

- Undergraduates: [CS 110](CS%20110.md), computer systems.
- Graduate students: [CS 282](CS%20282.md) (EE 282), computer architecture.

Students self-classify as "inexperienced" ([CS 110](CS%20110.md) or EE 108 only) or "experienced" (EE 282 or equivalent); the inexperienced replace the first dissertation with background reading. The site says plainly that it does not recommend the course to anyone with none of the three.
