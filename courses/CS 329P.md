# CS 329P: Practical Machine Learning

Taught by Alex Smola with Qingqing Huang and Mu Li — the AWS and *Dive into Deep Learning* lineage — on the gap between training a model and solving a problem. Its opening move is that real data is "often not independently and identically distributed", and much of the course is about detecting and surviving covariate and concept shift.

## Materials

The syllabus and all 53 slide decks are public, on the instructors' own documentation host rather than anywhere at Stanford, and they cover every one of the 18 units; an instructor also posted lectures for 6 of those units to his own channel. The practice is where it thins out: 1 assignment of the 4 ships its files, and nothing else does.

- **Syllabus**: the [syllabus](https://c.d2l.ai/stanford-cs329p/syllabus.html), 18 units each a table of topics against slides and optional readings, plus the [landing page](https://c.d2l.ai/stanford-cs329p/) with the dated schedule, the grading split (midterm 10%, homework 40%, project 50%) and the exam and project formats.
- **Slides**: [53 PDFs](https://c.d2l.ai/stanford-cs329p/_static/pdfs/cs329p_slides_1_1.pdf), 1 to 4 per unit and at least 1 for every unit, plus a [Jupyter slide deck](https://c.d2l.ai/stanford-cs329p/_static/notebooks/cs329p_notebook_eda.slides.html) for the exploratory-data-analysis segment.
- **Notes**: none of the course's own; the syllabus's fourth column cites optional readings instead: *Challenges in Deploying Machine Learning*, *Data Collection for Machine Learning*, and chapters of *Dive into Deep Learning*.
- **Videos**: [17 lectures](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=yZStH6jlkAA) on Alex Smola's [channel](https://www.youtube.com/@smolix), 2 to 4 parts each, covering 6 of the 18 units: covariate shift, label shift, data beyond i.i.d., fairness and explainability. The logistics deck promised "Videos on Youtube in 1-2 weeks after class"; the other 12 units never appeared. A reader's [playlist](https://www.youtube.com/playlist?list=PLuQUxYHEVjGmekUNx8OySHpD9-x5GJt0N) puts the 17 in order.
- **Assignments**: [assignment 1](https://c.d2l.ai/stanford-cs329p/assignments.html) ships its notebook and a 131 MB house-sales dataset ("collect and process data, train models to predict on the test dataset… we will provide the test dataset, and an automl based baseline"). Assignments 2 and 3 are one sentence each with no file, and the landing page says there were 4.
- **Solutions**: none published, for the assignments or the midterm.
- **Exams**: a midterm on 10/27 worth 10%, described on the landing page (theory questions, spotting mistakes in code, modeling challenges) and never published.
- **Projects**: a team project worth half the grade, presented twice and written up in ICML style; no student work is published.
- **Repo**: none; assignment 1's notebook is the only code.
- **Textbook**: none required. The optional readings include 4 chapters of the instructors' own [*Dive into Deep Learning*](https://d2l.ai/), free on the same host.

## Syllabus

The [syllabus](https://c.d2l.ai/stanford-cs329p/syllabus.html) runs eighteen units in three parts:

1. **Basic ML modeling** (units 1–7): data acquisition, web scraping, labeling and exploratory analysis; cleaning, transformation, feature engineering; a recap of trees, linear methods and neural networks; evaluation metrics, over- and underfitting, validation; bias and variance, bagging, boosting, stacking.
2. **Broken assumptions** (units 8–11): generalization and covariate shift, twice over, with adversarial data and invariants; label shift and two-sample tests; data beyond i.i.d. — time series and graphs.
3. **Making it work** (units 12–18): model tuning and deep-network tuning, transfer learning, model compression, multimodal data, fairness and explainability.
