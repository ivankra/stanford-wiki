# CS 329P: Practical Machine Learning

Taught by Alex Smola with Qingqing Huang and Mu Li — the AWS and *Dive into Deep Learning* lineage — on the gap between training a model and solving a problem. Its opening move is that real data is "often not independently and identically distributed", and much of the course is about detecting and surviving covariate and concept shift.

## Materials

The whole course is public, on the instructors' own documentation host rather than anywhere at Stanford: syllabus, fifty-odd slide decks, the first assignment with its dataset, and the full lecture recordings.

- **Syllabus**: the [syllabus](https://c.d2l.ai/stanford-cs329p/syllabus.html) — eighteen units, each a table of topics against slides and optional readings — plus the [landing page](https://c.d2l.ai/stanford-cs329p/) with the dated schedule and the grading split (midterm 10%, homework 40%, project 50%).
- **Slides**: [about fifty PDFs](https://c.d2l.ai/stanford-cs329p/_static/pdfs/cs329p_slides_1_1.pdf), one per topic rather than per lecture, with exploratory data analysis and a few other units delivered as Jupyter slide decks.
- **Notes**: the optional readings beside each topic — "Challenges in Deploying Machine Learning", "Data Collection for Machine Learning", chapters of the instructors' own *Dive into Deep Learning*.
- **Videos**: [the full quarter](https://www.youtube.com/playlist?list=PLuQUxYHEVjGmekUNx8OySHpD9-x5GJt0N) on YouTube. The site explains the delay and the empty room: recordings went up one to two weeks after class, filmed without students for FERPA.
- **Assignments**: [three](https://c.d2l.ai/stanford-cs329p/assignments.html), the first with its notebook and a 131 MB house-sales dataset — "collect and process data, train models to predict on the test dataset… we will provide the test dataset, and an automl based baseline". The second and third are described in one sentence each and ship nothing.
- **Exams**: a midterm, not posted. **Projects**: a team project worth half the grade, written up in ICML style; not published.

## Syllabus

The [syllabus](https://c.d2l.ai/stanford-cs329p/syllabus.html) runs eighteen units in three parts:

1. **Basic ML modeling** (units 1–7): data acquisition, web scraping, labeling and exploratory analysis; cleaning, transformation, feature engineering; a recap of trees, linear methods and neural networks; evaluation metrics, over- and underfitting, validation; bias and variance, bagging, boosting, stacking.
2. **Broken assumptions** (units 8–11): generalization and covariate shift, twice over, with adversarial data and invariants; label shift and two-sample tests; data beyond i.i.d. — time series and graphs.
3. **Making it work** (units 12–18): model tuning and deep-network tuning, transfer learning, model compression, multimodal data, fairness and explainability.
