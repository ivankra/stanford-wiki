# CS 231N: Deep Learning for Computer Vision

Ten weeks on neural-network methods for visual recognition, built around implementing, training and debugging the models rather than calling them. Three assignments, an in-class midterm and a group project.

## Materials

Everything a self-studier needs is public: schedule, slides, notes, assignments, starter code and last year's recordings.

- **Syllabus**: the [schedule](https://cs231n.stanford.edu/schedule.html), with topics, deadlines and per-lecture readings, all public papers or blog posts.
- **Slides**: on the schedule, lectures 1–16 plus three sections; lectures 17 and 18 have none.
- **Notes**: [cs231n.github.io](https://cs231n.github.io/), module notes on classification, optimization, backpropagation and CNNs.

## Syllabus

Eighteen lectures in three arcs, per the [schedule](https://cs231n.stanford.edu/schedule.html).

1. **Deep learning basics** (1–4): kNN and linear classifiers, softmax loss; regularization, SGD, momentum, Adam; backpropagation.
2. **Perceiving and understanding the visual world** (5–11): convolution and pooling; AlexNet, VGG, ResNet, batch norm, transfer learning; RNNs and captioning; self-attention and transformers; detection, segmentation and adversarial examples; video understanding; distributed training.
3. **Generative and interactive visual intelligence** (12–18): self-supervised learning; VAEs, GANs, autoregressive models, diffusion; 3D shape and neural implicit representations; vision and language; world modeling; human-centered AI.

Friday sections review Python/NumPy, backprop, PyTorch and transformers.

## Prerequisites

- Programming: Python and NumPy; experience in C/C++, MATLAB or JavaScript transfers.
- Calculus and linear algebra: derivatives and matrix-vector notation (MATH 19, MATH 51).
- Probability and statistics: distributions, mean, standard deviation ([CS 109](CS%20109.md) or equivalent).
