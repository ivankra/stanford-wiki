# CS 231N: Deep Learning for Computer Vision

10 weeks on neural-network methods for visual recognition, built around implementing, training and debugging the models rather than calling them, with 3 assignments, an in-class midterm and a group project. Its notes and every offering's site since Winter 2015 are public, and 3 offerings' lectures are on YouTube.

## Materials

A reader gets the whole course bar the midterm: the schedule, 16 of the 18 decks, the module notes, all 3 assignments with starter code, and Spring 2025's recordings, which track this offering lecture for lecture. Every offering back to the first, Winter 2015, keeps its own site at its year path.

- **Syllabus**: the [schedule](https://cs231n.stanford.edu/schedule.html), with topics, deadlines and per-lecture readings, all public. Older offerings keep theirs, [Winter 2015's](https://cs231n.stanford.edu/2015/syllabus.html) through [Spring 2025's](https://cs231n.stanford.edu/2025/schedule.html).
- **Slides**: [lectures 1–16](https://cs231n.stanford.edu/slides/2026/lecture_2.pdf) plus 3 discussion sections, linked from the schedule; lectures 17 (a guest lecture on world modeling) and 18 have none.
- **Notes**: [cs231n.github.io](https://cs231n.github.io/), the module notes on image classification, linear classifiers, optimization, backpropagation and CNN architectures, shared across offerings. Winter 2016's schedule credits Andrej Karpathy for them.
- **Videos**: this offering records to Canvas, and the homepage warns that the public set doesn't reflect this offering. Stanford Online's [Spring 2025 playlist](https://www.youtube.com/playlist?list=PLoROMvodv4rOmsNzYBMe0gJY2XS8AQg16) holds all 18 of that offering's lectures, differing from this schedule only at lecture 17. Older public sets: [Winter 2016's](https://www.youtube.com/playlist?list=PLkt2uSq6rBVctENoVBg1TpCC7OQi31AlC) 15 lectures on Karpathy's channel and [Spring 2017's](https://www.youtube.com/playlist?list=PL3FW7Lu3i5JvHM8ljYj-zLfQRF3EO8sYv) 16.
- **Assignments**: handouts [1](https://cs231n.github.io/assignments2026/assignment1/), [2](https://cs231n.github.io/assignments2026/assignment2/) and [3](https://cs231n.github.io/assignments2026/assignment3/) with Colab notebooks, and the [project spec](https://cs231n.stanford.edu/project.html). - **Solutions**: none published; the assignments page says past solutions circulate online and asks students not to use them.
- **Exams**: the in-class midterm is never released. [Spring 2025's review handout](https://cs231n.stanford.edu/slides/2025/section_6.pdf) has 2 worked midterm-style problems, on self-attention and backprop.

## Syllabus

18 lectures in 3 arcs, per the [schedule](https://cs231n.stanford.edu/schedule.html) and decks.

1. **Deep learning basics** (1–4): kNN and linear classifiers, softmax loss; regularization, SGD, momentum, Adam and AdamW, learning-rate schedules; backpropagation through vectors and matrices.
2. **Perceiving and understanding the visual world** (5–11): convolution and pooling; normalization, dropout, initialization, augmentation, VGG and ResNet, transfer learning; RNNs, LSTMs and captioning; attention, transformers and ViT, with RMSNorm, SwiGLU and mixture of experts; segmentation with U-Net, detection from R-CNN to YOLO and DETR, Mask R-CNN; saliency maps and Grad-CAM; video from 3D CNNs to video transformers; distributed training (FSDP and pipeline, tensor and context parallelism), worked through Llama 3-405B.
3. **Generative and interactive visual intelligence** (12–18): self-supervised learning from pretext tasks through MAE, SimCLR, MoCo and DINOv2; autoregressive models and VAEs; GANs, then diffusion as rectified flow, with classifier-free guidance, latent diffusion, diffusion transformers and video diffusion; 3D as depth, voxels, point clouds (PointNet), meshes, NeRF and Gaussian splatting; CLIP, SigLIP and CoCa, then LLaVA, Flamingo and Qwen3-VL; world modeling; human-centered AI.

Sections review Python/NumPy, backprop, PyTorch and transformers. Assignment 3 ends with SimCLR, a text-conditioned DDPM, and pretrained CLIP and DINO.

## History

First offered in Winter 2015, the course has kept its opening (kNN, linear classifiers, backprop by hand) and its module notes; the lectures after CNN architectures change almost every year.

- **Winter 2015–2016**: Fei-Fei Li and Andrej Karpathy, joined by Justin Johnson in 2016, as Convolutional Neural Networks for Visual Recognition. The 2016 schedule credits Karpathy with the notes and lectures, and Johnson with the assignments and lectures. The 2016 run added a full lecture on RNNs and LSTMs for image captioning, with an assignment that captions COCO images. Its 15 lectures survive on [Karpathy's playlist](https://www.youtube.com/playlist?list=PLkt2uSq6rBVctENoVBg1TpCC7OQi31AlC), though the site took its own links down.
- **Spring 2017–2019**: Li, Johnson and Serena Yeung. Generative models (PixelRNN, VAEs, GANs) and deep RL came in, PyTorch joined the frameworks, and the third assignment added style transfer and a GAN. Karpathy returned for a guest lecture in 2018. Stanford posted 2017's 16 lectures [publicly](https://www.youtube.com/playlist?list=PL3FW7Lu3i5JvHM8ljYj-zLfQRF3EO8sYv).
- **Spring 2020–2021**: scene graphs and guest lectures on fairness and 3D deep learning in 2020; in 2021, the first lecture on attention and transformers, one on self-supervised learning, and Jon Barron on neural radiance fields; Assignment 3 added Transformer captioning and SimCLR and dropped style transfer. That June the notes site gained pages on RNNs, attention, transformers, generative models and NeRF, though its index, still Karpathy's 2015 modules, links only the RNN one.
- **Spring 2022**: renamed Deep Learning for Computer Vision; 3D vision got its own lecture.
- **Spring 2023–2026**: diffusion arrived in 2023 but got only a few slides after VAEs and GANs in 2024, when OpenAI's Bill Peebles and Tim Brooks lectured on Sora and Ehsan Adeli began co-teaching. 2025 gave diffusion a lecture of its own, taught as rectified flow with latent diffusion and diffusion transformers, added lectures on distributed training and on vision and language, and folded visualizing and understanding into the detection lecture, a year after it left the assignments. Assignment 3 swapped GANs for a DDPM that draws emoji from text prompts and for CLIP and DINO, and Stanford Online posted all 2025 lectures [publicly](https://www.youtube.com/playlist?list=PLoROMvodv4rOmsNzYBMe0gJY2XS8AQg16). 2026 swapped robot learning for world modeling.

## Prerequisites

- Programming: Python and NumPy; experience in C/C++, MATLAB or JavaScript transfers.
- Calculus and linear algebra: derivatives and matrix-vector notation (MATH 19, MATH 51).
- Probability and statistics: distributions, mean, standard deviation ([CS 109](CS%20109.md) or equivalent).
