# CS 22A: The Social & Economic Impact of Artificial Intelligence

One unit, one hour a week, no programming, and pre-approved for credit at the Law School and the GSB. Alongside the large questions it does something unusual for a lecture course: it explicitly sets out to demystify the vocabulary — "transformers", "prompt engineering", "chain of thought", "hallucination", "jailbreaking", "context rot", "deepfakes", "stochastic parrots", "algorithmic bias".

## Materials

## Syllabus

No published syllabus. The catalog gives the questions the course is organized around: "How will AI reshape labor markets and jobs? What roles will it play in medical research, healthcare, education, entertainment, engineering, transportation, and the legal system? How will it be used - or misused - in democratic societies and autocratic regimes? Will it alter the geopolitical balance of power and the nature of warfare? How can we align these powerful systems with human values? Can machines genuinely create, or possess consciousness? Does the prospect of superintelligence mean the human race is doomed? Will the benefits of this technological revolution be broadly distributed, or accrue to a lucky few? Are we currently experiencing an 'AI bubble'?"

It describes itself as offering "a non-technical exploration of its foundational principles, inherent strengths and limitations, and a comprehensive overview of its profound impact on our society and economy".

## Prerequisites

None. "No programming or prior technical knowledge is required." GSB students must enrol under SYMSYS 122 or INTLPOL 200 for GSB credit.
