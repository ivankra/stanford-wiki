# CS 339H: Human-Computer Interaction and AI/ML

Norvig and Russell on the half of AI that isn't the model: what users believe a system is doing, where those beliefs come from, and how a cryptic depiction of the mechanism leads to disappointed people. Roughly half the sessions bring in a guest working on exactly that — Ed Chi on recommendation engines, Carrie Cai on whether explaining an algorithm changes how people experience it, Merrie Morris on accessibility, Been Kim on communicating with machines. Weekly assignments and an in-class team presentation as the final.

## Syllabus

[10 weeks, 2 sessions each](https://docs.google.com/document/d/1-dJuja_zLHNYFHdjTsFSXMR7IVfiPFZH82m1QHc1HXc/edit).

1. Week 1: what the course is, and initial definitions — "AI, ML, DL, HCAI. What's the difference?"
2. Week 2: recommendation engines with Ed Chi, both the key issues and the engineering.
3. Weeks 3-4: instructor-led sessions on mental and user models.
4. Week 5: interpreting and explaining AI algorithms, with Carrie Cai.
5. Weeks 6-7: further instructor sessions and a guest lecture.
6. Week 8: accessibility as a north-star challenge for human-centered AI, with Merrie Morris.
7. Weeks 9-10: ethics and fairness, then Been Kim on "Hopes and dreams of communicating with machines".
