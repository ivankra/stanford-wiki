# CS 206: Exploring Computational Journalism

A studio run jointly out of the Journalism Program and computer science, meeting once a week for three hours on Tuesday mornings. Interdisciplinary teams take on a problem facing newsrooms — discovering stories in data, presenting them, understanding an audience, defending free speech — and build something. The staff is three lecturers and a professor rather than a single instructor, and enrollment is by application. The course is repeatable for credit, with priority to first-time students.

## Syllabus

No schedule is published. The catalog describes the scope:

> This project-based course will explore the field of computational journalism, including the use of Data Science, Info Visualization, AI, and emerging technologies to help journalists discover and tell stories, understand their audience, advance free speech, and build trust.

The site's About page adds the working definition the course uses — "the combination of algorithms, data, and knowledge from the social sciences to supplement the accountability function of journalism" — and a second dimension, "changing how stories are discovered, presented, aggregated, monetized, and archived". It distinguishes computational journalism from computer-assisted reporting by scale and by algorithmic sophistication, with recent work centring on "reporting by algorithms, about algorithms, and through algorithms".

## Prerequisites

None stated. Enrollment is by application, open to students from any academic discipline through the end of the first week; teams are meant to be interdisciplinary, so no specific technical background is assumed.
