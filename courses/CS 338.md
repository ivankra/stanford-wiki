# CS 338: Aligning Superintelligence

Takes seriously the premise that an AI smarter than humans in practically every domain may arrive within a couple of decades, and asks a technical audience three questions about it: why such a system might diverge from its designer's intent, whether that divergence is a catastrophic risk, and what has been proposed to stop it. Roughly two-thirds theory, one-third empirical, with guest lectures from alignment researchers.

## Syllabus

The [course page](https://web.stanford.edu/class/msande338/) is the only public statement of content. It organizes the course around three questions: why a superintelligence may become misaligned with its designer's intent; whether misalignment might pose a catastrophic risk; and what solutions have been proposed. The page presses on the obvious objection — "if the AI were to misbehave, wouldn't the designer just modify it or shut it down?" — and on the disagreement about whether these risks rank with pandemics and nuclear war or are overhyped.

It is equally explicit about what it leaves out: misuse by ill-intentioned humans ("misalignment between humans, rather than between a human and an AI"), aggregating conflicting human preferences, bias in AI products, post-superintelligence governance and redistribution, misinformation and provenance, and the moral status of a future superintelligence. The subject is a future superintelligence, not today's systems.

## Prerequisites

Recommended, not required:

- One graduate-level machine learning course.
- One course that models decision making — AI, reinforcement learning, decision analysis or economics.

The emphasis is on formalizing ideas, so the background is needed for the theory.
