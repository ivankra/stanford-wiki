# CS 52: CS + Social Good Studio: Implementing Social Good Projects

The build-and-ship half of the studio. Teams arrive with a tested MVP, a defined target user and a community partner, and spend the quarter making it something that can keep running after the quarter ends.

## Materials

Nothing is public. The course's own address redirects to the [CS 51](CS%2051.md) site, which is a JavaScript shell: the only course-facing text in its bundle is a notice from 2019.

- **Syllabus**: closed. Spring 2026 uploaded a syllabus to Canvas, marked Stanford-only.
- **Assignments**, **Exams**, **Videos**: not found.
- **Projects**: the quarter "culminates in a showcase where students share their projects with stakeholders and the public", and nothing from a recent one is posted — but the [`cs52-stanford`](https://github.com/cs52-stanford) organization still holds seven partner-project repos from 2019 to 2021: `thehive20`, "a React based web application created in partnership with UNHCR", alongside `freefrom`, `khanacademy`, `google`, `CZI`, `accufact` and `UNHCR`. Five to seven years old, so none of it carries a rating.
- **Older syllabus**: that organization's [`Home`](https://github.com/cs52-stanford/Home) repo is headed "The source of truth for all things CS52", and its README is the **Spring 2020 syllabus** in full — how the quarter ran on Zoom, class times, what an SGM is, the attendance policy, team GitHub Pages, Piazza, office hours and Slack. Also six years old.
- **Extra**: one unlinked PNG on the [CS 51](CS%2051.md) server, [`2020-course-info.png`](https://web.stanford.edu/class/cs51/2020-course-info.png), describes both quarters — Studio is "a 2 quarter class where students work with a partner organization to design… and implement… a solution to a social challenge" — and says what the spring showcase expects: "a fully functional implementation of their solution". Almost seven years old, so it carries no rating.

## Syllabus

No published syllabus. The catalog gives the shape: "Teams enter the quarter having completed and tested a MVP with a well-defined target user, and a community partner. Students will learn to apply scalable technical frameworks, tools for deployment, and user testing techniques. The purpose of the class is to facilitate students to build a sustainable infrastructure around their product idea. Main class components are workshops, team collaboration, and software development."
