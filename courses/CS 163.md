# CS 163: The Practice of Theory Research

Reingold's answer to the students who have the coursework and no idea how research starts. The subject is the practice rather than any body of knowledge: read a paper critically, search the bibliography around it, propose a direction, work out which technical areas it needs, and produce and communicate an insight. Everyone runs a limited-scope project, and the class is small.

## Materials

No Stanford host serves anything under either of the course's numbers, but the instructor keeps the whole course on his own page: the schedule, every deliverable with its deadline, and the reading list. Nothing on it goes through a login, because there is nothing else.

## Syllabus

The [Winter 2022 schedule](https://omereingold.wordpress.com/cs-163-the-practice-of-theory-research/), twenty sessions, is a research project run in slow motion with the method taught alongside it:

1. **Weeks 1–3**: introduction and group forming; two research-driven paper-reading exercises; collaborations and creativity; first paper presentations, each preceded by a written review.
2. **Weeks 3–5**: "The characteristics and methodology of ToC" and a session on giving good talks, while groups present first ideas for further research and the surrounding literature; a collaboration contract is due.
3. **Weeks 5–7**: bibliography search work, a second paper per group presented alongside a session on ethics, then progress reports aimed at a research proposal.
4. **Weeks 8–10**: deeper progress reports, dry runs of the final presentation, peer review of each other's drafts, and final presentations to an external audience.

The instructor's framing: "A human-centric perspective on research: how to facilitate better research by addressing the conditions needed for an individual researcher and groups of researchers to succeed" — and "an opportunity to openly discuss what is usually only implicit and silenced."
