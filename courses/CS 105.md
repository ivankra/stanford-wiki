# CS 105: Introduction to Computers

The course for students who will make decisions about computing without writing it for a living: hardware, networks, security and privacy on one side, and a quarter spent actually building and styling websites on the other. Programming arrives in week 6 and stays light.

## Materials

## Syllabus

From the [published schedule](https://web.stanford.edu/class/cs105/schedule.html):

1. **Weeks 1–2, how machines and networks work**: hardware and the anatomy of a laptop, bits and binary, digital images, digital sound, networks and protocols.
2. **Weeks 3–5, the web**: HTML, CSS, images and linking, tables and colors, page layout worked through real newspaper front pages, responsive design, forms, and HCI-flavoured website design.
3. **Week 6–7, programming**: after the midterm, conditionals, lists and loops, functions and objects.
4. **Week 8**: programming on the web, CPUs and machine language, operating systems.
5. **Weeks 9–10, security and privacy**: mechanisms, threats and attacks, defensive measures, then privacy.

## Prerequisites

- "Minimal math skills", per the catalog. No programming background.
- The catalog steers students in technical fields, or anyone wanting real programming skills, to [CS 106A](CS%20106A.md) instead; students with CS 106-level experience need the instructor's consent.
