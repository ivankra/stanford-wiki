# CS 292: AI and Data for Science

A weekly research seminar where researchers, mostly from Stanford, talk about AI models and scientific datasets, from biology and medicine to neuroscience and social behavior. Many speakers use Stanford's Marlowe GPU cluster. Autumn 2026 is its first quarter. Talks are in person only and open to everyone, including people outside Stanford. It's organized by Emmanuel Candès, Balasubramanian Narasimhan, Brian Wandell and Gordon Wetzstein.

## Materials

A seminar's material is its speaker list, and this one's is fully public, abstract by abstract; the seminar is also open to anyone who walks in, Stanford or not. Nothing is withheld because nothing else is produced: the talks aren't recorded for anybody, the speakers' decks aren't collected, and the single requirement for credit is a write-up the staff hasn't specified yet.

- **Syllabus**: the [schedule](https://aida4sci.stanford.edu/schedule.html) gives all 10 Wednesday talks with speaker, affiliation, room, title and a paragraph-long abstract, and flags the 2 weeks that move to Packard 101. The [home page](https://aida4sci.stanford.edu/) adds the logistics and the enrollment rule; the [archive](https://aida4sci.stanford.edu/past.html) is empty, waiting for this inaugural quarter to end.
- **Slides**: none posted, and nothing says any will be.
- **Notes**: none; no talk is written up beyond its abstract.
- **Videos**: none. Talks are "in person only — there is no remote option", with no recording offered.
- **Assignments**: enrolled students owe "a short write-up on one of the seminar topics, chosen by the student", and "Details will follow"; through week 2 they hadn't.
- **Solutions**, **Exams**, **Projects**, **Repo**: none. The write-up is the whole of the coursework, graded credit/no-credit.
- **Self-study**: the [mailing list](https://aida4sci.stanford.edu/subscribe.html) is the seminar's standing channel and is open to everyone — "Anyone may join. You do not need a Stanford affiliation, and neither does attending." It sends the coming week's speaker, title and abstract every Sunday with a calendar file, and handles its own unsubscribes.

## Syllabus

Ten Wednesday talks, September 23 to December 2:

1. Baccus: interpretable CNN models of retina and early visual cortex
2. Wetzstein: from generative video world models to world agents
3. Gevaert: multimodal modeling and imputation for precision medicine
4. Langlotz: training methods for a medical imaging foundation model
5. Kundaje: decoding regulatory DNA with deep learning
6. Yamins: large-scale models at the neuroscience-AI intersection
7. Li: multimodal foundation models for precision medicine
8. Pentland: detecting coordinated behavior across encrypted domains
9. Tolias: title TBA
10. Guibas: title TBA
