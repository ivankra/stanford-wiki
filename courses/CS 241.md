# CS 241: Embedded Systems Workshop

Teams of students engineer FLIGHT, an interactive light sculpture of 76 programmable "Fractal Flyers" for the Packard stairwell, built across offerings since 2019. The emphasis is engineering parts robust enough to hang for years. Enrollment is capped at 25, selected by a first-day questionnaire. CS 241 and EE 285 students do more design and tradeoff analysis than those in EE 185.

## Materials

The installation is the course and the installation is a public repository, so an outside reader gets what the teams get: the design document, the construction manual, this quarter's assignments, the whole firmware and GUI codebase, and every past team's charters, plans and reports. ## Syllabus

Content follows the project. Autumn 2026 has five teams:

1. Body shell gasket: CAD, acrylic, laser cutting and mechanical tolerances
2. LED scepter: even lighting, diffusion, cable and connector strength
3. Firmware: making the Feather M4 embedded-Python firmware run for years; sensor drivers, interrupts
4. FlightGUI: controlling any number and arrangement of Flyers reliably, end to end
5. Signaling: USB over an Ethernet twisted pair, from enumeration and framing to signal integrity

Tuesdays open with 5-minute team progress talks, Thursdays with a 30-minute deep dive into one FLIGHT component; the rest is team work.

## Prerequisites

The catalog lists none. The signaling team needs some students with signals, communication and circuits background (e.g. EE 102B, EE 114, EE 133).
