# CS 106M: Enrichment Adventures in Programming Abstractions

Twenty [CS 106B](CS%20106B.md) students in a room once a week, taking the week's topic further than the main course can: discussion, hands-on activities and collaborative coding, with the excursions chosen partly by the students.

## Materials

This offering's site is one page, with the format and the credit rule but no topics; what the seminar actually does is readable only in Stanford's class archive, which keeps 4 older offerings' meeting lists. The prep tasks themselves are on Ed.

- **Syllabus**: the [site](https://cs106m.stanford.edu/) gives the format, the 20-student cap and what credit requires, and no schedule — the excursions are chosen with the cohort in week 1. [Autumn 2025](https://web.stanford.edu/class/archive/cs/cs106m/cs106m.1252/) and 3 earlier archived offerings publish theirs in full.
- **Slides**, **Notes**: neither; a discussion seminar whose preparation is other people's papers, videos and articles.
- **Videos**: no offering mentions recordings, and credit requires attending every session. Autumn 2020 ran on Zoom and put its meeting details behind a `restricted/` path.
- **Assignments**: on [Ed](https://edstem.org/us/courses/106929), which asks for an account. The archived meeting pages name each week's prep — read the Brin and Page paper, hand-trace the random surfer — and then send the reader to that week's Ed post for the detail. Each also carries a "further resources" reading list of public links.
- **Solutions**: nothing is graded beyond attendance and participation, and no answers are posted.
- **Exams**, **Projects**: none; the course sets neither.
- **Repo**: meetings involve collaborative coding, but no code is published.

## Syllabus

The course follows the [CS 106B](CS%20106B.md) syllabus and picks supplemental material alongside it; the catalog says past topics have included search engines, pattern recognition, data compression and encryption, error correction, digital signatures and numerical recipes. This offering's excursions are not published, but the archive shows a stable sequence: [Autumn 2025](https://web.stanford.edu/class/archive/cs/cs106m/cs106m.1252/) ran binary search, page rank, fractals, backtracking, dynamic programming, sorting, generics and skip lists over 9 Thursdays, and [Autumn 2021](https://web.stanford.edu/class/archive/cs/cs106m/cs106m.1222/), [Autumn 2022](https://web.stanford.edu/class/archive/cs/cs106m/cs106m.1232/) and [Autumn 2023](https://web.stanford.edu/class/archive/cs/cs106m/cs106m.1242/) ran nearly the same list with Monte Carlo simulation, compression and a lexicon week in place of the last three.

## Prerequisites

- Concurrent enrollment in [CS 106B](CS%20106B.md). Enrollment is capped at 20; credit requires attending every session, and auditing is offered to anyone who needs flexibility.
