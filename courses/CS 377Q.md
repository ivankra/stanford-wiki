# CS 377Q: Designing for Accessibility

A design studio organized around the universal-design argument: that accommodating disability tends to improve an interface for everyone. The catalog frames it around individual assignments, but the graded weight sits on a team project — 55% since at least Spring 2023 — and that project carries a hard requirement other design courses do not impose: the user study must be run with a person with a disability, not a proxy. Scope runs across computers, mobile phones and VR. It carries a design-course prerequisite, so the HCI fundamentals are a refresher rather than the content.

## Syllabus

From [Spring 2024's schedule](https://canvas.stanford.edu/courses/188170/assignments/syllabus). Weeks 1-3 are the survey: disability demographics, assistive technology for blind and low-vision users — sensory substitution, screen readers, alt text — then variation in disability and its effects online, neurodiversity, Deaf and hard-of-hearing users, Down syndrome, and the Web Content Accessibility Guidelines. Week 3 turns to brainstorming and accessibility and AI, which that year framed the projects.

Weeks 4-10 are the project: need finding, interview analysis and terminology; a proposal; prototyping for accessibility and accessible UX; then user-study design, a pilot, data collection and analysis; closing with presentations and a Project Fair in Packard. Guest sessions cover ADHD, AI and people with disabilities, and an accessibility-in-industry panel.
