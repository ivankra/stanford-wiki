# CS 377Q: Designing for Accessibility

A design studio organized around the universal-design argument: that accommodating disability tends to improve an interface for everyone. Unlike most of the CS 377 series it is built on individual assignments rather than team projects, and the project has a hard requirement other design courses do not impose — the user study must be run with a person with a disability, not a proxy. Scope runs across computers, mobile phones and VR. It carries a design-course prerequisite, so the HCI fundamentals are a refresher rather than the content.

## Syllabus

No schedule is published. The catalog describes the argument and the work:

> Designing for accessibility is a valuable and important skill in the UX community. As businesses are becomeing more aware of the needs and scope of people with some form of disability, the benefits of universal design, where designing for accessibility ends up benefiting everyone, are becoming more apparent. This class introduces fundamental Human Computer Interaction (HCI) concepts and skills in designing for accessibility through individual assignments. Student projects will identify an accessibility need, prototype a design solution, and conduct a user study with a person with a disability. This class focuses on the accessibility of UX with computers, mobile phones, VR.
