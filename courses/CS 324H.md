# CS 324H: History of Natural Language Processing

Jurafsky and Manning reading their own field's primary sources, with the people who wrote them invited in. Week 3 is Kuhn and Latour — how to do history of science at all — before the course reaches GUS, Jelinek's statistical turn, the PDP era and finally "Attention is all you need" as a historical document. Each Monday runs three hours in three parts: staff-led discussion, a student-run activity, then a guest. No exams; the final project may be an interview with a primary figure or a scientometric study of the ACL.

## Syllabus

[Ten Mondays](https://web.stanford.edu/class/cs324h/), chronological after a methodological start.

1. Week 1: the instructors' own account of the field's history, with a student panel.
2. Week 3: history of science — Kuhn on paradigms, Winner's "Do Artifacts have Politics?", Latour's *Science in Action*.
3. Week 4: doing computational history, on NLP's own corpora — topic models over the ACL, the diversity-innovation paradox, a material lens on coloniality.
4. Week 5: discourse and dialog in 1970s-80s Palo Alto — GUS, task-oriented dialogue, Grosz and Sidner.
5. Week 6: the statistical revolution — Jelinek's own account, Xiaochang Li on "there's no data like more data", Brown and Mercer on bitext.
6. Week 8: the PDP era — Feldman and Ballard, Elman's "Finding Structure in Time".
7. Week 9: the 2000s and 2010s — Collobert and Weston, then Vaswani et al.
8. Week 10: project presentations.
