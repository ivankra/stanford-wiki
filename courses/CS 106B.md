# CS 106B: Programming Abstractions

The bridge out of the introductory course: C++ replaces Python, recursion and pointers replace loops and lists, and the weekly assignments — written in pairs this quarter — end in an interactive grading session with a section leader.

## Materials

The site is the course, and every quarter back to Winter 2020 keeps its own: slides, section handouts with their solutions, assignments and policies are all public, and recent quarters publish four practice midterms and four practice finals with worked answers. Only the recordings are gated, which the syllabus states outright.

- **Syllabus**: [topics, attendance rules, exam dates and grade weights](https://web.stanford.edu/class/archive/cs/cs106b/cs106b.1272/course/syllabus), with [About Assessments](https://web.stanford.edu/class/archive/cs/cs106b/cs106b.1272/course/about_assessments/) giving the exam policy and the [schedule](https://web.stanford.edu/class/archive/cs/cs106b/cs106b.1272/schedule) a dated week-by-week calendar. The schedule builds its table in JavaScript, so a plain fetch shows only its caption.
- **Slides**: a Google Slides deck on each lecture page — [lecture 1](https://web.stanford.edu/class/archive/cs/cs106b/cs106b.1272/lectures/01-welcome/) through lecture 3 so far — each exporting to PDF without an account. [Summer 2026](https://web.stanford.edu/class/archive/cs/cs106b/cs106b.1268/lectures/) has all 28 of its own.
- **Notes**: weekly [section handouts](https://web.stanford.edu/class/archive/cs/cs106b/cs106b.1272/sections/section1/) and standing guides: [Python to C++](https://web.stanford.edu/class/archive/cs/cs106b/cs106b.1272/course/python_to_cpp), [style](https://web.stanford.edu/class/archive/cs/cs106b/cs106b.1272/course/style_guide), [testing](https://web.stanford.edu/class/archive/cs/cs106b/cs106b.1272/course/testing_guide), the [Stanford C++ library docs](https://web.stanford.edu/dept/cs_edu/resources/cslib_docs/) and the free [beta course reader](https://web.stanford.edu/dept/cs_edu/resources/textbook/Reader-Beta-2012.pdf).

- **Textbook**: Eric Roberts' *Programming Abstractions in C++*, written for this course. Readings are suggested, not assigned, and the pre-publication course reader is free.

## Syllabus

The [syllabus](https://web.stanford.edu/class/archive/cs/cs106b/cs106b.1272/course/syllabus) gives the topics in approximate order rather than by date:

1. C++ basics, coming from Python.
2. Abstract data structures.
3. Recursion.
4. Classes and object-oriented programming.
5. Memory management and implementation-level abstractions.
6. Linked data structures.
7. Advanced algorithms.

Grading is 25% assignments, 40% two midterms, 25% final, 10% lecture and section participation. Missing a lecture means watching the recording and passing a Gradescope quiz within 48 hours.

## Prerequisites

- Introductory programming: [CS 106A](CS%20106A.md), or a comparable course including high-school AP. No C++ needed — the course starts from Python.

## Related

- [CS 106A](CS%20106A.md): the prerequisite, in Python.
- [CS 106L](CS%20106L.md): a 1-unit companion on modern C++ itself, taken alongside or after.
- [CS 106M](CS%20106M.md): a 1-unit enrichment seminar that follows this course's syllabus.
- [CS 107](CS%20107.md): the sequel, dropping from C++ to C and the machine.

## Source notes

- `cs106b.stanford.edu` redirects to `web.stanford.edu/class/archive/cs/cs106b/cs106b.1272/`; the quarter's number is in the path, and past quarters keep working at their own numbers.
- The schedule page carries only a caption in its HTML ("a preview of our planned schedule", last modified July 27, 2026) — the table is rendered client-side.
- **Every quarter since Winter 2020 is still up**, at `web.stanford.edu/class/archive/cs/cs106b/cs106b.<term id>/`. Autumn 2023 through Summer 2024 use a site shape whose nav is collapsed until clicked, so a plain fetch finds two links; those four also carry a banner naming the quarter they archive, which the other shapes don't.
- The practice exams sit one level deeper than their links suggest: the hrefs on `exams/1-midterm` resolve against `exams/1-midterm/`, so the PDFs are at `exams/1-midterm/practice-mid-1.pdf` (checked 2026-09-30).
- Stanford Online's channel lists no CS 106B playlist (checked 2026-09-30).
- Units: the catalog lists 3–5; the syllabus requires 5 of undergraduates and allows 3–4 for graduate students only as an administrative convenience.
