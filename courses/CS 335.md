# CS 335: Fair, Accountable, and Transparent (FAT) Deep Learning

Landay and Wei's one-off course treats fairness as a computer-science problem rather than a policy one: seventeen lectures of formal definitions, representation learning, interpretability and differential privacy, each with a reading list of five research papers, and a computational term project. Thirty students by application.

## Materials

Unusually complete for a course that ran once: slides, recordings, readings and every student report.

- **Syllabus**: the [schedule](https://hci.stanford.edu/courses/cs335/2020/sp/schedule.html) gives each lecture its textbook chapters (Barocas, Bishop, Molnar) and three to six papers, with separate pages for [grading](https://hci.stanford.edu/courses/cs335/2020/sp/grading.html), attendance policy, weekly reading guidelines, textbooks and resources.
- **Slides**: `lec1.pdf` through `lec17.pdf` on the [lectures page](https://hci.stanford.edu/courses/cs335/2020/sp/lectures.html) — lecture 11 is absent from the list and lecture 15 was canceled.
- **Videos**: every posted lecture has a Zoom recording beside its deck. These are `stanford.zoom.us/rec/play/` links from 2020 and may no longer resolve.
- **Assignments**: the term project is the whole of the coursework; [guidelines](https://hci.stanford.edu/courses/cs335/2020/sp/guidelines.html) and a datasets-and-ideas page are public.
- **Projects**: ten [final reports](https://hci.stanford.edu/courses/cs335/2020/sp/reports.html) as PDFs — fair image captioning, translating unfair to fair embeddings, FairGAN against facial-recognition bias, an interpretable schizophrenia classifier on fMRI, differentially private normalizing flows — most with a session recording.

## Syllabus

Seventeen lectures over ten weeks, Apr 8 to Jun 10 ([schedule](https://hci.stanford.edu/courses/cs335/2020/sp/schedule.html)).

1. **Fairness** (Apr 8-10): bias and fairness definitions, formalizing fairness in prediction, equality of opportunity, discrimination-aware data mining, fairness constraints; then fair representation learning — adversarial fair representations, inherent trade-offs in risk scores, FairGAN, disentanglement, Wasserstein fair classification.
2. **Interpretability** (Apr 15-17): interpretability and transparency (the mythos of model interpretability, the XAI survey, falling rule lists), then post hoc interpretability and proxy models.
3. **Robustness, accountability and privacy** (Apr 22 onward): the remaining lectures work through robustness, accountability and privacy-preserving learning in the same paper-per-session form.

## Prerequisites

- Deep learning at graduate level: one of [CS 224N](CS%20224N.md), [CS 230](CS%20230.md), [CS 231N](CS%20231N.md), [CS 236](CS%20236.md) or [CS 273B](CS%20273B.md) — or [CS 229](CS%20229.md) with the instructors' permission.
- Intermediate statistics.

Admission is by an enrollment survey, capped at thirty.
