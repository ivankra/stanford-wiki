# CS 335: Fair, Accountable, and Transparent (FAT) Deep Learning

Landay and Wei's one-off course treats fairness as a computer-science problem rather than a policy one: 17 lectures of formal definitions, representation learning, interpretability and differential privacy, each with a reading list of 5 research papers, and a computational term project. 30 students by application.

## Syllabus

17 lectures over 10 weeks, Apr 8 to Jun 10 ([schedule](https://hci.stanford.edu/courses/cs335/2020/sp/schedule.html)).

1. **Fairness** (Apr 8-10): bias and fairness definitions, formalizing fairness in prediction, equality of opportunity, discrimination-aware data mining, fairness constraints; then fair representation learning — adversarial fair representations, inherent trade-offs in risk scores, FairGAN, disentanglement, Wasserstein fair classification.
2. **Interpretability** (Apr 15-17): interpretability and transparency (the mythos of model interpretability, the XAI survey, falling rule lists), then post hoc interpretability and proxy models.
3. **Robustness, accountability and privacy** (Apr 22 onward): the remaining lectures work through robustness, accountability and privacy-preserving learning in the same paper-per-session form.

## Prerequisites

- Deep learning at graduate level: one of [CS 224N](CS%20224N.md), [CS 230](CS%20230.md), [CS 231N](CS%20231N.md), [CS 236](CS%20236.md) or [CS 273B](CS%20273B.md) — or [CS 229](CS%20229.md) with the instructors' permission.
- Intermediate statistics.

Admission is by an enrollment survey, capped at 30.
