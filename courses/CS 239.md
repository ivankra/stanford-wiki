# CS 239: Advanced Topics in Sequential Decision Making

Where [CS 238](CS%20238.md) teaches the algorithms, this reads the papers and asks students to add one. The survey runs through POMDPs, approximate dynamic programming and multiagent planning, with a bias toward domains where the stakes are physical — aerospace, air traffic control, robotics. Each student presents a paper with a working implementation, sits two open-book take-home midterms, and writes an original research paper; the 4-unit option adds 30 hours turning that paper into a conference submission.

## Materials

The course's design is published in unusual detail. Its reading list is not, because the schedule that holds every PDF link is a private sheet.

- **Syllabus**: the [site](https://aa229.stanford.edu/) gives learning outcomes, the unit options, and a fully itemised grading split — presentation 35% (draft, talk, implementation writeup, exercises), midterms 20%, final project 40%, participation 5% — plus the midterm rules. The dated schedule is a Google Sheet that returns a sign-in.
- **Assignments**: the [paper-presentation](https://aa229.stanford.edu/paper-presentations/) and [final-project](https://aa229.stanford.edu/final-project/) briefs are open.
- **Exams**: closed. Two take-home midterms, released Monday 3pm and due Friday 5pm through Canvas, open-resource but not open-collaboration. None is published.
- **Slides and videos**: neither is posted.
- Readings are "papers that are available for free through Stanford's subscription", linked only from the gated schedule.

## Syllabus

No public schedule exists for this offering. The catalog and site describe the scope: efficient algorithms for single and multiagent planning with and without a known model; partially observable Markov decision processes, approximate dynamic programming and reinforcement learning; and "new approaches for overcoming challenges in generalization from experience, exploration of the environment, and model representation so that these methods can scale to real problems".
