# CS 239: Advanced Topics in Sequential Decision Making

Where [CS 238](CS%20238.md) teaches the algorithms, this reads the papers and asks students to add one. The survey runs through POMDPs, approximate dynamic programming and multiagent planning, with a bias toward domains where the stakes are physical — aerospace, air traffic control, robotics. Each student presents a paper with a working implementation, sits 2 open-book take-home midterms, and writes an original research paper; the 4-unit option adds 30 hours turning that paper into a conference submission.

## Materials

The course's design is published in unusual detail and its content is not. The schedule that holds every reading is a sheet that answers 401, the student presentations that are the teaching go to Canvas, and the readings themselves are papers "available for free through Stanford's subscription".

- **Syllabus**: the [site](https://aa229.stanford.edu/) gives the description, learning outcomes, the unit options and an itemized grading split (presentation 35%, split across draft, talk, implementation writeup and exercises; midterms 20%; final project 40%; participation 5%), plus the midterm rules. The dated schedule is a Google Sheet that answers 401.

- **Videos**: no offering mentions recording and nothing public turned up.
- **Assignments**: the [paper-presentation](https://aa229.stanford.edu/paper-presentations/) brief, with its 7 grading criteria, the 15-plus-5-minute format, the draft deadline and the exercise requirement, and the [final-project](https://aa229.stanford.edu/final-project/) brief, with its proposal, 6-minute presentation and paper. The implementation writeup's [Overleaf template](https://www.overleaf.com/read/npsvgqsmzdzt) opens; the presentation template is a Stanford-only Google Slides file, and the page says so: "Please use your Stanford account to log into the Google Drive links".
- **Solutions**: each presenter writes 2 exercise questions with solutions after them, for the class to study from. They are not published.
- **Exams**: 2 take-home midterms through Canvas, open-resource but not open-collaboration. No paper is published.
- **Projects**: the original research paper goes to Canvas; none is published.
- **Repo**: none found. The implementation writeup asks for pseudocode rather than running code.

## Syllabus

No public schedule exists for this offering. The catalog and site describe the scope: efficient algorithms for single and multiagent planning with and without a known model; partially observable Markov decision processes, approximate dynamic programming and reinforcement learning; and "new approaches for overcoming challenges in generalization from experience, exploration of the environment, and model representation so that these methods can scale to real problems".
