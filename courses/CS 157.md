# CS 157: Computational Logic

Logic taught as a tool rather than a branch of mathematics: fifteen lessons through propositional, relational and term logic, proofs written in Fitch inside a browser tool, three quizzes and an optional final. The course sits inside Stanford's public Intrologic materials, which the instructor has also run as a MOOC to hundreds of thousands of people.

## Materials

The course is a Stanford schedule page on top of a public logic curriculum, so the textbook, the videos, the exercises and their answers are open to anyone. Only the graded tests and the offering's own lecture recordings are held back.

- **Syllabus**: the [offering's page](http://intrologic.stanford.edu/stanford/index.php), with the 10-week schedule of lessons and quizzes, the office-hour grid and running weekly notes.
- **Slides**: [17 lecture decks](http://intrologic.stanford.edu/stanford/slides.php) as PDFs, introduction through Fitch proofs, induction and equality, plus 3 dated review decks.
- **Notes**: the [Intrologic lessons](http://logic.stanford.edu/intrologic/public/lessons.php) — a chapter per lesson, a page per section, and a dictionary.
- **Videos**: a video for every section of every lesson, on the same index and keyed to the schedule. The offering's own lecture recordings are a Canvas link.
- **Assignments**: [97 interactive exercise pages](http://logic.stanford.edu/intrologic/exercises/exercise_01_01.html), one per section, plus 33 puzzle and demonstration pages from Alice to a minefield solver.
- **Solutions**: each exercise page marks itself as you answer and has a "Show Answers" button.
- **Exams**: 3 unit quizzes, one each on propositional, relational and term logic, plus optional exams that can replace a quiz score. The Tests page redirects to an Intrologic sign-in whose sign-up asks for "your official Stanford email address, as that is the one we will use to connect your scores to the University's grading system".
- **Projects**: none; the assessment is 3 unit quizzes, with optional exams that can replace a quiz score.
- **Repo**: [Logica](http://logic.stanford.edu/logica), the browser-based proof and query tool the exercises and Fitch proofs run in.
- **Textbook**: the Intrologic chapters themselves; the course sets no other text.
- **Self-study**: the curriculum's [front door](http://logic.stanford.edu/intrologic/homepage/index.html) sends non-students straight to the lessons — "Click here to take the course. Open to all" — and reports over 650,000 enrollments across its offerings. Its [Coursera run](https://www.coursera.org/learn/logic-introduction/), quarterly, is the graded path the Stanford tests are closed to.

## Syllabus

Three blocks, from the [published schedule](http://intrologic.stanford.edu/stanford/index.php):

1. **Propositional Logic** (weeks 1–4): syntax and semantics, propositional analysis — validity, satisfiability, equivalence, entailment, consistency — direct proofs, natural deduction and refutation proofs, then Quiz 1.
2. **Relational Logic** (weeks 5–7): relational logic and analysis, Fitch proofs, then Quiz 2.
3. **Term Logic** (weeks 8–10): logic with function symbols, mathematical induction, equality, "Logic in Logic", then Quiz 3 and an optional final.

## Prerequisites

- None stated. The materials are pitched from secondary-school level upward.
