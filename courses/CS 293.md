# CS 293: Empowering Educators via Language Technology

Dora Demszky's seminar on applying NLP to what teachers and students actually say, and on whether the resulting tools help. It is built around roughly 70 papers, half of them from education and learning-sciences venues rather than ACL, and student pairs lead a 30-45 minute discussion each. Its distinguishing feature is structural: every student works with a **teacher buddy** through the quarter, so the final project is shaped by a practitioner rather than only by the literature. Guests come from Google's LearnLM team and from education research; sessions alternate between method (topic modeling, measurement, LLM simulation) and deployment.

## Syllabus

18 sessions over 9 weeks, per the [schedule](https://web.stanford.edu/class/cs293/).

1. **Weeks 1–3, discovery**: 3 sessions on exploring educational language data — data exploration, parsing and lexical analysis, topic modeling and clustering — interleaved with 2 on centering teachers in tool design, including a guest visit from Dan Meyer.
2. **Weeks 4–5, measurement**: the Google LearnLM team on their work; 2 sessions on turning expert measurement of teaching practice into automated and multimodal scoring.
3. **Weeks 5–6, generative AI**: supporting teachers, then supporting tutors, with a guest from the University of Vienna.
4. **Weeks 7–8, modeling and design**: synthetic students and LLM simulation of classroom interaction; practitioner-centered design; generative models for pre-service teacher training.
5. **Week 9**: deploying NLP tools in real schools, then frontiers and open questions. 2 in-class project work sessions sit in weeks 6 and 8.

## Prerequisites

The catalog states none, and the course is cross-listed into the education school, so it is written for a mixed audience. In practice the final project requires applying NLP methods, and the reading assumes comfort with the machine-learning vocabulary of a paper's methods section.
