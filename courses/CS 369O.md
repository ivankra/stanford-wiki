# CS 369O: Optimization Algorithms

The rates course: not which solver to call, but how fast any method can possibly be for a given class of functions, and which algorithm meets that bound. It works upward from Lipschitz lower bounds through smoothness and convexity to acceleration, then relaxes each assumption — non-smooth, constrained, minimax — and ends at interior point methods. Everything is proved.

## Syllabus

No course schedule exists publicly; the [notes](https://drive.google.com/file/d/1FXdbI0hDFyDrig749h_epmf2p19nwy_k/view) are the closest thing, and their four parts give the shape.

1. Introduction: what "efficient minimization" means, how an algorithm may access *f*, and lower bounds for minimizing a general Lipschitz function.
2. Smooth unconstrained minimization: smoothness, gradient descent to critical points, convexity and strong convexity, acceleration and momentum with matching lower bounds, then non-Euclidean smoothness, composite minimization and coordinate descent.
3. Non-smooth constrained minimization: subgradients, feasibility problems and cutting plane methods, online linear optimization, mirror descent, stochastic subgradient descent, minimax optimization.
4. Higher-order methods: higher-order optimization, linear programming, interior point methods and Newton's method.
