# CS 224G: Apps With LLMs Inside

A startup studio rather than a lecture course: 60 students in teams of 2-5, four two-week sprints, and a three-hour Demo Day in front of VCs and founders that carries 45% of the grade. Most lectures are guests from industry — PydanticAI orchestration from a manufacturing-AI startup, realtime voice over WebRTC, a talk on evaluating agents built from 179 analyzed failures. The honor code actively encourages AI coding assistants and only asks that students document and understand what they ship. Taught by two entrepreneurs rather than faculty; there are no exams and no problem sets.

## Syllabus

Eighteen sessions over ten weeks, per the [schedule](https://web.stanford.edu/class/cs224g/schedule.html), with four of them given over to sprint presentations.

1. **Weeks 1–2, landscape**: where LLMs are in 2026; student pitches and the modern LLM stack; reasoning models (o1/o3, DeepSeek R1, Claude Sonnet 4) and when not to use them; the four pillars of building with GenAI — iteration, evaluations, deployment, observability.
2. **Week 3, context**: context engineering as the successor to prompt engineering — the context stack, RAG architecture, memory systems, token budget economy, and prompt injection with its defenses. Sprint 1 opens.
3. **Weeks 4–6, agents and production**: ReAct and multi-agent architectures with LangGraph and CrewAI; what "AI-native" means against "AI-assisted"; the Theory of Constraints applied to non-deterministic systems, and proper evals against vibe evals; code generation benchmarked on HumanEval and SWE-Bench, with coding-agent setup and environment engineering.
4. **Weeks 7–8, orchestration and data**: building a near-production agent in PydanticAI, weighed against heavier frameworks; data as a moat, the flywheel, and memory layers for agents.
5. **Weeks 9–10**: a realtime voice AI workshop on WebRTC, ephemeral session tokens and scaling; then ethics and guardrails. Demo Day on 19 March.

## Prerequisites

- Python: [CS 106B](CS%20106B.md) or equivalent programming experience.
- Machine learning fundamentals: [CS 221](CS%20221.md) or [CS 229](CS%20229.md), or equivalent understanding.
- Hands-on with a framework: PyTorch, TensorFlow or JAX.
- Recommended: familiarity with the OpenAI or Anthropic APIs. Students with substantial practical LLM experience can enroll on instructor permission instead.
