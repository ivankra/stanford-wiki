# CS 7: Personal Finance for Engineers

One unit, ten Tuesday evenings, and a syllabus aimed squarely at the money decisions a new engineer meets first: what an offer is actually worth, what to do with it, and what debt and a house cost. Taught by an industry practitioner, in its ninth year, with every deck published publicly.

## Materials

The instructor publishes the whole seminar on his own blog, a post of dated sessions and their decks for each year since 2017. This year's post has not appeared yet — it has come in early or mid-October every year — so the newest complete set is Autumn 2025's.

- **Syllabus**: the [administrative details](https://cs007.blog/administrative-details/) page gives the format, the pass/fail basis, the 2-absence rule and that homework is optional, but it still reads "Fall Quarter 2024-25" and names a Monday slot in 420-040 where the catalog now says Tuesdays in STLC 111. This offering's own syllabus is a Stanford-only Canvas upload. Autumn 2020's [Canvas course](https://canvas.stanford.edu/courses/124836/assignments/syllabus) is one of the rare Stanford courses left publicly readable, and carries the same 10 sessions.
- **Slides**: [Autumn 2025's 9 decks](https://cs007.blog/2025/10/11/cs-007-course-material-2025/) and [Autumn 2024's 10](https://cs007.blog/2024/10/07/cs-007-course-material-2024/), as PDFs; [Autumn 2023](https://cs007.blog/2023/10/09/cs-007-course-material-2023/) has a full set too, Autumn 2022 only session 10's, and the 2020 and 2021 posts link none.
- **Notes**: none; the decks are the material.
- **Videos**: no offering's sessions are posted anywhere, and nothing says whether they are recorded.
- **Assignments**: "Homework is optional, but is provided to aid in learning the material presented" — and none of it is published.
- **Solutions**, **Exams**, **Projects**, **Repo**: none; a 1-unit pass/fail seminar graded on attendance.

## Syllabus

The published session list:

1. Introduction.
2. Behavioral finance — "Predictably Irrational".
3. Compensation, including equity and stock options.
4. Saving and budgeting.
5. Assets and net worth.
6. Debt.
7. Investing.
8. Financial planning and goals.
9. Real estate.
10. Additional topics.

The catalog adds the framing: "actual industry-based financial information from technology companies and realistic financial issues", with no finance or economics background required.

## Prerequisites

- None. The catalog states no prior finance or economics experience is needed.
