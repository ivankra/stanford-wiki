# CS 529: Robotics and Autonomous Systems Seminar

The campus robotics seminar with a course number attached. One unit, satisfactory/no credit, repeatable, and the only requirement is turning up: seven attendance forms over the quarter, or paragraphs on the talks you missed. It runs every quarter, and the talks themselves are open to anyone on campus whether or not they register.

## Materials

Unusually good for a seminar, because the organizers keep an archive rather than overwriting each quarter.

- **Syllabus**: the [archive](https://stanfordasl.github.io/robotics_seminar/archive/) holds a dated speaker table with abstracts for every quarter from Winter 2018 on, Spring 2024 included; the [class page](https://stanfordasl.github.io/robotics_seminar/class/) states the credit rule and the recording policy.
- **Videos**: the [YouTube playlist](https://www.youtube.com/playlist?list=PLoROMvodv4rMeercb-kvGLUrOq4HR6BZD) carries edited talks, but only "publically available past seminar recordings" — the full set is on Canvas, so the public playlist is a subset.
- **Slides**: speakers' decks aren't posted.
- **Assignments, exams and projects**: none.

## Syllabus

[Ten Friday talks](https://stanfordasl.github.io/robotics_seminar/archive/) in Spring 2024, all 12:30 in Skilling Auditorium.

- Apr 5, Andreas Krause (ETH Zurich): safe Bayesian optimization and model-based RL in the physical world.
- Apr 12, Karen Leung (Washington): trusted human-centric robot autonomy.
- Apr 19, Ken Goldberg (Berkeley): whether data is all you need for large robot action models.
- Apr 26, Elliot Hawkes (UCSB).
- May 3, two Stanford student speakers: Somrita Banerjee on adaptation to evolving conditions, Elliot Weiss on VR headsets and vehicle safety.
- May 10, Cynthia Sung (UPenn): when design equals planning.
- May 17, Hiro Ono (NASA JPL): the EELS robot, from Mars to Enceladus.
- May 24, Richard Linares (MIT): transformer-based constraint prediction for powered descent.
- May 31, Aaron Ames (Caltech): why learning needs control.
