# CS 155: Computer and Network Security

Each layer is taught attack-first: students write the exploit before they build the defense, across three projects that run most of the quarter.

## Materials

Everything the lectures use is public: 17 of the 18 decks, the attack papers that stand in for a textbook, and 9 years of sample finals. Two gaps: the recordings go to Canvas, and project 1, the control-hijacking exploit the course opens with, has no public handout, although projects 2 and 3 are posted with their starter code.

- **Syllabus**: the [lecture list with per-lecture readings](https://cs155.stanford.edu/syllabus.html) and the [overview page](https://cs155.stanford.edu/info.html) with the coursework structure, the 72-hour extension budget and the exam format. Canvas holds a Stanford-only copy for 4 of the 6 older offerings; Spring 2022 and Spring 2025 uploaded none.
- **Slides**: [17 decks](https://cs155.stanford.edu/lectures/01-intro.pdf), each as a PDF and as the Keynote original; lecture 13, on HTTPS pitfalls, has no file.
- **Notes**: no textbook, "instead, we will use research papers posted on the course syllabus page". [The format-string paper](https://cs155.stanford.edu/papers/formatstring-1.2.pdf) and Ken Thompson's "Reflections on Trusting Trust" sit on the site; Aleph One's "Smashing the Stack", "Once upon a free()" and the GOT-hijacking write-up are linked to Phrack and exploit-db.
- **Videos**: Canvas only. "Video cameras located in the back of the room will capture the instructor presentations", and the recordings are reached "by logging into the course Canvas site". No public copy exists for any offering, and a YouTube search for the code returns other universities' courses.
- **Assignments**: [project 2](https://cs155.stanford.edu/hw_and_proj/proj2/proj2_handout.pdf) (web security) and [project 3](https://cs155.stanford.edu/hw_and_proj/proj3/proj3.pdf) (network security) with handouts and starter code, and both written homework pages. Project 1 is listed with its due dates and its reading list but no handout: `hw_and_proj/proj1/` 404s.
- **Solutions**: nothing posted, for homework or the sample finals. The overview page calls it an honor code violation "to consult solutions from previous offerings" without saying where those go.
- **Exams**: [9 sample finals](https://cs155.stanford.edu/final_exams/2024-final.pdf), 2014 to 2019 and 2022 to 2024, "intended for practice only" and without solutions. This offering's final is a 3-hour remote Gradescope exam inside a 24-hour window.
- **Projects**: none; the coursework is the 2 homeworks and the 3 programming projects, and no student work is published.
- **Repo**: [project 2's starter archive](https://cs155.stanford.edu/hw_and_proj/proj2/proj2_starter_code.zip), 5.4 MB, and [project 3's](https://cs155.stanford.edu/hw_and_proj/proj3/proj3_starter.zip).

## Syllabus

Eighteen lectures on the [posted syllabus](https://cs155.stanford.edu/syllabus.html), in four blocks:

1. **Memory safety** (1–4): course overview, control hijacking attacks, control hijacking defenses, and a guest lecture by Brandon Azad of Google on testing for vulnerabilities.
2. **Systems** (5–7): security principles and operating system security, isolation and sandboxing, hardware and microarchitecture security.
3. **Web and crypto** (8–12): the web security model, web attacks, web defenses, a cryptography overview, HTTPS goals and pitfalls.
4. **Network and beyond** (14–18): internet protocols, internet security, DoS attacks and network defenses, privacy and anonymity and censorship, and security of AI systems.

Project 1 is control hijacking, project 2 web security, project 3 network security; each runs two to three weeks and splits into parts.

## Prerequisites
