# CS 155: Computer and Network Security

Each layer is taught attack-first: students write the exploit before they build the defense, across three projects that run most of the quarter.

## Materials

The site is open apart from recordings and solutions: every deck, all three project handouts with their starter code, and nine years of final exams.

- **Syllabus**: the [lecture list with per-lecture readings](https://cs155.stanford.edu/syllabus.html); a separate overview page carries grading, textbooks and exam policy.
- **Slides**: [seventeen decks](https://cs155.stanford.edu/lectures/01-intro.pdf), each posted as PDF and Keynote. Lecture 13 has no file.
- **Notes**: the attack literature the projects lean on — [the format-string paper](https://cs155.stanford.edu/papers/formatstring-1.2.pdf) and Ken Thompson's "Reflections on Trusting Trust" on the site, Aleph One's "Smashing the Stack" and "Once upon a free()" on Phrack.
- **Videos**: captured in the lecture hall, released only through Canvas.
- **Assignments**: [Project 2's handout](https://cs155.stanford.edu/hw_and_proj/proj2/proj2_handout.pdf) and those for projects 1 and 3, plus two homework pages.
- **Solutions**: not posted, for homework or exams.
- **Exams**: [nine sample finals, 2014 through 2024](https://cs155.stanford.edu/final_exams/2024-final.pdf), "intended for practice only". The 2026 final is a remote three-hour Gradescope exam with a 24-hour window.
- **Projects**: none beyond the three coursework projects.
- **Code**: [starter archives](https://cs155.stanford.edu/hw_and_proj/proj2/proj2_starter_code.zip) for the web- and network-security projects.

## Syllabus

Eighteen lectures on the [posted syllabus](https://cs155.stanford.edu/syllabus.html), in four blocks:

1. **Memory safety** (1–4): course overview, control hijacking attacks, control hijacking defenses, and a guest lecture by Brandon Azad of Google on testing for vulnerabilities.
2. **Systems** (5–7): security principles and operating system security, isolation and sandboxing, hardware and microarchitecture security.
3. **Web and crypto** (8–12): the web security model, web attacks, web defenses, a cryptography overview, HTTPS goals and pitfalls.
4. **Network and beyond** (14–18): internet protocols, internet security, DoS attacks and network defenses, privacy and anonymity and censorship, and security of AI systems.

Project 1 is control hijacking, project 2 web security, project 3 network security; each runs two to three weeks and splits into parts.
