# CS 375: Large-Scale Neural Network Modeling for Neuroscience

Dan Yamins' course on what the site calls "convergent man-machine evolution": the finding that units inside trained deep networks predict responses in real cortex, and what follows from taking that seriously. Half the sessions are guest lectures from the NeuroAI community — Tolias, Saxe, Nayebi, Linderman, Tuckute, Zador. The distinctive part is the final project: rather than a paper, students contribute a model, metric or dataset to BBScore, the lab's model-brain comparison package, and the deliverable is a pull request to a public repository. No neuroscience background is assumed.

## Syllabus

Twenty sessions over ten weeks, per the [calendar](https://cs375.stanford.edu/course-calendar.html).

1. **Weeks 1–3, vision**: an introduction to NeuroAI; visual systems neuroscience background; two lectures on DNN models of the ventral stream; model-brain mapping methods — the metrics the rest of the course depends on.
2. **Weeks 4–5, learning and recurrence**: unsupervised learning and the brain; a guest lecture on modeling infant development; recurrent models of vision; the Enigma Project.
3. **Weeks 6–8, beyond vision**: auditory and somatosensory models; decision making; a BBScore coding session paired with navigation, memory and the medial entorhinal cortex; models of agents; the motor system.
4. **Weeks 9–10, dynamics, language and evolution**: dynamical systems in the brain; language, LLMs and human language areas; models of brain evolution; functional organization and learning rules. Project presentations close the term.

## Prerequisites

The catalog states none. The site advises:

- Modern machine learning, e.g. [CS 229](CS%20229.md), and practical neural network training, e.g. [CS 230](CS%20230.md) or [CS 231N](CS%20231N.md). The course description also names [CS 234](CS%20234.md), [CS 236](CS%20236.md) and [CS 330](CS%20330.md) as useful.
- No cognitive science or neuroscience: "we will do a gentle introduction to neuro concepts as they arise."
- Python with PyTorch or JAX, since both assignments and the project are code.
