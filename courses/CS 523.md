# CS 523: Research Seminar in Computer Vision + X

The "+ X" is a rotating second subject: in Spring 2021 it was healthcare, run by Julia Gong on how deep learning was changing diagnosis. The format is unusual in a good way — each week the class reads the visiting speaker's own paper and posts a response the night before, so the discussion starts from the work rather than an introduction to it. One unit, graded on participation and those responses.

## Materials

A seminar's core is its speaker schedule and the papers that go with it, and Spring 2021's is public in full — 10 sessions, every speaker named, every paper linked, with the weekly assignment written out. Spring 2022 added nothing: the site still carries the earlier year, and that offering's speakers and papers are not published anywhere.

- **Syllabus**: the [site](https://web.stanford.edu/class/cs523/) gives Spring 2021's description, its grading rule — "Completing all required assignments + active participation during the class = a passing grade" — and a dated 10-session table naming each week's speaker, their affiliation, their visiting advisor and the paper they wrote, with all 17 papers linked, most to arXiv, CVPR Open Access, bioRxiv or the journals. A copy also went to Canvas for Stanford accounts.
- **Slides**, **Videos**: neither is linked for either offering. The sessions are discussions with the author, not lectures, and nothing turned up under the code or the instructor's name.
- **Notes**: none. The course writes none; the speakers' published papers are the reading.
- **Assignments**: the weekly work is specified exactly — read the speaker's paper, post at least 4 pre-discussion points to Canvas by 5pm the day before, drawn from at least 2 of 5 named categories, and send the speaker a thank-you note naming one takeaway. The last week adds a reflection paragraph.
- **Solutions**, **Exams**, **Projects**, **Repo**: none. A 1-unit seminar graded on participation and those weekly posts sets nothing else.

## Syllabus

The catalog's title states the design — computer vision paired with a rotating second area — and Spring 2021 paired it with healthcare: "With advances in deep learning, computer vision (CV) has been transforming healthcare, from diagnosis to prognosis, from treatment to prevention."

The [schedule](https://web.stanford.edu/class/cs523/) is ten weekly sessions, each built on one paper discussed with its author. That year: bedside computer vision and ICU patient-mobilization detection with Serena Yeung and Christopher Holsinger; depth estimation in monocular endoscopy (Xingtong Liu, Johns Hopkins); objective assessment of surgical skill in cataract surgery (Tae Soo Kim, Johns Hopkins); disentangling human error from ground truth in medical image segmentation (NeurIPS 2020); predicting cortical response to naturalistic stimuli (Gia Ngo, Cornell); HyperMorph and amortized hyperparameter learning for image registration (Adrian Dalca, MGH/Harvard); scanner-invariant representations for diffusion MRI (Daniel Moyer, MIT); multi-view deep networks for breast cancer screening (Nan Wu, NYU); Motion2Vec on surgical video (Ajay Tanwani, Google Research); and hormonal-receptor status from H&E stains (Nature Communications 2020).

Several weeks add "encouraged further reading" — the crowdsourced surgical-instrument annotation paper behind the cataract dataset, the annotator-confusion work behind the segmentation session, and the dermatologist-level skin-cancer classification paper.
