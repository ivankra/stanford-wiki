# CS 339N: Machine Learning Methods for Neural Data Analysis

Follows one dataset's journey: raw electrode and imaging traces to sorted spikes, then encoding and decoding models, then latent dynamics. Every lecture pairs a neuroscience topic with the statistical method behind it, and the seven labs apply each to real recordings. Graded on labs (70%) and a final project, no exam.

## Syllabus

Four units ([syllabus](https://slinderman.github.io/stats320/)). **I. Extracting signals from raw data**: basic neurobiology and probabilistic modeling; spike sorting by clustering and then by deconvolution; calcium deconvolution; markerless pose tracking. **II. Encoding and decoding**: Poisson GLMs for neural encoding; Poisson processes; Bayesian decoding and inference. **III. Unsupervised models**: HMMs and EM for behavioral segmentation; switching linear dynamical systems and how to fit them; variational inference; sequential VAEs (LFADS). **IV. Current research**: Gaussian-process SLDS models, foundation models for neuroscience, and a closing session. The final project is due in exam week.

## Prerequisites

- Statistics or machine learning: STATS 202 or [CS 229](CS%20229.md).
- Probability (STATS 116), multivariate calculus and linear algebra.
- Python — a coding primer runs in week 1 for students coming from R or Matlab.
