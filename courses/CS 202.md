# CS 202: Law for Computer Science Professionals

A one-unit seminar surveying the intellectual property and computer law that people building technology meet in their careers, taught through recent lawsuits in an open discussion format with guest speakers.

## Syllabus

From the catalog description; no session-by-session schedule is published.

- **Foundations**: patent, trademark, copyright and trade secret law.
- **Current issues**: patents for software and business methods; copyrightability of programs and APIs; IP and artificial intelligence; evolving trademark and trade-secret protection.
- **Computer crime**: the Computer Fraud and Abuse Act and hacking.
- **Employment**: proprietary-information and invention-assignment agreements, work made for hire, confidentiality and non-compete agreements, other post-employment restrictions.
- **Cases**: Apple v. Samsung, Alice Corp. v. CLS Bank, Oracle v. Google, Waymo v. Uber, hiQ v. LinkedIn, plus headline cases that arise during the term.
- **Guest lectures**: typically open source software, legal issues facing founders, and expert work in IP litigation.
- IP in art, music, photography and literature is also covered.
