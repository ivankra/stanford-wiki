# CS 257: Introduction to Automated Reasoning

How SAT and SMT solvers actually work, taught by the people who build them: 6 of the 20 lectures are guests, including Clark Barrett on proof systems and DPLL, Leonardo de Moura on proof automation in Lean, and a Mathias Preiner lecture on bit-vectors. The arc runs propositional logic → DPLL → CDCL → first-order resolution → individual theories → DPLL(T) and Nelson-Oppen combination, with model checking, symbolic execution and text-to-SQL verification dropped in as applications. Coursework is 3 homeworks, a 24-hour take-home midterm, and a project that is either implementing a decision procedure and benchmarking it against SAT competition instances, or a research problem.

## Syllabus

20 lectures, per the [schedule](https://web.stanford.edu/class/cs257/).

1. **Weeks 1–3, propositional logic** (1–5): syntax, semantics, satisfiability and validity; normal forms, SAT-solving and resolution; proof systems; DPLL; CDCL.
2. **Weeks 3–4, first-order logic** (6–8): syntax; semantics, normal forms, first-order resolution and unification across 2 lectures; then first-order theories.
3. **Weeks 5–6, theories** (9–12): model checking as an application; an overview of theories and the DPLL(T) calculus; 2 lectures on arithmetic.
4. **Weeks 7–8, more theories** (13–16): decision procedures for arrays, strings and sequences; symbolic execution; from decision procedures to proof automation in Lean; bit-vectors.
5. **Weeks 9–10, SMT** (17–20): Herbrand's theorem and quantifier instantiation; theory combination; formal verification for text-to-SQL; a buffer lecture on program synthesis.
