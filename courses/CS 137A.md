# CS 137A: Principles of Robot Autonomy I

The undergraduate listing of the Autonomous Systems Lab's autonomy course: planning, perception and decision-making for mobile robots, with every assignment run against a local ROS2 install and a staff-written textbook that is free on arXiv. [CS 237A](CS%20237A.md) is the graduate version, with its own lecture slot and its own site.

## Materials

The syllabus, homework and starter code are public and the textbook is on arXiv; lecture content and recordings are the Canvas half.

- **Syllabus**: the [syllabus PDF](https://stanfordasl.github.io/PoRA-I/aa174a_aut2627/pdfs/syllabus.pdf), with meeting and section arrangements on the [site](https://stanfordasl.github.io/PoRA-I/aa174a_aut2627/).
- **Notes**: the course textbook, [*Principles of Robot Autonomy*](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2608.03496) (Gammelli, Lorenzetti, Luo, Zardini and Pavone, pre-print 2026).
- **Slides** and **Videos**: Canvas, which the site says carries "course content, recordings, and announcements".
- **Assignments**: [Homework 1](https://stanfordasl.github.io/PoRA-I/aa174a_aut2627/pdfs/homework/hw1.pdf) on controlling nonholonomic wheeled robots, with a LaTeX template.
- **Code**: a public repository per homework, [AA174a-HW1](https://github.com/StanfordASL/AA174a-HW1) and the rest under the [Principles of Robot Autonomy](https://github.com/PrinciplesofRobotAutonomy) organization.
- **Exams**: none.
- **Projects**: submitted through Gradescope; nothing published.

## Syllabus

The site's description sets the sequence: trajectory optimization; robot motion planning; robot perception, localization and simultaneous localization and mapping; and state machines for behavior — with ROS used throughout for demonstrations and the weekly two-hour section. Students taking the course for 4 units also write a paper review by the end of the quarter.

## Prerequisites

- Programming: [CS 106A](CS%20106A.md) or equivalent; the homeworks are in Python against ROS2.
- Mathematics: CME 100 or equivalent for calculus and linear algebra, CME 106 or equivalent for probability.
