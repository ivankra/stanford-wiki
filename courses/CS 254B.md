# CS 254B: Computational Complexity II

Where [CS 254](CS%20254.md) sets up the classes, this one works on why the obvious ways of separating them fail, and on trading hardness for randomness. Nineteen lectures, no exams: you scribe one lecture and run a research project.

## Syllabus

[Five parts](http://theory.stanford.edu/~liyang/teaching/complexity2.html#topics), mapped onto nineteen lectures with a paper against most of them.

1. Barriers and tradeoffs: Baker-Gill-Solovay relativization, Hopcroft-Paul-Valiant on time versus space, Ladner's theorem, time-space tradeoffs for SAT.
2. Hardness versus randomness, six of the nineteen lectures: Nisan-Wigderson pseudorandom generators, hardness amplification via Yao's XOR lemma, Impagliazzo's hardcore set lemma, the Impagliazzo-Wigderson theorem, the duality with boosting.
3. Beyond worst case: hardness within P, SAT algorithms, the Exponential Time Hypothesis and its variants.
4. Tools: expander graphs, derandomization, Fourier analysis of Boolean functions.
5. Two research talks, a discussion of where complexity theory is going, an AMA on the instructor's own work.

## Prerequisites

- Complexity theory: [CS 254](CS%20254.md), the first half of the sequence — the classes, space complexity, the polynomial hierarchy, randomness and circuit lower bounds. It is the only stated prerequisite.
