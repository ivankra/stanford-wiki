# CS 222: AI Agents and Simulations

Its first offering, and a deliberately long view: Schelling and Rittel before Soar, Soar before generative agents. The organizing question is what a simulation is *for* — the course separates believability from accuracy, asks which validation method licenses which claim, and spends a session building "AgentBank-CS222", a bank of agents representing the class itself. Two assignments, then a final project presented twice, at proposal and at completion.

## Syllabus

[Fourteen lectures](https://joonspk-research.github.io/cs222-fall24/index.html) over ten weeks, with four days given to project presentations.

1. Why simulate (weeks 1-2): the history from cellular automata and game theory to generative AI; "wicked problems"; choosing a granularity between individuals, groups and populations.
2. How to build agents (weeks 2-3): cognitive architectures and Soar, generative agent architectures, the environments and social networks agents inhabit.
3. Whether to believe them (weeks 4-6): believability versus accuracy, models of individuals, AgentBank, generative agent-based models, equilibria and chaos, schemas and languages for authoring simulations.
4. What they cost (weeks 8-9): ethics and limitations — bias, privacy, autonomy, and whether an LLM can stand in for an identity group — then guest lectures from Meredith Ringel Morris on agentic clones and "ghosts", and Serina Chang on simulating human networks.
