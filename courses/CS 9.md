# CS 9: Problem-Solving for the CS Technical Interview

1 unit, twice a week, on the specific skill of solving algorithm problems under interview conditions. The prerequisite is labeled "soft" on the site itself.

## Materials

The week splits in two and so does the material: all 9 Thursday problem sessions are public PDFs, and all 8 Tuesday recruiting lectures sit behind Stanford SSO, along with the recordings. A reader can practice the problems and read none of the advice.

- **Syllabus**: the [syllabus page](https://web.stanford.edu/class/cs9/syllabus) — course goals, grading, and what each half of the week does. [Autumn 2021's](https://web.stanford.edu/class/archive/cs/cs9/cs9.1222/) survives in Stanford's class archive, when the course met once a week for 9 sessions instead of twice.
- **Slides**: the [slides directory](https://web.stanford.edu/class/cs9/slides/) is an open listing of the 9 Thursday decks, `w1-2` through `w9-2`, 320-800 KB each. The 8 Tuesday decks — overview of tech recruiting, resumes and behavioral, resume reviews and career fairs, coding challenges, technical interviews 1 and 2, offers and negotiation, final thoughts and ML interviews — are linked from the same page under `slides/restricted/`, which redirects to `login.stanford.edu`. Autumn 2021 published no deck at all; its schedule's Resources column holds a single entry, a sample resume to critique.
- **Notes**, **Solutions**: nothing separate. Each Thursday deck carries its problems and walks one solution through.
- **Videos**: recorded on a staff laptop and uploaded to Canvas Panopto, in both offerings.
- **Assignments**: the Thursday decks are the practice. Submission ran through Gradescope, and no handout is posted apart from the session PDFs.
- **Exams**, **Projects**: none; a 1-unit course graded satisfactory/no credit sets neither.
- **Repo**: none found; the course writes no code of its own.

## Syllabus

From the [syllabus page](https://web.stanford.edu/class/cs9/syllabus): Tuesday lectures walk the CS technical recruiting process — resumes and behavioral screens, career fairs, coding challenges, the technical interview itself, then offers, negotiation and ethics, closing on ML interviews. Thursday sessions are practice: "three problems of varying difficulty", half the class to attempt one or two, half spent walking a solution through together. The 9 published Thursday decks are those problem sets.
