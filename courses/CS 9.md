# CS 9: Problem-Solving for the CS Technical Interview

One unit, twice a week, on the specific skill of solving algorithm problems under interview conditions. The prerequisite is labeled "soft" on the site itself.

## Materials

Nine of the seventeen decks are public, all of them problem sessions; the eight lecture decks need a Stanford login, as do the recordings.

- **Syllabus**: the [syllabus page](https://web.stanford.edu/class/cs9/syllabus) — course goals, grading, and what each half of the week does.
- **Slides**: the [slides directory](https://web.stanford.edu/class/cs9/slides/) serves the nine Thursday decks (`w1-2` through `w9-2`, 320–800 KB each). The eight Tuesday decks — overview of tech recruiting, resumes and behavioral, resume reviews and career fairs, coding challenges, technical interviews 1 and 2, offers and negotiation, final thoughts and ML interviews — are linked from the same page under `slides/restricted/` and redirect to Stanford login.
- **Videos**: lectures were recorded on a staff laptop and uploaded to Canvas Panopto.
- **Assignments**: submission ran through Gradescope; nothing is posted beyond the session PDFs.
- **Notes**, **Solutions**, **Code**: not found. **Exams**, **Projects**: none.

## Syllabus

From the [syllabus page](https://web.stanford.edu/class/cs9/syllabus): Tuesday lectures walk the CS technical recruiting process — resumes and behavioral screens, career fairs, coding challenges, the technical interview itself, then offers, negotiation and ethics, closing on ML interviews. Thursday sessions are practice: "three problems of varying difficulty", half the class to attempt one or two, half spent walking a solution through together. The nine published Thursday decks are those problem sets.
