# CS 330: Deep Multi-task and Meta Learning

The premise is that a model trained for one task wastes the structure shared with every neighboring task. The course works through the ways to exploit it: share a network across tasks, pre-train and fine-tune, or learn the learning algorithm itself — black-box, optimization-based (MAML, from this instructor), or metric-based. Four PyTorch assignments and a poster-session project.

## Materials

One of the more completely public courses in the catalog: slides, homework PDFs, starter code and templates are all on the open site, and three previous offerings' lectures are on YouTube.

- **Syllabus**: the [course page](https://cs330.stanford.edu/) is the schedule — each lecture with its deck, its optional reading (linked to arXiv) and what's due that day.

- **Code**: the starter code, plus a Colab notebook for the week-1 PyTorch tutorial.
- **Exams**: none — four graded homeworks, one optional, and a project.

## Syllabus

[The schedule](https://cs330.stanford.edu/) moves from sharing a model to learning to learn.

1. Sharing (Sep 27-Oct 4): multi-task learning, weighting losses across tasks, transfer learning and fine-tuning — including when fine-tuning distorts pretrained features.
2. Meta-learning (Oct 9-16): black-box meta-learning and in-context learning, optimization-based meta-learning and MAML, few-shot learning via metric learning with Matching and Prototypical Networks.
3. Pre-training (Oct 18-23): contrastive methods (CPC, SimCLR) and generative ones (BERT) as routes to few-shot ability.
4. Advanced topics (Oct 25 onward): task construction, variational inference, Bayesian meta-learning, domain adaptation and generalization, then open challenges.

## Prerequisites

- Machine learning: [CS 229](CS%20229.md) or equivalent — SGD, cross-validation, probability, multivariable calculus, linear algebra.
- Some deep learning: backpropagation, CNNs and RNNs. Assignments are PyTorch; week 1 includes a review tutorial.
