# CS 264: Beyond Worst-Case Analysis

Built on the observation that worst-case bounds often rank the wrong algorithm first: each unit swaps in a different model — smoothed inputs, stable instances, planted structure, predictions — and asks what can still be proved. Follows Roughgarden's book, which Stanford licenses electronically; four homeworks and a project.

## Materials

The schedule and its lecture notes are public; homework lives on Canvas.

- **Syllabus**: the [schedule](https://vitercik.github.io/bwca/schedule/) maps each lecture to a chapter of Roughgarden's *Beyond the Worst-Case Analysis of Algorithms* and to supplemental papers; [course information](https://vitercik.github.io/bwca/) gives the 60/40 homework-project split and [policies](https://vitercik.github.io/bwca/policies/).
- **Notes**: fourteen sets of typed [lecture notes](https://vitercik.github.io/bwca/assets/notes/l1.pdf), one per lecture, plus intro slides.
- **Assignments**: the [project page](https://vitercik.github.io/bwca/project/) carries the handout and rubric; the four homeworks are posted on Canvas and submitted through Gradescope, so they aren't public.
- **Exams**: none.
- The course's [original Roughgarden version](https://timroughgarden.org/w17/w17.html) (Winter 2017) is still online with its own notes and videos.

## Syllabus

Fourteen lectures ([schedule](https://vitercik.github.io/bwca/schedule/)) in nine blocks: motivating examples and instance optimality (caching, linear programming, clustering); instance optimality in computational geometry; resource augmentation and online paging; parameterized algorithms and condition numbers; perturbation stability for clustering; spectral algorithms for planted bisection and planted clique; semi-random models and semidefinite programming; nonnegative matrix factorization; random-order models; smoothed analysis; automated algorithm configuration; and algorithms with predictions. The last sessions are project presentations.
