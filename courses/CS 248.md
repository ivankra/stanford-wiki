# CS 248: Interactive Computer Graphics

The breadth course the rest of the graphics sequence assumes: how a triangle becomes pixels, what the GPU pipeline actually does, how geometry is represented and subdivided, and what it takes to move any of it convincingly. [CS 348B](CS%20348B.md)'s site names this course as the background it expects.

## Materials

Published on the graphics group's own course host, with a page per lecture.

- **Syllabus**: the [site](https://gfxcourses.stanford.edu/cs248/winter22) gives the description with a full topic list, and a [lecture index](https://gfxcourses.stanford.edu/cs248/winter22/lecture/) with a page per session.
- **Slides**: each lecture page carries its deck, as on the other `gfxcourses` sites.
- **Assignments**: partial. Nothing is linked from the site, but the [`stanford-cs248`](https://github.com/stanford-cs248) organization — shared with [CS 248A](CS%20248A.md) — holds this course's own repos: [assignment 1](https://github.com/stanford-cs248/draw-svg), "Implement a simple SVG renderer"; [assignment 3](https://github.com/stanford-cs248/shading), real-time shading; and the [final-project instructions](https://github.com/stanford-cs248/project). Assignments 2 and 4 were not found.
- **Videos**: lectures were virtual, but no recordings are linked.
- **Exams**: none.

## Syllabus

[The topic list](https://gfxcourses.stanford.edu/cs248/winter22) runs across four areas rather than in a single line: drawing and rasterization (2D and 3D drawing, sampling, interpolation, image compositing), the GPU pipeline and parallel rendering, geometry (transformations, curves and surfaces, subdivision, meshing, spatial hierarchies), and image processing, compression, time integration, physically based animation and inverse kinematics.
