# CS 248: Interactive Computer Graphics

The breadth course the rest of the graphics sequence assumes: how a triangle becomes pixels, what the GPU pipeline actually does, how geometry is represented and subdivided, and what it takes to move any of it convincingly. [CS 348B](CS%20348B.md)'s site names this course as the background it expects.

## Materials

All 3 offerings keep their whole site on the graphics group's host — schedule, a page per lecture with the deck, the written exercises and the assignment repositories — and the only gaps are the recordings, which go to Canvas, and the exam.

- **Syllabus**: the [site](https://gfxcourses.stanford.edu/cs248/winter22) dates 20 lectures and gives each a paragraph of topics; the [course info page](https://gfxcourses.stanford.edu/cs248/winter22/courseinfo) holds the grading split (3 programming assignments 50%, written exercises 10%, final project 20%, one exam 20%), the 5 late-day points and the 2-student team rule. [Winter 2020](https://gfxcourses.stanford.edu/cs248/winter20/) and [Winter 2021](https://gfxcourses.stanford.edu/cs248/winter21) keep theirs at their own term paths.
- **Slides**: each lecture page under [/lecture/](https://gfxcourses.stanford.edu/cs248/winter22/lecture/) carries its deck as slide images and a PDF, as on the other `gfxcourses` sites. Winter 2021 publishes 19 and Winter 2020 20, the latter reachable only by name because its Lectures nav entry is commented out.
- **Notes**: none beyond the decks; the course sets no required textbook.
- **Videos**: this was a virtual quarter, and the course info page says lectures "will be recorded for offline viewing on Canvas". No public set exists for any offering.
- **Assignments**: the home page links all 4 as public repositories — [draw-svg](https://github.com/stanford-cs248/draw-svg), an SVG renderer; [Cardinal3D](https://github.com/stanford-cs248/Cardinal3D), the mesh editor; [shading](https://github.com/stanford-cs248/shading), real-time shading; and the [final project](https://github.com/stanford-cs248/project) — and serves 7 written exercises as PDFs. Winter 2021 posted 8 and Winter 2020 6.
- **Solutions**: nothing published.
- **Exams**: one exam worth 20%, taken virtually this offering; no paper is published.
- **Projects**: the final project's instructions are public, but no student work is.
- **Repo**: the starter code is in those same repositories, in the [stanford-cs248](https://github.com/stanford-cs248) organization this course shares with [CS 248A](CS%20248A.md).

## Syllabus

[The topic list](https://gfxcourses.stanford.edu/cs248/winter22) runs across four areas rather than in a single line: drawing and rasterization (2D and 3D drawing, sampling, interpolation, image compositing), the GPU pipeline and parallel rendering, geometry (transformations, curves and surfaces, subdivision, meshing, spatial hierarchies), and image processing, compression, time integration, physically based animation and inverse kinematics.
