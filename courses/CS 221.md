# CS 221: Artificial Intelligence: Principles and Techniques

Stanford's broad introduction to AI: the foundational principles behind learning, search, planning under uncertainty, probabilistic reasoning and logic, each with a written-and-programming homework built around an application. Graded mostly on an in-person final exam.

## Materials

Nearly everything is public; recordings, Ed and submissions sit behind Canvas and Gradescope. The [course site](https://stanford-cs221.github.io/autumn2026/) has the full syllabus and schedule and links each homework (page plus zip with starter code and grader) as it's released. Lecture slides and code are in the [lectures repo](https://github.com/stanford-cs221/lectures).

- **Assignments**: only HW1 is out; the [Autumn 2025 site](https://stanford-cs221.github.io/autumn2025/) has all eight homeworks.
- **Videos**: the [Autumn 2024 modules page](https://stanford-cs221.github.io/autumn2024/modules/index.html) links short YouTube videos recorded in Autumn 2021, with slides.
- **Notes and solutions**: the [Autumn 2024 site](https://stanford-cs221.github.io/autumn2024/) has weekly section problems, slides and solutions.
- **Projects**: eight undated past reports in a [sample-projects repo](https://github.com/stanford-cs221/sample-projects).

## Syllabus

Nineteen lectures over ten weeks, grouped by unit with the homework released alongside:

1. Overview; learning I–III (HW: foundations, sentiment classification)
2. Search I–II (HW: route planning)
3. Reinforcement learning I–IV (HW: controlling mountaincar)
4. Games I–II (HW: Pac-Man)
5. Bayesian networks I–III (HW: Bayesian networks)
6. Logic I–II (HW: from language to logic)
7. AI & society; fireside chat and conclusion
