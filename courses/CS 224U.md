# CS 224U: Natural Language Understanding

Where [CS 224N](CS%20224N.md) teaches the models, this teaches doing research with them. Three assignment-and-bakeoff pairs — a shared task with a public leaderboard — carry the technical half, and the back half of the quarter is method: how to write a literature review, design an experiment protocol, choose metrics, present results. It is built to be taken asynchronously, and the codebase and slides are maintained publicly across offerings.

## Materials

Among the most openly published courses in the catalog, and deliberately so: the site says the course can be taken entirely online.

- **Syllabus**: the [course site](https://web.stanford.edu/class/cs224u/) gives each session its handout and a reading list often a dozen papers long, alongside [policies](https://web.stanford.edu/class/cs224u/requirements.html), [projects](https://web.stanford.edu/class/cs224u/projects.html) and [background](https://web.stanford.edu/class/cs224u/background.html) pages.
- **Slides**: a handout PDF for every session, [introduction](https://web.stanford.edu/class/cs224u/slides/cs224u-intro-2023-handout.pdf) onward, including guest segments on diffusion objectives for text and on building language models.
- **Assignments and code**: the [public repository](https://github.com/cgpotts/cs224u/) holds the notebooks the assignments and bakeoffs run on.
- **Videos**: unknown. The site says meetings are recorded and that core content is delivered partly by video, but links no playlist.
- **Projects**: the requirements are public, and three sessions are devoted to research method.
- Also public: a [podcast](https://web.stanford.edu/class/cs224u/podcast/index.html) of interviews with NLP researchers, and eleven previous offerings archived under year directories back to 2012.

## Syllabus

[The schedule](https://web.stanford.edu/class/cs224u/) alternates technical sessions with assignment overviews.

1. Foundations (Apr 3-5): what "understanding" would mean — Levesque, Manning, the Foundation Models report — then contextual word representations from the Transformer through BERT, RoBERTa, ELECTRA and T5.
2. Methods (mid-quarter): information retrieval, in-context learning, advanced behavioral evaluation of NLU models, analysis methods.
3. Research practice (late quarter): literature review, experiment protocol, NLP methods and metrics, presenting your research.

Three assignment/bakeoff pairs run through the first half; the final project carries the rest.

## Prerequisites

The catalog lists none. The reading assumes a first NLP or machine learning course, and the assignments are Python notebooks.
