# CS 224U: Natural Language Understanding

Where [CS 224N](CS%20224N.md) teaches the models, this teaches doing research with them. Three assignment-and-bakeoff pairs — a shared task with a public leaderboard — carry the technical half, and the back half of the quarter is method: how to write a literature review, design an experiment protocol, choose metrics, present results. It is built to be taken asynchronously, and the codebase and slides are maintained publicly across offerings.

## Materials

The site, the slide handouts, the whole codebase, this offering's own screencasts and 11 years of archived offerings are public. What stays behind the login is the assessment: the quizzes and the past students' papers.

- **Syllabus**: the [course site](https://web.stanford.edu/class/cs224u/) gives each session its handout and a reading list often a dozen papers long, alongside [policies](https://web.stanford.edu/class/cs224u/requirements.html) (quizzes 15%, homeworks and bakeoffs 35%, literature review 10%, protocol 10%, final paper 30%), [projects](https://web.stanford.edu/class/cs224u/projects.html) and [background](https://web.stanford.edu/class/cs224u/background.html) pages.
- **Slides**: a handout PDF for every session, [introduction](https://web.stanford.edu/class/cs224u/slides/cs224u-intro-2023-handout.pdf) onward, guest segments on diffusion objectives for text and on building language models included. [Spring 2021](https://web.stanford.edu/class/cs224u/2021/) posts 58 and [Spring 2022](https://web.stanford.edu/class/cs224u/2022/) 53.
- **Notes**: the [background index](https://web.stanford.edu/class/cs224u/background.html) is the written course, pairing each topic with a handout and an explanatory notebook — Jupyter, NumPy and PyTorch tutorials, vector-space designs and distances, dimensionality reduction, retrofitting, supervised sentiment.
- **Videos**: this offering's own [50 screencasts](https://www.youtube.com/playlist?list=PLoROMvodv4rOwvldxftJTmoR3kRcWkJBp) are on Stanford Online, 2 to 10 per unit, following the 2023 handouts from contextual word representations through information retrieval, in-context learning, behavioral evaluation and analysis methods to presenting research, plus an overview per bakeoff. - **Assignments**: the 3 homework-and-bakeoff notebooks — sentiment, ReCOGS, open-domain QA — in the [public repository](https://github.com/cgpotts/cs224u/), and the [project requirements](https://web.stanford.edu/class/cs224u/projects.html) with 3 Overleaf templates and a paper template that all open.
- **Solutions**: none published; the bakeoffs are scored on a held-out test set rather than against a key, and no source says whether enrolled students see worked answers.
- **Exams**: there is no exam, and the requirements page's Exam section is commented out. The 5 quizzes that carry 15% of the grade are Canvas quizzes.
- **Projects**: the past final papers sit under [`restricted/past-final-projects/`](https://web.stanford.edu/class/cs224u/restricted/past-final-projects/), which redirects to Stanford SSO.
- **Repo**: [cgpotts/cs224u](https://github.com/cgpotts/cs224u/), Apache-2.0 and maintained across offerings: the NumPy and PyTorch model classes, the SST and vector-space modules, and every notebook the schedule links.
- **Extra**: a [podcast](https://web.stanford.edu/class/cs224u/podcast/index.html) of interviews with NLP researchers — Bommasani, Kiela, Khattab, Socher, Pavlick, Liang and others — and every offering back to 2012 archived under its year.

## Syllabus

[The schedule](https://web.stanford.edu/class/cs224u/) alternates technical sessions with assignment overviews.

1. Foundations (Apr 3-5): what "understanding" would mean — Levesque, Manning, the Foundation Models report — then contextual word representations from the Transformer through BERT, RoBERTa, ELECTRA and T5.
2. Methods (mid-quarter): information retrieval, in-context learning, advanced behavioral evaluation of NLU models, analysis methods.
3. Research practice (late quarter): literature review, experiment protocol, NLP methods and metrics, presenting your research.

Three assignment/bakeoff pairs run through the first half; the final project carries the rest.

## Prerequisites

The catalog lists none. The reading assumes a first NLP or machine learning course, and the assignments are Python notebooks.
