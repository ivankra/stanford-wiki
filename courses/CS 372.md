# CS 372: Artificial General Intelligence for Reasoning, Planning, and Decision Making

Edward Chang's course takes a position rather than surveying one: that LLMs are stuck in reactive pattern matching, and that the route past it is orchestrating several of them against each other under explicit validation. The vehicle is MACI, his own multi-LLM collaborative intelligence framework, and the reading is his two-volume AGI book. Lectures alternate between concrete machinery — Socratic prompting, epistemic regret minimization, SagaLLM's transaction guarantees for agent workflows, transactional swarm orchestration — and two sessions on computational consciousness and free will. Projects target planning problems in supply chains, emergency-room logistics and disaster response, and are meant to beat frameworks like LangGraph.

## Syllabus

Eighteen sessions over ten weeks, per the [schedule](http://infolab.stanford.edu/~echang/cs372/cs372-syllabus.html), interleaving three threads the page labels Reasoning, AGI and ASI.

1. **Weeks 1–3, reasoning**: can LLMs reach AGI at all; Pearl's causality hierarchy; a PID control architecture for sycophancy and skepticism; prompting with the Socratic method; a recap that turns into the planning introduction.
2. **Weeks 4–5, collaboration and consciousness**: multi-LLM agent collaborative intelligence; what consciousness is and a System 2 introduction; a MACI survey with the pathologies of multi-agent debate systems; computational consciousness, free will and ethics modeling.
3. **Weeks 6–7, mechanisms**: epistemic regret minimization; SagaLLM — context management, validation and transaction guarantees for multi-agent planning.
4. **Weeks 7–8**: two midterm project dash sessions.
5. **Weeks 9–10**: transactional swarm orchestration; Collatz dynamics as a model world for temporal-spatial optimization; then final presentations.
