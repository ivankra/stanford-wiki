# CS 182: Ethics, Public Policy, and Technological Change

Co-taught by a computer scientist and a political philosopher, and assessed the way a philosophy course is: five written assignments, one of them a philosophy paper with its own TA session on how to write one, and a take-home final. Fourteen Friday discussion sections run alongside the MWF lectures.

## Syllabus

The catalog sets out four units, each considering "the promise, perils, rights, and responsibilities at play":

1. **Algorithmic decision-making and bias.**
2. **Data privacy and civil liberties.**
3. **The power of private computing platforms.**
4. **The impact of generative AI.**

Assessment runs five assignments across the quarter — released late January, mid-February, 23 February and 9 March — mixing programming work with a philosophy paper.

## Prerequisites

- Programming: [CS 106A](CS%20106A.md). No philosophy background is assumed.
