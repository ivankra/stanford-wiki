# CS 336: Language Modeling from Scratch

Every layer of a language model, built by hand: tokenizer, transformer, Triton kernels, multi-GPU training loop, Common Crawl pipeline, scaling law, and post-training. The course takes its shape from operating-systems courses that build an OS from scratch, and gives students "minimal scaffolding" — the site warns the code volume is "at least an order of magnitude greater than for other classes".

## Materials

Fully open, and deliberately so: the site includes cloud-GPU pricing for readers "following along at home".

- **Syllabus**: the [schedule](https://cs336.stanford.edu/#schedule) lists all nineteen lectures with their material and assignment deadlines; logistics and policies are on the same page.
- **Slides**: PDFs for lectures 3, 4, 5, 8, 9, 11, 15 and 16 in the [lectures repository](https://github.com/stanford-cs336/lectures).
- **Notes**: the other lectures are *executable lectures* — a Python trace per lecture, stepped through in the site's [viewer](https://cs336.stanford.edu/lectures/?trace=lecture_01).
- **Videos**: the [Spring 2026 playlist](https://www.youtube.com/playlist?list=PLoROMvodv4rMqXOcazWaTUHhq-yembLCV) on Stanford Online.
- **Assignments**: five public repositories, each with a handout PDF — [basics](https://github.com/stanford-cs336/assignment1-basics/tree/main), [systems](https://github.com/stanford-cs336/assignment2-systems/tree/main), [scaling](https://github.com/stanford-cs336/assignment3-scaling/tree/main), [data](https://github.com/stanford-cs336/assignment4-data/tree/main) and [alignment](https://github.com/stanford-cs336/assignment5-alignment/tree/main).
- **Exams**: none.
- **Projects**: none; the five assignments are the whole coursework.

- Grading and submission run through Gradescope and Slack, neither public.

## Syllabus

Nineteen lectures over ten weeks, per the [schedule](https://cs336.stanford.edu/#schedule), each assignment opening the unit it belongs to.

1. **Basics**: overview and tokenization; PyTorch and `einops`; resource accounting in FLOPs, memory and arithmetic intensity; architectures and hyperparameters; attention alternatives and mixture of experts.
2. **Systems**: GPUs and TPUs; kernels and Triton; two lectures on parallelism.
3. **Scaling**: two lectures on scaling laws, with inference and evaluation between them.
4. **Data**: sources and datasets, then filtering, deduplication, mixing and synthetic data.
5. **Alignment**: mid- and post-training (SFT and RLHF), RLVR, and multimodality.

Guest lectures from Daniel Selsam and Dan Fu close the term.

## Prerequisites

The course site expects:

- Python and software engineering: enough to write an order of magnitude more code than a typical AI class, with almost no starter scaffolding.
- Deep learning and systems: strong familiarity with PyTorch, and systems concepts like the memory hierarchy, because much of the course is making models run fast across GPUs and machines.
- Calculus and linear algebra: MATH 51 or CME 100.
- Probability and statistics: [CS 109](CS%20109.md) or equivalent.
- Machine learning: [CS 221](CS%20221.md), [CS 229](CS%20229.md), [CS 230](CS%20230.md), [CS 124](CS%20124.md) or [CS 224N](CS%20224N.md).
