# CS 123: A Hands-On Introduction to Building AI-Enabled Robots

Twenty-four students, eight teams, one quadruped each: the labs walk from ROS and PD control through kinematics and gait design to reinforcement learning, vision and language, and the robot they build — Pupper — is the thing being graded. Taught by two Google DeepMind roboticists with a Stanford co-instructor.

## Syllabus

Seven labs, one per week, each paired with a lecture:

1. ROS introduction and PD control.
2. Forward kinematics.
3. Inverse kinematics and trajectory tracking.
4. Model-based control and a trotting gait.
5. Reinforcement learning — "How to Train Your Dog".
6. Large language models for robotics — "Do What I Say".
7. Computer vision for robotics — "The World I See".

The remaining weeks are the project: agile locomotion, a vision system so Pupper plays fetch, or an LLM interface. Two optional labs, released in weeks 3 and 4, are deliberately harder than the course and have no deadlines.

Grading is group-based for labs and project, with individual quizzes and a 3% attendance component. AI coding agents are explicitly allowed, discouraged on some labs, and encouraged on the project — "students are responsible for understanding, testing, and being able to explain the code and work they submit".

## Prerequisites

- Python: [CS 106A](CS%20106A.md) — all labs are in Python.
- Command line: [CS 107](CS%20107.md); the site says familiarity with the terminal is enough.
- Mathematics: MATH 51 or CME 100, for gradients.
- No robotics background is expected.
