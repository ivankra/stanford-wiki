# CS 208E: Great Ideas in Computer Science

Gregg's survey of what computer science is *about*, built for people who will not go on to take [CS 106B](CS%20106B.md): it opens on Babbage's analytical engine, works through algorithms, binary representation and how a microchip is actually fabricated, and lands on public key cryptography. The assignments match the register — program the analytical engine, build logic gates a toddler could operate.

## Materials

Everything is public, including a course reader that stands on its own as a text, and the lecture directory is a plain open listing rather than a page of links. Nothing is gated; the only thing unaccounted for is recordings, which no source mentions.

- **Syllabus**: a [dated lecture list](https://web.stanford.edu/class/cs208e/syllabus) with a page per session, alongside FAQ, getting-help and honor-code pages, and the 50/50 split between weekly assignments and the final project.
- **Slides**: the [lecture directory](https://web.stanford.edu/class/cs208e/lectures/) holds a folder per session, and 18 of the 20 carry a deck, usually as both Keynote and PDF — Babbage machines, the analytical engine, digital logic, stored-program machines, Turing machines, uncomputable functions, P vs NP, cryptography, "Reflections on Trusting Trust", self-driving cars, quantum computing. - **Notes**: the complete [course reader](https://web.stanford.edu/class/cs208e/reader/CS54N-CS208E-Reader.pdf), Eric Roberts's, 66 MB and shared with CS 54N, plus a [style guide](https://web.stanford.edu/class/cs208e/resources/style_guide.html), a [testing guide](https://web.stanford.edu/class/cs208e/resources/testing_guide.html) and a submission checklist.
- **Videos**: nothing mentioned on the site, and nothing turned up under this code, under CS 54N or under the instructor's name.
- **Assignments**: [6](https://web.stanford.edu/class/cs208e/assignments/), each its own page — the analytical engine, educational programming, logic gates and Toddler, Turing machines, encryption, and a final project.
- **Solutions**: none posted. **Exams**: none; the grade is the assignments and the project.
- **Projects**: the final-project brief is public with the assignments; no student project is.

## Syllabus

[Twenty lectures](https://web.stanford.edu/class/cs208e/syllabus) from September 20.

1. Mechanism (9/20-9/24): Babbage machines, then the analytical engine.
2. Programs (9/27-10/4): introductory programming, algorithms, binary representation.
3. Hardware (10/8 onward): computer hardware and how microchips are fabricated, logic gates.
4. 10/29: public key cryptography.
