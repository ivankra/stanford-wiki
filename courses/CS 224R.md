# CS 224R: Deep Reinforcement Learning

Algorithms for learning behavior from experience with deep networks mapping high-dimensional observations to actions, taught through robotics and language models. Twice-weekly lectures, three PyTorch homeworks, an in-class midterm and a final project. The catalog calls it complementary to [CS 234](CS%20234.md), neither being a prerequisite for the other, with a more applied and deep-learning focus.

## Syllabus

10 weeks of paired lectures, per the [timeline](https://cs224r.stanford.edu/) and its decks, each anchored to one or two papers rather than a textbook.

1. **Weeks 1–2, imitation and policy gradients**: MDPs; imitation learning with diffusion policies and action chunking, then DAgger; REINFORCE and its off-policy form; actor-critic with learned value functions.
2. **Weeks 3–4, value-based and offline RL**: PPO against fully off-policy SAC; Q-learning made stable, with multi-step targets; offline RL through advantage-weighted regression and IQL's expectile regression; reward learning from goal classifiers and from human or AI preferences.
3. **Week 5, RL for LLMs**: instruction tuning, RLHF and DPO (guest Archit Sharma); reasoning from poker and Go search to o1, GRPO, R1-Zero and test-time compute (guest Noam Brown).
4. **Weeks 6–7**: model-based RL with learned simulators and planning; multi-task and goal-conditioned RL with hindsight relabeling; meta-RL, black-box and with learned exploration; then the midterm.
5. **Weeks 8–9, robots**: hierarchical imitation and RL with language and image subgoals; sim-to-real with domain randomization, RMA and real-to-sim (guest Guanya Shi); RL on pretrained vision-language-action models; frontiers and how to do research.
6. **Weeks 10–11**: poster session and final report.

## History

First offered in Spring 2023 by Chelsea Finn and Karol Hausman. Finn has taught it alone since 2025.

- **Spring 2023**: a robotics course, with 2 lectures each on offline RL and meta-RL, one on practical implementation techniques, hierarchical RL with skill discovery, and guest lectures on transfer learning (Jie Tan), reset-free RL (Archit Sharma) and RL in the real world (Anna Goldie). 4 homeworks ran from DAgger to meta-RL with DREAM, beside a project students define; no exam.
- **Spring 2025**: language models arrive in week 5, with guest lectures on preference optimization (Archit Sharma) and reasoning (Aviral Kumar), and a default project that fine-tunes Qwen 2.5 0.5B with DPO and RLOO. Offline RL and meta-RL shrink to a lecture each, an exploration lecture follows meta-RL, hierarchy pairs with imitation, the imitation lecture takes up diffusion policies, and the robot weeks cover autonomous learning and sim-to-real (guest Ashish Kumar). Stanford Online posted all [19 lectures](https://www.youtube.com/playlist?list=PLoROMvodv4rPwxE0ONYRa_itZFdaKCylL).
- **Spring 2026**: Noam Brown gives the reasoning lecture, and RL for vision-language-action models replaces the exploration and autonomous-learning lectures. CQL leaves the offline RL lecture and its homework, an in-class midterm arrives as the goal-conditioned and meta-RL homework goes, and the default project adds an SFT stage. Homework 1 adds flow-matching policies, which no deck teaches beyond a mention; the project's RLOO, used since 2025, appears in no deck at all.

## Prerequisites

- Machine learning: [CS 229](CS%20229.md) or equivalent — stochastic gradient descent, cross-validation, probability, multivariable calculus and linear algebra.
- Deep learning: backpropagation, convolutional networks and transformers; assignments are in PyTorch, with a review tutorial in week 1.
- Reinforcement learning basics: the site points at [CS 221](CS%20221.md)'s MDP and RL lectures or chapters 3–4 of Sutton & Barto.
