# CS 114: Selected Reading of Computer Science Research

Eight papers, eight authors, each visiting to discuss their own work — from theory and architecture to robotics, sensing, security and systems. The subject is comparative: not the results, but how each field decides what counts as a problem and how to present a solution.

## Syllabus

From the [site](https://web.stanford.edu/class/cs114/): "Selected Readings of CS Research examines 8 selected papers by leading scholars in computer science and electrical engineering. The papers cover a wide range of subfields within CS and EE. Students taking this course will learn about a broad view of research fields in EE and CS, including what kinds of problems different fields tackle as well as how they think about them. Students learn how to read research papers and see how different fields structure and present their ideas."

The eight visiting authors: Jelani Nelson (UC Berkeley), Kunle Olukotun (Stanford), Monroe Kennedy (Stanford), Kris Dorsey (Northeastern), Jeremiah Onaolapo (University of Vermont), Anthony Joseph (UC Berkeley), James Mickens (Harvard), Audrey Bowden (Vanderbilt).

The course is open to three audiences at once: "Undergraduates interested in research, beginning graduate students who want to learn about how to approach publications, and more senior graduate students who want to read a selection of excellent work from a wide variety of fields."
