# CS 31N: Counterfactuals: The Science of What Ifs?

A seminar on one technique and its reach: what would have happened otherwise. The catalog's own examples set the range — whether climate change worsened economic inequality, and whether historical medical records can be used to design better treatment protocols.

## Syllabus

No published syllabus. The catalog describes the content: "In this course I will introduce some of the most common quantitative approaches to counterfactual reasoning, as well as give a wide sampling of some of the many important problems and questions that can be addressed through the lens of counterfactual reasoning, including in climate change, healthcare and economics."

It frames counterfactual inference as serving two purposes at once — "understand the impact of past decisions" and "inform future choices" — and states that no prior experience with counterfactual reasoning or probability is required.
