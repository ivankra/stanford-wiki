# CS 238V: Validation of Safety Critical Systems

The question this course asks is not how to build an autonomous system but how to argue it will not fail: specify the property in temporal logic, hunt for a counterexample by optimization, estimate the failure probability when counterexamples are rare, and then prove reachability bounds where you can. Everything is worked in Julia through Pluto notebooks against simulators from air traffic control, aircraft taxiing and self-driving. The course is built on its instructors' own textbook, which doubles as the lecture notes, and attendance — including for exams — is explicitly not required.

## Syllabus

Twelve chapters over ten weeks, per the [schedule](https://docs.google.com/spreadsheets/d/1abqRaImmCEy7p5XKknAhVRqQB6VjOXRtJ4u2vZEosFY/edit?usp=sharing), each lecture keyed to the textbook.

1. **Weeks 1–2, setting up the problem**: system modeling; property specification in temporal logic.
2. **Weeks 3–4, falsification**: finding failures by optimization, then by planning; the failure distribution.
3. **Weeks 5–6, estimating rare failures**: a guest lecture on adaptive stress testing; importance sampling, adaptive importance sampling, and the ratio of normalizing constants.
4. **Weeks 7–8, formal methods**: reachability for linear systems, for nonlinear systems, and for discrete systems; a guest lecture from Waymo.
5. **Week 9**: explainability and runtime monitoring. Week 10 is left clear for the final project.

## Prerequisites

- Basic probability theory — the sampling half of the course is estimation of rare-event probabilities.
- Multivariable calculus.
- Fluency in a high-level programming language. The projects are in Julia; a dedicated page on the site introduces it, and no prior Julia is assumed.
