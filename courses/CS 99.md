# CS 99: Functional Programming and Theorem Proving in Lean 4

One unit, sixteen lectures, and an unusual shape: Lean is taught as a programming language first and a proof assistant second, so the functional-programming third has to land before any theorem proving starts. Built by the Centaur Lab and published under a Creative Commons license.

## Materials

Everything is public and runnable — the handouts and exercises are Lean source files, not PDFs about Lean.

- **Syllabus**: the [dated lecture table](https://web.stanford.edu/class/cs99/), with per-lecture readings and exercises, plus the overview, prerequisites and a reference list.
- **Slides**: a [reveal.js deck per lecture](https://web.stanford.edu/class/cs99/lecture/fp-control), each also servable as `?print-pdf`.
- **Notes**: a [`handout.lean`](https://web.stanford.edu/class/cs99/lecture/fp-control/handout.lean) beside every lecture, with extra files where the topic needs them — `stdio`, `tasks`, `ref-race`, `ref-sync`, `cancel` and `channel` for the concurrency lecture, `rsa.lean` for number theory.
- **Assignments**: [ten exercise files](https://web.stanford.edu/class/cs99/assignment/a01.lean), a01–a09 and a14.
- **Videos**, **Solutions**: not found. **Exams**, **Projects**: none.

## Syllabus

The [table](https://web.stanford.edu/class/cs99/) divides the quarter into thirds, as the site says: "Functional Programming, Theorem Proving, and Metaprogramming."

1. **Functional programming** (lectures 1–5): proof assistants as an idea; control flow, recursion, structures and inductives; type polymorphism, classes and instances; monads for impurity and fallibility; IO, exceptions and concurrency.
2. **Theorem proving** (lectures 6–14): the calculus of inductive constructions; congruence and subtyping; tactics and the existential quantifier; rewrite, simp and tactic combinators; then the mathematics — sets and functions, elementary number theory, abstract and linear algebra, convergence and differentiation, computability and termination.
3. **Metaprogramming** (lectures 15–16): compilation and the kernel; elaboration, macros and tactics.

Readings point into the Lean Reference Manual, *Functional Programming in Lean*, *Theorem Proving in Lean* and *Metaprogramming in Lean 4*.

## Prerequisites

None formally. The site asks for "a basic understanding of programming, mathematical proofs, and calculus", with functional programming and logic "strongly recommended", and is explicit that the mathematics "stops at differential calculus".
