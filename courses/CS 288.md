# CS 288: Applied Causal Inference with Machine Learning and AI

The practical version of the causal-inference-meets-ML literature, taught by one of the authors of the textbook it uses. The move is to keep the classical apparatus — potential outcomes, DAGs, adjustment sets, structural equation models — and swap the estimation step for lasso, random forests or neural nets, then recover valid confidence intervals anyway. Graded almost entirely on eight homeworks that alternate proofs with notebooks on real data.

## Materials

Everything except the homework is public, including the textbook and its notebook repository.

- **Syllabus**: the [course site](https://stanford-msande228.github.io/winter25/) gives format, grading (homework 90%, participation 10%) and a [lecture schedule](https://stanford-msande228.github.io/winter25/calendar/) where each session carries required reading and a "further reading" list running back to Fisher and Neyman.
- **Slides**: all eighteen decks, in PPTX and PDF, from the introduction through DAGs, difference-in-differences and heterogeneous treatment effects.
- **Notes**: [*Applied Causal Inference Powered by ML and AI*](https://causalml-book.org), the open-access book by Chernozhukov, Hansen, Kallus, Spindler and the instructor, is the course text and is cited chapter by chapter.
- **Code**: the book's [notebook repository](https://github.com/CausalAIBook/MetricsMLNotebooks), plus a Colab demo linked from most lectures.
- **Videos**: none — "Lectures will be held in person and will not be recorded."
- **Assignments**: closed. Eight homeworks go out on Canvas and come back through Gradescope.

## Syllabus

[The schedule](https://stanford-msande228.github.io/winter25/calendar/) moves from experiments to observational data and then to heterogeneity.

1. Experiments (weeks 1-2): causality via randomized experiments, analyzing experiments with OLS, inference in linear models, the role of covariates in precision.
2. High dimensions (weeks 3-5): prediction and then inference in high-dimensional linear models, modern nonlinear prediction with forests and neural networks, and inference with those nonlinear methods.
3. Observational data (weeks 6-8): causality without an experiment, valid adjustment sets, DAGs, structural equation models, unobserved confounding.
4. Designs and heterogeneity (weeks 9-10): difference-in-differences, dynamic treatment regimes, heterogeneous treatment effects.

## Prerequisites

- Probability and statistics. The catalog and site recommend MS&E 226 or equivalent; the catalog also asks for mathematical maturity in optimization, linear algebra and calculus.
