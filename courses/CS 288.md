# CS 288: Applied Causal Inference with Machine Learning and AI

The practical version of the causal-inference-meets-ML literature, taught by one of the authors of the textbook it uses. The move is to keep the classical apparatus — potential outcomes, DAGs, adjustment sets, structural equation models — and swap the estimation step for lasso, random forests or neural nets, then recover valid confidence intervals anyway. Graded almost entirely on 8 homeworks that alternate proofs with notebooks on real data.

## Syllabus

[The schedule](https://stanford-msande228.github.io/winter25/calendar/) moves from experiments to observational data and then to heterogeneity.

1. Experiments (weeks 1-2): causality via randomized experiments, analyzing experiments with OLS, inference in linear models, the role of covariates in precision.
2. High dimensions (weeks 3-5): prediction and then inference in high-dimensional linear models, modern nonlinear prediction with forests and neural networks, and inference with those nonlinear methods.
3. Observational data (weeks 6-8): causality without an experiment, valid adjustment sets, DAGs, structural equation models, unobserved confounding.
4. Designs and heterogeneity (weeks 9-10): difference-in-differences, dynamic treatment regimes, heterogeneous treatment effects.

## Prerequisites
