# CS 224C: NLP for Computational Social Science

NLP pointed at social-science questions rather than benchmarks: what Reddit's 2015 ban actually did to hate speech, what a century of word embeddings says about stereotypes, whether a language model can stand in for a survey respondent. Causal inference sits beside the modeling, because most of the data is observational. Graded mostly on a research project, with nine reading responses that are explicitly not summaries.

## Syllabus

[Twenty sessions](https://web.stanford.edu/class/cs224c/) from April 2.

1. Methods (Apr 2-11): computational social science as a field, hypothesis testing, working with text data, inferring sentiment and affect.
2. Social phenomena in text (Apr 16-25): social influence, framing and persuasion, topic modeling, networks and social media.
3. Representation (Apr 30): word embeddings, read against Garg et al. on a century of gender and ethnic stereotypes.
4. Applications (May 7 onward): hate speech and the efficacy of platform bans, fake news and misinformation, causal inference, prosocial behavior, and LLMs as models of social behavior.

Project proposals are due Apr 25, the midway report mid-term, and the last week is given to final projects.
