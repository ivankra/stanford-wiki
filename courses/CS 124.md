# CS 124: From Languages to Information

The undergraduate on-ramp to Stanford's NLP sequence, and unusually explicit about being usable from outside: "all the course material is public, and we encourage anyone who wants to learn the material to go through it on their own, as long as they don't ask the TAs to work for them!" It is flipped — lectures are prerecorded, and the Tuesday slot is five live lectures and five labs, none of them recorded.

## Materials

Slides, labs, lab solutions and every programming assignment are public. Only the prerecorded video sits behind Canvas.

- **Syllabus**: the [single-page site](https://web.stanford.edu/class/cs124/) — ten weeks of quizzes, labs, readings and the attendance rules for each session.
- **Slides**: about eighteen decks under [`/lec/`](https://web.stanford.edu/class/cs124/lec/llm_cs124_26.pdf), most as both `.pptx` and `.pdf`.
- **Notes**: the [labs repository](https://github.com/cs124/labs), five labs written out as Markdown.
- **Videos**: closed. Prerecorded lectures are in Canvas Modules, and the live sessions are deliberately not recorded — for the transformers quiz the site says "There is no recording, but the slides are listed above in week 6."
- **Assignments**: public repos under the [cs124 organization](https://github.com/cs124/pa1-regular-expressions).
- **Solutions**: published for labs 2, 3 and 5.
- **Exams**: none — no midterm, no final, and the scheduled final-exam slot goes unused.
- **Code**: the [organization](https://github.com/cs124) also holds the pa0 Jupyter tutorial and a NumPy tutorial.

## Syllabus

Ten weeks, each anchored by a Gradescope quiz, from the [schedule](https://web.stanford.edu/class/cs124/):

1. Tokenization and n-gram language modeling; Lab 1 on Unix text processing.
2. Logistic regression and text classification; Lab 2.
3. Information retrieval; Lab 3.
4. Embeddings; a live lecture on NLP for computational social science.
5. Neural networks.
6. Transformers — a live lecture on "LLMs and Transformers!", with both decks posted.
7. Speech processing.
8. Recommendation systems; Lab 4 on the final agent assignment and Git.
9. Pagerank and networks; Lab 5 on collaborative filtering and the ethical use of LLMs in the classroom.
10. A closing live lecture; the final programming assignment is due 11 March.

## Prerequisites

- Programming and data structures: [CS 106B](CS%20106B.md), plus Python at the level of [CS 106A](CS%20106A.md).
- Probability: [CS 109](CS%20109.md) or equivalent.
- Unix and programming maturity: [CS 107](CS%20107.md), which may be taken concurrently.
