# CS 124: From Languages to Information

The undergraduate on-ramp to Stanford's NLP sequence, and unusually explicit about being usable from outside: "all the course material is public, and we encourage anyone who wants to learn the material to go through it on their own, as long as they don't ask the TAs to work for them!" It is flipped — lectures are prerecorded, and the Tuesday slot is five live lectures and five labs, none of them recorded.

## Materials

An outside reader gets what an enrolled one does, and the FAQ says so: slides, labs with their solutions, every programming assignment as a public repository, and the prerecorded lectures on the course's own YouTube channel. Only this offering's copies of those videos, in Canvas, and the graded quizzes are out of reach.

- **Syllabus**: the [single-page site](https://web.stanford.edu/class/cs124/) — 10 weeks of quizzes, labs, readings and the attendance rules for each session, plus an FAQ that spells out what a non-student may use. Every offering back to Winter 2020 survives whole, 4 in [Stanford's class archive](https://web.stanford.edu/class/archive/cs/cs124/cs124.1244/) and Winter 2020 and [Winter 2025](https://web.archive.org/web/20250309090159/https://web.stanford.edu/class/cs124/) in the Internet Archive.
- **Slides**: the 18 decks the schedule links under [`lec/`](https://web.stanford.edu/class/cs124/lec/intro26.pdf), 17 of them also as `.pptx`.
- **Notes**: the [labs repository](https://github.com/cs124/labs) — lab 1 "Unix for Poets" as a deck, labs 2 to 5 as Markdown, with an information-retrieval handout beside them.
- **Videos**: the prerecorded lectures are on the course's own [YouTube channel](https://www.youtube.com/channel/UC_48v322owNVtORXuMeRmpA), an [80-video full course](https://www.youtube.com/playlist?list=PLaZQkZp6WhWyvdiP49JG-rjyTPck_hvEu) plus a playlist a week, which Stanford's NLP group dates to Spring 2021 and which the FAQ sends outside readers to. This offering's own copies are in Canvas Modules, and the 5 live lectures and 5 labs are not recorded at all: for the transformers quiz the site says "There is no recording, but the slides are listed above in week 6."
- **Assignments**: all 8, pa0 to pa7, as public repositories in the [cs124 organization](https://github.com/cs124/pa1-regular-expressions) — regular expressions, logistic regression, information retrieval, embeddings, neural networks, transformers, speech, and an agent.
- **Solutions**: a solutions file beside each of labs 2, 3, 4 and 5. The weekly Gradescope quizzes have none.
- **Exams**: none — no midterm, no final, and the scheduled final-exam slot goes unused.
- **Projects**: none; the course sets programming assignments and labs, not a term project.
- **Repo**: the same [organization](https://github.com/cs124), 14 repositories, holds each assignment's starter code, the pa0 Jupyter tutorial and a NumPy tutorial.

## Syllabus

Ten weeks, each anchored by a Gradescope quiz, from the [schedule](https://web.stanford.edu/class/cs124/):

1. Tokenization and n-gram language modeling; Lab 1 on Unix text processing.
2. Logistic regression and text classification; Lab 2.
3. Information retrieval; Lab 3.
4. Embeddings; a live lecture on NLP for computational social science.
5. Neural networks.
6. Transformers — a live lecture on "LLMs and Transformers!", with both decks posted.
7. Speech processing.
8. Recommendation systems; Lab 4 on the final agent assignment and Git.
9. Pagerank and networks; Lab 5 on collaborative filtering and the ethical use of LLMs in the classroom.
10. A closing live lecture; the final programming assignment is due 11 March.
