# CS 273B: Deep Learning in Genomics and Biomedicine

Two fields that became "big data" disciplines at the same time, taken together. The course supplies the genomics itself — no prior biology is assumed — but expects you to arrive able to train a model, and treats interpreting the trained network as a goal in its own right.

## Materials

Nothing from the current offering is public — the course has no site of its own, and Spring 2026's material sits in a Canvas course behind SUNet. Spring 2023's Canvas course, however, was left open, and it holds most of the course.

- **Syllabus**: Spring 2026's is gated, but [Spring 2023's](https://canvas.stanford.edu/courses/174437/assignments/syllabus) is public: the overview, the prerequisites ([CS 109](CS%20109.md) and [CS 229](CS%20229.md) level, "no prior knowledge of biology is necessary"), the grading split — project 80%, peer review 10%, participation 10% — and a dated ten-week schedule listing the papers for each session.
- **Slides**: thirteen decks hang off that schedule, and all thirteen still download without a login: bioimaging and cellular image analysis, OpenCell, the regulatory-genomics run from Basset through base-resolution TF binding and interpretability, molecular QTLs, 3D genome architecture, and protein sequence with EVE, ESMFold and AlphaFold2.
- **Notes**, **videos**, **assignments**, **solutions**, **exams**, **projects**: nothing found for any offering.
- **Older material**: the Kundaje lab's public repository [kundajelab/cs273b](https://github.com/kundajelab/cs273b) holds the section tutorial notebooks (Jupyter, TensorFlow, Keras, PyTorch, and two DragoNN tutorials on simulated regulatory sequence), last updated September 2020. Not rated.

## Syllabus

No syllabus is public; this is the catalog's own order.

1. Introduction to deep learning
2. Genomics and high-throughput biotechnology: what data exists and why it matters
3. Supervised, unsupervised and generative models applied to biomedical data
4. Visualizing models and extracting interpretable biological insight from them
5. Recent papers from the literature, presented and discussed, and guest lectures from experts in the field

Students work with standard deep learning frameworks and, in groups, on a final project using real-world datasets.

## Prerequisites

- Calculus and linear algebra at college level.
- Probability and statistics: [CS 109](CS%20109.md) or equivalent.
- Machine learning: [CS 229](CS%20229.md) or equivalent.
- No genomics. The catalog says so explicitly; the biological background is taught in the course.
