# CS 273B: Deep Learning in Genomics and Biomedicine

Two fields that became "big data" disciplines at the same time, taken together. The course supplies the genomics itself — no prior biology is assumed — but expects you to arrive able to train a model, and treats interpreting the trained network as a goal in its own right.

## Materials

The course has no site of its own, and this offering sits in a Canvas course behind SUNet. What saves it is Spring 2023's, left publicly readable: the overview, the grading split, a dated 10-week schedule and 13 lecture decks that still download. No problem sets are set — the grade is one team project and the review of another team's draft, both stated in full there — so what is missing is the suggested-project list, the example notebooks, and the short videos that stand in for recitations.

- **Syllabus**: this offering's is gated, as are Autumn 2020's and Spring 2024's. [Spring 2023's](https://canvas.stanford.edu/courses/174437/assignments/syllabus) is public and complete: the overview, the prerequisites ([CS 109](CS%20109.md) and [CS 229](CS%20229.md) level, "no prior knowledge of biology is necessary"), the grading split and every session's papers.
- **Slides**: 13 decks hang off that schedule, each behind a Canvas download token that still works without a login: bioimaging and cellular image analysis, OpenCell, the regulatory-genomics run from Basset through base-resolution TF binding and interpretability, molecular QTLs, 3D genome architecture, and protein sequence models.
- **Notes**: none; the readings are published papers.
- **Videos**: the course makes them and keeps them inside Canvas. It holds no recitations, and in their place "short videos and example notebooks will be posted to Canvas". Spring 2023's otherwise public course gates these too: its file listing holds only the decks, and its Panopto tab wants a login.
- **Assignments**: Spring 2023's syllabus states both graded pieces in full — a team of 3 to 5 producing a conference-style paper through 4 milestones, 80%, and a peer review of another team's draft, 10% — but the suggested projects and the notebooks behind them are on Canvas.
- **Solutions**, **Exams**: none of either; the grading split has no exam and no problem set to answer.
- **Projects**: nothing from Spring 2023 on. [Autumn 2019's Canvas course](https://canvas.stanford.edu/courses/105563) is public and keeps 8 student paper drafts, the only look at what the project produces; at 7 years old they carry no weight here.
- **Repo**: the Kundaje lab's [kundajelab/cs273b](https://github.com/kundajelab/cs273b) holds Autumn 2020's section tutorials, in TensorFlow, Keras and PyTorch, plus 2 DragoNN notebooks on simulated regulatory sequence.

## Syllabus

No syllabus is public; this is the catalog's own order.

1. Introduction to deep learning
2. Genomics and high-throughput biotechnology: what data exists and why it matters
3. Supervised, unsupervised and generative models applied to biomedical data
4. Visualizing models and extracting interpretable biological insight from them
5. Recent papers from the literature, presented and discussed, and guest lectures from experts in the field

Students work with standard deep learning frameworks and, in groups, on a final project using real-world datasets.

## Prerequisites

- Calculus and linear algebra at college level.
- Probability and statistics: [CS 109](CS%20109.md) or equivalent.
- Machine learning: [CS 229](CS%20229.md) or equivalent.
- No genomics. The catalog says so explicitly; the biological background is taught in the course.
