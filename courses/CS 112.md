# CS 112: Operating systems kernel implementation project

The Pintos projects unbundled from the lectures. Students who already took [CS 111](CS%20111.md) write the kernel code without repeating the concepts, and the projects are 100% of the grade.

## Materials

The whole of this course is public, because the whole of it is the 4 Pintos projects and they are published: the handouts, the design-document templates, the Pintos manual and the kernel source itself all open, and the grade is nothing else. There is no separate CS 112 site; every page is shared with [CS 212](CS%20212.md), and each offering since Winter 2022 keeps its own copy at a term path.

- **Syllabus**: the [shared schedule](https://www.scs.stanford.edu/26sp-cs212/sched/) marks with an asterisk which sessions matter here: the 4 Friday Pintos sections, "highly recommended", and the concurrency and synchronization lectures, optional and not examined. [Winter 2022's](https://www.scs.stanford.edu/22wi-cs212/sched/) through [Spring 2025's](https://www.scs.stanford.edu/25sp-cs212/sched/) are all still served; Canvas also holds a Stanford-only copy for each.
- **Slides**: [17 lecture decks and 6 section decks](https://www.scs.stanford.edu/26sp-cs212/notes/), each as a PDF, a 6-up print PDF and a LaTeX source tarball; the notes index grants permission to copy and redistribute.
- **Notes**: the [Pintos manual](https://www.scs.stanford.edu/26sp-cs212/pintos/pintos.html), also as one PDF, and the [reference page](https://www.scs.stanford.edu/26sp-cs212/reference/) that sequences it.
- **Videos**: nothing. Neither the site, the schedule nor the FAQ mentions recording, and nothing turned up for this course or its lecture half.
- **Assignments**: the [4 project handouts](https://www.scs.stanford.edu/26sp-cs212/labs/project.html) with design-document templates, plus [Lab 0](https://www.scs.stanford.edu/26sp-cs212/labs/lab0.html) and a [git setup guide](https://www.scs.stanford.edu/26sp-cs212/labs/git.html).
- **Solutions**: solutions to most of the exam archive; none for the projects.
- **Exams**: the [archive](https://www.scs.stanford.edu/26sp-cs212/exams/) holds about 35 [CS 140](CS%20140.md) midterms and finals from 1999 to 2007, the predecessor course's. Nothing from this offering, and this course's own exams cover only Pintos.
- **Projects**: none; the 4 Pintos projects are the assignments, and no student's work is published.
- **Repo**: the [Pintos distribution](https://web.stanford.edu/class/cs212/pintos.tar.gz), also clonable as a [git repository](https://web.stanford.edu/class/cs212/pintos.git/), both without a login.

## Syllabus

The course is the four Pintos projects, in the order the [project handout](https://www.scs.stanford.edu/26sp-cs212/labs/project.html) sets:

1. **Threads** — the scheduler, alarm clock and priority donation.
2. **User programs** — argument passing, system calls and process management.
3. **Virtual memory** — paging, swapping and memory-mapped files.
4. **File systems** — extensible files, subdirectories and a buffer cache.

Each project is submitted with a design document. Teams may mix CS 112 and [CS 212](CS%20212.md) students; the projects are identical across the two.

## Prerequisites

- Operating systems concepts: [CS 111](CS%20111.md), or permission of the instructor. The staff are explicit that this is not negotiable — "If you take only one of CS111 and CS112, it should be CS111… it doesn't make sense to write kernel code without also learning OS concepts."

## Related

- [CS 111](CS%20111.md): the prerequisite, with lighter projects of its own.
- [CS 140E](CS%20140E.md): the alternative for more kernel hacking: a bare-metal OS for the Raspberry Pi, written from scratch, instead of Pintos on a simulator.
- [CS 212](CS%20212.md): the same projects plus the full lecture sequence in one quarter, for students who have not taken [CS 111](CS%20111.md). CS 112's Pintos work is 100% of its grade; in [CS 212](CS%20212.md) it is 50%.
