# CS 294W: Writing Intensive Research Project in Computer Science

The Writing in the Major counterpart to [CS 294S](CS%20294S.md): the same lectures, the same Almond research project, and a written report that satisfies Stanford's writing requirement. The catalog gives it one sentence — students enroll in the CS 294W section attached to the CS 294 project they have chosen — and restricts it to Computer Science and Computer Systems Engineering undergraduates. It is graded on the letter-only basis, unlike its sibling.

## Materials

Everything public comes from the shared [CS 294S](CS%20294S.md) site, which does not separate the two numbers — and it is unusually open about what the students produced, if not about what the writing section asks of them. The slides directory is browsable and holds the project pitches, proposals and final presentations alongside the teaching decks.

- **Syllabus**: the [shared schedule](https://web.stanford.edu/class/cs294s/), with readings, deadlines and the grading split. Nothing on it marks the writing requirement.
- **Slides**: 7 teaching decks in the [open slides directory](https://web.stanford.edu/class/cs294s/slides/) — the [course overview](https://web.stanford.edu/class/cs294s/slides/CS294-2020fall.pdf), an NLP primer, ML for NLP, dialogue, Genie multi-turn, Schema2QA and a confidence-modeling mini-lecture.
- **Notes**, **Videos**, **Repo**: not found.
- **Assignments**: the [project guidelines](https://web.stanford.edu/class/cs294s/projects2020fall.html) are public — what the 10-minute proposal and final presentation must answer, and what the final report must contain, with "no page limit (minimum or maximum)". The work itself goes through Canvas, and nothing specific to the writing requirement — rubrics, draft rounds, feedback — is published anywhere.
- **Solutions**, **Exams**: none; the course is assessed on the project and the report.
- **Projects**: 34 student artifacts in the same directory — 12 project pitches, 7 proposals, 9 mini-lectures and 4 final presentations, on topics from controllable generation and multi-language support to multimodal chatbots and "are we conversational yet".
- **Older offerings**: [Spring 2020](https://web.stanford.edu/class/cs294s/spring2020.html) keeps its own page beside the current one, and the site also keeps project pages for 2017 and 2018.

## Syllabus

Identical to [CS 294S](CS%20294S.md): building the Almond open virtual assistant, with research openings in NLP, HCI and systems.

## Prerequisites

None stated. Enrollment is restricted to Computer Science and Computer Systems Engineering undergraduates, who take it alongside the CS 294 project they have joined.
