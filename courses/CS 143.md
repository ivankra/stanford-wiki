# CS 143: Compilers

The course is organized around one artefact: a compiler for Cool, a small object-oriented language, built over five programming assignments from lexer to code generator.

## Materials

The site publishes everything it promises to, "all lectures notes, handouts and assignments": 17 decks, both assignment tracks, the written solutions, the Cool documentation and 2 full years of exams. What a reader cannot get is the Cool support code, which PA1 fetches from a Stanford-only AFS path and on which half the grade rests. Nothing is said about recordings.

- **Syllabus**: the [dated schedule](https://web.stanford.edu/class/cs143/syllabus.html) keys each PA and WA to the lecture that assigns it, and the [course information page](https://web.stanford.edu/class/cs143/course_info.html) carries the grading scheme (4 written assignments 10%, 4 programming assignments 50%, midterm 15%, final 25%) and a [policies page](https://web.stanford.edu/class/cs143/policies/index.html) on partners and collaboration. Canvas holds a Stanford-only copy for all 6 older offerings.
- **Slides**: [17 decks](https://web.stanford.edu/class/cs143/lectures/lecture01.pdf), Course Overview through Automatic Memory Management. The 18th lecture has no file: the nav calls it "JIT Compilers and Fast Compilation" and the schedule reads TBD.
- **Notes**: the [Cool reference manual](https://web.stanford.edu/class/cs143/materials/cool-manual.pdf), the [tour of the support code](https://web.stanford.edu/class/cs143/materials/cool-tour.pdf), the Cool runtime document, the SPIM manual and off-site Flex and Bison manuals.
- **Videos**: nothing. No offering's site, policies page or information page mentions recording, and no playlist for this course turned up.
- **Assignments**: [PA1–PA5](https://web.stanford.edu/class/cs143/handouts/PA1.pdf), the 5 compiler phases from lexer to optimizer, and WA1–WA4, each with its LaTeX source. PA1 begins "log in to one of the myth machines and run `/afs/ir/class/cs143/bin/pa_fetch PA1`", and warns that private copies of the files it links may make the assignment impossible, so an outside reader has the briefs without the scaffolding.
- **Solutions**: [all 4 written assignments](https://web.stanford.edu/class/cs143/handouts/WA1sol.pdf); none for the programming ones.
- **Exams**: the [2024 and 2025 midterms and finals](https://web.stanford.edu/class/cs143/finals/final-2025sol.pdf), each with solutions; for 2026 the solutions are posted without the papers.
- **Projects**: none; the compiler is the assignment sequence.
- **Repo**: no public copy of the support code; work happens on `myth.stanford.edu`, submissions on `rice.stanford.edu`.
- **Textbook**: Aho et al, *Compilers: Principles, Techniques, and Tools* ("Dragon Book"; optional).
- **Extras**: Aiken's self-paced [Compilers](https://online.stanford.edu/courses/soe-ycscs1-compilers) on edX, free to audit, sets the same PA1–PA5 Cool project and ships its support code, with short lecture videos and quizzes. It is a separate course, not an offering of this one.

## Syllabus

From the [posted schedule](https://web.stanford.edu/class/cs143/syllabus.html), eighteen lectures over ten weeks:

1. **Front end** (lectures 1–8): course overview, Cool as the project, lexical analysis and finite automata, introduction to parsing, syntax-directed translation, top-down parsing, bottom-up parsing in two parts.
2. **Semantics** (lectures 9–13): semantic analysis and type checking in two parts, runtime organization, code generation, operational semantics. An in-class midterm falls on 30 April, between lectures 9 and 10.
3. **Back end** (lectures 14–18): local optimization, global optimization, register allocation, garbage collection, and a final session on JIT compilation.

PA1–PA4 track the front end through code generation; PA5, an optimizer, is due on the last day of classes.

## Prerequisites

- Discrete mathematics and proofwriting: [CS 103](CS%20103.md) or CS 103B.
- Systems programming in C: [CS 107](CS%20107.md) or equivalent. The catalog also allows consent of instructor in place of either.
