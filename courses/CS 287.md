# CS 287: Foundations of Healthcare Data for Machine Learning

A course built on one claim: most clinical model failures are not modeling failures. Before a dataset exists, a clinician has decided what to chart and when, an institution has decided what gets coded and billed, and those habits put bias, noise, missingness and spurious correlations into the data that no architecture will fix. The course works through what that means in practice — data quality assessment, label leakage, annotation strategy, synthetic data, drift monitoring — and includes observation of real clinical data workflows, not just case studies of them. It is graded on the medical option, and is the data-side companion to the modeling courses in the same program.

## Syllabus

No schedule is published. The catalog describes the arc:

> This course explores how healthcare data is generated in practice - and how these processes influence the development of robust, trustworthy machine learning models. Students will examine how clinical workflows, documentation habits, and institutional practices introduce bias, noise, missingness, and spurious correlations into datasets. The course emphasizes the often-overlooked reality that many model failures stem not from algorithmic design, but from subtle flaws in how data is collected, labeled, and interpreted.

Delivery is "lectures, real-world case studies, and observation of clinical data workflows". Named topics: assessing data quality; addressing missingness and label leakage; developing scalable and accurate annotation strategies; leveraging synthetic data; monitoring data and models for drift over time; and evaluating how these data challenges affect model generalization and robustness.

## Prerequisites

None stated. The course assumes enough machine learning to know what label leakage and distribution shift break, but no clinical background — the clinical side is what it teaches.
