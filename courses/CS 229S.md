# CS 229S: Systems for Machine Learning

Where [CS 229](CS%20229.md) asks whether a model learns, this asks what it costs to run: FLOP counts and arithmetic intensity for multi-head attention, KV caching and speculative decoding, FlashAttention's tiling, and how any of it changes across accelerators and nodes. The two short projects are unusually concrete — hardcode a two-layer Transformer's weights to solve associative recall, then add speculative decoding to Karpathy's nanoGPT — before a 7.5-week final project.

## Syllabus

[The calendar](https://cs229s.stanford.edu/fall2024/calendar/) runs ten weeks.

1. Foundations (weeks 1-2): Transformer architecture and pretraining, compilers and the GPU memory hierarchy, arithmetic intensity, FLOP accounting for training and inference, KV caching and speculative decoding.
2. Efficient kernels and models (weeks 3-4): CUDA and GPU programming, attention bottlenecks and FlashAttention, quantization, pruning, structured sparsity, butterfly and Monarch matrices.
3. Training and adaptation (weeks 4-5): scaling laws, emergent abilities, RLHF and Constitutional AI, parameter-efficient finetuning, data in AI pipelines.
4. Architectures and serving (weeks 6-10): linear attention, state-space models and SSM convolutions, LLM serving efficiency, sparse mixture-of-experts, parallelism, cluster scheduling, efficient retrieval.

Guest lectures from Dylan Patel (SemiAnalysis) and Albert Gu (CMU, Cartesia AI) fall in weeks 8 and 9; the term ends with a poster session.

## Prerequisites

- Programming: enough Python and NumPy for a non-trivial program — [CS 106A](CS%20106A.md), [CS 106B](CS%20106B.md) or CS 106X.
- Probability: [CS 109](CS%20109.md), MATH 151 or STATS 116.
- Multivariable calculus and linear algebra: MATH 51 or CS 205.
- Preferred, not required: a machine learning foundation — [CS 221](CS%20221.md), [CS 229](CS%20229.md), [CS 230](CS%20230.md) or [CS 124](CS%20124.md).
