# CS 171: Causality, Decision Making and Data Science

Taught by an econometrician around four real questions rather than a method list: does unearned income reduce labour supply, what is schooling worth, what did a policy actually do. Each module pairs the economics with the estimator it demands, and students analyze the data themselves.

## Syllabus

Five modules on the [Materials page](https://stanford-causal-inference-class.github.io/materials.html):

0. **Introductions and statistics refresher** — causality and correlation; an optional Python bootcamp; Assignment 0.
1. **The gold standard** — the magic of randomized control trials, problems with experiments, online experiments; Assignment 1.
2. **Universal basic income** — Friedman's permanent income hypothesis, regression motivated by the UBI question, empirical evidence on unearned income, heterogeneous treatment effects and random forests; Assignment 2.
3. **Returns to education** — returns to education, instrumental variables over two lectures, regression discontinuity; Assignment 3.
4. **Panel data** — panel data, difference-in-differences, sparsity and low-rankness, synthetic control and the course recap.

Stated goals: "Distinguish causation from correlation. Analyze random and quasi-experiments. Critically evaluate data-driven claims. Communicate findings effectively."
