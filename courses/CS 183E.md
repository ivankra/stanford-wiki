# CS 183E: Effective Leadership in High-Tech

One unit, one evening a week, in a small group with two industry veterans in engineering leadership and product management. The subject is the part of the job the technical courses skip: being heard, resolving conflict, moving into leadership, and choosing and negotiating the next role.

## Materials

No course site, in this or any year. But the instructors published the whole thing once, deliberately: Jocelyn Goldfein's 2019 post ["Effective Leadership in High Tech" Syllabus and Reading List](https://medium.com/@jocelyngoldfein/effective-leadership-in-high-tech-syllabus-and-reading-list-5569a3fbf067), written "for those considering taking the class, or simply curious about a tech leadership curriculum for college students". At seven years old it is context rather than this offering's material, so nothing here is rated open.

## Syllabus

From the instructors' 2019 syllabus, in three sections over ten weeks.

**Being a great individual contributor.** Personal productivity — driving outcomes rather than process, and when to ask for help (readings on maker versus manager schedules). Thought leadership — what it is, and how to get buy-in (Cialdini's *Influence*). Resolving conflict — types of tough conversation, debugging a disagreement to root cause.

**Transitioning into leadership.** Becoming a leader: influence versus authority, being respected *and* trusted. Giving and receiving feedback (*Radical Candor*, "Staying on Your Side of the Net"). Interviewing, taught from the interviewer's side — high-stakes judgement calls and how to get better at making them. Team culture: values versus perks, and "culture is the behavior you reward and punish", read alongside the Netflix culture deck, Twilio's "Draw the Owl" and the Valve employee handbook.

**Managing your career.** First job to early career — evaluating opportunities, valuing employee options, asking for a raise or a promotion, and when to leave. Networking and mentorship, including how to be a good mentee. A closing reflection week built around an AMA with both instructors.

The catalog's wording for the same three movements: being a great individual contributor — "communicating with clarity, getting traction for your ideas, resolving conflict, and delivering your best work"; moving into leadership — "finding leadership opportunities, creating a great team culture, hiring and onboarding new team members"; and the career itself — "picking your first job and negotiating your offer, managing your career changes, building a great network, and succeeding with mentors".
