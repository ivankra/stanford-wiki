# CS 102: Working with Data - Tools and Techniques

Aimed squarely outside the major, and deliberately broad rather than deep: "Tools and techniques are hands-on but at a cursory level, providing a basis for future exploration and application." It ran for the last time in Spring 2020, and the instructor moved the material to her own site rather than let it lapse.

## Materials

The whole course is readable. Every slide deck, every in-class exercise and every assignment and project handout is on the Spring 2020 site, and the notebooks and data sets are maintained separately on the instructor's own site. Only the Zoom recordings and the exams stayed private.

- **Syllabus**: the [schedule](https://web.stanford.edu/class/cs102/) — every class date with its topic, readings and material, and the "5 assignments, 2 projects, and 2 exams… 33.3% each" scheme.
- **Slides**: thirteen decks, one per unit, from [Working with Data — Overview](https://web.stanford.edu/class/cs102/lectureslides/Overview102.pdf) through spreadsheets, Tableau, SQL, Python, regression, classification, clustering, R, data mining, networks and unstructured data.
- **Notes**: the "Your Turns" in-class exercises with answers, one set per tool — [spreadsheets](https://web.stanford.edu/class/cs102/lecturenotes/SpreadsheetNotesP1.docx.pdf) as PDFs, SQL, Python, pandas and R as plain text you can paste straight into a shell.
- **Videos**: Zoom, with links on Canvas; the optional Wednesday setup bootcamps were recorded to Canvas too.
- **Assignments**: all five handouts are public pages — [spreadsheets](https://web.stanford.edu/class/cs102/assignments/assignment1.htm), Tableau and SQL, Python, machine learning and R, data mining and network analysis — as are both projects, a [personal data analysis](https://web.stanford.edu/class/cs102/projects/project1.htm) and movie-rating prediction.
- **Solutions**: the schedule offers a sample solution for three of the five assignments; all three links 404.
- **Exams**: two in-class exams, not posted. **Projects**: the winners' write-up link 404s.
- **Code**: the [materials page](http://www.professorwidom.org/materials) on the instructor's own site — eleven public Google Drive folders of Jupyter notebooks and data files, one per module, plus its own copies of the decks and three "getting started" guides for Sheets, Tableau Public and Colab.

## Syllabus

The [schedule](https://web.stanford.edu/class/cs102/) is one tool per unit over ten weeks, each introduced in lecture and exercised in a "Your Turns" set, with a Wednesday bootcamp for whatever has to be installed:

1. **Spreadsheets** (weeks 1–2): analysis then visualization in Google Sheets.
2. **Tableau** (week 2): advanced visualization, paired with a reading on common visualization mistakes.
3. **SQL** (week 3): the relational model, basic then advanced SQL, run in Jupyter on Instabase.
4. **Python** (weeks 4–5): Python for analysis and visualization, pandas, PyPlot.
5. **Machine learning** (weeks 5–7): regression, classification, clustering; then R as a second language for the same work.
6. **Data mining and networks** (weeks 7–9): mining algorithms in Python and SQL, then network analysis.
7. **Unstructured data** (week 10): images and video, text and NLP, correlation and causation, big data in the real world, and a "Pathways after CS 102" session.
