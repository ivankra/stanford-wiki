# CS 448I: Computational Imaging

Gordon Wetzstein's course on recovering an image you did not measure. It runs the same problem — denoising, deconvolution, phase retrieval — through three generations of method in sequence: classical estimation, then CNNs as learned priors, then diffusion models, held together by the proximal framework of half-quadratic splitting and ADMM so the learned pieces slot into a formal optimization. The last two lectures push back into optics, with wave propagation and end-to-end optimization of the lens alongside the reconstruction. Six Python assignments, an 80-minute midterm and a project ending in a poster session.

## Syllabus

Fifteen lectures over ten weeks, per the [schedule](https://web.stanford.edu/class/ee367/).

1. **Weeks 1–2, imaging as measurement**: introduction; the human visual system — color, depth, contrast, resolution; digital photography in two parts, from ray optics and sensor noise to the camera ISP, demosaicking and bilateral filtering.
2. **Weeks 3–4, groundwork**: a math review; great ideas in computational photography; a guest lecture from Google's computational photography group.
3. **Weeks 5–6, learned priors**: neural networks; solving inverse problems with them; image deconvolution with half-quadratic splitting; regularized inverse problems with ADMM.
4. **Weeks 7–8, diffusion**: diffusion models, then diffusion-model priors for inverse problems. The instructor-provided default project drops here.
5. **Week 9, optics**: wave optics and deep optics — jointly optimizing the lens and the reconstruction — then phase retrieval and holography.
