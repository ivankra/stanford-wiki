# CS 481: Digital Technology and Law: Foundations

Taught by a team of law and engineering faculty and open to the whole university with no prerequisite of either kind. Each module takes one law-technology intersection and teaches both halves of it — the technology well enough to reason about, the doctrine well enough to apply — through case studies, in-class exercises and guests from industry, government, academia and civil society. Law students leave with a technical grounding and a map of career paths; everyone else leaves able to assess the legal implications of a new technology.

## Syllabus

From the catalog description; the module list is named but not expanded anywhere public.

1. Two bases of knowledge, developed in parallel: a working technical grasp of key digital technologies — AI and machine learning, internet structure, encryption, blockchain — and basic fluency in the legal frameworks each implicates: privacy, cybersecurity, anti-discrimination, free speech, torts, procedural fairness.
2. Modules on distinct law-technology intersections: platform regulation, speech and intermediary liability; algorithmic bias and civil rights; autonomous systems, safety and tort liability; "smart" contracting; data privacy and consumer protection; legal tech, litigation and access to justice; government use of AI; encryption and criminal procedure.
3. Each module through a mix of technical and legal instruction, case-study discussion, in-class practical exercises and guest speakers.

## Prerequisites

None. The catalog states that no prior legal or technical training is assumed. Non-law students needed a consent application by mid-March.
