# CS 269O: Introduction to Optimization Theory

The gentler of Sidford's two optimization courses, and the one he created first: the same question — how fast can any method minimize a function of this class, and which algorithm meets the bound — but surveyed rather than proved end to end. It works through first-order methods for smooth and non-smooth convex minimization, then structured problems, ending at Newton's method and interior point methods.

## Syllabus

No course schedule exists publicly. The catalog description gives the scope:

1. Core algorithmic techniques and proof strategies behind the best known provable guarantees for minimizing high-dimensional convex functions.
2. First-order methods for smooth convex minimization: gradient descent, accelerated gradient descent.
3. First-order methods for non-smooth convex minimization: mirror descent.
4. Structured convex minimization.
5. Second-order and interior-point methods: Newton's method, interior point methods.

The stated aim is a theoretical foundation for further study rather than coverage of any particular application.

## Prerequisites

- Multivariable calculus.
- Linear algebra.

No optimization background is assumed; this is the entry point.
