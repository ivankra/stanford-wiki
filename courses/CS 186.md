# CS 186: How to Make a Moral Agent

The schedule is organized as questions rather than topics — "Can you be selfish without a self?", "What do moral benchmarks actually measure?" — and the readings run from Hume-descended moral philosophy to alignment papers published this year.

## Syllabus

Nineteen sessions on the [schedule](https://web.stanford.edu/class/cs186/):

1. **Agents and moral agents** (1 and 3 April): what it means to have a value, whether we have agents already. Readings: Railton, "Ethical Learning, Natural and Artificial"; optionally Haas on moral gridworlds and Awad et al. on computational ethics.
2. **Moral sentimentalism** (8, 10 April) and **moral rationalism** (15, 17 April): whether reason alone can persuade, or whether feeling another's pain is essential.
3. **Identity and self** (22 April), then **"What makes us so righteous?"** (24 April – 1 May): moral psychology and the evolution of cooperation.
4. **"If it's bad that my AI lies, is it bad if I turn it off?"** (6, 8 May): sentience and moral patienthood.
5. **How to make a moral agent** (13, 15 May): implementations, and what moral benchmarks measure.
6. **Alignment** (20, 22, 27 May) and **Where are we going?** (29 May, 3 June).
