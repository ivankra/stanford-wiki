# CS 251: Cryptocurrencies and blockchain technologies

The technical side of blockchains: consensus, smart contracts, economics, privacy and scalability, with Bitcoin and Ethereum as case studies. Four written homeworks and four programming projects in Python and Solidity.

## Materials

The [course site](https://cs251.stanford.edu/) is public; submissions go through Gradescope and discussion through Ed.

- **Syllabus**: the [syllabus page](https://cs251.stanford.edu/syllabus.html) lists all 19 lectures with dates and readings.
- **Slides**: pdf and pptx linked per lecture. So far only [lecture 1](https://cs251.stanford.edu/lectures/lecture1.pdf) is the Fall 2026 deck; the others are last year's.
- **Assignments**: [project 1](https://cs251.stanford.edu/hw/proj1.pdf) (Merkle trees) with [starter code](https://cs251.stanford.edu/hw/proj1.zip); the rest are released through the term.
- **Videos**: lectures are recorded, but only on Canvas.

## Syllabus

19 lectures, grouped as on the site:

1. Intro to cryptography and cryptocurrencies
2. Bitcoin mechanics: Bitcoin nuts and bolts; wallets
3. Consensus: classical consensus and state machine replication; Nakamoto consensus and sybil resistance; proof-of-stake, accountability and incentives
4. Ethereum: the EVM; programming in Solidity
5. DeFi and economics: stablecoins and lending; decentralized exchanges; MEV; legal aspects and regulation
6. Privacy: de-anonymization and mixing; zk-SNARKs and how to construct one
7. Scaling: payment and state channels; rollups
8. Final topics: account abstraction, bridging, post-quantum blockchains and AI; a guest lecture on the future of blockchains

Projects: Merkle trees in Python, Bitcoin transactions with python-bitcoinlib, an Ethereum payment app, an on-chain wallet.
