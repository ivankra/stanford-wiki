# CS 140M: Introduction to Embedded Systems

Four units on one microcontroller: lab assignments that work an ARM Cortex-M part through GPIO, clocks, interrupts, buses and storage, ending in a team-built wireless device. The course is an adaptation of Michigan's EECS 373 and is listed primarily as EE 186.

## Materials

All 4 lab handouts, 30% of the grade and the course's main work, sit in the site's own repository and open to anyone, though the assignments page names them without linking them. The schedule, policies and hardware references are public too. What a reader doesn't get is the lecture room: the staff's Drive of lecture files answers 401, week 2's deck was posted redacted, and the lab-section recordings go through Canvas.

- **Syllabus**: the [schedule](https://ee186.github.io/EE186/docs/lectures/), 12 weeks of dated lectures with every due date, a release calendar and optional chapter readings per lecture; the [overview](https://ee186.github.io/EE186/docs/overview/) adds grading (labs 30%, project 45%, midterm 20%, homework 5%) and the rule that homework may be done in groups but labs may not.

The staff's [Drive of lecture files](https://drive.google.com/drive/folders/1Y4pivF9kJYisE1K3zExEtMTYn3A6Uqmn) is not shared.
- **Notes**: none; the per-lecture readings point at Zhu's textbook and the STM32 manual.
- **Videos**: the Friday lab sections are recorded into Canvas, through a link an outside reader can't follow. Nothing says whether the lectures are recorded.
- **Assignments**: the 4 labs are published as PDFs in the [site repository](https://github.com/ee186/EE186) — GPIO and LEDs, then interrupts with the vendor HAL banned, then an LSM303AGR I²C driver, then a light-controlled theremin from an ADC and a DAC. The 4 homeworks are dated in the release calendar but no handout appears anywhere, and the final project has only its deadlines.
- **Solutions**: none published, and no source says whether students get any.

- **Textbook**: none officially. The [resources page](https://ee186.github.io/EE186/docs/resources/) recommends Yiu, Zhu and Valvano and links what the labs need: the ARMv7 architecture reference, the AHB and APB specifications, and the STM32L4 and Nucleo-144 manuals.

## Syllabus

The [schedule](https://ee186.github.io/EE186/docs/lectures/) works up one microcontroller over twelve weeks:

1. **Weeks 1–2**: architecture, the instruction set and ABI, assembly; memory-mapped I/O over the Advanced Peripheral Bus, and GPIO.
2. **Weeks 2–4**: interrupts across two lectures; timers and pulse-width modulation; then the serial buses, UART, SPI and I²C.
3. **Weeks 5–8**: analog signals and DACs, wireless communication, the system timer and direct memory access, and printed-circuit-board design — around a midterm in week 6 and its solutions review in week 8.
4. **Weeks 9–12**: a guest lecture on wireless embedded systems research, then class time given over to the final project, with progress updates in week 11 and a poster session on December 10.

Four labs and four homeworks run through weeks 1 to 7, each released Thursday and due the following week.

## Prerequisites

The catalog states none; the course overview lists two.

- Programming: [CS 106B](CS%20106B.md), programming abstractions, for the C-level work the labs are written in.
- Electronics: EE 40M, making stuff with electronics, for the board, the sensors and the logic analyzer.
