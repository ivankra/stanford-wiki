# CS 339R: Collaborative Robotics

Monroe Kennedy's project course on the teamwork problem in robotics: not how a robot achieves a task, but how it achieves one alongside another agent whose intentions it must infer. The tools the catalog names follow from that framing — characterizing task objectives, teammate behavioral modeling, inter-agent communication, team consensus — and the applications run from robot collaborators to wearable robotics. It is heavily laboured: eight weekly lab sections run alongside the twice-weekly lecture, and the implementation work is in Python or C++.

## Materials

The course page is on the instructor's lab site, [arm.stanford.edu/courses/collaborative-robotics](https://arm.stanford.edu/courses/collaborative-robotics), and it is unusually forthcoming about the project while saying nothing about the lectures.

- **Syllabus**: open. The page sets out four components and how a 90-minute block divides between them — about 60 minutes of lecture, about 30 of small-group discussion on selected papers with a plenary summary — plus Jupyter-notebook coding homework, a group project and an individual research paper in IEEE conference format.
- **Assignments**: the **Winter 2026 group project** is specified in full. Teams of 4–5 program a [bimanual Tidybot](https://tidybot2.github.io/) — mobile base, two 6-DoF arms, camera and lidar — to take a spoken or gestured request, perceive the scene and act on it. Three tasks are required: object retrieval ("locate the apple in the scene and retrieve it"), a sequential task ("find the red apple and place it in the basket"), and a team-chosen task involving perception, planning and control, proposed by week 4. The homework notebooks and the paper brief are not posted.
- **Projects**: open, and the richest part. Team websites are published for **four consecutive offerings**, Winter 2023 through Winter 2026 — roughly 25 of them, on Google Sites and GitHub Pages, each naming its seven or eight students.
- **Slides**, **Videos**: none found. **Exams**: none.

## Syllabus

No schedule is published. The catalog states the scope:

> This course focuses on how robots can be effective teammates with other robots and human partners. Concepts and tools will be reviewed for characterizing task objectives, robot perception and control, teammate behavioral modeling, inter-agent communication, and team consensus. We will consider the application of these tools to robot collaborators, wearable robotics, and the latest applications in the relevant literature. This will be a project-based graduate course, with the implementation of algorithms in either python or C++.

## Prerequisites

None stated. The implementation work assumes fluency in Python or C++, and the perception and control material assumes the manipulator and state-estimation background a first robotics course gives — [CS 223A](CS%20223A.md) or its ME equivalent in practice.
