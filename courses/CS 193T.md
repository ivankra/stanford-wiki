# CS 193T: Thinking with AI

An AI course for the whole university rather than the department: a weekly tutorial with a TA who follows one student's work all quarter, assignments aimed at whatever that student actually has to produce, and a term project that is a working agentic system they can show an employer. Its syllabus is explicit that it isn't selling anything — "we're not purveyors of mindless pro-AI hype".

## Materials

Everything the course writes down goes on the open web as the quarter runs: the syllabus, each lecture's deck, each week's assignment with its instructions. The one thing an outside reader can't reach is the recordings, which the syllabus confirms exist and reserves for auditors and excused absences. The rest of what's missing is the half of the course that is a conversation — the weekly tutorial, the TA check-in, the Ed forum — and that isn't written down for anyone.

- **Syllabus**: the [syllabus page](https://web.stanford.edu/class/archive/cs/cs193t/cs193t.1272/course/syllabus) carries the 10 topics, the named tool list, the lecture and tutorial format, and the pass bar (attend every lecture and tutorial, have every assignment and the final project signed off). The [FAQ](https://web.stanford.edu/class/archive/cs/cs193t/cs193t.1272/course/faq), honor-code and [about-assignments](https://web.stanford.edu/class/archive/cs/cs193t/cs193t.1272/course/about_assignments/) pages fill in the rest. The dated schedule page is a stub: one sentence, last changed September 2.
- **Slides**: a Google Slides deck on each lecture page published so far — [lecture 1](https://docs.google.com/presentation/d/1cMc1zd16swT77ebR2aJZuNk7fTztFho-14XVHNyZyYA/edit?usp=sharing) on tokens, training and inference, [lecture 2](https://docs.google.com/presentation/d/1bouvylhakkc5X9aClrnd7THlQ2d6YA_HPYnxAQoaXyE/edit?usp=sharing) on prompting patterns and context rot, and [lecture 4](https://docs.google.com/presentation/d/1IyUCQYP21QPZiHkuZRqCXoRHuFOn8whhqEhYdk8YPKs/edit?usp=sharing) for the research guest panel.
- **Notes**: the [Ethical AI Tools](https://web.stanford.edu/class/archive/cs/cs193t/cs193t.1272/resources/ethical_ai_tools) page, which is a written argument rather than a link list: run the model locally, use an open model, use the smallest model that does the job, then a graded catalog from in-browser Apertus Mini through OLMo and Granite. The [optional readings](https://web.stanford.edu/class/archive/cs/cs193t/cs193t.1272/resources/optional_extra_readings) page is so far 10 empty week headings.
- **Videos**: recorded, but only "for auditors and excused absences", with no public copy.
- **Assignments**: a page per week. [Assignment 1](https://web.stanford.edu/class/archive/cs/cs193t/cs193t.1272/assignments/1-ai-foundations/) is setup plus a prompt you stress-test for adherence; [assignment 2](https://web.stanford.edu/class/archive/cs/cs193t/cs193t.1272/assignments/2-models-prompting/) adds a full-instructions PDF and runs one task through several models against criteria you set first. Every week pairs tool work with a written reflection.
- **Solutions**: none; the work is graded as a 10-minute conversation with your TA about your own use cases.
- **Exams**: none. "Will there be exams? No", says the FAQ; finals week is a poster session instead.
- **Projects**: that showcase is open to guests from campus and industry, but nothing from it is posted.
- **Repo**: none; no code is distributed, and the tools are hosted products.

## Syllabus

From the [published topic list](https://web.stanford.edu/class/archive/cs/cs193t/cs193t.1272/course/syllabus): how AI really works; advanced prompting; probing models' strengths and weaknesses; agentic AI and reusable workflows; two guest panels, one on research use cases and one on industry; educational AI tools and reflective self-governance of AI use; societal impacts; building audit and accountability systems; and efficiency, tokens and budget management.

The tools are named rather than abstracted: Claude Chat, Cowork and Code; Gemini Enterprise, NotebookLM, Deep Research and Code Assist; ChatGPT, ChatGPTEdu and Codex; Microsoft 365 and GitHub Copilot; and open-weight models including Deepseek, Mistral and Llama. Concerns about bias, copyright, labor and environmental cost are stated as part of the material, alongside "emotional manipulation and impaired learning" as individual risks.
