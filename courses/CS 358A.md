# CS 358A: Programming Language Foundations

The formal-systems course behind Patrignani's secure-compilation work: calculi first, then type systems chosen for what they prove about security — non-interference, robust safety, ownership, session discipline — and the proof machinery, ending at logical relations and semantic models. The catalog hedges the list with "possible systems of study", so the selection varies by offering.

## Materials

Everything except the lecture material is public on the instructor's own page: the syllabus, all seven assignments and the final exam. The lectures were whiteboard, and the photographs of them went to a Drive folder linked from Piazza.

- **Syllabus**: the [course page](https://theory.stanford.edu/~mp/mp/CS358-2019.html) — a seven-part outline, a dated lecture-by-lecture list of what each session covered, the 70/30 split between assignments and exam, and every due date.
- **Assignments**: [all seven](https://theory.stanford.edu/~mp/mp/CS358-2019_files/ass1.pdf), roughly weekly. "You can collaborate on solutions to exercises together, but you must write your solution individually, in your own words."
- **Exams**: the [take-home final](https://theory.stanford.edu/~mp/mp/CS358-2019_files/exam.pdf).
- **Notes**: a [language reference](https://theory.stanford.edu/~mp/mp/CS358-2019_files/langref.pdf) with its LaTeX source and a macros file, so students can typeset the course's inference rules; the reading is Derek Dreyer's lecture notes, Pierce's *Types and Programming Languages*, and Marco Vassena's lectures on information-flow control, all linked.
- **Slides**: closed. "The whiteboard shots of each lecture are collected on a google drive folder whose link is on piazza."

## Syllabus

The [outline](https://theory.stanford.edu/~mp/mp/CS358-2019.html) is seven parts, the first of them about LaTeX:

0. **Typesetting PL notions in LaTeX**, with a template and macros file.
1. **The lambda calculus**: untyped and simply typed, structural and contextual operational semantics, pairs and products, progress, preservation, strong normalization, semantic type soundness.
2. **System F**: universal and existential types, encoding them in a semantic model of type soundness, and parametricity.
3. **Recursion**: recursive types and Kripke logical relations as the semantic model for possibly-diverging computation.
4. **Mutable state**: a heap with allocation, reads and writes, and the semantic model for impure computation.
5. **Program equivalence**: when two programs behave the same, and how to build a semantic model for it.
6. **Advanced type systems**: linear types, security types for non-interference, robust-safety typing, multi-language semantics.

The catalog's list is the same ground plus the Pi and Spi calculi, ownership types and session types, which the Winter 2020 outline does not reach.
