# CS 362: Research in AI Alignment

Eight outside speakers, each presenting "their relationships to the alignment problem" rather than a finished result, and a reading list that runs from Transformer Circuits to the text of a California bill. The course's stated premise is that alignment has drifted toward political questions, and that the philosophical ones still worth asking need technical fluency in language models and RL to pose at all. One written response per reading, due before its speaker arrives.

## Materials

A seminar whose whole core is a schedule and a reading list, and both offerings' are public on the instructor's own site, with every reading linked and almost none of it paywalled. The assignment rules are on the same page; nothing was found behind a login.

- **Syllabus**: the [course page](https://scottviteri.com/teaching/courses/cs362/) gives the Autumn 2024 speaker schedule, the per-week reading list and the grading rules. The [Spring 2023 offering](https://scottviteri.com/teaching/courses/cs362/) sits on the same page, with its own logistics, 10-session schedule and a suggested reading prompt per speaker; a copy of that offering's syllabus also went to Canvas for Stanford accounts only.
- **Slides**: not posted. Each session is a visiting speaker presenting their own work, and the site says nothing about whether enrolled students get the decks.
- **Notes**: none. The course writes none; the reading is the linked posts, papers and the text of SB 1047.
- **Videos**: no recording is mentioned or linked for either offering, and nothing turned up under the code or the instructor's name.
- **Assignments**: the brief is fully public: one written response per reading, "at least two hours worth of effort", accepted as a blog post, a GitHub repo, a Colab, an Overleaf document or "a small interactive javascript demo", due 30 minutes before the speaker arrives, lowest score dropped. Spring 2023 instead offered a weekly choice between a problem set and a short research assignment extending a paper's abstract.
- **Solutions**: none; the responses are open-ended, so there is nothing to key.
- **Exams**: none. The grade is the assignments and the final presentation.
- **Projects**: week 11 is student presentations on course takeaways, and none are published.
- **Repo**: the course publishes no code; a student's answer may be a repo, but those aren't collected anywhere public.

## Syllabus

[Nine sessions](https://scottviteri.com/teaching/courses/cs362/); weeks 7, 10 and 12 fall on Election Day, Thanksgiving and finals.

1. Sep 24 — Scott Viteri: course and alignment landscape overview.
2. Oct 1 — Joe Carlsmith: otherness and control in the age of AGI, read against "Gentleness and the Artificial Other".
3. Oct 8 — Joscha Bach: life, intelligence, consciousness and the future of humans.
4. Oct 15 — Jesse Hoogland: singular learning theory.
5. Oct 22 — Jack Lindsey: towards monosemanticity, on Anthropic's interpretability work.
6. Oct 29 — Rachel Freedman: reinforcement learning and value alignment, read against choice-set misspecification in reward inference.
7. Nov 12 — Dan Hendrycks: California Senate Bill 1047, with the bill text as the reading.
8. Nov 19 — Jessica Taylor: fixed points in AI alignment — reflective oracles, Garrabrant induction, the obliqueness thesis.
9. Dec 3 — student presentations.
