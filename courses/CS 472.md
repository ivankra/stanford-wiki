# CS 472: Data science and AI for COVID-19

James Zou's answer to the same spring: not "build something", but "check the models". Two units, one Friday morning a week, aimed at students who already had machine learning, and organized around critically examining the epidemic models then being used to predict infection rates and to argue for herd immunity or social distancing — with guest lectures from biomedical experts and a project meant to produce something usable.

## Syllabus

From the catalog description; no public syllabus.

1. Background: the biology and epidemiology of the COVID-19 virus.
2. Critical examination of the models then used to predict infection rates in the population.
3. Models used to support public health interventions, such as herd immunity and social distancing.
4. Projects aimed at tools for the global health effort: data visualization and education platforms, improved modeling and prediction, social-network and NLP analysis of how COVID-19 information propagated, and behavior-nudging tools.
5. Guest lectures by biomedical experts.

## Prerequisites

- Machine learning and statistics: [CS 229](CS%20229.md), STATS 216 or equivalent.
- Biological background is helpful but not required.
