# CS 361: Engineering Design Optimization

Optimization treated as the formal framework you design engineering systems inside: the algorithms first — derivative, derivative-free, constrained, stochastic — then the things that make real design hard, namely multiple objectives, uncertain evaluations, and evaluations too expensive to repeat. Applications run from aircraft to automated vehicles, and the final project is whatever optimization problem the student chooses.

## Materials

The textbook and syllabus are fully public; the handouts, quizzes and student reports are not.

- **Syllabus**: the [syllabus sheet](https://docs.google.com/spreadsheets/d/1e81on8JUhErhCiC4ESuNQIcGqgz4Kj_h_OrQQwMPtKQ/edit?usp=sharing) has all twenty lecture slots, deadlines and the optional topics excluded from quizzes; the [home page](https://aa222.stanford.edu/) has grading and every policy.

## Syllabus

Sixteen taught lectures over eight weeks, per the [syllabus sheet](https://docs.google.com/spreadsheets/d/1e81on8JUhErhCiC4ESuNQIcGqgz4Kj_h_OrQQwMPtKQ/edit?usp=sharing), tracking the textbook chapter by chapter; the last two weeks are given over to projects.

1. **Unconstrained optimization** (weeks 1-3): derivatives and gradients; bracketing; local descent; first- and second-order methods; direct methods; stochastic and population methods.
2. **Constrained optimization** (weeks 4-5): constraints; duality; linear and quadratic programming; disciplined convex programming; multiobjective optimization.
3. **Expensive and uncertain evaluations** (weeks 6-7): sampling plans; surrogate models, probabilistic surrogates and surrogate optimization; optimization under uncertainty and uncertainty propagation; discrete optimization.
4. **Design** (week 8): expression optimization; multidisciplinary optimization; course summary.

Quizzes replace the second lecture of weeks 3 and 5, each covering the chapters up to that point.
