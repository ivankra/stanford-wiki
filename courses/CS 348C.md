# CS 348C: Computer Graphics: Animation and Simulation

Doug James' simulation course, taught in two registers at once: the lecture derives the mathematics (constraint Jacobians, Lagrange multipliers, DAE integration schemes) and the assignment asks you to make something move in Houdini. There are no exams and no written problem sets; the 6 graded items are all artifacts, each shown to the class in a Show-&-Tell. What makes the page worth opening from outside Stanford is the archive: 8 years of student results, every homework and final project as a video, from Hello Houdini up to fluid and fracture work.

## Syllabus

Ten weeks, per the [schedule](https://graphics.stanford.edu/courses/cs348c/), which the page marks tentative.

1. **Weeks 1–3, tooling and particles**: introduction; Houdini itself, with a project file; procedural modeling; particle system dynamics — integration, collisions, energy-based forces, custom solvers and POP networks.
2. **Weeks 4–6, constraints and bodies**: Houdini dynamics; constrained dynamics — holonomic constraints, the constraint Jacobian, Lagrange multipliers, DAE integration and post-step projection, applied to linkages, inextensible cloth and contact; rigid-body motion with impulse-based collision response and Euler's equations.
3. **Weeks 7–8, deformation and breakage**: position-based dynamics and XPBD; shape matching via the deformation gradient and polar decomposition; fracture animation.
4. **Weeks 8–10, fluids**: grid-based simulation and SPH, APIC and MPM, sand as a fluid, rigid-fluid coupling and ocean waves. Final project discussion sits in week 7 and the project runs the last three weeks.

## Prerequisites

- Linear algebra, or permission of instructor — the only hard requirement.
- Recommended: [CS 148](CS%20148.md) or [CS 248B](CS%20248B.md). Neither is required, and the Houdini work starts from installation.
