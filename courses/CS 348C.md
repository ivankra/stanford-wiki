# CS 348C: Computer Graphics: Animation and Simulation

Doug James' simulation course, taught in two registers at once: the lecture derives the mathematics — constraint Jacobians, Lagrange multipliers, DAE integration schemes — and the assignment asks you to make something move in Houdini. There are no exams and no written problem sets; the six graded items are all artefacts, each shown to the class in a Show-&-Tell. What makes the page worth opening from outside Stanford is the archive: five years of student results, every homework and final project as a video, from Hello Houdini up to fluid and fracture work.

## Materials

Slides, the Houdini project files and five years of student output are public; the assignment specs and recordings are on Canvas.

- **Syllabus**: the [course page](https://graphics.stanford.edu/courses/cs348c/) is the whole term — lecture schedule, the six assignments with their weights and lengths, the late policy, and per-lecture reading lists running to well over a hundred papers.
- **Slides**: a deck per lecture, hosted on Dropbox — [particle systems](https://www.dropbox.com/scl/fi/zbcax1kq57funa3q6cqbz/04ParticleSystems-2026.pdf?rlkey=c447iyv1k1g17so562jq3qqvw&dl=0), constrained dynamics, rigid-body motion, position-based dynamics, fluids — all opening without a login.
- **Videos**: live-streamed and recorded to Canvas/Panopto. The Houdini Dynamics lecture's examples exist only there: "See video recording for live examples."
- **Assignments**: the six "Assignment Link" targets all go into Canvas.
- **Exams**: none, as the site states plainly.
- **Projects**: galleries of student results for Winter 2022 through Winter 2026 — each homework and each final project as a video on Google Drive.
- **Code**: the Houdini `.hipnc` project files used in lecture download freely, including `particles.hipnc` with its four demonstrations and `eulerRBMotion.hipnc`.
- **Readings**: Baraff and Witkin's *Physically Based Modeling* SIGGRAPH course notes carry much of the theory and are free; so are Bridson's fluid simulation notes and Jiang et al.'s MPM course notes.

## Syllabus

Ten weeks, per the [schedule](https://graphics.stanford.edu/courses/cs348c/), which the page marks tentative.

1. **Weeks 1–3, tooling and particles**: introduction; Houdini itself, with a project file; procedural modeling; particle system dynamics — integration, collisions, energy-based forces, custom solvers and POP networks.
2. **Weeks 4–6, constraints and bodies**: Houdini dynamics; constrained dynamics — holonomic constraints, the constraint Jacobian, Lagrange multipliers, DAE integration and post-step projection, applied to linkages, inextensible cloth and contact; rigid-body motion with impulse-based collision response and Euler's equations.
3. **Weeks 7–8, deformation and breakage**: position-based dynamics and XPBD; shape matching via the deformation gradient and polar decomposition; fracture animation.
4. **Weeks 8–10, fluids**: grid-based simulation and SPH, APIC and MPM, sand as a fluid, rigid-fluid coupling and ocean waves. Final project discussion sits in week 7 and the project runs the last three weeks.
