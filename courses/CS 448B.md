# CS 448B: Data Visualization

Maneesh Agrawala's visualization course, for students who want to use visualization in their own work or build better visualization tools. Lectures pair research readings with Observable notebooks, and the coursework builds from a static design to an interactive article.

## Materials

Every one of the 8 offerings since Winter 2020 keeps a complete site — schedule, readings, a deck per lecture and the assignment briefs with their data. Only the recordings are missing: the course says it is videotaped and publishes nothing.

- **Syllabus**: the [Autumn 2026 schedule](https://magrawala.github.io/cs448b-fa26/#schedule), 10 weeks with each lecture's required and optional readings and its Observable notebook, and the grading split — participation 10%, the 3 assignments 10/15/25%, the final project 40%.

- **Notes**: a [Tableau tutorial](https://magrawala.github.io/cs448b-fa26/assets/docs/Tableau_tutorial_handout_fall24.pdf) with its [worked solution](https://magrawala.github.io/cs448b-fa26/assets/docs/Tableau_tutorial_handout_fall24_solutions.pdf). Where those recordings go it never says, and none were found on Stanford Online or YouTube.
- **Assignments**: a [visualization design](https://magrawala.github.io/cs448b-fa26/a1.html) brief and an [exploratory data analysis](https://magrawala.github.io/cs448b-fa26/a2.html) brief with their data; the third and the final project are dated for release in October. Autumn 2025's 3 briefs and project spec are all up, and [Winter 2023](https://cs448b.subramonyam.com/assignments) puts its whole set on one page.
- **Solutions**, **Exams**: neither is published, and the course sets no midterm or final.
- **Projects**: the final project is a visual-explainer article, 40% of the grade, and no offering publishes the results.
- **Repo**: none; the assignments ask for Vega-Lite and D3.js code but no starter repository is distributed.
- **Textbook**: Tufte's *The Visual Display of Quantitative Information* and *Envisioning Information*, with Munzner's *Visualization Analysis and Design* optional; none is public.

## Syllabus

Two lectures a week for nine weeks, then design reviews of final projects.

1. Purpose of visualization; data and image models
2. Design and redesign; exploratory data analysis (Polaris)
3. Chart design and small multiples; interaction
4. Introduction to D3; D3 tutorial
5. Graphical perception; visual explainers and narrative visualization
6. Color; animation
7. Network layout; network analysis
8. Deconstructing visualizations (chart classification and redesign)
9. Text visualization; visualization and natural language processing
10. Final project design reviews

## Prerequisites

None official. Familiarity with [CS 147](CS%20147.md), [CS 148](CS%20148.md) and [CS 142](CS%20142.md) helps; most important is working knowledge of, or willingness to learn, JavaScript, Vega-Lite and D3.js.
