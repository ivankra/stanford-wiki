# CS 320: Value of Data and AI

A seminar on a question the field mostly assumes away: what a dataset is worth. Three instructors from different corners — Eglash, Zou and Zaharia — ask how to price a new dataset or an improved algorithm when the companies built on them are among the most valuable in the world and "our understanding about the economic value of data, networks and algorithmic assets remains at an early stage".

## Syllabus

From [Winter 2022's schedule](https://canvas.stanford.edu/courses/147388/assignments/syllabus). The course alternates topic sessions with practitioners. It opens on data business models, then the business value of data through a Netflix case study, evaluating ML impact, data-platform design through Databricks, and data valuation proper (Data Shapley, the HAI valuation work). The second half turns to the ML-as-a-service market and FrugalML, building data pipelines, business models and scaling effects, and closes on privacy, security and regulation — GDPR, the California Consumer Privacy Act, and the NYTimes cell-phone tracking investigation.

The guest list is the course's substance as much as the topics: NASDAQ's CTO and its head of alternative data; a data-market panel with AWS, Snowflake, GCP and Ticksmith; Joellen Russell on data and AI for climate change; Keith Winstein; David Engstrom of Stanford Law on government by algorithm; and a fireside chat with Reid Hoffman.
