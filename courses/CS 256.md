# CS 256: Algorithmic Fairness

An entry route into fairness research rather than a survey of bias in practice: the quarter starts from fair division and political philosophy, then works through the formal notions — group, individual, multi-group — and what each one can and cannot promise. Aimed at advanced undergraduates through PhD students, theory and ML alike.

## Syllabus

From the Winter 2024 schedule, the most recent complete one: fair division — divisible goods as an exercise in formalization, then algorithms, then indivisible goods and tradeoffs "from behind the veil"; notions from political philosophy and US anti-discrimination law; earlier CS notions of fairness (stable matching, resource allocation, envy-freeness, voting); ML basics with running examples (admissions, recruitment, loans, bail); group notions — definitions, algorithms, abuses, impossibilities; individual and metric fairness; multi-group fairness, multicalibration, outcome indistinguishability and omnipredictors; faults in the data; fairness and causality with guest lectures; explainability. Autumn 2025 opens on the same fair-division sequence.
