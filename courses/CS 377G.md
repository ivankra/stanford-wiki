# CS 377G: Designing Serious Games

A hands-on game-design studio: through lectures and extensive readings, students learn the fundamentals of game design and design and prototype games that teach, aimed at social change and civic engagement.

## Materials

The course has no site, but it has a blog, and the blog carries most of what a studio produces on paper: all 4 project briefs with their rubrics, the reading library, a 10-week breakdown, and every student's work posted as they go. What it does not carry is this offering's own dated schedule or any lecture material.

- **Syllabus**: this offering's went to Canvas, Stanford-only, as every one since Winter 2020 has. The blog's [week-by-week breakdown](https://mechanicsofmagic.com/serious-play-study-group-overview/serious-games-study-group-week-by-week-breakdown/) stands in: 10 weeks, each with its reading, sketchnote and critical-play assignment and its project milestone. It is the 2022 study group's version, so its dates are not this quarter's.
- **Slides**: the blog's lecture pages are [CS 247G](CS%20247G.md)'s; none of this course's own lecture material was found.
- **Notes**: the blog's Library — game design resources, graphic design for game designers, the formal elements of game design, a chapter from *Game Balance* — plus the readings the weekly breakdown assigns.
- **Videos**: a studio meeting 3 hours twice a week; nothing says whether anything is recorded.
- **Assignments**: [Project 1, "Those Who Play, Teach"](https://mechanicsofmagic.com/serious-play-study-group-overview/project-1-those-who-play-teach/) and its 3 successors, each with a concept-statement template, a deliverable list down to the print-at-home PDF and a grading rubric — 30% fun, 40% learning objectives, 20% documentation. The current offering runs the same P1 to P4 sequence under [its own categories](https://mechanicsofmagic.com/category/377g-serious-games/p1-those-who-play-teach/).
- **Solutions**, **Exams**, **Repo**: none; design work is critiqued in studio, there is no exam, and the course distributes no code.
- **Projects**: students post to the [blog](https://mechanicsofmagic.com/category/377g-serious-games/) as they go — concept statements, playtest write-ups, rules, playable prototypes and reflections — so each offering's output is readable, Autumn 2025's under "P2: The Empathy Machine" and this quarter's from late September on.

## Syllabus

The catalog gives no unit list. The blog's [study-group breakdown](https://mechanicsofmagic.com/serious-play-study-group-overview/serious-games-study-group-week-by-week-breakdown/) is the fullest public account: week 1 builds a vocabulary for discussing games through their formal elements, week 2 asks why learning is fun and starts Project 1, week 3 covers the information design of board games, and each later week pairs a reading and a critical play with a project milestone. The 4 projects are a teaching game, an interactive-fiction game about a possible future, a game that models a system of unexpected consequences, and a refinement of one of them.

The [Autumn 2018 outline](https://hci.stanford.edu/courses/cs377g/2018-fall/) ran the same shape as short two-week team assignments, then a final four weeks polishing one game:

1. Game design fundamentals
2. Games for learning (tabletop)
3. Persuasive games (Twine or Inform7)
4. Games that model complexity (tabletop or a game engine)
5. Refining one chosen game, ending in an open house

Learning goals in 2018 included playtesting and iterative development, and balancing learning goals with fun.
