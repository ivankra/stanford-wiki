# CS 151: Logic Programming

Programs here are sets of logical sentences, written in Epilog and run in the browser-based Sierra environment; the catalog calls them "runnable specifications". The term project asks teams to formalize the rules of a game from the Gardner corpus so that Gamemaster can play it.

## Materials

The textbook, its self-checking exercises, the lecture decks, the Epilog tools and the past-project archive are all open on the course's own server, and the same site serves every offering. The 15 graded assignments are the exception: their statements sit behind the site's own account, and that account is issued only against a SUNet id.

- **Syllabus**: the [Lessons page](http://logicprogramming.stanford.edu/stanford/lessons.php), which the site calls the syllabus: every chapter, lecture, extra reading and assignment, in 5 blocks by week. Canvas holds a Stanford-only copy for Spring 2020, 2022, 2023, 2024 and this offering; Spring 2021's is marked public but its Canvas syllabus page is switched off, and Spring 2025 uploaded none.
- **Slides**: [15 lecture PDFs](http://logicprogramming.stanford.edu/lectures/lecture_03.pdf), lectures 1–14 plus a general-game-playing deck.
- **Notes**: the [course textbook as web chapters](http://logicprogramming.stanford.edu/chapters/chapter_03.html), 14 of 17 posted, with chapters 11, 13 and 14 marked forthcoming. Each ends in [exercise pages](http://logicprogramming.stanford.edu/exercises/exercise_03_01.html) that mark your answers in the browser with no account, and the Lessons page insists on them: "Do the exercises!" McCarthy's "Programs With Common Sense" and 3 more papers are linked as PDFs.
- **Videos**: nothing. No offering's site mentions recording, and the Panopto tab on the course's Canvas shell is not readable.
- **Assignments**: 15 across the 4 teaching blocks, from a movie database to the zebra puzzle, nine-board tic-tac-toe and scheduling. `problem.php` redirects to the site's sign-in, and signing up takes a SUNet id: "We will send your password to your Stanford email address."
- **Solutions**: none posted, for the assignments or the chapter exercises, which mark answers without explaining them.
- **Exams**: none; the grade is the assignments and the project.
- **Projects**: the [projects directory](http://logicprogramming.stanford.edu/projects/) is the standing archive, by year: 2019 has 14 project sites, 2020 13 HTML reports, 2021 one, 2022 nothing, and 2024 25 project videos (Battleship, Othello, Minesweeper, course and coterm planners). 2023, 2025 and this offering have no folder, although the site promises each year's submissions will be uploaded.
- **Repo**: [Epilog](http://logicprogramming.stanford.edu/epilog/homepage/index.php), the Sierra IDE, Sierralite and EpilogJS, with worked circuit, Peano-arithmetic and tournament examples; the project targets the [Gamemaster](http://gamemaster.stanford.edu) environment.

## Syllabus

The [Lessons page](http://logicprogramming.stanford.edu/stanford/lessons.php) divides the quarter into five blocks:

1. **Introduction** (week 1): datasets; readings include McCarthy's "Programs With Common Sense". Assignments on movies, metadata and Escher.
2. **Queries and transitions** (weeks 2–3): relational queries, query evaluation, functional queries, transitions. Assignments on arithmetic, cryptarithmetic, popularity and "narrow and wide".
3. **Views** (weeks 4–5): view definitions and evaluation, simple examples, lists, sets and trees. Assignments on chess, permutations, satisfiability and the zebra puzzle.
4. **Functions and operations** (weeks 6–7): operations, abductive logic programming, applications, constraints, goals, and variants of logic programming. Assignments on algebra, nine-board tic-tac-toe, pelican hunters and scheduling.
5. **Project** (weeks 8–10): general game playing. No lectures; consultation sessions, then peer evaluation of other teams' presentations. Teams are three students, "four ok and two possible in extreme circumstances", and submit a video, a report and evaluations.

## Prerequisites

- Programming and data structures: [CS 106B](CS%20106B.md) or equivalent. No logic background is assumed — the textbook starts from datasets.
