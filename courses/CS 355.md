# CS 355: Advanced Topics in Cryptography

A second cryptography course, aimed at people who might go on to do research in it: foundations proved properly, then a tour of the constructions the field currently runs on. The catalog allows it to be repeated for credit, and the topic changes — Spring 2026 is a broad survey the site calls "Topics in Cryptography", where Spring 2025 was a course on SNARKs.

## Materials

Everything is on the public course server: lecture notes, problem sets and their LaTeX sources.

- **Syllabus**: the [schedule](https://cs355.stanford.edu/schedule/) lists all nineteen lectures by unit, and each lecture page adds readings and clarifying notes; [policies](https://cs355.stanford.edu/policies/) has grading and collaboration rules.
- **Notes**: a [notes PDF](https://cs355.stanford.edu/lecture-1.pdf) per lecture, linked from the schedule.
- **Videos**: none — "lecture notes will be posted, but the lectures will not be recorded".
- **Assignments**: five [problem sets](https://cs355.stanford.edu/psets/), each with `.tex` source and a shared preamble, plus the required [write-up template](https://cs355.stanford.edu/homework.tex).
- **Exams**: none; the five problem sets are 20% each.
- **Projects**: none in this offering.
- Solutions are not published, and looking at past offerings' solutions is an honor-code violation.

## Syllabus

Nineteen lectures in six units, per the [schedule](https://cs355.stanford.edu/schedule/).

1. **Foundations** (L1-4): one-way functions, pseudorandom generators and hardcore bits; pseudorandom functions; pseudorandom permutations from PRFs; commitment schemes.
2. **Cryptanalysis** (L5-6): the Infineon attack; attacks on groups.
3. **Elliptic curves** (L7-8): introduction to elliptic curves; pairings.
4. **Zero-knowledge proofs** (L9-13): interactive proofs and zero knowledge; sigma protocols; non-interactivity and Fiat-Shamir; polynomial commitments; polynomial IOPs.
5. **Multi-party computation** (L14-16): oblivious transfer and garbled circuits; secret sharing; private information retrieval.
6. **Lattices** (L17-18): learning with errors and Regev encryption; fully homomorphic encryption.

A guest lecture by Amit Behera closes the term.

## Prerequisites

- An introductory cryptography course: [CS 255](CS%20255.md) or equivalent.
- The site adds only "curiosity about cryptography".

The Boneh-Shoup book is usable as a reference but no textbook is required.
