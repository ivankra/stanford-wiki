# CS 355: Advanced Topics in Cryptography

A second cryptography course, aimed at people who might go on to do research in it: foundations proved properly, then a tour of the constructions the field currently runs on. The catalog allows it to be repeated for credit, and the topic changes — Spring 2026 is a broad survey the site calls "Topics in Cryptography", where Spring 2025 was a course on SNARKs.

## Materials

Everything the course hands out is on its public server — a notes PDF for each of the nineteen lectures and all five problem sets — and there is nothing else to get: "Lecture notes will be posted, but the lectures will not be recorded."

- **Syllabus**: the [schedule](https://cs355.stanford.edu/schedule/) lists all nineteen lectures by unit, and each lecture page adds readings and clarifying notes; [policies](https://cs355.stanford.edu/policies/) has grading, late days and the collaboration and LLM rules.
- **Slides**: none. The course teaches from notes, and the schedule links a notes PDF per lecture and no decks. Spring 2025, run as a SNARK course, did post [two](https://securitylab.github.io/cs355-spring25/lectures/lecture1.pdf) of its nineteen.
- **Notes**: a [notes PDF](https://cs355.stanford.edu/lecture-1.pdf) per lecture, linked from the schedule and from each lecture page.
- **Videos**: none, by the home page's own statement quoted above; attendance is "strongly encouraged" instead.
- **Assignments**: five [problem sets](https://cs355.stanford.edu/psets/), each with its `.tex` source and a shared preamble, plus the required [write-up template](https://cs355.stanford.edu/homework.tex). Spring 2025's three homeworks and its [SNARK programming project](https://securitylab.github.io/cs355-spring25/hw/proj1.pdf) are also up.
- **Solutions**: none published. The policy page goes further and makes looking at past offerings' solutions an honor-code violation, asking students who find any online to report them.
- **Exams**: none — "There is no midterm or final exam"; the five problem sets are 20% each.
- **Projects**: none in this offering. Spring 2025's programming project is the exception, and it is public.
- **Repo**: none; nothing in this offering is programmed.
- **Older offerings**: every Spring from [2020](https://crypto.stanford.edu/cs355/20sp/schedule) to [2024](https://crypto.stanford.edu/cs355/24sp/schedule) keeps its own site on the crypto group's host, each with its schedule, 14 to 20 lecture-note PDFs and five problem sets — six full syllabuses for a course whose topic rotates.
- **Textbook**: the free [Boneh–Shoup book](https://cryptobook.us/) is an optional reference, with no required text.

## Syllabus

Nineteen lectures in six units, per the [schedule](https://cs355.stanford.edu/schedule/).

1. **Foundations** (L1-4): one-way functions, pseudorandom generators and hardcore bits; pseudorandom functions; pseudorandom permutations from PRFs; commitment schemes.
2. **Cryptanalysis** (L5-6): the Infineon attack; attacks on groups.
3. **Elliptic curves** (L7-8): introduction to elliptic curves; pairings.
4. **Zero-knowledge proofs** (L9-13): interactive proofs and zero knowledge; sigma protocols; non-interactivity and Fiat-Shamir; polynomial commitments; polynomial IOPs.
5. **Multi-party computation** (L14-16): oblivious transfer and garbled circuits; secret sharing; private information retrieval.
6. **Lattices** (L17-18): learning with errors and Regev encryption; fully homomorphic encryption.

A guest lecture by Amit Behera closes the term.

## Prerequisites

- An introductory cryptography course: [CS 255](CS%20255.md) or equivalent.
- The site adds only "curiosity about cryptography".

The Boneh-Shoup book is usable as a reference but no textbook is required.
