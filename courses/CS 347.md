# CS 347: Human-Computer Interaction: Foundations and Frontiers

A reading course on HCI's animating theories and the recent research built on them: about two papers per lecture, a commentary on each, and a weekly small-group tutorial with no laptops. Concepts, not design practice.

## Materials

Everything a reader needs is public and stays public: each of the 8 offerings since Winter 2020 keeps its own site, with the whole reading list, every commentary prompt and a deck per lecture. Lectures are not recorded, for anyone.

- **Syllabus**: the [Autumn 2026 schedule](https://stanfordhci.github.io/cs347-fall-2026/syllabus) gives 10 weeks in 6 units, about 2 papers a lecture, each with the prompt its commentary must answer; the [logistics page](https://stanfordhci.github.io/cs347-fall-2026/logistics) adds the grading split — tutorials 40%, 4 quizzes 40%, final 20% — and the attendance rule.
- **Slides**: 2 decks so far, and the set is still being released; every recent offering has a full one, most recently [Spring 2026's 18](https://magrawala.github.io/cs347-sp26/syllabus.html) and [Winter 2025's 19](https://stanfordhci.github.io/cs347-winter-2025/syllabus). [Winter 2020](https://hci.stanford.edu/courses/cs347/2020/slides/), [Spring 2021](https://hci.stanford.edu/courses/cs347/2021/slides/) and [Spring 2022](https://hci.stanford.edu/courses/cs347/2022/slides/) serve theirs as plain directory listings.
- **Notes**: none; a reading course writes none. The syllabus hosts scans of 5 book chapters, and links the papers themselves, a good share of which sit behind ACM, Science or JSTOR paywalls with a pointer to Stanford's off-campus proxy.
- **Videos**: none — "Lectures are not recorded", and the grade rests on in-person tutorial discussion.
- **Assignments**: the [commentaries](https://stanfordhci.github.io/cs347-fall-2026/commentaries), one per reading, due at noon on the day of class; the page explains what a good one does, and the schedule carries every prompt. [Winter 2020](https://hci.stanford.edu/courses/cs347/2020/syllabus.php) also posted its statistics tutorial's exercise sheet and data.
- **Solutions**: not posted for the quizzes. Winter 2020's statistics exercises are the exception: [worked answers](https://hci.stanford.edu/courses/cs347/2020/slides/13-research-stats-solutions.html) sit in its slides listing, as HTML and a Jupyter notebook.
- **Exams**: 4 in-class closed-note quizzes and a 45-minute closed-note final, 60% of the grade; no paper or practice paper is published by any offering.
- **Projects**: none this term. The offerings from Winter 2020 to Spring 2022 ran a team research project instead of quizzes, with abstracts, video rounds, a project fair and a final paper, and their schedules keep the deadlines.
- **Repo**: none; the course is "focused on conceptual understanding rather than implementation and design practice".

## Syllabus

Six units over ten weeks:

1. **Ubiquitous computing**: Weiser's vision, tangible bits, activity sensing.
2. **Design**: design cognition, participatory design, reflection-in-action, LLM feedback on UI mockups.
3. **Social computing**: remote collaboration, groupware, feminist HCI, reranking feeds against polarization.
4. **Software and tools**: direct manipulation versus agents, LLM prompt chains, agentic coding, generative content-creation tools.
5. **Cognition**: visualization, the keystroke-level model, generative agents, Simon's science of design.
6. **Global citizenship**: ability-based design, accessible image generation, "As We May Think", a UIST 2026 paper of one's choice.
