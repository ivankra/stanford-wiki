# CS 154: Introduction to the Theory of Computation

The theory course for students who already write proofs: the same regions as [CS 103](CS%20103.md) — automata, computability, complexity — taken to Cook-Levin, the polynomial hierarchy and interactive proofs, with streaming and communication complexity along the way. It runs flipped, on a pre-recorded lecture series that anyone can watch.

## Materials

Unusually open for reasons of format: because the class is flipped, the whole lecture series is on YouTube and every deck is on the instructor's own site. Only the problem sets and exams are held back.

- **Syllabus**: the [site](https://theory.stanford.edu/~liyang/teaching/computability.html) — topic list, twenty lectures with their Sipser chapters and video numbers, grading and problem-set dates.
- **Slides**: [Reingold's slide page](https://omereingold.wordpress.com/cs-154-introduction-to-automata-and-complexity-theory/) publishes a PDF per topic for the entire course.
- **Notes**: supplementary notes on the same page — [DFA minimization and Myhill-Nerode](https://omereingold.wordpress.com/wp-content/uploads/2017/06/notemindfa.pdf), streaming algorithms — plus linked papers (Angluin on learning DFAs, Cormode on frequent elements).
- **Videos**: the [full lecture series](https://www.youtube.com/channel/UCrz06enpAptXhu4pYJfR-EQ/videos), numbered to match the schedule.
- **Assignments**: seven problem sets, dated on the site but not posted there.
- **Exams**: an in-class midterm in week 6 and a final; no papers.
- **Projects**: none.

## Syllabus

Three blocks over twenty lectures, tracking Sipser:

1. **Automata theory** (lectures 1–7): DFAs, NFAs and closure properties, regular expressions, the pumping lemma, DFA minimization, Myhill-Nerode, then streaming algorithms and communication complexity.
2. **Computability theory** (8–14): Turing machines, unrecognizability and undecidability, mapping reductions, Rice's theorem, oracle reductions and self-reference, Kolmogorov complexity.
3. **Complexity theory** (14–20): time complexity, NP and polynomial-time reductions, two lectures on Cook-Levin, further NP-completeness, coNP and the polynomial hierarchy, space complexity.

Grading is 50% problem sets (seven, done in groups of up to three), 20% midterm, 30% final.

## Prerequisites

- [CS 103](CS%20103.md) or CS 103B. The catalog notes the other direction too: [CS 103](CS%20103.md) tells students with significant proofwriting experience to take CS 154 instead.
