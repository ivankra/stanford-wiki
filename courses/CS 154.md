# CS 154: Introduction to the Theory of Computation

The theory course for students who already write proofs: the same regions as [CS 103](CS%20103.md) — automata, computability, complexity — taken to Cook-Levin, the polynomial hierarchy and interactive proofs, with streaming and communication complexity along the way. It runs flipped, on a pre-recorded lecture series that anyone can watch.

## Materials

Because the class is flipped, its instruction is all public: the lecture series is on YouTube and the decks and notes are on the instructor's own site, and the schedule maps each lecture to both. What a student does with it is not: the 7 problem sets and the 2 exams, 100% of the grade, are published nowhere.

- **Syllabus**: the [site](https://theory.stanford.edu/~liyang/teaching/computability.html) is the whole course on one page — topic list, 20 lectures with their Sipser chapters and the video numbers covering each, grading, and the dated problem-set and midterm schedule.
- **Slides**: [Reingold's page for the course](https://omereingold.wordpress.com/cs-154-introduction-to-automata-and-complexity-theory/), 51 PDFs covering every topic. The files date from 2014 to 2020 and the current schedule still points at them.
- **Notes**: written notes on the same page for [DFA minimization and Myhill-Nerode](https://omereingold.wordpress.com/wp-content/uploads/2017/06/notemindfa.pdf), streaming, Rice's theorem, logic and NP-completeness, with the papers they draw on (Angluin on learning DFAs among them).
- **Videos**: the course's own [YouTube channel](https://www.youtube.com/channel/UCrz06enpAptXhu4pYJfR-EQ/videos), Reingold's pre-recorded series numbered to 42, which the schedule assigns lecture by lecture. The slide page warns that it "contains ads".
- **Assignments**, **Solutions**: 7 problem sets, done in groups of up to 3 and worth half the grade; the schedule gives their due dates and nothing else, and no answers are posted.
- **Exams**: an in-class midterm in week 6 and a final, the other half of the grade; no paper or practice paper is published.
- **Projects**, **Repo**: neither; a proof course with no programming component.
- **Textbook**: Sipser, *Introduction to the Theory of Computation*, which the schedule maps chapter by chapter.

## Syllabus

Three blocks over twenty lectures, tracking Sipser:

1. **Automata theory** (lectures 1–7): DFAs, NFAs and closure properties, regular expressions, the pumping lemma, DFA minimization, Myhill-Nerode, then streaming algorithms and communication complexity.
2. **Computability theory** (8–14): Turing machines, unrecognizability and undecidability, mapping reductions, Rice's theorem, oracle reductions and self-reference, Kolmogorov complexity.
3. **Complexity theory** (14–20): time complexity, NP and polynomial-time reductions, two lectures on Cook-Levin, further NP-completeness, coNP and the polynomial hierarchy, space complexity.

Grading is 50% problem sets (seven, done in groups of up to three), 20% midterm, 30% final.

## Prerequisites

- [CS 103](CS%20103.md) or CS 103B. The catalog notes the other direction too: [CS 103](CS%20103.md) tells students with significant proofwriting experience to take CS 154 instead.
