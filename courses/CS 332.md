# CS 332: Advanced Survey of Reinforcement Learning

Brunskill's research seminar, sitting above [CS 234](CS%20234.md): each session pairs two recent papers with student presenters, working across offline RL, meta-learning, theory, multi-agent settings, causal RL and the then-new use of transformers as sequence models for control. The opening reading is "Deep Reinforcement Learning at the Edge of the Statistical Precipice", which sets the tone — the course is as interested in how RL progress is measured as in the methods. Two exams and an original research paper.

## Materials

The syllabus and its reading list are entirely public, papers included, and nothing else is: every presentation deck sits behind a Google sign-in, and the 2 exams and the research paper are published nowhere.

- **Syllabus**: the [session table](https://cs332.stanford.edu/syllabus.md) dates every topic, links both papers, names their student presenters, and marks the exam and project deadlines.

- **Notes**: none of the course's own; the readings are published papers and each one opens.
- **Videos**: nothing mentioned.
- **Assignments**, **Solutions**: none; the work is presenting 2 papers, a reading-questions form, the exams and the paper.
- **Exams**: 2 are scheduled, on 10/20 and 11/17; neither is published.
- **Projects**: students write an original research paper and present it in the last 2 weeks, but none is published.
- **Repo**: none; the course sets no code.

## Syllabus

[The draft syllabus](https://cs332.stanford.edu/syllabus.md) runs 20 sessions.

1. Foundations (9/27-10/6): evaluating RL progress, models and representation learning, online RL, multi-task and meta-RL.
2. Offline and multi-objective (10/11-10/18): two sessions on offline RL, then multiple objectives.
3. Theory and structure (10/25-11/15): two sessions of RL theory, multi-agent RL, optimization-based RL, transformers and sequence modeling, causal RL.
4. Projects (11/29-12/8): presentations, a panel, and peer review.
