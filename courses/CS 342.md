# CS 342: Building for Digital Health

A project course whose distinguishing constraint is that the application has to be allowed to exist. Teams pick from pre-vetted School of Medicine projects and build a full-stack research application, but the deliverable is a *deployed* one, which means going through data privacy clearance and IRB approval as part of the coursework rather than around it. It is run out of the Byers Center for Biodesign's digital health group, whose Spezi framework and study apps are the working substrate. Enrollment is limited and by application, and the grading is the medical option.

## Syllabus

No schedule could be read. The catalog states the shape:

> This project-based course will provide a comprehensive overview of key requirements in the design and full-stack implementation of a digital health research application. Several pre-vetted and approved projects from the Stanford School of Medicine will be available for students to select from and build. Student teams learn about all necessary approval processes to deploy a digital health solution (data privacy clearance/IRB approval, etc.) and be guided in the development of front-end and back-end infrastructure using best practices. The final project will be the presentation and deployment of a fully approved digital health research application.

The class meets once a week for three hours.

## Prerequisites

- Programming: CS 106A and CS 106B are required.
- Recommended: CS 193P or CS 193A (mobile development), CS 142 (web applications), CS 47 and CS 110.
