# CS 342: Building for Digital Health

A project course whose distinguishing constraint is that the application has to be allowed to exist. Teams pick from pre-vetted School of Medicine projects and build a full-stack research application, but the deliverable is a *deployed* one, which means going through data privacy clearance and IRB approval as part of the coursework rather than around it. It is run out of the Byers Center for Biodesign's digital health group, whose Spezi framework and study apps are the working substrate. Enrollment is limited and by application, and the grading is the medical option.

## Materials

The teaching goes to Canvas: no decks, no schedule, no handouts. The output does not — every team's repository is public in the course's GitHub organization, year after year. Filling the gap is [med253.stanford.edu](https://med253.stanford.edu/), the Winter 2022 site, still live and completely open.

- **Syllabus**: the [Notion site](https://stanfordbdhg.notion.site/CS342-MED253-Building-for-Digital-Health-ea1a66dfc9b44c90b6908de0d083d4e1) gives the 6 instructors, the Wednesday 3:30-6:20 slot in Littlefield L107, the grading split (10% attendance, 10% participation, 30% assignments, 20% midterm, 30% final), a workload of "8-10 hours of work each week beyond classroom hours", an attendance rule where two unexcused absences mean No Credit, and the HIPAA and IP terms. Canvas is named as the home of the course material.
- **Slides**, **Assignments**: closed, on Canvas. [Winter 2022](https://med253.stanford.edu/) published its whole set instead: 20 decks, one per session, from Swift and SwiftUI through ResearchKit, CardinalKit on Firebase, Apple Health Records, interoperability and BigQuery analytics; and 5 assignment documents with the midterm template and rubric.
- **Notes**, **Videos**: not found; no source says the sessions are recorded. **Solutions**, **Exams**: none, the course grading deliverables and two presentations instead.
- **Projects**: this offering's 4 teams each have a public repository in the [course organization](https://github.com/CS342) — SchoolKit, PIP, HeartSense, Stream — and Biodesign Digital Health runs a [showcase](https://bdh.stanford.edu/projects/cs-342-projects) of finished apps, Intake and PICS among them.
- **Repo**: that organization keeps every team's code back to 2023 plus each year's example application, and [StanfordBDHG](https://github.com/StanfordBDHG) holds the frameworks they build on: Spezi, SpeziVibe, HealthKitOnFHIR, ResearchKitOnFHIR.

## Syllabus

Winter 2026 rebuilt the course around AI-assisted development and says so plainly: "This year marks a transformative shift: for the first time, you'll learn to prototype healthcare applications with the help of AI coding agents." Students work with GitHub Copilot, Cursor and Claude Code against **SpeziVibe**, a new cross-platform framework using React Native and Expo, previewing on a phone by scanning a QR code with Expo Go. Project paths integrate either the open-source EHR MedPlum or Google Cloud, both under HIPAA-compliant architectures. The site's own summary of the ambition: "Digital health innovation shouldn't take months - it should take hours."

Over ten weeks teams "tackle a project and launch an app-enabled MVP solution", covering digital health software development, HealthIT policy, hardware integration, wearables, and health data privacy and security, and present to a panel of digital health experts in the final week.

The Winter 2022 schedule shows the weekly arc before the rewrite, and much of it is structural rather than framework-specific: what makes health apps different (HIPAA, IRB protocols) in week 1; mobile development and a research framework in weeks 2-3; Apple Health Records and data-model, dashboard and backend-security work in weeks 4-5; interoperability and midterm presentations in week 6; research IT and cloud analytics in week 7; then workshops, 2 mentor days and final presentations. The catalog still describes that older version.
