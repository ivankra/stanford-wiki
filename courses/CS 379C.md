# CS 379C: Computational Models of the Neocortex

Dean's premise is that the shortest route to agents that collaborate with people runs through what neuroscience already knows about how people do it. The course is almost entirely project: a proposal at midterm and a report at finals are the whole grade, and researchers from DeepMind, Google Brain and local startups sit in as consultants. Spring 2021's theme was curriculum-learning protocols for systems that ground language acquisition in the skills they are learning — with automated code synthesis as the target task.

## Syllabus

The course is organized around project themes rather than a lecture sequence; the [projects page](https://web.stanford.edu/class/cs379c/projects.html) is the closest thing to a syllabus.

1. **Strange loop memories**: modeling the interplay between conscious awareness as Dehaene and Graziano describe it and episodic memory as implemented in a Differentiable Neural Computer, reduced to the simplest instantiation that shows emergent behavior.
2. **On hippocampal replay**: replay as a mechanism for consolidation and planning.
3. **Differentiable programs**: program structure that gradient descent can shape.
4. **Two streams hypothesis**: the dorsal/ventral split and its architectural consequences.
5. **Cognition in cerebellum**: what a structure usually filed under motor control contributes to thought.
6. **On cortical connections**: connectivity as a design constraint on architectures.

Around these run the introductory notes on cortical circuit models and the invited talks.
