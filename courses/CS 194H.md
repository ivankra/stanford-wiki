# CS 194H: User Interface Design Project

The follow-on to [CS 147](CS%20147.md): where that course teaches the design process, this one runs a single project the length of the quarter and ends in a public presentation. Branded "dt+UX2" on its own site.

## Materials

The site is fully public and the handouts behind it are not: every assignment is a private Google Doc. What is public, on a different host, is what students made with them — an open directory under the class path holding four years of team project sites and the per-student studio sites.

- **Syllabus**: open. The [calendar](https://hci.stanford.edu/courses/cs194h/2024/wi/calendar.html) gives the ten weeks with each session's topic, the [course details](https://hci.stanford.edu/courses/cs194h/2024/wi/logistics.html) page the teaching team and policies, and the landing page a numbered table of deliverables with their due dates.
- **Assignments**: closed. That table links roughly twenty documents — five studio tasks (tagging and hierarchy, Gestalt principles, color, control, online user study), four project presentations, the Lean Business Model Canvas, website and prototype checkpoints, the poster, the video prototype, the final presentation, prototype and report — and all of them return 401.
- **Notes**: closed. The Resources document linked from the header of every page is private too.
- **Projects**: six Winter 2024 team sites at [`class/cs194h/projects/`](https://web.stanford.edu/class/cs194h/projects/), with separate archives for 2017, 2020 and 2023. Each walks its own process — QueerX, "where the good doctors are", publishes its needfinding, POVs and prototyping decks in PowerPoint and PDF across the two quarters it spans with [CS 147](CS%20147.md).
- **Slides**, **Videos**, **Solutions**, **Code**: not found. **Exams**: none.
- **Extra**: `class/cs194h/studio/` holds about a hundred per-student studio sites, dated 2017 to 2024. They are the studio assignments as completed rather than as set — one student's four pages cover visualizing news aggregation, Gestalt principles on four real websites, a geocache-hiding map that "simplifies the process for novice users but maximizes flexibility for expert users", and a color scheme. Many of the directories are empty.

## Syllabus

From the site's own description: "Advanced methods for designing, prototyping, and evaluating user interfaces to computing applications. Novel interface technology, advanced interface design methods, and prototyping tools. Substantial, quarter-long course project that will be presented in a public presentation."

The structure is the assignment table: around a dozen numbered deliverables with due dates, carrying one project from brief to public presentation.
