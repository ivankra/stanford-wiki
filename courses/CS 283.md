# CS 283: Governing Artificial Intelligence: Law, Policy, and Institutions

A survey of AI governance, taught at the Law School by faculty from law, political science and computer science. It is reading- and discussion-heavy, needs no technical background, and is graded on writing rather than exams.

## Syllabus

Nineteen lectures in eight units, plus optional weekly sections:

1. Introduction: competing visions of the AI transition
2. Technical foundations: history of governing frontier technology, AI agents and multi-agent dynamics, AI evaluations
3. Individual risks: bias, discrimination and fairness; copyright, creativity and privacy
4. Societal risk: defamation and mental-health harms, democracy, infrastructure and the environment, existential risks (AGI, autonomous R&D, CBRN)
5. National and international approaches: EU, US federal and state, rest of world, geopolitics
6. The AI economy: competition, open models and antitrust; the future of labor
7. Living with AI: being human in an AI age
8. Outlook: the future of AI governance
