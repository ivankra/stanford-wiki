# CS 263: Counting and Sampling

Two problems that turn out to be the same problem: drawing a sample from a complicated distribution, and counting how many objects satisfy a constraint. Most of the course is the Markov chain toolkit for bounding mixing times — coupling, canonical paths, Poincaré and log-Sobolev inequalities — with correlation decay, holographic algorithms and polynomial interpolation as the alternatives to sampling your way to an answer.

## Syllabus

The [archived site](https://web.archive.org/web/20230921110139/https://nimaanari.com/cs263-autumn2023/) splits the course into classic material and recent work. Classic: counting complexity and #P; the reductions between counting and sampling; "exact counting gems: spanning trees, planar perfect matchings"; sampling via Markov chains, analyzed probabilistically (stationary times, path coupling, coupling from the past) and then functionally (spectral gap, Poincaré and log-Sobolev inequalities, conductance, canonical flows); and deterministic counting through correlation decay, zeros of polynomials and Barvinok's method. Modern: "analysis of Markov chains via high-dimensional expanders, stochastic localization and localization schemes, exact sampling of infinite spin systems."
