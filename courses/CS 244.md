# CS 244: Advanced Topics in Networking

Three classes a week, each on one or two papers from Clark 1988 onward, with several taught by their authors — McKeown on switch scheduling, Tennenhouse on active networks. A written critique is due the night before every class, and the project reproduces a published figure and reports the result as a public blog post.

## Materials

Every assigned paper is hosted on the course site, so the whole reading list opens without a login, and the course's output — 14 years of replication write-ups — is a public blog. What the current offering doesn't post is decks: 1 guest lecture's, against about 30 classes. Older offerings did better.

- **Syllabus**: the [course page](https://web.stanford.edu/class/cs244/) is the schedule — each date, speaker and paper with a [PDF](https://web.stanford.edu/class/cs244/papers/DesignPhilosophyDARPA.pdf), 30 in all — followed by the critique format, grading and participation policy.
- **Slides**: 1 deck, [McKeown on switches](https://web.stanford.edu/class/cs244/slides/cs244-sp25-mckeown.pdf). [Spring 2020](https://web.stanford.edu/class/archive/cs/cs244/cs244.1206/) posted 16 and [Spring 2022](https://2022-cs244.github.io/schedule) 13.
- **Notes**: none written; the papers are the text.
- **Videos**: no offering's site mentions recording, and no public set turned up.
- **Assignments**: the 4 project steps as separate public handouts — [team formation](https://web.stanford.edu/class/cs244/assignment-1.html), [proposal](https://web.stanford.edu/class/cs244/assignment-2.html), [midterm report](https://web.stanford.edu/class/cs244/assignment-3.html), [blog post and final presentation](https://web.stanford.edu/class/cs244/assignment-4.html). Spring 2022 also posted a [bufferbloat-on-Mininet handout](https://2022-cs244.github.io/assignments/a1.pdf).
- **Solutions**, **Exams**: none of either. Critiques (20%), participation and the project carry the grade, and none of them has an answer.
- **Projects**: the whole point of the course is public. Each team replicates a published networking result and writes it up on [Reproducing Network Research](https://reproducingnetworkresearch.wordpress.com/), an open blog carrying CS 244 posts every year since 2012 — the most recent replicating "ONCache: A Cache-Based Low-Overhead Container Overlay Network". The handout is blunt about why: "We say try because you might not be able to! Whether you can or can't, both are useful scientific results."
- **Repo**: the [`stanford-cs244`](https://github.com/stanford-cs244) organization holds [bufferbloat starter code](https://github.com/stanford-cs244/cs244-bufferbloat) and an ns-3 mirror; each team's replication code lives in its own repository, linked from its blog post rather than collected here.

## Syllabus

Roughly thirty classes ([schedule](https://web.stanford.edu/class/cs244/)): Internet architecture (the DARPA design philosophy, end-to-end arguments, RFC 1958); switching (high-speed switch scheduling, 100% throughput in input-queued switches); programmability (active networks, Ethane, virtual switch platforms); LANs; congestion control across three sessions; measurement; queueing; DNS and bug-finding versus verification; empirical security over two classes; Internet governance; ethics; NICs; simulation; and a closing pair on learning in networks and SIGCOMM 2024. Project time is built into the calendar three times, with final presentations in the last week.

## Prerequisites

- Introductory networking — packet switching, routing, sockets, congestion control: [CS 144](CS%20144.md) or EE 284. Kurose & Ross or Peterson & Davie serve as refreshers.
- Python helps for the project.
