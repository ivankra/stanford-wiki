# CS 244: Advanced Topics in Networking

Three classes a week, each on one or two papers from Clark 1988 onward, with several taught by their authors — McKeown on switch scheduling, Tennenhouse on active networks. A written critique is due the night before every class, and the project reproduces a published figure and reports the result as a public blog post.

## Materials

Every assigned paper is hosted on the course site, so the whole reading list opens without a login.

- **Syllabus**: the [course page](https://web.stanford.edu/class/cs244/) is the schedule — each date, speaker and paper with a [PDF](https://web.stanford.edu/class/cs244/papers/DesignPhilosophyDARPA.pdf) — followed by the critique format, grading and participation policy.
- **Slides**: only guest decks, e.g. [McKeown on switches](https://web.stanford.edu/class/cs244/slides/cs244-sp25-mckeown.pdf).
- **Assignments**: the four project steps are separate public handouts — [team formation](https://web.stanford.edu/class/cs244/assignment-1.html), [proposal](https://web.stanford.edu/class/cs244/assignment-2.html), [midterm report](https://web.stanford.edu/class/cs244/assignment-3.html), [blog post and final presentation](https://web.stanford.edu/class/cs244/assignment-4.html).
- **Exams**: none — critiques (20%), participation and the project carry the grade.
- Submissions go by email to the staff list rather than Gradescope.

## Syllabus

Roughly thirty classes ([schedule](https://web.stanford.edu/class/cs244/)): Internet architecture (the DARPA design philosophy, end-to-end arguments, RFC 1958); switching (high-speed switch scheduling, 100% throughput in input-queued switches); programmability (active networks, Ethane, virtual switch platforms); LANs; congestion control across three sessions; measurement; queueing; DNS and bug-finding versus verification; empirical security over two classes; Internet governance; ethics; NICs; simulation; and a closing pair on learning in networks and SIGCOMM 2024. Project time is built into the calendar three times, with final presentations in the last week.

## Prerequisites

- Introductory networking — packet switching, routing, sockets, congestion control: CS 144 or EE 284. Kurose & Ross or Peterson & Davie serve as refreshers.
- Python helps for the project.
