# CS 147: Introduction to Human-Computer Interaction Design

The department's design studio, and a 20–25 hour week: interdisciplinary teams take one set of user needs from field interviews through paper, video and code prototypes to a working high-fidelity demo shown at the Industry Project Fair. Eleven assignment briefs carry the arc; lectures and readings support it.

## Materials

Every offering since Winter 2021 keeps its own site, and each one serves its decks and assignment briefs as open directory listings. Only the midterm, which runs on Gradescope, is gated.

## Syllabus

The [calendar](https://hci.stanford.edu/courses/cs147/2026/au/calendar.html) runs the project as a sequence of deliverables, each with its own brief:

1. **Discovery**: needfinding in the field — contextual inquiry, the d.school empathy fieldguide, the IDEO "Deep Dive" film — then a point of view and experience prototypes.
2. **Form**: a concept video, then an interactive medium-fidelity prototype.
3. **Evaluation**: heuristic evaluation individually, then a group summary of the evaluations of other teams' prototypes.
4. **Delivery**: a high-fidelity interactive prototype, a poster pitch, and the final report, demoed at the Industry Project Fair.

Lectures and studios supply the methods — user-centered design, cognitive principles, visual design, social software — and the studios are where deliverables are critiqued.

## Prerequisites

- Programming: [CS 106B](CS%20106B.md) or CS 106X, or equivalent. The catalog recommends CS majors also have [CS 142](CS%20142.md), [CS 193P](CS%20193P.md) or CS 193A, or take [CS 147L](CS%20147L.md) concurrently.
- The course must be taken for 5 units; only graduate students may take it for fewer, and the workload doesn't change.
