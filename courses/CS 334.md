# CS 334: Robots and Arts: Creative Applications and Projects

A studio course at the Stanford Robotics Center (SRC): teams of 3–4 from art, theater, music, engineering, CS and design build a quarter-long project with both an engineering and an artistic element, ending in a public exhibition. Mostly hands-on work, with workshops, critiques and guest lectures.

## Materials

The shape of the quarter is public and so is the robot documentation a team would need, but what students are actually asked to make is not: every assignment description and the proposal, midpoint and final-report guidelines are handed out in class and submitted on Canvas, and no deck, recording or finished project is published. The reading list is the one place an outside reader gets the course's substance, and only from Winter 2026.

- **Syllabus**: the [course site](https://robots-and-arts.github.io/) carries the 11-week dated schedule, the assessment split (project 40%, midpoint review 25%, assignments 20%, proposal 10%, reading and participation 5%), the late policy and the group-project description. This offering's readings sit "under Files in Canvas"; the [Winter 2026 site](https://robots-and-arts.github.io/win26/) links 36 of them across 8 weeks, from *Against Interpretation* and *Critical Making* through robot readability and *Toward Robotic Musicianship* to *Why AI Isn't Going to Make Art*.
- **Slides**, **Notes**: none found. The sessions are workshops, critiques and guest lectures (Amy Kurzweil, Ken Goldberg, Jaron Lanier this quarter), not slide-driven lectures.
- **Videos**: nothing found, and no source says whether the guest lectures or the exhibition are recorded.
- **Assignments**: on Canvas. The schedule names assignments 0 to 2, the project proposal, the midpoint review and the final report with their dates, and the site describes what a project should be (interactive robotic sculpture, robots in performance or storytelling, real-time human-robot painting), but no brief is linked in either offering.
- **Solutions**, **Exams**: none; the grade is the project, its reviews, the assignments and participation.
- **Projects**: the quarter ends in a public exhibition at the Stanford Robotics Center, with final reports and videos due a week later. Nothing from Winter 2026 was posted.
- **Repo**: the [robots-and-arts organization](https://github.com/robots-and-arts) holds onboarding guides and starter code for the 5 course robots — CoDrone EDU, Gita Mini, OptiTrack, Reachy and Stretch — the Reachy one with installation, quickstart and core-concepts pages, plus an OptiTrack quick start for the dance studio.

## Syllabus

Ten Wednesday sessions:

1. Introduction to robotics and arts; tools background
2. Tools workshop (assignment 0 due)
3. Assignment 1 presentations
4. Assignment 2 presentations
5. Guest lecture (Amy Kurzweil); project proposals
6. Guest lecture (Ken Goldberg); project checkpoint
7. Midpoint review
8. Guest lecture (Jaron Lanier); project checkpoint
9. Risk introduction
10. Final gallery (final report due a week later)

The Winter 2026 readings ran from art theory and neuroaesthetics through robot choreography and musicianship to critiques of AI-made art. Example projects: interactive robotic sculptures, robots in theater or dance, real-time human-robot painting.

## Prerequisites

None formal. The site asks for curiosity about robotics and AI; some programming (Python, C++) or design-tool experience helps.
