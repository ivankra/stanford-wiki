# CS 121: Equity and Governance for Artificial Intelligence

Four units that satisfy both the ethics and Writing in the Major requirements, and the writing is the work: op-eds, policy memos and research papers, argued out in sections and debates. The one technical component asks students to run the evaluation regimes that policy proposals would impose on model developers.

## Materials

The course publishes no site. One assignment is public — through Stanford's Center for Teaching and Learning, not the course.

## Syllabus

No published syllabus. The catalog sets out the shape: students "critically engage AI policy documents while debating norms of fairness, accountability, and transparency", work through "real-world legislative proposals and case studies topics including human rights, artwork, the environment, and geopolitics", and do "hands-on practice evaluating generative AI models with interactive red-teaming and automated test suites".

The published group project states its own aim: to "conduct both interactive red-teaming and automated evaluation of AI model(s) to get hands-on experience with the kinds of evaluation regimes that many policy proposals would impose on AI model developers as a condition of licensing, voluntary certifications, or as a condition of certain benefits such as government contracting", then to "reflect on what we can learn about models from these evaluation regimes, their practical feasibility, and what their limitations are". It is assessed by oral presentation of hypotheses, methods, results and analysis.

The catalog describes the course as preparing "juniors, seniors, and graduate students to participate in AI public policy design at the national and global levels", with the warning that "students will be expected to speak frequently in sections and navigate the social dynamics of multi-stakeholder negotiations."

## Prerequisites

- Writing: PWR 1 and PWR 2.
- Programming: [CS 106A](CS%20106A.md) and [CS 106B](CS%20106B.md). Comfort with short Python programs and a Unix command line "will be helpful" for the evaluation project.
