# CS 227B: General Game Playing

A general game player is given a formal description of a game it has never seen and must play it well, with no game-specific algorithm and under a start clock and a play clock. Teams build one in JavaScript over ten weeks and it is graded on weekly assignments, competition performance and a final report.

## Materials

Everything a reader needs is public on the General Game Playing site; only the Gamemaster developer account gates anything.

- **Syllabus**: the [course page](http://ggp.stanford.edu/stanford/index.php) carries the overview, the ten-session table, the grading policy and weekly notes.
- **Slides**: nine lecture decks as PDFs, from [Introduction](http://ggp.stanford.edu/lectures/lecture_01.pdf) to [Competition Strategies and the Future](http://ggp.stanford.edu/lectures/lecture_09.pdf), indexed on [Lessons](http://ggp.stanford.edu/stanford/lessons.php).
- **Notes**: the staff's [online textbook](http://ggp.stanford.edu/chapters/preface.html), chapters 1-10, with interactive [minimax and alpha-beta exercises](http://ggp.stanford.edu/exercises/exercise_03_01.html) and a dozen GGP papers linked per lesson.
- **Assignments**: all ten, e.g. [assignment 1](http://ggp.stanford.edu/stanford/assignment.php?assignment=assignment1), each naming the test games to run a player against.
- **Exams**: none; the last session is the competition.
- **Code**: the starter code and the rulechecker, Standalone and Manager tools are on [Gamemaster](http://gamemaster.stanford.edu/) behind a sign-in; its game library, match viewer and leaderboard are open.
- A [public self-study version](http://ggp.stanford.edu/public/index.php) of the course carries the same slides and chapters without the assignments.

## Syllabus

From the [course page](http://ggp.stanford.edu/stanford/index.php), ten Wednesday sessions:

1. Introduction
2. Game management: reading and writing GDL, the rule and style checkers, running matches
3. Complete search: a first player for small games, minimax and alpha-beta
4. Incomplete search: depth- and time-limited search, mobility, focus and goal-value heuristics, time management
5. Statistical search: Monte Carlo search and MCTS over random depth charges
6. Metagaming: work done offline, during the start clock
7. Grounding and symbolizing: grounding rule sets to speed up play
8. Game reformulation: pruning needless actions, splitting compound games into subgames
9. Competition strategies and the future of GGP
10. Final competition: preliminary round, semifinal and final ladders, then a challenge round against the champion

## Prerequisites

- Programming: required, and players must be written in JavaScript; the site links two tutorials for anyone new to it.
- Recommended, not required: discrete mathematics and logic, [CS 103](CS%20103.md) or equivalent.
