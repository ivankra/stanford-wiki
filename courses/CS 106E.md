# CS 106E: Exploring Computing

Not a programming course: the aim is understanding what is under the hood. The site pitches it at "future managers and PMs who will work with or who will lead programmers", and the handouts it reuses from [CS 105](CS%20105.md) show the lineage.

## Syllabus

The [lecture-note index](https://web.stanford.edu/class/cs106e/) is the fullest published outline, 26 lectures in five arcs:

1. **Representation** (1–3): bits, bytes and binary; digital images; digital music.
2. **Hardware and systems** (4–7): computer hardware overview, the CPU, memory, operating systems.
3. **Networks and the web** (8–12): network hardware and naming, protocols, the World Wide Web, HTML and CSS, webpage layout.
4. **Software** (13–18): databases, programming languages, server-side programming, client-side programming, cloud computing and the Internet of Things, software engineering.
5. **Consequences** (19–26): computer security — introduction, attacks, defenses; privacy and big data; AI and machine learning over two lectures; human-computer interaction and design; theory.

## Prerequisites

None. The catalog says some high-school programming "will help students get the most out of the class, but the course can be successfully completed with no prerequisites".
