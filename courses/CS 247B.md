# CS 247B: Design for Behavior Change

Christina Wodtke's studio on designing software that changes what people do — and on whether it should. One project, *Change is Hard*, runs the whole quarter in teams of 4-5: students measure their own behavior in week 1, recruit and interview participants, run a baseline study, design an intervention, run a second study on it, and usability-test a clickable prototype. Running alongside is a weekly ethics discussion — persuasive technology, when nudging becomes manipulation, design justice — worth 15% on its own. Coding is explicitly optional; the deliverables are studies, prototypes and write-ups.

## Syllabus

Ten weeks, per the [syllabus](https://cs247b.stanford.edu/syllabus.html), each pairing a method with an ethics reading.

1. **Weeks 1–2, framing**: core concepts and self-monitoring — students run a "Measuring Me" study on themselves; then recruiting, screeners and interviewing.
2. **Weeks 3–4, research**: the baseline study, a psychology intensive and secondary research; then synthesis and ideation into behavioral personas and journey maps.
3. **Weeks 5–6, intervention**: exploring solution spaces and running the intervention study; architecture design and assumption mapping.
4. **Weeks 7–8, making it real**: social bricolage, assumption testing and visual design — wireflows, sketchy screens, mood boards, style tiles; then onboarding, premortems and an ethical creative project.
5. **Weeks 9–10**: usability testing against a clickable prototype and a final round of iteration, closing with the expo.

Weekly sketchnotes accompany the readings (habit science, tiny prompts, *Thinking Fast & Slow*, "Three Myths of Behavior Change"), and each week's ethics discussion takes an article or podcast.

## Prerequisites

- An introductory design course: [CS 147](CS%20147.md), which the CS 247 series builds on with "advanced methods and tools for research, prototyping, and user interface design".
- No programming is required. Students need a way to sketch low-fidelity prototypes and to build interactive ones — Balsamiq, Figma, InVision or similar — and a customizable life-tracker app for the self-monitoring exercise.
