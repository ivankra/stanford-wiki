# CS 349F: Fabric Architectures For AI Systems

How the network fabrics inside AI GPU clusters, clouds and "time-sensitive systems" such as trading platforms and multiplayer games deliver deterministic, ultra-low latency at near-100% goodput. Industry experts who build and run large GPU and CPU clusters speak to the class.

## Materials

Nothing from this offering is public: the course site, [web.stanford.edu/class/cs349f](https://web.stanford.edu/class/cs349f/), answers 403 Forbidden on every path tried.

- **Older offering**: the whole of Autumn 2020 is still served, from before the course was re-scoped, at [`cs349f.1212`](https://web.stanford.edu/class/archive/cs/cs349f/cs349f.1212/). Its [handouts page](https://web.stanford.edu/class/archive/cs/cs349f/cs349f.1212/handouts.html) links about twenty [decks](https://web.stanford.edu/class/archive/cs/cs349f/cs349f.1212/slides/) — fairness and low latency, clock synchronization and its applications, CloudEx, guest talks from Deutsche Börse, Exablaze, Nasdaq and Goldman Sachs on precise timing, ultra-low-latency trading infrastructure and forex algorithms — three CloudEx review sessions with slides, four Jupyter notebooks and a C++ API bundle, and week-by-week summaries of the trading competition that closed the course. It is six years old and covers the financial-systems subject rather than the current AI-fabric one, so no rating rests on it.

## Syllabus

From the catalog description; no public syllabus.

1. Data center fabric architectures: the fat tree topology
2. Transport protocols: congestion control and load balancing
3. Scheduling: job scheduling, fabric scheduling and how they interact
4. Edge-centric vs network-centric approaches to high-performance fabrics, and how they combine
5. Guest talks from industry
