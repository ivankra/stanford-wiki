# CS 349F: Fabric Architectures For AI Systems

How the network fabrics inside AI GPU clusters, clouds and time-sensitive systems such as trading platforms and multiplayer games deliver deterministic, ultra-low latency at near-100% goodput. Industry experts who build and run large GPU and CPU clusters speak to the class.

## Syllabus

From the catalog description; no public syllabus.

1. Data center fabric architectures: the fat tree topology
2. Transport protocols: congestion control and load balancing
3. Scheduling: job scheduling, fabric scheduling and how they interact
4. Edge-centric vs network-centric approaches to high-performance fabrics, and how they combine
5. Guest talks from industry

## History

First offered in Spring 2019, the course has been re-scoped once, from financial technology to the network fabrics of AI clusters.

- **Spring 2019–2023, Technology for Financial Systems**: Balaji Prabhakar and Mendel Rosenblum's seminar on how finance and computing drive each other: switch and data-center fabrics, ultra-low-latency trading systems, cloud risk computation, fairness and synchronized clocks in cloud exchanges, AI in fintech and distributed ledgers. [Spring 2019](https://web.archive.org/web/20190406193219/http://web.stanford.edu:80/class/cs349f/index.html) ran 9 lectures, graded on attendance and a 2-page report. [Autumn 2020](https://web.stanford.edu/class/archive/cs/cs349f/cs349f.1212/handouts.html) gave 7 weeks to lectures, with guests from Deutsche Börse, Exablaze, Nasdaq, Goldman Sachs and Columbia's Costis Maglaras, and 3 to a competition in which students traded as high-frequency traders on CloudEx, a cloud-hosted exchange.
- **Winter 2025–Autumn 2026, Fabric Architectures For AI Systems**: the same 2-unit seminar of lectures and industry speakers, now on the fabrics inside GPU clusters and clouds: fat-tree topologies, congestion control and load balancing, job and fabric scheduling, and edge- versus network-centric design. Trading platforms remain only as one example of a latency-critical system. Nothing from these offerings is public.
