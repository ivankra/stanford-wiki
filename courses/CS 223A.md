# CS 223A: Introduction to Robotics

Oussama Khatib's manipulator course, the entry point to Stanford's robotics sequence: how to describe a robot arm's geometry, differentiate it, give it a dynamics model, and close a control loop around that model. It stays model-based and analytical — eight pen-and-paper problem sets, a midterm and a final, no programming assignment and no project — and the payoff is operational space control, Khatib's own formulation, in week ten.

## Syllabus

Ten weeks, per the [timeline](https://cs.stanford.edu/groups/manips/teaching/cs223a/), with two review sessions bolted on (essential math in week one, essential physics after the midterm).

1. **Weeks 1–2, geometry**: spatial descriptions, articulated body systems, forward kinematics.
2. **Weeks 3–4, differential kinematics**: Jacobians across three lectures, then inverse kinematics and workspace, and trajectory generation.
3. **Week 6**: midterm.
4. **Weeks 7–8, dynamics**: acceleration and inertia, the Newton-Euler formulation, the explicit form of the equations of motion.
5. **Weeks 9–10, control**: joint space control, then operational space control; a tour of the Stanford Robotics Center closes the term.

## Prerequisites

The catalog states none and recommends matrix algebra. In practice the problem sets assume comfort with linear algebra — rotation matrices, homogeneous coordinates, pseudo-inverses — and with the mechanics in a first physics sequence.
