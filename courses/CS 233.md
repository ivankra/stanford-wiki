# CS 233: Geometric and Topological Data Analysis

Methods for data that has shape — images, video, 3D scans, GPS traces — using classical geometry and topology alongside deep learning. The arc runs from representing one data set, to comparing two, to extracting shared structure across a whole network of them. Four programming homeworks and a midterm; no project.

## Syllabus

Twenty lectures, per the [schedule](https://graphics.stanford.edu/courses/cs233-26-spring/schedule.html).

1. **Linear methods** (weeks 1–2): PCA and kernel PCA; CCA and multidimensional scaling.
2. **Graphs** (week 2): Laplacians, Laplacian embeddings, spectral clustering.
3. **Topology** (weeks 3–4): complexes and homology groups; persistent homology, barcodes and diagrams; topological inference and Mapper.
4. **Geometry** (weeks 4–5): voxel grids, point clouds, meshes and solid models; volumetric, mesh and graph CNNs.
5. **Embeddings and data sets** (week 5): LLE, Laplacian eigenmaps, Isomap, autoencoders, t-SNE; ImageNet and ShapeNet through LAION-5B and ObjaverseXL, and annotation transport.
6. **Matching** (weeks 6–8): rigid and non-rigid alignment; intrinsic descriptors and heat and wave kernel signatures; Laplace–Beltrami operators; PointNet and successors; symmetries.
7. **Joint analysis** (weeks 8–10): functional maps; networks of shapes and cycle consistency; shape differences and variability; a guest lecture on geometric regularization for 3D shape generation.
