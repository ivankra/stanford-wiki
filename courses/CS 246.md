# CS 246: Mining Massive Data Sets

Jure Leskovec's course on algorithms that survive contact with web-scale data: the constraint throughout is one or two passes over data that does not fit in memory, and the answers are approximation, hashing and sketching. Half the syllabus tracks the instructors' own free textbook chapter by chapter; the other half — graph representation learning, GNNs, relational deep learning — comes from the SNAP group's recent papers. Assignments run on Spark. It is the successor to CS 345A, split into this course and [CS 341](CS%20341.md).

## Materials

The course publishes everything but the recordings, and says so: "We are happy for anyone to use these resources, but we cannot grade the work of any students who are not officially enrolled." Lectures go to Canvas, and the only public video is the decade-old Coursera MOOC.

- **Syllabus**: the [home page](https://web.stanford.edu/class/cs246/) is the whole course: dated schedule, per-lecture readings, prerequisites and an [information handout](https://web.stanford.edu/class/cs246/handouts/CS246_info_handout_26.pdf). The SNAP group keeps the same page for every offering back to [Winter 2020](https://snap.stanford.edu/class/cs246-2020/), and 10 more back to Winter 2011.
- **Slides**: all 20 decks under `slides/`, from [MapReduce and Spark](https://web.stanford.edu/class/cs246/slides/01-intro.pdf) to the Anthropic guest lecture. Each archived offering keeps its own set, so the curriculum's drift is readable: [Winter 2020](https://snap.stanford.edu/class/cs246-2020/) still taught SVMs, [Spring 2021](https://snap.stanford.edu/class/cs246-2021/) added the first GNN lecture, and [Winter 2025](https://snap.stanford.edu/class/cs246-2025/WWW/) added relational deep learning.
- **Notes**: *[Mining of Massive Datasets](http://www.mmds.org/)* by Leskovec, Rajaraman and Ullman, free to download, with the schedule linking individual chapters.
- **Videos**: Canvas, for enrolled students, in this and every offering back to Winter 2020. The site's public alternative is the [Coursera MOOC's channel](https://www.youtube.com/channel/UC_Oao2FYkLAUlUVkBfze4jg/videos), short segments uploaded about 10 years ago that cover the textbook half of the course and none of the graph-learning half.
- **Assignments**: 4 [homework bundles](https://web.stanford.edu/class/cs246/homework/hw1-bundle.zip), up to about 300 MB with their data, and 11 Colab notebooks, all of which download without an account. [Winter 2022](https://snap.stanford.edu/class/cs246-2022/) was the first offering to keep its bundles, and every one since does.
- **Solutions**: none found, for any offering, and nothing says whether any are released.
- **Exams**: one in-person final, on 19 March, with no paper published. [Winter 2022's schedule](https://snap.stanford.edu/class/cs246-2022/homework/cs246_exam_2019.pdf) links a practice exam, the 2019 final; no later offering posts one.
- **Projects**: none. CS 345A's project went to [CS 341](CS%20341.md) in the split.
- **Repo**: the homework bundles ship Spark starter code, and the Colab notebooks run in the browser.
- **Textbook**: *Mining of Massive Datasets*, the instructors' own, which doubles as the course notes above.

## Syllabus

20 lectures over 10 weeks, per the [schedule](https://web.stanford.edu/class/cs246/), with 3 recitations in the opening weeks on Spark, linear algebra, and probability and proof techniques.

1. **Weeks 1–2, scale and similarity**: MapReduce and Spark; frequent itemsets and association rules; locality-sensitive hashing in 2 lectures, first the construction then its theory.
2. **Weeks 3–4, unsupervised structure**: clustering; dimensionality reduction; recommender systems in 2 lectures.
3. **Weeks 5–7, graphs**: PageRank; its extensions to recommendation and spam detection; community detection; graph representation learning; graph neural networks; relational deep learning on databases.
4. **Weeks 8–9, streams and decisions**: decision trees for large-scale supervised learning; mining data streams; computational advertising; submodular function maximization.
5. **Week 10**: multi-armed bandits, then a guest lecture from Anthropic.

## Prerequisites

The catalog requires [CS 107](CS%20107.md) or [CS 145](CS%20145.md). The site adds, as expectations rather than requirements:

- Java and Python, "extremely helpful since most assignments will require the use of Spark".
- Basic probability: [CS 109](CS%20109.md) or STATS 116, "sufficient but not necessary".
- Writing rigorous proofs, at the level of [CS 103](CS%20103.md).
- Basic linear algebra and algorithmic analysis — MATH 51 or [CS 161](CS%20161.md) are described as "much more than necessary".
