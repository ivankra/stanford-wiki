# CS 329R: Race and Natural Language Processing

A once-weekly, permission-only practicum co-taught by a psychologist and an NLP researcher, where mixed teams of computer and social scientists take a societal question about race to a study on real data — often police, school or hiring corpora from the instructors' community partnerships. Graded on reaction papers, weekly discussion questions and a 60% final project, not on code.

## Materials

## Syllabus

Ten Tuesday sessions ([reading list](https://web.stanford.edu/class/cs329r/)), each a domain rather than a method: the transmission of bias and the mechanics of inequality; how we police (linguistic analysis of body-camera stops, the California reporting-project database); how we work (LLM discrimination in hiring decisions, name audits, algorithmic homogeneity); how we teach (classroom-management language); how we treat (racial bias in medical report generation and clinical decisions, LLMs for health equity); how we connect (silencing of racism reports online, conflict-resolution simulation); how we advance (interventions on incarceration attitudes, belonging, political discourse, redacting racial covenants). Week 10 is final project presentations; the writeup follows a week later.
