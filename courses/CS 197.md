# CS 197: Computer Science Research

An on-ramp into research rather than a survey of it: students are placed on a team in an area, meet weekly with a PhD-student mentor, file a progress report every Saturday, and aim at something publishable — a poster or workshop paper — by December. The lectures, watched at home, are about how research works.

## Materials

Slides and assignment specifications are all public, and two unlinked directories hold more than the site shows: `lectures/` has all eleven decks and `resources/` the LaTeX bundles for every write-up. The gap is the lectures themselves, which students are meant to watch at home and which the site links only through Canvas.

- **Syllabus**: the [landing page](https://web.stanford.edu/class/cs197/) with the dated schedule, plus a [logistics page](https://web.stanford.edu/class/cs197/logistics.html).
- **Slides**: the [lectures directory](https://web.stanford.edu/class/cs197/lectures/) — [Research Contributions: Bit Flips](https://web.stanford.edu/class/cs197/lectures/cs197-02-research-contribution.pdf), Argument, Vectoring, Velocity, Evaluation, Writing, Career Paths, Giving Talks, Peer Review, and a clear-talk tips sheet — with a 5 MB Google Slides deck beside them.
- **Notes**: the [resources directory](https://web.stanford.edu/class/cs197/resources/), seven Overleaf LaTeX bundles matching the write-ups: introduction, related work, proposed solution, evaluation, progress report, draft proposal.

## Syllabus

The [schedule](https://web.stanford.edu/class/cs197/) alternates a concept lecture with the matching deliverable:

1. **Weeks 0–2, finding the claim**: what research is; research contributions as "bit flips"; arguing a project — with assignments on reading a paper and writing related work.
2. **Weeks 3–5, running the project**: vectoring (choosing direction), velocity (moving fast), evaluation — with the introduction and evaluation-plan assignments.
3. **Weeks 6–8, writing it up**: writing a paper, picking projects, research career paths, giving talks — draft paper and draft talk.
4. **Weeks 9–10**: publication and peer review, then the final talk in person and the final paper.

A progress report is due every Saturday at 8:00pm from week 0.
