# CS 272H: Methods for Reproducible Population Health and Clinical Research

A two-unit elective on making health research someone else could repeat: the professional norms first, the toolchain second. Built for graduate students on NIH training grants that require reproducibility training, who the catalog says should confirm with their program director that it counts.

## Materials

Nothing is public. No course site exists under any CS or partner-code pattern, and the course runs on Canvas.

- **Syllabus**: a syllabus file is posted, but Stanford's syllabus repository records its visibility as Stanford accounts only, and the course's only site, [Canvas course 226179](https://canvas.stanford.edu/courses/226179), requires a SUNet login. The three-module outline on the [SPoRR course list](https://med.stanford.edu/sporr/education/courses.html) is the fullest public description.
- **Assignments**: gated. The finished work is visible only because students publish it themselves, each in their own GitHub repository.

## Syllabus

Three modules, from the [SPoRR course list](https://med.stanford.edu/sporr/education/courses.html):

1. Ethical, regulatory and legal aspects of research integrity: authorship, collaboration, conflicts of interest, data-sharing agreements.
2. Design and reporting for rigour and reproducibility: threats to validity, proper interpretation of statistical measures, reporting guidelines.
3. Technical training: collaborative workflows and reproducible programming with GitHub and R.

The catalog adds academic incentives and preregistration to the first two. The course ends in a reproducible Quarto report, written in a Git repository against a shared cohort dataset.

## Prerequisites

- R: basic knowledge, enough to write and render an analysis script. The catalog asks for nothing else.
