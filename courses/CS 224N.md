# CS 224N: Natural Language Processing with Deep Learning

The department's NLP course, and one of the most openly published courses it runs. Winter 2026 spends its first three weeks on the classical spine — word vectors, backpropagation, RNNs, transformers — and the remaining seven on what has been built on top: pretraining, post-training, PEFT, RAG and agents, evaluation, two lectures on reasoning, and four invited talks. The default final project is a from-parts GPT-2 implementation applied to three downstream tasks; it replaced a BERT-based project after Spring 2024.

## Syllabus

Ten weeks of Tuesday/Thursday lectures, per the [schedule](https://web.stanford.edu/class/cs224n/#schedule).

1. **Weeks 1–3, foundations**: history of NLP; word vectors (two sets of notes); backpropagation and neural network basics; language models and RNNs; transformers. Tutorial sessions on Python, PyTorch and Hugging Face run alongside.
2. **Weeks 4–5, building LLMs**: pretraining — scaling, systems, data; post-training — RLHF, SFT, DPO; efficient adaptation through prompting and PEFT; agents, tool use and RAG.
3. **Weeks 6–7, evaluating and reasoning**: benchmarking and evaluation; reasoning across two lectures, from chain-of-thought and self-consistency to DeepSeek-R1, process supervision and test-time compute scaling.
4. **Weeks 7–9, guest lectures**: tokenization and multilinguality (Julie Kallini), interpretability (Been Kim), multimodality (Luke Zettlemoyer), Tinker and LoRA (John Schulman); plus a staff lecture on the social and broader impacts of NLP.
5. **Week 10**: open questions in NLP, then the poster session.

## Prerequisites

- Python, with NumPy and PyTorch — every assignment is in it; a week-1 review session and a PyTorch tutorial are provided.
- Multivariable calculus and linear algebra: MATH 51 or CME 100.
- Probability and statistics: [CS 109](CS%20109.md) or equivalent.
- Some machine learning: [CS 221](CS%20221.md), [CS 229](CS%20229.md), [CS 230](CS%20230.md) or [CS 124](CS%20124.md). The site says the course is possible without it, and suggests Hal Daumé's *A Course in Machine Learning* as substitute background.
