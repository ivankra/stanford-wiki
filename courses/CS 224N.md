# CS 224N: Natural Language Processing with Deep Learning

Stanford's NLP course since Spring 2002, statistical until Autumn 2015 and deep learning since Winter 2017, with nearly every offering's site still online. Winter 2026 covers the classical spine in 3 weeks and spends the other 7 on how LLMs are pretrained, post-trained, adapted, evaluated and made to reason. ## Syllabus

10 weeks of lectures, per its [schedule](https://web.stanford.edu/class/cs224n/#schedule) and decks.

1. **Weeks 1–3, foundations**: NLP's history; word2vec, GloVe and their evaluation; window-based NER, Jacobians and backpropagation; n-gram and RNN language models, vanishing gradients and seq2seq translation; attention, self-attention, position encodings and the transformer.
2. **Weeks 4–5, building LLMs**: pretraining with BPE, BERT, GPT and in-context learning, and scaling laws; post-training from instruction tuning through RLHF with PPO to DPO; prompting and chain of thought, then PEFT (LoRA and QLoRA, prefix tuning, adapters); RAG with trained retrievers, and agents with ReAct, memory and tool use.
3. **Weeks 6–7, evaluating and reasoning**: benchmarks from MMLU to Humanity's Last Exam, Chatbot Arena, LLM judges and contamination; decoding, DeepSeek-R1, GRPO and DAPO; then speculative decoding, on-policy distillation, RoPE and long context, and test-time compute.
4. **Weeks 7–9, guest lectures**: tokenization and multilinguality (Julie Kallini), interpretability (Been Kim), multimodality (Luke Zettlemoyer), Tinker and LoRA (John Schulman); plus a staff lecture on hallucination, calibration and AI's social effects.
5. **Week 10**: open questions (small reasoning models, synthetic data, RL in pretraining), then the poster session.

## History

First offered in Spring 2002, the course has been rebuilt twice: from statistical to neural NLP in 2017, and around large language models in 2024.

- **Spring 2002–Autumn 2015**: statistical NLP under Christopher Manning, taught from the Manning–Schütze and Jurafsky–Martin textbooks. The early runs cover Naive Bayes text classification, word sense disambiguation, n-gram models, HMM tagging, information extraction and PCFG parsing; from 2005 the course opens with statistical machine translation and adds maximum entropy classifiers and named entity recognition. By 2013 the 4 programming assignments build a word aligner and MT system, a CYK parser, a coreference system and a neural sequence model, and deep learning gets its first lectures, 4 by 2015. Every archived run keeps its slides and handouts, and most their assignment handouts; [Spring 2002](https://web.stanford.edu/class/archive/cs/cs224n/cs224n.1026/) also keeps solutions to all 4 homeworks.
- **Winter 2017–2018**: renamed NLP with Deep Learning after merging with CS 224D, and taught by Manning and Richard Socher in TensorFlow: word2vec, dependency parsing, RNNs, neural MT with attention, and an in-class midterm. A default final project starts in 2017, on SQuAD question answering by 2018, when a Transformer lecture arrives. [2017's 19 lectures](https://www.youtube.com/playlist?list=PL3FW7Lu3i5Jsnh1rnUwq_TcylNr7EkRe6) are on YouTube; 2018 keeps its 3 assignments with starter code, and [its midterm](https://web.stanford.edu/class/archive/cs/cs224n/cs224n.1184/midterm/cs224n-midterm-2018.pdf) and 3 practice midterms with solutions.
- **Winter 2019–2022**: PyTorch from 2019, adding subword models, pretraining and generation. Guest lectures follow the field: Ashish Vaswani on Transformers (2019), Jacob Devlin on BERT (2020), Colin Raffel on T5 (2021), Jared Kaplan on scaling laws (2022), with Margaret Mitchell on bias and fairness in 2019. [2019's lectures](https://www.youtube.com/playlist?list=PLoROMvodv4rOhcuXMZkNm7j3fVwBBY42z) are public. The default project stays SQuAD, with a Robust QA track in 2021 and 2022.
- **Winter 2023–2026**: prompting and RLHF arrive in 2023, with a multitask BERT default project. Winter 2024 rebuilds the course around LLMs (pretraining, post-training, efficient adaptation, evaluation), the spine every later run keeps; Spring 2024's lectures are the playlist the current site recommends. Guests track the frontier: Nicholas Carlini on security and privacy (2024), Noam Brown on reasoning (2025), John Schulman on Tinker and LoRA (2026). Since 2025 the default project has students implement parts of GPT-2 and apply it to 3 downstream tasks. 2026 gives reasoning 2 lectures, adding GRPO and DAPO, which Winter 2025's post-training lecture lacked, and drops the lectures on dependency parsing and LSTMs that Winter 2025 still gave, leaving parsing to the second assignment. Its last assignment has students red-team an LLM against its system prompt, which no lecture covers.

## Prerequisites

- Python with NumPy and PyTorch, used by every assignment, with a Python review and a PyTorch tutorial.
- Multivariable calculus and linear algebra: MATH 51 or CME 100.
- Probability and statistics: [CS 109](CS%20109.md) or equivalent.
- Some machine learning: [CS 221](CS%20221.md), [CS 229](CS%20229.md), [CS 230](CS%20230.md) or [CS 124](CS%20124.md). The site calls it optional, suggesting Hal Daumé's *A Course in Machine Learning*.
