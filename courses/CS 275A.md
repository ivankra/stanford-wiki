# CS 275A: Symbolic Musical Information

The music-as-data course run out of CCARH, the Center for Computer Assisted Research in the Humanities. Where a signal-processing course treats music as audio, this one treats a score as a structured document, and the practical question is what survives a translation between formats: a Humdrum spine, an MEI element tree and a MIDI event stream encode different things, and getting from one to another loses some of them. Optical music recognition supplies the other direction, from printed page to symbols. The catalog schedules no meeting time and lists no instructor.

The CCARH hosts still resolve but nothing answers on them, so the wiki, the slide server and the textbook are reachable only through Internet Archive snapshots, whose last captures are from January 2025. ## Syllabus

From the Winter 2024 syllabus. The course is organized by format, one or two weeks each.

1. **Representation** (week 1): what musical information is, and a "Rosetta stone" comparing the digital representations against each other.
2. **Input** (week 2): getting music in, by computer keyboard and by MIDI keyboard, in MuseScore.
3. **Optical music recognition and MIDI** (weeks 3–4): SharpEye from printed page to symbols; then the MIDI protocol byte by byte, General MIDI and Standard MIDI Files.
4. **SCORE** (weeks 5–6): applied MIDI, then the SCORE format, the graphical extremes of common Western notation, and the non-staff notations that fall outside it.
5. **XML** (weeks 7–8): score semantics as textures and tracks, data archives, MusicXML for interchange and MEI for source markup, Base-40 arithmetic for transposition, MuseData, and the encoding of musical variants.
6. **Humdrum** (week 9): the file format, then what it is used for analytically.
7. **Wrap-up** (week 10), and the final essay.

The catalog's one-paragraph version:

> Properties of symbolic data for music applications including advanced notation systems, data durability, mark-up languages, optical music recognition, and data-translation tasks. Hands-on work involves these digital score formats: Guido Music Notation, Humdrum, MuseData, MEI, MusicXML, SCORE, and MIDI internal code.

## Prerequisites

None stated. The hands-on work is format translation, so comfort with structured text and scripting is implied, as is enough music notation to know what the formats are encoding.
