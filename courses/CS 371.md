# CS 371: Computational Biology in Four Dimensions

The seminar counterpart to [CS 279](CS%20279.md): same territory — structure and dynamics of biomolecules — but taught entirely by reading, presenting and critiquing recent papers, with students named as presenter and critiquer for each session. The fourth dimension is time: molecular dynamics and simulation analysis sit alongside static structure prediction, and the reading reaches sideways into crowdsourced folding games and cryo-EM.

## Materials

Nothing from this offering is public. What a reader gets instead is the whole Winter 2018 site, still at the course's canonical URL 5 years later: a session-by-session schedule, 26 decks and the coursework rules. Its reading, which is most of what the seminar does, sits behind Stanford's SSO.

- **Syllabus**: the Winter 2018 [schedule](https://web.stanford.edu/class/cs371/schedule.html) gives each session its topic, its main and additional papers and the students assigned to present and critique; the [home page](https://web.stanford.edu/class/cs371/) adds the coursework rules and the 40/40/20 split between presentation, critiques and participation. Nothing of the kind was found for this offering.
- **Slides**: 26 decks in the open [2018_slides/](https://web.stanford.edu/class/cs371/2018_slides/) listing, the instructor's own for the sessions he led and an intro deck for each student-led topic, from cryo-EM and virtual screening to protein design and multiplayer folding games.
- **Notes**: the assigned papers are under `2018_papers/`, which redirects to Stanford's SSO.
- **Videos**: no recording is mentioned for any offering.
- **Assignments**: Winter 2018's briefs are specified in full on its home page: present one topic, write critiques of 3 papers at 2 to 3 pages each, "not just summarize the paper but demonstrate critical thinking". The 2 model critiques and the paper they discuss are gated with the rest of the reading.
- **Solutions**, **Exams**, **Projects**, **Repo**: none. "This course has no problem sets, exams, or projects."

An older [Winter 2017 site](https://web.stanford.edu/class/cs371/cs371_2017/index.html) survives under the same host, and links a Winter 2016 one that no longer resolves.

## Syllabus

[The Winter 2018 schedule](https://web.stanford.edu/class/cs371/schedule.html) shows the shape: an introduction, then alternating instructor-led and student-led sessions. Early sessions cover simulation of drug targets and change-point detection in biomolecular simulations; substrate translocation in transporters; and "citizen computing", reading the Nature and PNAS papers on predicting protein structures and RNA designs through multiplayer games. The catalog lists the full topic range: structure prediction for proteins, DNA, RNA and macromolecular complexes; drug screening; protein-protein interactions; protein design; superresolution imaging, single-particle electron microscopy and x-ray crystallography; and machine learning in structural biology.

## Prerequisites

- Programming: [CS 106A](CS%20106A.md) or equivalent.
- An introductory course in biology or biochemistry.
- Recommended: some experience in mathematical modeling. The site adds that prerequisites "are not strictly enforced. What really matters is that students feel comfortable discussing computational biology research papers".
