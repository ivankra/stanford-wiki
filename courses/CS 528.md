# CS 528: Machine Learning Systems Seminar

The credit-bearing wrapper around the Stanford MLSys Seminar: an hour a week of outside speakers on how machine learning changes the programming stack — ML platforms, debugging, parallel computing, hardware. One unit, satisfactory/no credit, repeatable. The catalog's Autumn 2024 entry has no instructor, no time and no room.

## Materials

Nothing was published for this offering: no schedule, no speaker list, no talks that can be tied to it. What is public belongs to earlier ones — the seminar's standing YouTube channel, where every series it has run is free to watch, and a course page from Spring 2022 that is still the only statement of what taking CS 528 involves.

- **Syllabus**: none for Autumn 2024. The [Spring 2022 course page](https://mlsys.stanford.edu/cs528) survives with the meeting time, the 6 instructors, the CR/NC rule and the weekly requirements, and the [seminar's front page](https://mlsys.stanford.edu/) carries the Autumn 2023 series schedule, 6 Monday talks with abstracts and speaker bios.
- **Slides**: speakers' decks are not posted, for any offering.
- **Notes**: none; nothing is written up beyond the abstracts.
- **Videos**: the seminar's [YouTube channel](https://www.youtube.com/channel/UCzz6ructab1U44QPI3HpZEQ) is a standing public archive, and the front page links some 47 talks into it. None of them is Autumn 2024's: the archive jumps from the Autumn 2023 series — Martin Maas on a taxonomy of ML for systems, Tim Dettmers on k-bit quantization, Deepak Narayanan on training LLMs at scale, Travis Addair on serving hundreds of fine-tuned LLMs on one GPU, Tianqi Chen on ML compilation to consumer devices, Dan Fu on Monarch Mixer — to a 2025 "Hidden Layer" series announced with "WE'RE BACK!".
- **Assignments**: none published for this offering. Spring 2022's page states the whole of the work: watch the seminar live, stay for the half-hour private discussion with the speaker, "ask a minimum of 2 questions", and write "a short (2-3 sentence) reflection every week", missing at most two and watching the recording instead.
- **Solutions**, **Exams**, **Projects**, **Repo**: none. It is a talk series with no coursework beyond attendance. The site's own [source repository](https://github.com/stanford-sysml-seminar/stanford-sysml-seminar.github.io) is public, but it holds the website, not course material.

## Syllabus

No schedule is published for this offering. The catalog describes the scope: "this weekly talk series" invites "speakers working at the frontier of machine learning systems", focusing on "how machine learning changes the modern programming stack", with topics including programming models for ML, ML platforms and other infrastructure, debugging, parallel computing, and hardware for ML.
