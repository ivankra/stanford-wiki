# CS 528: Machine Learning Systems Seminar

The credit-bearing wrapper around the Stanford MLSys Seminar: an hour a week of outside speakers on how machine learning changes the programming stack — ML platforms, debugging, parallel computing, hardware. One unit, satisfactory/no credit, repeatable. The catalog's Autumn 2024 entry has no instructor, no time and no room.

## Materials

The talks are public, which is the point of a seminar; the Autumn 2024 term's own schedule is not.

- **Syllabus**: the seminar site keeps a [Spring 2022 course page](https://mlsys.stanford.edu/cs528) with the format, the requirements and the CR/NC rule. No Autumn 2024 schedule exists — that catalog section is TBA with no instructor, and the seminar site runs from its Autumn 2023 series straight to a 2025 one.
- **Videos**: all six talks of the [Autumn 2023 series](https://mlsys.stanford.edu/), which the site says was run jointly with CS 528 and [CS 229S](CS%20229S.md), are on YouTube: Martin Maas on a taxonomy of ML for systems, Tim Dettmers on k-bit quantization, Deepak Narayanan on training LLMs at scale, Travis Addair on serving hundreds of fine-tuned LLMs on one GPU, Tianqi Chen on ML compilation to consumer devices, and Dan Fu on Monarch Mixer.
- **Slides**: speakers' decks aren't posted.
- **Assignments**: the whole of the work is on the [course page](https://mlsys.stanford.edu/cs528) — watch the seminar live, stay for the half-hour private discussion with the speaker, "ask a minimum of 2 questions", and write "a short (2-3 sentence) reflection every week". Miss at most two, and watch the YouTube recording instead.
- **Exams**, **Projects**: none — it is a talk series.

## Syllabus

No schedule is published for this offering. The catalog describes the scope: "this weekly talk series" invites "speakers working at the frontier of machine learning systems", focusing on "how machine learning changes the modern programming stack", with topics including programming models for ML, ML platforms and other infrastructure, debugging, parallel computing, and hardware for ML.
