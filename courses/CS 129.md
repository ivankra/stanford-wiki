# CS 129: Applied Machine Learning

Formerly CS 229A, and the inverse of [CS 229](CS%20229.md): the algorithms are learned from Ng's Coursera videos at home, and the one weekly Stanford session is spent deriving what the videos asserted and working practice problems. The quarter ends in an open-ended project and a poster session. Enrollment is limited.

## Materials

Everything Stanford hosts is public, down to the worked solutions, and the three Coursera courses the syllabus assigns are ones anyone can audit free. What a reader can't reach is the course's own Coursera session: the quizzes and autograded programming assignments that carry a quarter of the grade are private to enrolled students.

- **Syllabus**: the [schedule](https://web.stanford.edu/class/cs129/syllabus) pairs each of the 10 Thursday sessions with the Coursera weeks due the following Tuesday and marks where the PSETs, the proposal, the milestone and the poster session fall. Grading is on the front page: problem sets 20%, Coursera work 25%, midterm 25%, project 30%.
- **Slides**: none. The lecture content is the Coursera videos, about 10 ten-minute videos a week; the Thursday session works problems instead.
- **Notes**: 2 handouts, on [advice for ML systems](https://web.stanford.edu/class/cs129/advice.pdf) and the [bias-variance trade-off](https://web.stanford.edu/class/cs129/discussion-sessions/bias_variance_notes.pdf).
- **Videos**: the course adds students to a private Coursera session in week 1. The three courses it runs on are the public [Machine Learning Specialization](https://www.coursera.org/specializations/machine-learning-introduction), free to audit without a certificate, so the lectures themselves are reachable; the deadlines, autograder and cohort are not.
- **Assignments**: 7 discussion-session problem sets and 3 PSET handouts, e.g. [PSET 1](https://web.stanford.edu/class/cs129/pset_2026/HW1_2026.pdf), all public PDFs, plus the [project brief](https://web.stanford.edu/class/cs129/projects.html) with its proposal, milestone and poster guidelines. The Coursera quizzes and programming assignments are in the private session.
- **Solutions**: a worked [solutions PDF](https://web.stanford.edu/class/cs129/discussion-sessions/cs129_ds_1_sols.pdf) beside every Winter 2026 discussion set. PSET solutions are not posted this year, but the [Spring 2022 archive](https://web.stanford.edu/class/archive/cs/cs129/cs129.1226/HW3-sols.pdf) carries all three of its own, and Winter 2025's HW1 and HW2 solutions are still on the live site.
- **Exams**: no Winter 2026 paper. The ["CS129 Winter 2023 Sample Midterm"](https://web.stanford.edu/class/cs129/Practice-Midterm.pdf) is still served, though the schedule's link to it is commented out.
- **Projects**: the poster session isn't published; no report or poster from any offering is linked.
- **Repo**: none. All code work happens in the Coursera session's notebooks.
- **Older offerings**: the class archive keeps [Spring 2022](https://web.stanford.edu/class/archive/cs/cs129/cs129.1226/), [Winter 2023](https://web.stanford.edu/class/archive/cs/cs129/cs129.1234/) and [Winter 2024](https://web.stanford.edu/class/archive/cs/cs129/cs129.1244/) whole, and the Internet Archive has [Autumn 2020](https://web.archive.org/web/20201113181524/https://web.stanford.edu/class/cs129/syllabus)'s schedule, from when the course still followed Ng's original eleven-week Coursera class. EdStem and Gradescope are gated throughout.

## Syllabus

Ten sessions tracking two Coursera courses ([syllabus](https://web.stanford.edu/class/cs129/syllabus)).

1. Jan 8 — the AI world, logistics, the syllabus. Coursera: *Supervised Machine Learning: Regression and Classification*, weeks 1-2.
2. Jan 15 — linear regression: derivations and practice problems. Coursera: week 3.
3. Jan 22 — logistic regression, text processing, derivations. Coursera: *Advanced Learning Algorithms: Neural Networks*, week 1. PSET 1 out.
4. Jan 29 — neural networks, vectorized gradients, softmax.
5. The remaining sessions continue through decision trees, unsupervised learning and k-means, reinforcement learning and Q-learning, anomaly detection and recommender systems, in the same video-then-derivation pattern, to the midterm and the final poster session.

## Prerequisites

- Probability: [CS 109](CS%20109.md) or STATS 116.
- Linear algebra: MATH 51.
