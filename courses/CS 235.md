# CS 235: Computational Methods for Biomedical Image Analysis and Interpretation

Imaging informatics for people who will build the software: how to process clinical images, pull quantitative and semantic features out of them, index and retrieve them, and connect them to the genomic, phenotypic and clinical record of the same patient. Taught in the School of Medicine, with a project.

## Materials

Everything since 2020 is on Canvas behind a SUNet login, and the recordings are a product Stanford Online sells. What a reader can open is one offering, Spring 2019, whose Canvas course was left public: the whole of it, coursework included. That is 7 years and 2 changes of instructor ago, and the level rests on it.

- **Syllabus**: on Canvas since Spring 2020. [bmi260.stanford.edu](http://bmi260.stanford.edu/) lands on the login gateway, and the syllabus repository marks every upload from Spring 2020 on Stanford-only, with none at all for Spring 2024. [Spring 2019's](https://canvas.stanford.edu/courses/98045/assignments/syllabus) is open and complete: 20 dated sessions, the reading list, the 4 deliverables with their dates and the split of problem sets 45%, project 30%, midterm 15%, participation 10%.
- **Slides**: 14 Spring 2019 decks, numbered 1 to 9 and then 16 and 17, so the weeks between deep learning and the clinical applications are missing; 12 of the schedule's reading PDFs sit beside them. Nothing from any later offering.
- **Notes**: none; the course teaches from decks and papers.
- **Videos**: recorded for SCPD, not released. Stanford Online sells on-demand access under the BMDS 260 code for $4,725.
- **Assignments**: all 3 Spring 2019 problem sets, Jupyter notebooks carrying the questions and the skeleton code — lung field segmentation, then digital image processing on mammograms, then machine learning on the same mammograms — with the TIFFs and masks they read, a 975 MB chest-CT archive and an Anaconda setup guide. The final project's guidelines and grading rubric are there too.
- **Solutions**: no problem-set solution was posted, in that offering or any other.
- **Exams**: 4 past in-class midterms with full solutions, from 2011, 2012, 2017 and 2018, kept in that same course, plus its own 2019 answer key without the paper. Whether the current offering sets an exam is unsaid.
- **Projects**: one sample Spring 2019 final report, a knee-MRI cartilage segmentation paper by 4 students. No showcase anywhere else.
- **Repo**: `BIOMEDIN260-master.zip` in the same course, the Python and DICOM tutorial notebook the problem sets assume.

All of that is reached through the Canvas [file listing](https://canvas.stanford.edu/api/v1/courses/98045/files), not the course's Files tab, which shows an anonymous visitor nothing.

## Syllabus

No syllabus for this offering is public; the outline below is the catalog description and the topic list Stanford Online publishes for [BMDS260](https://online.stanford.edu/courses/bmds260-computational-methods-biomedical-image-analysis-and-interpretation).

1. Biological and medical imaging modalities and how they are used in research and medicine
2. Image processing, enhancement and visualization
3. Major image databases, and the access and utility of public image data sources
4. Quantitative extraction of image features
5. Structured recording of image information: semantic features and ontologies; indexing, search and content-based image retrieval
6. Computer-assisted detection, diagnosis and decision support; computer reasoning with images
7. Case studies: linking image data to genomic, phenotypic and clinical data, and representations of image phenotypes

## Prerequisites

- Programming at the level of [CS 106A](CS%20106A.md), Stanford's introductory programming course. Python or Matlab is highly recommended.
- Familiarity with statistics.
- Basic biology.
- Enrolling for 3 units requires instructor consent.

## Related

- [CS 232](CS%20232.md): classical image processing (filtering, segmentation, keypoints), the methods under this course's clinical features.
- [CS 279](CS%20279.md): images of molecules and cells rather than of patients.
- [CS 286](CS%20286.md): vision and vision-language foundation models applied to biomedicine; this course covers the whole imaging-informatics pipeline, including the non-deep-learning parts.
