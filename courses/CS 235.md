# CS 235: Computational Methods for Biomedical Image Analysis and Interpretation

Imaging informatics for people who will build the software: how to process clinical images, pull quantitative and semantic features out of them, index and retrieve them, and connect them to the genomic, phenotypic and clinical record of the same patient. Taught in the School of Medicine, with a project.

## Materials

Nothing from the current offering is public: the course runs on Canvas.

- **Syllabus**: closed. [bmi260.stanford.edu](http://bmi260.stanford.edu/), the URL the course's own group publishes, redirects to the Stanford Canvas login.
- **Videos**: closed. Lectures are recorded for SCPD, and Stanford Online sells on-demand access to the same course as BMDS260 for $4,725.
- Older, and public: the [Spring 2019 Canvas page](https://canvas.stanford.edu/courses/98045) reads without a login and its lecture PDFs download without one — a full 20-lecture schedule, slide decks for about half of it (visualization, filtering, segmentation, geometric and texture features, registration, radiomics, deep learning for images, computer reasoning), a reading list and the grading breakdown. Seven years old, under different instructors, so it is not rated here.

## Syllabus

No syllabus for this offering is public; the outline below is the catalog description and the topic list Stanford Online publishes for [BMDS260](https://online.stanford.edu/courses/bmds260-computational-methods-biomedical-image-analysis-and-interpretation).

1. Biological and medical imaging modalities and how they are used in research and medicine
2. Image processing, enhancement and visualization
3. Major image databases, and the access and utility of public image data sources
4. Quantitative extraction of image features
5. Structured recording of image information: semantic features and ontologies; indexing, search and content-based image retrieval
6. Computer-assisted detection, diagnosis and decision support; computer reasoning with images
7. Case studies: linking image data to genomic, phenotypic and clinical data, and representations of image phenotypes

## Prerequisites

- Programming at the level of [CS 106A](CS%20106A.md), Stanford's introductory programming course. Python or Matlab is highly recommended.
- Familiarity with statistics.
- Basic biology.
- Enrolling for 3 units requires instructor consent.
