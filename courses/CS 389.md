# CS 389: Internet Censorship

A first-run seminar taught entirely from the censorship measurement literature, with 6 of the field's own researchers — Wustrow, Pan, Wails and Jansen, Fifield, Levin, Wang — leading sessions on their work. Reading summaries are due before each class, every student presents once, and 55% of the grade is an original research project written up as a conference-style paper.

## Materials

The whole reading list is public and every paper links to an open copy, so a reader gets the same literature an enrolled student does. So are the schedule, the grading split and every project deadline. Nothing is behind a login; what is absent was never posted.

- **Syllabus**: the [schedule](https://cs389.stanford.edu/#curriculum) gives 11 weeks of sessions with dates, guest leaders, required and optional readings and a focus note for each; the [grading split](https://cs389.stanford.edu/#logistics) and attendance policy sit on the same page.
- **Slides**: none posted. Each session is led by a student or by one of the 6 visiting researchers presenting their own work.
- **Notes**: every paper is linked to a public copy on CensorBib, gfw.report or USENIX, with 2 open networking textbooks and Keshav's "How to Read a Paper" as background.
- **Videos**: no recording is mentioned, and nothing turned up under the code or the instructor's name.
- **Assignments**: the [project section](https://cs389.stanford.edu/#project) sets out the proposal (due 10/10), the mid-quarter report (11/14), the presentation and a 6 to 10-page final paper (12/5), plus the roughly 400-word summary and discussion question due 9:00 AM before each class. Submissions go through Gradescope and announcements through Ed.
- **Solutions**, **Exams**: none; the grade is 25% summaries, 20% presentation, 55% project.
- **Projects**: the brief is public; no student paper from the single offering is.
- **Repo**: none found. The site does carry an ethics policy for censorship research and a consent form for measurement work.

## Syllabus

11 weeks ([schedule](https://cs389.stanford.edu/#curriculum)): an introduction on reading papers and scoping censorship; mechanisms of website censorship (Great Firewall DNS injection, TLS SNI and ESNI/ECH, regional "wall behind a wall" blocking); active probing of proxies; measurement platforms and an overview of circumvention strategies; refraction networking and TLS fingerprinting; circumvention user behavior and the physical repression around censorship; ethics in censorship research; traffic analysis of and defenses for fully encrypted proxies; circumvention design and papers written by censors; proxyless circumvention; effects of censorship on the encryption ecosystem; weaponizing the censorship machine, and throttling. Weeks 10-11 are recess and final presentations.

## Prerequisites

- Networking at the level of Ethernet, IP, TCP and routing: [CS 144](CS%20144.md), [CS 155](CS%20155.md) or EE 284. Kurose & Ross or Peterson & Davie are offered as a refresher.
