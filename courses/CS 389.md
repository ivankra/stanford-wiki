# CS 389: Internet Censorship

A first-run seminar taught entirely from the censorship measurement literature, with six of the field's own researchers — Wustrow, Pan, Wails and Jansen, Fifield, Levin, Wang — leading sessions on their work. Reading summaries are due before each class, every student presents once, and 55% of the grade is an original research project written up as a conference-style paper.

## Materials

The whole reading list is public, papers linked to open copies (CensorBib, gfw.report, USENIX).

- **Syllabus**: the [schedule](https://cs389.stanford.edu/#curriculum) gives eleven weeks of sessions with dates, guest leaders, required and optional readings, and a focus note for each; the [grading split](https://cs389.stanford.edu/#logistics) and attendance policy are on the same page.
- **Assignments**: the [project section](https://cs389.stanford.edu/#project) sets out proposal (due 10/10), mid-quarter report (11/14), presentation and a 6–10 page final paper (12/5), and the ~400-word summary plus discussion question due 9:00 AM before each class. Submissions go through Gradescope; announcements through Ed.
- **Exams**: none.
- **Slides**: student-led presentations; none are posted.
- The site also carries an ethics policy for censorship research and a consent form for measurement work.

## Syllabus

Eleven weeks ([schedule](https://cs389.stanford.edu/#curriculum)): an introduction on reading papers and scoping censorship; mechanisms of website censorship (Great Firewall DNS injection, TLS SNI and ESNI/ECH, regional "wall behind a wall" blocking); active probing of proxies; measurement platforms and an overview of circumvention strategies; refraction networking and TLS fingerprinting; circumvention user behaviour and the physical repression around censorship; ethics in censorship research; traffic analysis of and defences for fully encrypted proxies; circumvention design and papers written by censors; proxyless circumvention; effects of censorship on the encryption ecosystem; weaponizing the censorship machine, and throttling. Weeks 10–11 are recess and final presentations.

## Prerequisites

- Networking at the level of Ethernet, IP, TCP and routing: CS 144, CS 155 or EE 284. Kurose & Ross or Peterson & Davie are offered as a refresher.
