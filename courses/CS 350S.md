# CS 350S: Privacy-Preserving Systems

A paper-reading seminar on systems that use cryptography and secure hardware to give strong privacy guarantees while keeping overheads low. Each class has a required paper, and students build a privacy-preserving system as a research project.

## Materials

Nothing on the course site asks for a login, and for a seminar whose work is reading papers and building a system, the site is the course: 58 linked papers, the grading rules and the full project spec. Decks appear as the quarter runs. Only Ed, Gradescope and the in-class quizzes stay inside the class, and the site says nothing about recording.

- **Syllabus**: the [schedule](https://cs350s.stanford.edu/schedule.html), 19 dated sessions each with a required paper, an optional presenter paper and further reading, 58 links in all; the [overview](https://cs350s.stanford.edu/overview.html) gives the grading split (project 55%, paper presentation 20%, reading quizzes 15%, participation 10%), the 2-quiz drop and an LLM policy that asks for an "AI usage" paragraph.
- **Slides**: the [course introduction](https://cs350s.stanford.edu/lectures/intro.pdf) and the [cryptography-basics deck](https://cs350s.stanford.edu/lectures/crypto.pdf), with the rest to follow. Autumn 2025 linked a deck for 17 of its 19 sessions; 6 of them are still readable in an [Internet Archive snapshot](https://web.archive.org/web/20260724045455/https://cs350s.stanford.edu/schedule.html) of that schedule, which is otherwise the only record of the offering.
- **Notes**: none; the papers themselves are the reading.
- **Videos**: nothing found, and no source says whether lectures or the guest talks by Tinfoil, ISRG and Cloudflare speakers are recorded.
- **Assignments**: the [assignments page](https://cs350s.stanford.edu/assign.html) is the whole research project — identify a privacy problem, survey the literature, design a system, state its security properties, implement and evaluate it — with a 1-2 paragraph proposal, a 1-2 page progress report and a 5-6 page USENIX-format final report that must link a public repository, plus the paper-presentation brief.
- **Solutions**: this offering publishes none. Autumn 2025's schedule carried staff answers for 15 of its papers' reading questions, of which only the [Riposte file](https://web.archive.org/web/20260724045505if_/https://cs350s.stanford.edu/answers/riposte.txt) was archived.
- **Exams**: a short reading quiz opens most classes and is 15% of the grade; none is published.
- **Projects**: student systems are presented in the last week and their reports link public repositories, but the course posts no index of them.
- **Repo**: none.
- **Textbook**: the [resources page](https://cs350s.stanford.edu/resources.html) recommends Boneh and Shoup's *A Graduate Course in Applied Cryptography*, free online, and Katz and Lindell's *Introduction to Modern Cryptography*, then points at the IEEE S&P, USENIX Security, SOSP and OSDI proceedings.

## Syllabus

One paper-based class per topic, several with guest speakers from industry:

1. Cryptography basics
2. Secure hardware: enclaves (Opaque, SGX/TDX/SEV-SNP), private AI, encrypted backups (SafetyPin, SVR3)
3. Transparency logs: Certificate Transparency, CONIKS
4. Oblivious RAM: Path ORAM, Obladi, Compass
5. Private information retrieval: CGKS'95, Splinter, Tiptoe, SimplePIR
6. Anonymous messaging: Tor, Myco, SecureDrop
7. Multi-party computation (Pragmatic MPC book, MAGE) and private aggregate statistics (Prio)
8. Differential privacy (Dwork-Roth, PINQ)
9. Private training and private inference (Nudge, Piranha, Delphi)
10. Student project presentations
