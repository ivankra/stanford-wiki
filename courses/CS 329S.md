# CS 329S: Machine Learning Systems Design

About everything around the model: where training data comes from, how a model reaches production, what breaks when the input distribution drifts, and how the system is kept alive afterwards. Two-thirds of the grade is building an actual ML application, shown at a public demo day. Its lecture notes had an unusual afterlife: they became Chip Huyen's *Designing Machine Learning Systems*, which is how most people now encounter the material.

## Materials

The teaching is public, in Google Docs and Slides the schedule links session by session: a lecture note for 8 sessions and a deck for 9, plus 6 guest decks, and the notes are the draft of the book they became. ## Syllabus

Nineteen dated sessions from January 3 ([schedule](https://stanford-cs329s.github.io/syllabus.html)), roughly three lectures to one tutorial or guest slot.

1. **Weeks 1–3, the design problem and the data**: understanding machine learning production; ML and data systems fundamentals; training data; feature engineering.
2. **Weeks 3–5, models and evaluation**: model selection, development and training; offline evaluation; then model evaluation as a tutorial, with Goku Mohandas and RecList.
3. **Weeks 5–7, getting it out**: deployment, followed by a tutorial day on evaluating MLOps tools and deploying with Ray Serve.
4. **Weeks 7–9, keeping it alive**: diagnosing ML system failures, data distribution shifts and monitoring; continual learning, with Shreya Shankar on shifts in streaming data; model deployment at Stitch Fix and experiment tracking with Weights & Biases; a monitoring tutorial day on WhyLogs and Evidently; time-series and graph deployment, and ML beyond accuracy.
5. **Weeks 9–10, the business**: ML infrastructure and platforms; a final-project workshop; integrating ML into business, with venture-capital guests on pitching and go-to-market; then demo day.
