# CS 329S: Machine Learning Systems Design

About everything around the model: where training data comes from, how a model reaches production, what breaks when the input distribution drifts, and how the system is kept alive afterwards. Two-thirds of the grade is building an actual ML application, shown at a public demo day. Its lecture notes had an unusual afterlife — they became Chip Huyen's *Designing Machine Learning Systems*, which is how most people now encounter the material.

## Materials

The course's content largely left the site: the notes became a book, and what remains public is the structure plus the demo day.

- **Syllabus**: the [schedule](https://stanford-cs329s.github.io/syllabus.html) is public and dated session by session — understanding ML production, ML and data systems fundamentals, and so on — each with its case study and breakout exercise ("Lessons learned from 150 ML models at Booking.com", "Predict Value of Homes On Airbnb", "Designing Twitter's Trending Hashtags"). The home page carries logistics and the grading split (project 65%, assignments 30%, participation 5%).
- **Notes**: closed. The site states they "have been expanded into the book *Designing Machine Learning Systems* (Chip Huyen, O'Reilly 2022)" — a paid title.
- **Videos**: partial. The [demo day](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=AZNTqytOhXk) is on YouTube; the lectures ran over Zoom and are not posted.
- **Assignments**: closed — two to three short ones through Canvas.
- **Slides**: partial. The schedule names a deck for every session but links almost none; the site's [`slides/`](https://stanford-cs329s.github.io/slides/) directory does serve six guest decks — Shreya Shankar on data distribution shifts and streams, Uber's Ludwig, Stefan Krawczyk on model deployment at Stitch Fix, Sara Hooker's, and a 31 MB monitoring deck.
- **Projects**: open. Winter 2021's teams published [nine final reports](https://stanford-cs329s.github.io/reports/) as a site of their own — a COVID chatbot, a public-health fact-checking tool, a context-graph generator, Stylify, recipe recommendations, two Virufy cough-detection systems and a dashcam data-valuation study — with three also as PDFs. The [demo day](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=AZNTqytOhXk) recording shows the same work.

## Syllabus

No dated schedule survives. The catalog and book table of contents give the shape: training data and sampling, feature engineering, model development and evaluation, deployment, monitoring and distribution shift, continual learning, and the infrastructure and business considerations around all of it.
