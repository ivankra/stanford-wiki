# CS 207: Antidiscrimination Law and Algorithmic Bias

Listed in the Autumn 2022 catalog with no instructor, meeting time or room, and no description beyond its title — the thinnest entry in the term. The subject the title names is the legal counterpart to [CS 256](CS%20256.md)'s technical treatment: what existing antidiscrimination doctrine does and does not reach when the decision is made by a model.

## Syllabus

No public syllabus exists; the catalog's description is the only account of the content. The course "surveys the legal principles for assessing bias of algorithms, examines emerging techniques for how to design and assess bias of algorithms, and assesses how antidiscrimination law and the design of algorithms may need to evolve to account for the potential emergence of machine bias", opening from four displacements of human judgement: judges sentencing on "risk scores", regulators acting on predicted violations, advertisers targeting on demographics, and employers evaluating applicants with machine-learned models.

Admission is by consent of instructor, capped at 20 students, through the Law School's consent application form. Assessment is class participation, response papers and a final project.
