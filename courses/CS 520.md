# CS 520: Knowledge Graphs

A one-unit seminar built as a sequence of questions — what a knowledge graph *is*, which data models exist, how to design a schema, how to build one from structured and unstructured sources, how to infer over it, how users query it, how it evolves. Each week pairs a synthesis lecture distilling the previous edition's material with an invited speaker from academia or industry. Explicitly open to the public, and the recordings bear that out.

## Materials

One of the most openly published courses in the catalog: slides, written notes and recordings are all public, and the seminar states it is open to anyone.

- **Syllabus**: the [site](https://web.stanford.edu/class/cs520/) lays out each week's question, both sessions, and what credit requires.
- **Slides**: a [synthesis deck](https://web.stanford.edu/class/cs520/What_Is_A_Knowledge_Graph.pdf) per week.
- **Notes**: written [HTML notes](https://web.stanford.edu/class/cs520/2020/notes/What_is_a_Knowledge_Graph.html) accompanying each synthesis lecture.
- **Videos**: the invited lectures are recorded and posted, with two [YouTube playlists](https://www.youtube.com/playlist?list=PLDhh0lALedc7LC_5wpi5gDnPRnu1GSyRG) linked.
- **Assignments**: closed — ten quizzes and eight written session summaries, all on Canvas.

## Syllabus

[Eleven questions](https://web.stanford.edu/class/cs520/), one a week.

1. What is a Knowledge Graph? What are some Knowledge Graph data models?
2. How to design the schema of one; how to create one from data, and from unstructured input.
3. What inference algorithms apply; how users interact with one; how to evolve one.
4. High-value use cases, implementation tools, and open research issues.
