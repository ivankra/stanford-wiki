# CS 349D: AI Inference Infrastructure

A paper seminar on how LLMs and compound AI systems are served in production: two required papers a lecture, discussion led by two students, guest speakers from industry, and one build that runs all quarter — a minimal serving engine, in groups of 2–3.

## Materials

The reading list is the course and it is entirely public: 19 sessions, each with its required and optional papers linked at arXiv or USENIX, plus the grading split. The build that runs alongside it is not: the 4 milestones are described in a paragraph each and the starter notebook, benchmark scripts and shared environment stay inside the class, as do the student discussions.

- **Syllabus**: the [schedule](https://cs349d.stanford.edu/#schedule) lists all 19 sessions with topic, required and optional readings and the milestone deadlines, and the [grading split](https://cs349d.stanford.edu/#lectures) is coursework 50%, leading a lecture 25%, participation 25%. - **Solutions**, **Exams**: none; the grade is coursework, discussion leadership and participation.

## Syllabus

[Schedule](https://cs349d.stanford.edu/#schedule), 19 sessions, marked tentative.

1. Introduction; parallelism (FlexFlow, scaling transformer inference)
2. Serving engines (PagedAttention, SGLang); batching (Orca, Clipper)
3. Disaggregation (DistServe, Dynamo); speculative decoding
4. Context management and caching; efficient attention (FlashAttention, FlashInfer)
5. Hybrid LLMs; mixture of experts; load balancing and routing
6. Agentic workflows (Autellix, Parrot); memory (Cartridges, mem0)
7. Inference for reinforcement learning; multi-tenancy
8. Two project meetings, one industry guest lecture, and final presentations
