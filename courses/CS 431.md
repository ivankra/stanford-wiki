# CS 431: High-level Vision: From Neurons to Deep Neural Networks

Co-taught by a computational neuroscientist and a psychologist whose lab images the human ventral stream — the pairing is the point. The seminar reads each finding from three sides at once: what psychophysics shows about rapid object perception, what electrophysiology and fMRI show about the cortex doing it, and how well a deep network trained on images predicts either. The emphasis is on recent empirical findings, methods and theoretical disputes rather than settled material, and the unit range lets students take it as a light reading course or a project.

## Syllabus

[Seventeen sessions](https://canvas.stanford.edu/courses/133159/assignments/syllabus), built outward from one question: deep networks are good models of adult vision and bad models of how that vision came to be.

1. **Framing** (weeks 1-2): processing streams; goal-driven deep learning models of sensory cortex; from brain measurements to deep neural networks.
2. **Development** (weeks 3-5): synaptic activity and the construction of cortical circuits; what babies actually see, what they look at, and how they decide; using what they look at to train visual models; whether there is a critical period for developing representations.
3. **The debate** (week 6): is visual experience necessary for the formation of cortical domains?
4. **Maps and unsupervised learning** (weeks 7-8): how spatiotopic maps emerge; five lessons from infant learning; self-supervised learning and the brain.
5. **Category representations** (weeks 9-10): training networks on infant gaze data; which categories develop first in the baby brain; cortical recycling during childhood; semantic development models.
