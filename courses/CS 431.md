# CS 431: High-level Vision: From Neurons to Deep Neural Networks

Co-taught by a computational neuroscientist and a psychologist whose lab images the human ventral stream — the pairing is the point. The seminar reads each finding from three sides at once: what psychophysics shows about rapid object perception, what electrophysiology and fMRI show about the cortex doing it, and how well a deep network trained on images predicts either. The emphasis is on recent empirical findings, methods and theoretical disputes rather than settled material, and the unit range lets students take it as a light reading course or a project.

## Syllabus

From the catalog description; no public syllabus.

1. How computations in the brain enable rapid and efficient object perception.
2. The question taken from multiple perspectives, drawing on recent research in psychology, neuroscience and computer science.
3. Discussion of recent empirical findings, methods, and theoretical debates in the field.

None stated. The reading assumes familiarity with deep networks on one side and cortical anatomy on the other; the instructors' own courses ([CS 231N](CS%20231N.md), PSYCH 209) are the usual routes in.
