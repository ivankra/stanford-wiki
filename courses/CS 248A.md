# CS 248A: Computer Graphics: Rendering, Geometry, and Image Manipulation

Kayvon Fatahalian's introduction to graphics, organized around the claim its first lecture title makes: whether a representation is good depends on the task. Rasterization and ray tracing arrive together as two ways to do the same thing; meshes, voxel grids, signed-distance fields, Gaussian splats and NeuralSDFs arrive in one lecture as alternatives to be converted between. Winter 2026 rebuilt the four programming assignments from scratch in Python and Slang, a GPU shading language, and the last third of the term — differentiable rendering, ReSTIR, neural denoising, world models — is about learning-based methods rather than the fixed pipeline. Graded on assignments plus two exams, one of which is oral.

## Syllabus

Twenty lectures over ten weeks, per the [schedule](https://gfxcourses.stanford.edu/cs248a/winter26).

1. **Weeks 1–3, representations**: drawing lines and triangles by point sampling; sampling theory and aliasing; transforms and perspective projection; geometry representations and conversions between them; BVHs, K-D trees and sparse grids.
2. **Weeks 4–5, the forward pipeline**: texture mapping and pre-filtering; the GPU rasterization pipeline, Z-buffering and alpha compositing; radiometry, BRDFs and the reflection equation.
3. **Weeks 5–7, light transport and inversion**: differentiable rendering and scene recovery (NeRF, plenoxels, 3D Gaussians); specular reflection and Monte Carlo integration; direct lighting estimation; path tracing with Russian roulette; real-time ray tracing on GPU hardware with ReSTIR and neural denoising.
4. **Weeks 7–9, images**: color theory; image and video compression; the digital camera pipeline from RAW to tone mapping.
5. **Week 10**: VR and AR rendering — judder, latency, foveated rendering — then neural rendering and world models, and a research wrap-up.
